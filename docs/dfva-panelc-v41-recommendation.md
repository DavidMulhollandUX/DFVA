## Summary of the recommendation

Keep C1–C5 exactly as adopted. Add a second, separately reported sub-scale of **three items
(W1–W3, /9)** covering the capabilities and curriculum forms that make a graduate effective in
**any** workplace, AI-integrated or not — professional communication and conduct, authentic task
design, and work-situated learning. Anchor them on the Higher Education Standards Framework 2021
(clauses 1.4.2 and 5.4.1), the national Employer Satisfaction Survey domains, and the
authentic-assessment and work-integrated-learning literatures. Close the three places where the
new items would otherwise double-count against C1, C2 and C4. Report the two sub-scores side by
side and **never sum them**.

| | v4.0 Panel C | v4.1 Panel C |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sub-scale A | C1–C5, /15 | C1–C5, /15 — **unchanged constructs**, two anchor edits for non-overlap |
| Sub-scale W | — | W1 Professional communication & conduct · W2 Authentic task design · W3 Work-situated learning, /9 |
| Construct authority | TEQSA adaptive capabilities (Lodge et al. 2026) | …plus HESF (Threshold Standards) 2021 cl. 1.4.2/5.4.1, QILT ESS domains, Gulikers/Villarroel, Kuh, Jackson |
| Design rules | R1 declarative anchors · R2 assessment evidence · R3 cited evidence | …plus **R4** authenticity is scored from task features, never from the label |
| Position axis | adaptiveness | adaptiveness (unchanged) — W is reported beside the axis, not folded into it |
| Enumeration state space | 3⁵ = 243 | 3⁸ = 6,561 (same algorithm) |
| Gates | G1, G2 | G1, G2 — unchanged |

## 1. Why change again, eight weeks after v4

A/Prof Lyons's review of the v4 draft endorsed the adaptive-capability re-anchoring and named a
second gap:

> *"I agree with those factors as the ones that will make a student more successful in an AI
> world. Keep those in. Just need to also add in factors that make a student be successful in any
> workplace including AI ones… professional skills (eg teamwork), deeper learning approaches (eg
> placements), authentic assessments."*

This is the **same defect class as the one v4 fixed, in a different region of the construct
space** — construct underrepresentation (Messick 1989; the term used in Woods, Lyons et al. 2026)
— and it is structural rather than accidental. v4 derived its items top-down from one framework
whose scope is, by design, adaptation to generative AI. Anything the TEQSA capability set does
not name, v4 cannot see. Three families it does not name:

1. **Professional skills.** Teamwork enters v4 only inside C1, and only as *distributed
   cognition* — coordination coupled to tools and AI systems. Communicating to a client, a
   patient or the public; conducting oneself reliably and accountably; being judged by someone
   other than an examiner — none of these are scored anywhere (WR §2).
2. **Deeper learning approaches.** Work-integrated learning appears in v4 only as one exemplar
   inside a C4 anchor and one inside a C1 anchor. A program with a required clinical placement
   year and one with a self-scoped capstone can score identically (WR §2).
3. **Authentic assessment.** The construct is absent; the word does not occur in the instrument.

The stakes are the same as they were for v4: 9 of 34 reference programs tie exactly at the
adaptiveness median, so item definitions decide quadrant labels for a quarter of the placed
portfolio.

## 2. Why the fix is a sub-scale, not five more items

The three families share a construct — *effectiveness in a workplace, whatever technology it
runs on* — with its own authority, its own evidence base, and a different theory of change from
the adaptive capabilities. Folding them into C1–C5 would recreate exactly the defect this
review found: one construct's items silently absorbing another's content, which is the pathology
that retired the Irreplaceability bonus (LR §2.1 defect 4).

Two named sub-scales inside Panel C, reported separately, do three things a flat eight-item list
would not:

- They keep the endorsed instrument **visibly intact**. "Keep those in" is satisfied literally:
  C1–C5 and the /15 adaptiveness scale are unchanged, and the published position axis still runs
  on adaptiveness alone.
- They make the addition **falsifiable**. Whether C1–C5 and W1–W3 resolve as two correlated
  factors or collapse into one is a testable structural hypothesis (§6). A flat list cannot be
  wrong in this way; a two-sub-scale claim can.
- They keep the honest scope boundary visible: the W sub-scale is not a durability claim. A
  program can be excellent at preparing people for work and still be exposed.

## 3. The three new items

Design rules R1–R3 apply unchanged. R4 is added (§4).

### W1 · Professional communication & conduct

**Construct:** communicating disciplinary work, and conducting oneself, as a professional with
people who are not your examiner — the genres the profession actually uses, audiences beyond the
teaching team, and assessed standards of reliability, ethics and accountability.
**Evidence base:** HESF cl. 1.4.2(b) — generic skills *"and their application in the context of
the field(s) of education or disciplines involved"* — and 1.4.2(c), employment-related knowledge
and skills as a learning-outcome requirement; the ESS Foundation, Collaborative and Employability
domains; CSfW cluster 2; NACE Communication/Professionalism/Teamwork; Deming (2017) and Heckman
et al. (2006) on the labour-market return to social and non-cognitive capability.
**Non-overlap:** C1 scores coordination across people *and tools/AI systems*; W1 scores
communication to audiences and professional conduct. Barrie (2006, 2007) is the reason R2 binds
hardest here: stated graduate attributes are not evidence of anything.

| Level | Anchor (documented curriculum evidence) |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | All core assessment is in academic genres addressed to the marker (exams, essays, lab reports); no professional-genre or spoken communication is assessed. |
| 1 | Communication or professional conduct appears in learning outcomes or graduate attributes; core assessment adds presentation to peers/staff, but no professional genre and no audience beyond the teaching team. |
| 2 | At least one core assessment requires a recognised professional genre or an audience beyond the teaching team — client brief, consultancy or policy advice, clinical handover, pitch, public-facing artefact — judged against criteria drawn from professional practice. |
| 3 | Professional communication is assessed repeatedly and progressively across the program **and** at least one core assessment is delivered to, or judged by, a real external audience or practitioner, with professional conduct or accountability explicitly among the assessed criteria. |

### W2 · Authentic task design

**Construct:** how closely core assessment resembles the criterion situation of the discipline's
own professional practice — the task, its context, the form of the result, and the criteria used
to judge it (Gulikers et al. 2004), and its realism and cognitive challenge (Villarroel et al.
2018).
**Evidence base:** Gulikers et al.'s five dimensions, and their finding that authenticity is a
*continuum* defined *"in relation to the criterion situation derived from professional practice"*;
Villarroel et al.'s systematic review; Sokhanvar et al. (2021); Gibbs & Simpson (2004) for the
mechanism — assessment governs what and how students study; Fawns et al. (2024) for the
constraint.
**Non-overlap:** explicitly **excludes evaluative judgement**, Villarroel's third dimension,
because that is C2. W2 scores the fidelity of the task; C2 scores the capability to appraise
quality; W3 scores actual workplace immersion.

| Level | Anchor |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Core assessment is entirely decontextualised — exams, problem sets and essays with no situational framing. |
| 1 | Contextualised or scenario-framed tasks appear (case studies, worked scenarios), but the artefact produced and the criteria applied remain academic. |
| 2 | At least one core assessment reproduces a professional task end to end: a real or realistic problem, producing the artefact a practitioner would produce, judged against criteria drawn from practice. |
| 3 | Such tasks are the program's assessment spine rather than a single instance (a capstone plus earlier scaffolding, or at least one per stage) **and** at least one carries a genuine constraint of practice — an ambiguous or externally supplied problem, real resource/time limits, a consequential audience, or the profession's own standards of performance. |

### W3 · Work-situated learning

**Construct:** extended, supervised, assessed participation in a real workplace or professional
community — the "deeper learning approaches (eg placements)" sense of the feedback.
**Evidence base:** HESF cl. 5.4.1, which makes WIL a separately regulated category with a
supervision requirement (and therefore handbook-visible); Kuh (2008) on high-impact practices;
Jackson (2016) on pre-professional identity formed through communities of practice — the
mechanism simulation cannot supply; the Universities Accord (2024).
**Bounded claim:** Jackson & Collings (2018) found WIL did **not** raise full-time employment
rates in two Australian samples, with evidence of better employment *relevance*. W3's warrant is
skill development, employment relevance and professional identity — never employment rates. An
instrument that implied otherwise would be overclaiming and Panel D would not support it.

| Level | Anchor |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | No work-integrated learning, placement, practicum or community-based project appears anywhere in the course structure. |
| 1 | Work-situated learning exists only as an elective, an optional internship, or an unassessed extracurricular or careers activity. |
| 2 | A core unit places students in a real workplace or professional-community setting with practitioner supervision and assessment — placement, practicum, live client project, community-based project — but it is short or stands alone. |
| 3 | Substantial required work-situated learning: an extended placement or practicum, or a sequence of them, in the core, supervised by a practitioner, assessed, with accountability to the host and structured reflection on professional practice. |

Simulation is deliberately **not** scored in W3 — it scores in W2. This keeps W2 available to
every program regardless of placement capacity (a fully classroom-based program can score W2 = 3)
and stops W3 becoming a proxy for discipline type.

## 4. R4 — the new design rule

> **R4.** Authenticity is a continuum measured against the discipline's own criterion situation,
> not a label (Gulikers et al. 2004). Never score a program up because a handbook says
> "authentic", "real-world", "industry-relevant" or "work-ready" — score only the documented
> features of the task.

This is forced by Fawns, Bearman, Dawson et al. (2024), who warn that the label *"risks becoming a
distraction or a thought-terminating cliché"* and that authenticity should be treated as *"a set
of aspirational principles within a broader pedagogical framework"*. Without R4, W2 would reward
marketing prose — the failure mode R2 already prevents for outcome statements, in a new guise.

## 5. The three overlaps closed in the same change

Adding items without re-cutting the existing ones reintroduces halo. All three edits ship
together or none do.

| # | Edit | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **C1 level 3** drops the exemplar *"supervised placements with multidisciplinary accountability"*; level 3 is now human-plus-tool coordination only | placement evidence is W3's construct |
| 2 | **C4 level 3** drops the route *"work-integrated learning requiring performance in a context not taught"*, leaving the self-scoped capstone and assessed knowledge-gap identification | same |
| 3 | **W2 excludes evaluative judgement** by construct definition | it is C2 |

A fourth guard is written into the scoring prompt: *one construct, one home* — each piece of
evidence scores in exactly one item, and an apparent fit to two is recorded in `ambiguities`.

After these edits every construct has exactly one home, which is the property the retirement of
the Irreplaceability bonus was meant to establish and that this change would otherwise break.

## 6. What this does to the validity argument

Improvements at three steps of the v4 validation sequence (recommendation §5, ordered on Kane 2013):

| Step | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| 2 · Content validity | The content panel now rates a *complete* construct. Under v4.0 any competent reviewer asked "what's missing?" finds what Lyons found, so the CVI would have certified a known-incomplete instrument. |
| 5 · Internal structure | **A falsifiable prediction:** C1–C5 and W1–W3 should resolve as two correlated factors. If they collapse to one, the sub-scale distinction is wrong and should be abandoned — stated in advance, in writing. |
| 6 · Extrapolation | The W sub-scale connects to an external criterion v4.0 had no route to: ESS domains are measured nationally, by employers, at field-of-education grain. The prediction that W scores relate to ESS Collaborative/Employability ratings is checkable. |

The IRR study (decision 2 of the v4 log) must now be re-pointed at **eight** items and two gates,
not five and two. W2 and W3 are the items to watch: W3 should be the most reliable item in the
instrument (placements are unambiguously documented), W2 the least (it requires a judgement about
resemblance to professional practice). If W2's agreement is poor, the honest options are sharper
anchors or dropping the item — not a reliability figure quietly averaged over the panel.

## 7. The cost, stated

1. **The /15 scale.** v4 §3 promised not to break it. Panel C is now /24 as a bookkeeping total,
   which the reports must never present as a headline score. What was actually being protected —
   the exact-enumeration stability layer — survives: 3⁸ = 6,561 perturbation states against
   v3.1's 3⁵ = 243. The algorithm is unchanged; only the state space grows, and both are trivially
   enumerable. **The position axis stays adaptiveness /15**, so the published matrix, the
   envelopes and the reference medians are unaffected by W.
2. **Re-scoring, not back-filling.** W1–W3 need their own handbook evidence. A program scored
   under 4.0-draft has no W score, and none may be inferred from its C scores. `V4_META` tracks
   `workplaceScored` separately from `scored`, and the report page states the absence rather than
   rendering empty rows.
3. **One migration cycle, not two.** Both re-scalings ship in the single published v4 migration
   cycle (v4 §4) with the v3.1 → v4.1 table. Splitting them across two cycles would cost more and
   confuse the audit trail.

## 8. What this recommendation does not do

- **It does not score "employability".** CareerEDGE and Tomlinson's capital model include
  self-efficacy, emotional intelligence, career self-management and social capital — student and
  network properties, not curriculum properties, and invisible to a handbook. W1–W3 are the
  curriculum-visible residue (WR §6).
- **It does not add a self-management / initiative item.** ESS's Employability domain and CSfW's
  "Get the work done" cluster would support one, but its handbook-visible evidence is largely the
  evidence W1 and W2 already cite. Excluded on parsimony; revisit if the content panel disagrees.
- **It does not touch Panel A, the gates, Panel D, or the quadrant function.**
- **It does not change the publication gate.** v4.1 remains a working draft; nothing renders as an
  assessment of record before the §4 migration cycle.

## 9. Declared limitations (carried into the methodology note and every report)

1. A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required placements can
   disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or relocation (Accord 2024; Fawns et al.
   on ableism and elitism).
2. W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation, so cross-disciplinary W2
   comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
3. W3 excludes simulation by design, so programs whose professional norm is high-fidelity
   simulation may score low on W3 for a defensible pedagogic choice. The sub-scores are read
   together, never merged.
4. The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student self-report; W2's
   warrant is documented design fidelity plus the assessment-drives-learning mechanism.
5. WIL is not claimed to raise employment rates (Jackson & Collings 2018).
