Kayley Lyons: research profile and method comparison

This note summarises the research programme of A/Prof Kayley Lyons and compares the competency-mapping method reported in Mun, Chanchlani, Lyons & Gray (2024) with the DFVA v3.1 instrument. The comparison is structural: it sets out what each method takes as input, what operation it performs, and what it treats as a possible source of error.

METHODOLOGICAL PRECEDENT

Lyons, Lobczowski, Greene, Whitley & McLaughlin (2021), Computers & Education: “Using a design-based research approach to develop and study a web-based tool.”

The paper sets out a procedure for developing an educational web tool and studying it within the same research programme. This is the situation Evidura is in, and the paper offers an existing design rather than one that would need to be constructed.

02 — TWO 2026 EVIDENCE SYNTHESES

Reviews of artificial intelligence in health professions education

Lyons is a co-author on two 2026 syntheses that screened 18,607 records between them. Both bear on DFVA: the first provides an external reference for the AI-literacy construct, and the second reports both an absence of higher-level evaluation evidence and a general methodological limitation in the field.

Astbury, Fortune, Dal Ponte, Lyons & Shaw (2026)

Generative artificial intelligence for teaching and assessment in health professions education: a scoping review. Nurse Education in Practice 91:104697. The author-contributions statement records that Lyons and Shaw conceptualised the study.

The review reports that 41 of the 73 full texts were excluded because the studies did not use the term “artificial intelligence” in the title, abstract or keywords.

Relevance to DFVA. The review produces an implementation checklist and a taxonomy of curriculum topics, either of which could serve as an external reference for the AI-literacy item in Panel C. The review also reports that governance and regulation appeared in 11 per cent of the programs surveyed, which is relevant to whether the item discriminates between programs.

Woods, Lyons, Van Der Vegt, Olsen, Huang, Khor, Xu & Sullivan (2026)

Assessing the effectiveness of artificial intelligence education and training for healthcare workers: a systematic review. BMC Medical Education 26:549. PROSPERO CRD42024575061. 9,632 records screened; 27 studies included, covering approximately 2,160 learners. Lyons is second author.

Relevance to DFVA. No included study evaluated its program at Kirkpatrick-Barr Level 4. This absence of downstream outcome evidence is what the graduate-outcome dimension in DFVA attempts to detect, and the Kirkpatrick-Barr hierarchy is an established framework for describing it.

REPORTED LIMITATION IN THE FIELD
“most authors developed their own constructs, tests, and surveys without evidence of validity, reliability, or generalizability… increases the risk of multiple types of measurement errors, including construct underrepresentation and method bias.”

Woods, Lyons et al. (2026). The observation applies to DFVA as currently specified. Panel C is a five-item scale, scored 0 to 3 by a language model, for which no rater study has been published.

03 — METHOD COMPARISON

Two procedures for assessing a curriculum against a reference

Both methods reduce a curriculum to structured statements, compare those statements against a reference, and produce a judgment about the program's position. They differ in what each treats as the likely source of error, and therefore in what each does to guard against it.

04 — POINTS OF COMPLEMENTARITY

Each method addresses a limitation present in the other

The two methods have non-overlapping weaknesses. DFVA v3.1 quantifies rating uncertainty but assumes the rate at which raters err; the 2024 method obtains expert agreement but does not report what that agreement was or what would follow from disagreement.

The v3.1 error rate is assumed rather than measured

The uncertainty layer in v3.1 is parameterised by e, the per-item rater-error rate. The specification states this directly:

“No inter-rater study has been run, so none of the three is empirically grounded (v3 open item R9). Publishing only the middle column presents a choice of assumption as a property of the portfolio.”

An expert panel independently confirming a mapping, as used in stage 2 of the 2024 method, is one procedure by which e could be estimated from data.

The 2024 method reports no uncertainty

The mapping is reported as settled. No rater-agreement statistic, sensitivity analysis, or account of what a second panel's disagreement would have implied is given. The limitation the authors do state is narrower:

“Consultation for this project was informed by reports that included student evaluations but mainly occurred at the faculty educators and executive level.”

The enumeration procedure in v3.1 is a means of identifying which mapping decisions determine the result and which do not.

The number of programs reported as boundary cases depends on the assumed rate rather than on the underlying scores. Across the 34 placed programs:

The count varies by a factor of seven across the range of assumed rates. Estimating the rate from data would therefore constrain a result that is at present determined by an assumption. This is a study Lyons is qualified to design and conduct.

05 — LIMITATIONS DOCUMENTED IN v3.1

The modal-probability distribution is bimodal and the published threshold falls inside the lower cluster

Exact enumeration showed structure that the earlier sampling procedure had obscured. The 34 modal probabilities take 13 distinct values, forming two clusters separated by a band 0.13 wide in which no program falls. The published m ≥ 0.80 rule cuts within the lower cluster rather than between the two.

What v3.1 changed

The Monte-Carlo procedure was replaced by exact enumeration of the 243 states. The published dual-label count had previously depended on the random seed, returning 0, 1 or 2 on re-runs. It is now deterministic.

What v3.1 discloses

53 of 170 scored items, or 31 per cent, sit at the top of the scale, which makes the error model asymmetric with an expected drift of −0.075 points. A symmetric alternative was tested and no program changed label regime.

What remains open

The rater study (R9); the hybrid-crosswalk trade-off; the evidence-tier trade-off; coverage bias, since 32 of 66 programs are unplaced; and re-anchoring of the AI-literacy item.

SUMMARY

DFVA v3.1 applies a precise uncertainty procedure to a measurement that has not been validated. It enumerates the consequences of a rating error whose rate has not been estimated, across five items that are not anchored to an external framework.

The 2024 method has the opposite profile: an externally anchored and expert-confirmed mapping, with no account of its own uncertainty and a refresh cycle measured in years.

Neither method is complete on its own, and the limitations are of a kind that the other addresses. Combining them is a defensible basis for a joint study.
