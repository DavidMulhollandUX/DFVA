## Summary of the recommendation

Replace the five v3.1 Panel C items (D2, D3, D7, B, D5) with **five new items (C1–C5)
anchored on the four adaptive capabilities defined in TEQSA's *Assuring quality learning in a
gen AI-integrated future* (Lodge et al., June 2026), plus an inquiry item**, and move
disciplinary depth from the scored sum to a **gate**, where TEQSA's architecture places it
(foundation, not capability). Retire the Irreplaceability bonus (B). Keep the 5-item × 0–3
structure, so the adaptiveness scale remains /15 and **the entire v3.1 exact-enumeration
stability machinery (3⁵ = 243 perturbation states, envelopes, stability classes) carries over
unchanged**. Publish as a deliberate v4 assessment cycle with a v3.1 → v4 migration table;
scores are not comparable across the boundary and must not be presented as if they were.

| | v3.1 Panel C | v4 Panel C (recommended) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scored items | D2 Systems Thinking · D3 Technical Depth · D7 Research Rigour · B Irreplaceability · D5 AI Literacy | C1 Distributed cognition & relational capability · C2 Hybrid metacognition & evaluative judgement · C3 Digital & AI literacy incl. governance · C4 Life-long learning & transfer · C5 Inquiry & evidence generation |
| Scale | Σ 5 × (0–3) = /15 | Σ 5 × (0–3) = /15 (unchanged structure; **not comparable values**) |
| Gates | D4 Decision-Making · D6 Domain Depth | G1 Disciplinary foundation (absorbs D3 + D6) · G2 Decision-Making (D4, unchanged) |
| Bonus | B in the sum | **Retired** |
| Construct authority | Self-defined ("capabilities that resist AI substitution") | TEQSA adaptive capabilities (Lodge et al. 2026), externally defined and regulator-commissioned |

## 1. Why change at all — the four defects (LR §2.1)

1. **Self-referential construct.** v3.1's construct is defined only by its own five anchors;
   there is no external referent against which an item can be judged missing. The TEQSA
   framework now supplies exactly that referent, under nearly the same name, eight weeks old,
   regulator-commissioned, with four UoM co-authors (LR §3, Tier 1).
2. **Construct underrepresentation** (Messick 1989; the term Woods, **Lyons** et al. 2026 use).
   Metacognition, evaluative judgement, transfer, ethical reasoning, human–AI teaming and
   relational capability are all absent from v3.1 Panel C, and every one of them is named in a
   current authoritative framework (LR §3).
3. **The labour economics contradicts the item weighting.** D3's "substitution barrier"
   rationale is directly tested by Deming & Noray (2020, *QJE*): the applied-STEM premium falls
   44% → 14% between ages 24 and 35 because applied technical content obsolesces fastest.
   Deming (2017, *QJE*, Figure IV, verified): social-skill-intensive employment grew +11.8 pp
   of the US workforce 1980–2012 while math-intensive/low-social work shrank. v3.1 scores the
   former as adaptiveness and omits the latter entirely.
4. **B is halo, not signal.** Its anchors restate D3+D6+D8; its item–total r = 0.65 — the
   stated reason for keeping it — is what a general-impression item produces (LR §2.1
   defect 4).

The stakes are not cosmetic: 9 of 34 reference programs tie exactly at the adaptiveness
median, so item definitions decide quadrant labels for a quarter of the placed portfolio.

## 2. The recommended instrument

### 2.1 Design rules (applied to every anchor below)

- **R1 — Anchors are declarative statements about documented curriculum evidence**, on the
  model of Brynjolfsson, Mitchell & Rock's SML rubric (21 checkable statements, not holistic
  prose; LR §3, Tier 3). "Assessment requires students to justify when they relied on or
  overrode an AI system" is checkable in a handbook; "graduates exercise evaluative judgement"
  is not.
- **R2 — Level 3 requires *assessment* evidence; level 1 is the outcome-statement level.**
  A capability that appears in learning outcomes but is never assessed scores 1, everywhere,
  uniformly. This operationalises the constructive-alignment warrant (LR §4) instead of
  assuming it, and it attacks the ceiling problem: 53/170 v3.1 items (31%) sit at ceiling,
  driving the −0.075 asymmetric perturbation drift (v3.1 §3.4). Level 3 should be rare by
  construction.
- **R3 — Every score cites handbook evidence lines** in the program's evidence file, as now.

### 2.2 The five scored items

#### C1 · Distributed cognition & relational capability

**Construct** (TEQSA capability #2): how cognitive processes, information and tasks are shared
across people, tools, artefacts and gen AI systems — teams, human–AI collaboration,
coordinating roles and resources.
**Evidence base:** Deming (2017) — the strongest employment growth is where social and
analytical skills combine; Frey & Osborne (2017) — social intelligence is one of the three
engineering bottlenecks; TEQSA (2026). **Absorbs** D2 (the integrative-reasoning half) and
**restores D8**, which v2 dropped from the panel despite its own v1 grounding.

| Level | Anchor (documented curriculum evidence) |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | No collaborative, stakeholder or team-based work is assessed anywhere in the core. |
| 1 | Collaboration/teamwork/communication appears in learning outcomes, but no core unit assesses it. |
| 2 | At least one core unit assesses collaborative practice — group projects with individual accountability, client or stakeholder work, interprofessional activity. |
| 3 | Assessed collaborative practice recurs across the program **and** at least one assessment requires coordinating work across people *and* tools/AI systems (e.g. team capstones with documented role/tool allocation, supervised placements with multidisciplinary accountability). |

#### C2 · Hybrid metacognition & evaluative judgement

**Construct** (TEQSA capability #3): regulation of thinking and learning within any cognitive
system including human–AI networks — evaluative judgement, deciding when to rely on or
override a tool, co-regulation, ethical reasoning about use.
**Evidence base:** Tai, Ajjawi, Boud, Dawson & Panadero (2018) — evaluative judgement as "the
capability to make decisions about the quality of work of oneself and others"; Lodge et al.
(2023) — "the foundational skill for the AI era is self-regulated learning"; TEQSA (2026)
propositions 2 and 4 (process-focused assessment). **New** — the largest single gap in v3.1
(LR §2.1 defect 2).

| Level | Anchor |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | No assessment requires students to appraise the quality of work — their own, peers', or a tool's. |
| 1 | Reflection or self-assessment appears in outcomes or graduate attributes, but no core unit assesses it against criteria. |
| 2 | Core assessment includes criterion-referenced appraisal of quality: peer review, structured critique, portfolio with standards, marking against exemplars. |
| 3 | Assessment requires students to **document and justify reliance decisions** — when they relied on or overrode a tool, source or collaborator; defend judgements of AI-output quality; or evidence strategy adjustment over time (process-focused assessment). |

#### C3 · Digital & AI literacy, including governance

**Construct** (TEQSA capability #1): using digital tools including gen AI effectively,
ethically and safely — extended, per TEQSA, to critical understanding of principles,
limitations, ethics, societal impact and power structures.
**Evidence base:** DigComp 3.0 / Australian Digital Capability Framework (five areas, AI-inflected
competences verified in LR §3.1); Long & Magerko (2020); UNESCO (2024); MAILS and AICOS as
item-content sources. **Re-anchors D5** one level up: the Lodge et al. (2025) framework's
Principle 6 warns explicitly against "skills like 'prompt engineering' that will have limited
future utility" — which is the level v3.1's D5 anchors are written at.

| Level | Anchor |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | No digital/AI content in any core unit or program-level outcome. |
| 1 | AI or digital tools appear only as electives or as tool operation/training — the operational level TEQSA identifies as non-durable. |
| 2 | Core units address AI capabilities **and** limitations/ethics; use with limits discussed and assessed. |
| 3 | Core assessment requires **critique or governance** of AI systems in the discipline's own context — bias, accountability, transparency, regulation or data governance — beyond tool operation. |

#### C4 · Life-long learning & transfer

**Construct** (TEQSA capability #4): sustaining motivation, capability and adaptability to
learn continuously — identifying knowledge gaps, independently acquiring skills, transferring
learning across tasks, domains and tools.
**Evidence base:** NRC (2012) — deeper learning as the process producing *transferable*
knowledge; adaptive expertise (Hatano & Inagaki; the 2025 *AHSE* scoping review's flexibility
and metacognition themes); Lodge et al. (2025) Principle 6 ("cultivating adaptive expertise
that enables students to transfer learning strategies"). **Restores D9**, re-anchored on
*transfer* — observable in assessment design — rather than review recency, which was
unscoreable from handbooks (item–total r = 0.06, hence its v2 removal).

| Level | Anchor |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Fixed content sequence; no assessment requires applying methods outside the taught context. |
| 1 | Transfer is claimed in outcomes ("apply knowledge in new settings") but not assessed. |
| 2 | At least one core assessment requires application to **novel or unfamiliar** problems — case variation, unseen datasets, cross-context projects. |
| 3 | The program documents structured progression toward independent learning: self-scoped capstone or research project, assessed identification of one's own knowledge gaps, or work-integrated learning requiring performance in a context not taught. |

#### C5 · Inquiry & evidence generation

**Construct:** the capacity to generate primary evidence and defend methodology.
**Evidence base:** Boyer (1990); Brew (2006); Frey & Osborne's creative-intelligence
bottleneck. **Retains D7 essentially intact** — it was the defensible item; its anchors
already largely satisfy R1/R2.

| Level | Anchor |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Secondary synthesis only; no research-methods training. |
| 1 | Introductory methods unit; literature-review assessment. |
| 2 | Students design and conduct an inquiry with methodology selection and data collection, assessed. |
| 3 | A substantial project generating primary evidence is **required** (not one route among several), with methodology defended under scrutiny (viva, defence, or staged supervised review). |

### 2.3 The gates

**G1 · Disciplinary foundation (new; absorbs D3 and the D6 gate).** TEQSA's architecture is
explicit: deep disciplinary knowledge is "the epistemic foundation for authentic judgement and
expertise" — *underneath* the four capabilities, not one of them, and adaptive capabilities
"must be learned within the context of deep disciplinary knowledge." Deming & Noray (2020)
give the labour-market half of the same argument: technical depth is a real asset whose
premium decays, so it belongs as a precondition, not as adaptiveness evidence. G1 **PASSES**
when the program documents a coherent specialist core with progressive technical or
methodological depth (all-compulsory specialist core, accredited sequence, or staged
prerequisite chain); **FAILS** on generic/interchangeable content. A FAIL flags the program
regardless of C1–C5 — the v2 gate semantics, unchanged.

**G2 · Decision-making under uncertainty (D4, unchanged).** Retained as-is; saturated as a
scored item (62.1% modal) but still a meaningful floor.

Gates remain excluded from the sum and displayed alongside Panel C, exactly as in v2/v3.1.

### 2.4 What is retired, and why that is safe

**B (Irreplaceability)** leaves the instrument entirely. The claim it tried to score — that
the program combines depth, domain and judgement in a way that resists substitution — is what
the published **position** already expresses: exposure × adaptiveness, with G1 attesting the
depth. Scoring it separately double-counted its inputs and imported halo (LR §2.1 defect 4).

**D2's "systems thinking" label** disappears, but its content does not: trade-off and
failure-mode reasoning is where D2 overlapped D4 (gated) and the integrative reasoning is
absorbed into C1 and C2. Nothing v3.1 measured is silently dropped; every v3.1 item's
disposition is stated above, which is the migration-table discipline the v3.1 extension
protocol requires.

## 3. What deliberately does not change

- **5 items × 4 levels.** The v3.1 stability layer — exact enumeration over 3⁵ = 243
  perturbation states, adaptiveness envelopes, `stabilityClass`, the bimodality analysis —
  transfers to v4 **without modification of the algorithm**, only re-computation on new
  scores. Going to six scored items (as the LR §5 sketch had it) would grow the state space
  to 3⁶ = 729 (still enumerable) but break the /15 scale, every display surface, and the
  reference-table format for no measurement gain: the sixth item was disciplinary depth,
  which TEQSA itself places outside the capability set. The gate is the architecturally
  faithful home.
- **Panel A, the gates mechanism, Panel D, and the quadrant function** are untouched.
- **NA/renormalisation semantics** (v1 composite) are unaffected — this recommendation is
  scoped to Panel C.

## 4. Migration protocol (the honest cost, made procedural)

1. **v4 is a new instrument.** No v3.1 adaptiveness score is comparable to a v4 score. The
   reference medians (exposure 90.9 · adaptiveness 10) re-base on the re-scored reference
   cohort; every placed program can move quadrant. Per the v3.1 extension-cohort protocol,
   silent re-positioning is forbidden — so v4 ships as a **published assessment cycle** with:
   - a **migration table**: all placed programs, v3.1 position → v4 position, side by side
     (the pattern of the v1 → v2 migration table, which is the existing audit-trail precedent);
   - a re-derived reference stability table (the §5.3 guard, recomputed once and then frozen);
   - both instruments' pages co-visible during transition, as v2/v3/v3.1 are today.
2. **Score from the same handbook vintage** for all programs in the cycle, so v4 differences
   are instrument differences, not document-drift differences.
3. **Evidence files gain a `panelCv4` block** per program (rationale + handbook line citations
   per item, per R3) — extending `dfva/source/evidence/`, not replacing it.

## 5. Validation sequence (answers "what's your validity evidence?" in the right order)

Ordered by Kane (2013): scrutiny goes to the weakest inference, but *feasibility* ordering
starts with content — with the extrapolation warrant named in writing from day one.

| Step | What | Evidence aspect | Who / cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **State the IUA in the v4 methodology note**: scoring → generalization → extrapolation → implication, with the constructive-alignment warrant named as an explicit assumption (LR §6) | The argument frame | Free; drafting only |
| 2 | **Content validity**: expert panel + CVI over C1–C5 and both gates; crosswalk to CEPH-22 / WHO-ASPHER (Lyons's domain) and AMIA/AHICF (the 2024 paper's frameworks) | Content | The natural first co-authorship ask; candidate panel exists (Lyons + the four UoM TEQSA co-authors) |
| 3 | **Pilot scoring** on a stratified 10-program sample (the IRR protocol's existing sampling frame) to test R1/R2 scoreability and ceiling behaviour before committing | Response process (first pass) | In-house; days |
| 4 | **IRR study on v4** — the existing R1–R5 protocol re-pointed at the new items. **Do not run it on v3.1 first** (LR §5.2): certifying a retiring instrument wastes the most expensive evidence in the plan | Reliability + response process | The budgeted study |
| 5 | **Internal structure** once ≥60 programs are re-scored: do C1–C5 hold as factors; ceiling rate vs the 31% baseline; median behaviour | Internal structure | In-house analysis |
| 6 | **Extrapolation probes** (the weakest link): agreement of v4 scores with the published Mun/Chanchlani/Lyons/Gray CC-BY competency mappings on the same UoM subjects; relation to QILT/JIR outcomes | Extrapolation / relations | The second co-authored study — and the novel paper (LR §4) |

## 6. Scope notes (stated, not silent)

- **Indigenous data governance.** Named as uncovered by two independent sources — the
  Mun/Chanchlani/Lyons/Gray (2024) mapping and Principle 4 of the Lodge et al. (2025)
  framework (CARE Principles). v4 gives it a partial home — C3 level 3 counts assessed data
  governance, which includes Indigenous data governance where a program teaches it — but v4
  does **not** score it as a distinct construct. That is a declared limitation in the v4
  methodology note, with the CARE Principles cited, rather than an omission discovered by a
  reviewer.
- **Perception/manipulation** (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck) remains unscored: physical
  and clinical skill enters only through C1 level 3 and G1. Declared, acceptable for a
  handbook-based instrument.
- **The §7 display issue** (unmarked shift from observational to inferential sections in the
  reports — the substance of Lyons's "needs conceptual work") is independent of this
  recommendation and proceeds separately.
