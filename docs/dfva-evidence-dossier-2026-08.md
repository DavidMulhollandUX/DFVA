# Evidence dossier: AI, curriculum and graduate durability (August 2026)

Source material assembled from four parallel research sweeps on 2026-08-24, triggered by the
Melbourne CSHE symposium *Thriving in Uncertain Futures: Education, Employability and
Professional Agility* (8 September 2026).

Use this as the citation pool for v4 §4 (market evidence) and §5 (curriculum implications),
for the methods and limitations artefacts, and for collaborator targeting.

Status labels: **[PR]** peer-reviewed, **[WP]** working paper or preprint, **[Gov]**
government or regulator, **[Ind]** industry report, **[Unpub]** no locatable publication.

---

## 1. Headline findings

1. **No published instrument scores program curricula for AI automatability.** The nearest
   published work either maps curricula to occupational task taxonomies without grading
   automatability (Course-Skill Atlas), or grades occupations and then inherits the score
   onto majors (US Federal Reserve, Economic Innovation Group). DFVA's direct reading of
   learning outcomes remains the differentiator.
2. **The Peng and Chow EOG study is unpublished.** Two independent sweeps found no paper,
   preprint or indexed record for an Execution-Orchestration-Governance framework. The
   symposium abstract is the only public source for the 915-outcome analysis and the
   63.8% / 3.1% figures. Cite it as a conference presentation, not a paper, and do not
   attribute the framework to a publication that does not exist.
3. **A single exposure index does not predict employment outcomes.** Frank, Ahn and Moro
   show that AIOE alone is a poor predictor of unemployment risk, while an ensemble of
   indices adds about 18% explained variation. Panel A measures susceptibility, not
   realised harm, and the copy must say so.
4. **The graduate effect is a hiring-flow effect.** Brynjolfsson, Chandar and Chen find a
   16% relative employment decline for 22 to 25 year olds in the most exposed US
   occupations, driven by reduced hiring rather than separations. This is the strongest
   warrant for scoring graduate destinations rather than curriculum alone.
5. **Australia has not yet shown the effect.** QILT 2025 and the DEWR July 2026 report both
   show stable aggregate graduate outcomes. Australian reports must frame Panel A as a
   leading indicator, with the US evidence as mechanism precedent, not as observed local harm.

## 3. Curriculum AI-readiness: Panel C comparators

### 3.1 Rubric-over-text-units, the method precedent

- **[PR]** Eloundou, T., Manning, S., Mishkin, P., and Rock, D. (2024). GPTs are GPTs: labor
  market impact potential of LLMs. *Science*, 384(6702), 1306–1308.
  [DOI 10.1126/science.adj0998](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adj0998) ·
  [arXiv:2303.10130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10130). Human and GPT-4 raters grade O\*NET
  tasks for whether an LLM halves completion time. Structurally the same move as grading
  learning outcomes, applied to tasks.

### 3.2 Curriculum text to task taxonomy

- **[PR]** Javadian Sabet, A., Bana, S. H., Yu, R., and Frank, M. R. (2024). Course-Skill
  Atlas: a national longitudinal dataset of skills taught in U.S. higher education curricula.
  *Scientific Data*, 11, 1086. [DOI 10.1038/s41597-024-03931-8](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-024-03931-8).
  Maps roughly 3 million syllabi to O\*NET detailed work activities. The closest published
  precedent for Panel C's core move; it stops short of grading automatability.
- **[PR]** Jayalath, V., Barthakur, A., Dawson, S., Tingey, J., Crase, L., and Kovanović, V.
  (2025). Scaling curriculum mapping in higher education: evaluating generative AI's role in
  curriculum analytics. *AIED 2025*.
  [DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-98414-3_21](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-98414-3_21).
  LLM curriculum mappings reach Krippendorff's alpha of 0.76 against expert judgement,
  ahead of machine-learning baselines at 0.65. **Use 0.76 as the target benchmark for the
  DFVA inter-rater reliability study.**
- **[PR]** Zamecnik, A., Barthakur, A., Wang, H., and Dawson, S. (2024). Mapping employable
  skills in higher education curriculum using LLMs. *ECTEL 2024*.
  [DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-72312-4_2](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-72312-4_2)
- **[WP]** Xu, Z., Yu, R., and others (2025). From course to skill: evaluating LLM performance
  in curricular analytics. [arXiv:2505.02324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02324). Retrieval
  augmentation outperforms zero-shot prompting, which underperforms traditional NLP.
- **[WP]** Xu, Z., Guan, X., Shi, C., Chen, Q., and Yu, R. (2026). Evaluating 21st-century
  competencies in postsecondary curricula with large language models.
  [arXiv:2601.10983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10983). No model reaches human-level
  fine-grained pedagogical reasoning, which argues for retaining human judgement in Panel C.

### 3.3 Grading learning outcomes against AI capability

- **[PR]** Gonsalves, C. (2024). Generative AI's impact on critical thinking: revisiting

  [DOI 10.1177/02734753241305980](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/02734753241305980).

### 4.3 The graduate effect

- **[WP]** Brynjolfsson, E., Chandar, B., and Chen, R. (2025, updated 2026). *Canaries in the
  coal mine? Six facts about the recent employment effects of artificial intelligence*.
  Stanford Digital Economy Lab.
  [Publication](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recent-employment-effects-of-artificial-intelligence/).
  ADP payroll microdata: about 16% relative employment decline for 22 to 25 year olds in the
  most exposed occupations, widening to about 19% in the
  [August 2026 dashboard update](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/).
  The effect runs through reduced hiring and concentrates where AI use is substitutive.
- **[Ind]** SignalFire (2025, 2026). [State of tech talent](https://www.signalfire.com/blog/signalfire-state-of-talent-report-2025).
  New-graduate hiring down more than 50% against pre-pandemic; new graduates 7% of big-tech
  hires in 2024.
- **[Ind]** Lightcast (2025). [Global AI skills outlook](https://lightcast.io/resources/research/the-lightcast-global-ai-skills-outlook).
  28% salary premium on AI-skill postings; 51% of AI-skill demand now outside IT occupations.
- **[Gov]** QILT (2026). [2025 Graduate Outcomes Survey national report](https://www.qilt.edu.au/docs/default-source/default-document-library/2025-gos-national-report.pdf).
  Undergraduate full-time employment rose from 74.0% to 75.4%. No AI-driven deterioration
  visible in Australian aggregates.

### 4.4 Validity and counter-evidence

Read this cluster before strengthening any Panel A claim.

- **[PR]** Frank, M. R., Ahn, Y.-Y., and Moro, E. (2025). AI exposure predicts unemployment
  risk. *PNAS Nexus*. [PMC11983276](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11983276/).
  Individual indices, AIOE included, predict occupation-level unemployment poorly; an
  ensemble adds about 18% explained variation.
- **[WP]** Humlum, A., and Vestergaard, E. (2025). *Large language models, small labor market
  effects*. NBER Working Paper 33777.
  [PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33777/w33777.pdf). Danish
  administrative data: precise nulls on earnings and hours, ruling out effects above 2% two
  years after adoption, holding for early-career jobs.
- **[Gov]** DEWR (2026-07-08). [AI and employment in Australia](https://www.dewr.gov.au/workplace-relations/resources/ai-and-employment-australia).
  Most-exposed occupations still grew 5.6% from November 2022 to February 2026; software
  developer employment up 25%; youth outcomes have mostly held up.
- **[WP]** Lund, C., Euyang, T., Munyikwa, Z., and Fadaee, M. (2026). AI exposure scores: what
  they measure, what they miss, and what comes next.
  [arXiv:2606.23633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23633). Five remedial approaches to static
  exposure scores. Usable as the skeleton for the DFVA limitations artefact.
- **[Gov]** ILO (2026-02). [Workers' exposure to AI: what indicators tell us and what they
  don't](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/Research%20Brief_Workers%20exposure%20to%20AI.pdf)
- **[PR]** Bonney, K., and others (2024). The impact of AI on the workforce: tasks versus
  jobs? *Economics Letters*, 244, 111971.
  [DOI 10.1016/j.econlet.2024.111971](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165176524004555).
  About 27% of AI-using firms replaced worker tasks; only about 5% changed employment.
- **[PR]** Georgieff, A., and Hyee, R. (2022). Artificial intelligence and employment: new
  cross-country evidence. OECD Social, Employment and Migration Working Paper 265. Extends
  AIOE to 23 countries and finds no clear exposure-to-employment-growth relationship for
  2012 to 2019. Precedent for porting AIOE outside the United States, which is what DFVA does.

  [DOI 10.1007/s10734-020-00570-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-020-00570-x). Relevant paid
  work and quality curricular experience matter most; effects on actual employment outcomes
  are weaker and more conditional than work-integrated learning rhetoric implies. **Argues
  against crediting the presence of a placement in a handbook.**
- **[PR]** Jackson, D. (2024). Work-integrated learning in the humanities, arts and social
  sciences. *Studies in Higher Education*.
  [DOI 10.1080/03075079.2024.2409879](https://doi.org/10.1080/03075079.2024.2409879). Uses
  the same Graduate Outcomes Survey instrument DFVA draws on.

### 5.5 Signalling

- **[WP]** Galdin, A., and Silbert, J. (2025). Making talk cheap: generative AI and labor
  market signaling. [arXiv:2511.08785](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08785). Freelancer.com data
  around LLM introduction: the premium employers paid for customised written applications
  collapses once LLMs make customisation cheap. A clean demonstration of Spence-style signal
  destruction, and the economic mechanism behind the durability thesis.
- **[PR]** Tomlinson, M., and Anderson, V. (2021). Employers and graduates: the mediating role
  of signals and capitals. *Journal of Higher Education Policy and Management*, 43(4),
  384–399. [DOI 10.1080/1360080X.2020.1833126](https://doi.org/10.1080/1360080X.2020.1833126)
