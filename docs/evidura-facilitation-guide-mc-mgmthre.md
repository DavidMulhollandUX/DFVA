**Audience:** program director, subject coordinators, curriculum/T&L staff, faculty

## 0. The one thing to get right

The room will assume it is being graded. If that assumption survives the first five
minutes, the session produces defensiveness instead of evidence and the two hours are
wasted.

The actual purpose is the reverse. This program has been scored against a draft instrument
on documentary evidence alone, by one rater, with seven ambiguities already recorded as
resolved downward. **The people in this room hold evidence the instrument could not see and
know things about the program the handbook does not record.** They are the first external
check on both the scores and the instrument.

**Open with this, close to verbatim:**

> *"We have scored this program against a draft instrument, using only what the 2026
> handbook documents, with one rater. We have already had to correct this report twice —
> once for reading the wrong data file, once because a third of the evidence was missing and
> two scores fell when we found it. I am not here to defend the numbers. I am here to find
> out which of them are wrong, what evidence we couldn't see, and where the instrument
> itself is measuring the wrong thing. If you leave without having changed something, I've
> run this badly."*

Two facilitator disciplines for the whole session:

1. **Never change a score in the room.** A disputed score is recorded, the evidence behind
   the dispute is requested, and the item is re-scored against that evidence afterwards. A
   score changed under social pressure is a score with no evidentiary basis, which is the
   defect the instrument exists to avoid.
2. **Compliments are noise.** "This is really useful" is not a finding. Steer back:
   *"which item is wrong?"*

## 2. What Evidura is (3 minutes)

Three layers. Say all three; the room will otherwise collapse them.

| Layer | What it is | What it is called in the room |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Evidura** | The platform and the brand | "Evidura" |
| **Durability Rating** | The consumer-facing signal — score plus band | "the rating" |
| **DFVA** | The methodology and scoring engine underneath | Don't use externally; explain as "the method" |

**What it is not** — say these explicitly, because each one is an assumption someone in the
room is already making:

- **Not a ranking.** No program is compared against another in this report. For this
  program, no position label is reported at all (§5).
- **Not a compliance instrument.** It is not TEQSA, not AHRI, not an audit. It cites the
  HESF because the HESF is a construct authority for what a curriculum should evidence, not
  because anyone is being assessed against it here.
- **Not a judgement of teaching quality.** It scores what the curriculum documents it
  assesses. A brilliantly taught subject with an undocumented assessment scores low, and
  that is a known and declared limitation, not a hidden one.
- **Not automated marking of your program.** One rater applied declarative anchors to
  quoted handbook text. Where the rater judged, the judgement is written down and the
  ambiguity is listed.

## 3. The method in plain language (10 minutes)

### Two coordinates, never one number

| Coordinate | This program | How it is obtained | Who controls it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Destination AI exposure** | **94.14** | *Measured* — the program's own alumni destination record, crosswalked to a published occupational exposure index | Nobody in this room. It is a property of the occupations graduates enter |
| **Curriculum adaptiveness** | **8 / 15** | *Scored* — eight anchored items against documented curriculum evidence | This room, entirely |
| **Workplace practice** | **4 / 9** | *Scored* — same evidence, separate sub-scale | This room, entirely |

The two sub-scores are **reported side by side and never added**. A program strong on
workplace practice and weak on adaptiveness is a different problem from the reverse, and a
single total would hide which one you have.

### The eight items in one line each

| Item | Plain-English question | This program |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C1 Distributed cognition | Do students have to coordinate work across people *and* tools/systems? | 2/3 |
| C2 Hybrid metacognition | Are students assessed on judging quality — their own work, a tool's output? | 2/3 |
| C3 Digital & AI literacy incl. governance | Does the compulsory core teach and assess what AI can and can't do, and its governance? | 1/3 |
| C4 Life-long learning & transfer | Do students apply learning beyond the taught instance, and diagnose and plan their own gaps? | 2/3 |
| C5 Inquiry & evidence generation | Do students design and run an inquiry, not just apply a taught method? | 1/3 |
| W1 Professional communication & conduct | Do students produce professional genres, judged by someone other than a marker? | 2/3 |
| W2 Authentic task design | Real problem + practitioner's artefact + criteria drawn from practice, all three at once? | 1/3 |
| W3 Work-situated learning | Does the *required* path put students in a real workplace? | 1/3 |

Plus two gates, both PASS here: **G1** a coherent disciplinary foundation, **G2**
decision-making under uncertainty.

### The four rules that decide arguments

Most objections in the room are really objections to one of these four. Name them early and
you can answer objections by pointing rather than by arguing.

| Rule | What it says | Why it exists |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **R1** | Anchors are declarative statements about documented evidence, not judgements of quality | So two raters reading the same page reach the same score |
| **R2** | Level 3 requires *assessment* evidence. A stated learning outcome caps the item at level 1 | Stated graduate attributes are not evidence of attainment (Barrie 2006) |
| **R3** | Every score quotes the source verbatim | So any score can be checked, and overturned, by anyone |
| **R4** | Authenticity is scored from documented task features, never from the word "authentic" | The label has become a "thought-terminating cliché" (Fawns et al. 2024) |

And one convention that decides the rest: **ambiguity resolves downward.** Where evidence
straddles two levels, the lower level is awarded and the straddle is recorded. Seven were
recorded for this program. Three of them are live questions for this room (§6.B).

### Basis tags

Every section of the report is tagged with how much weight it can bear. Point at these
when someone challenges a claim — half the time you will agree with them, because the
report already conceded it.

- **measured** — computed from a dataset (the exposure figure)
- **scored** — an anchor applied to quoted evidence (the eight items, the gates)
- **reported** — restated from a named source (market signals)
- **inferred** — the report's own argument (curriculum implications, the improvement plan)

## 4. How this report was made (5 minutes)

Walk the provenance. It takes three minutes and buys the rest of the session.

**Curriculum evidence.** 35 handbook pages captured on 2026-08-14 through a browser-based
queue at roughly one page per 20–30 seconds: the course overview, attributes and structure,
plus the overview and assessment pages of all eleven compulsory subjects and five
capstone-selective and elective subjects. Sixteen of those pages carry assessment tables.
Every quotation in the report was verified verbatim against the captured text.

**Destination evidence.** The program's own exact-name alumni destination record from
JIR/LiveAlumni: n = 28, fifteen destination titles across three career stages. Each title
was crosswalked to the published Felten et al. AI Occupational Exposure index and rescaled
0–100 by the same procedure used for every other program. Twelve of the fifteen titles had
to be added to the shared crosswalk for this assessment; three of the fifteen carry medium
rather than high mapping confidence.

**Then say this, unprompted:**

> **Two corrections have already been published against this report.**
>
> 1. An earlier version said this program had no alumni destination record. That was wrong
>    — the check ran against a 41-program enrichment file instead of the 141-record source.
>    The record exists. It looked empty because thirteen of its fifteen titles were not in
>    the crosswalk.
> 2. An earlier version was scored on 22 of the 35 pages. Thirteen had been dropped from the
>    capture queue by an agent that used "failed" to mean "deprioritised". They were
>    recaptured and the program re-scored. **Two items changed, both downward** — W2 and W3.
>    The missing pages were not withholding evidence that would have raised the score; they
>    were withholding the structure that showed how much of the workplace provision is
>    optional.
>
> The second one matters for this session. When people tell us we've missed something, the
> correction does not reliably go up.

**What has not been done.** No content-validity panel. No inter-rater reliability study.
No external review of the W items at all. No dated job-advertisement extraction — the
market signals are attributed commentary and reported survey data, which is why they are
marked MEDIUM confidence. This is a single-rater application of a draft instrument.

## 5. The findings (15 minutes)

Do **not** read the scorecard. Put it on screen and land four things.

| | Adaptive capabilities | Workplace practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Score** | **8 / 15** | **4 / 9** |
| Items at 1/3 | C3, C5 | W2, W3 |
| Items at 2/3 | C1, C2, C4 | W1 |
| Gates | G1 PASS · G2 PASS | |
| Destination AI exposure | **94.14** (measured, n = 28) | |
| Position label | **Not reported** | |

### Finding 1 — the destination set is the finding

94.14 is above the portfolio median, and eleven of the fifteen destination titles sit
between 95 and 96 on the index. HR specialist, recruitment and HR-assistant occupations are
among the most exposed occupations Felten et al. score, because the tasks that define them —
screening, scheduling, records administration, first-pass matching, policy drafting — are
the tasks current systems already do. There is **no low-exposure tail**: the only
destination below 90 is Volunteer Coordinator, one title.

Say clearly: *this is not a judgement of the program.* Exposure is a property of the
occupations, computed identically for every program, and the program does not control it.
What the program controls is the other coordinate.

### Finding 2 — C3 is the gap, and it is the one the market is pricing

The program states, as a course-level graduate outcome, *"Leverage artificial intelligence
and digital tools to enhance human resource decision-making and processes."* No compulsory
subject assesses it. Under R2 a stated outcome caps the item at level 1. AI reaches the
required path nowhere else; MGMT90267 *Artificial Intelligence in Organisations* is an
elective, which is the level-1 anchor's own wording.

The nearest compulsory evidence — MGMT90280's *"power and limitations of optimisation and
data analytical techniques"* — is the right *shape* for level 2 and the wrong subject
matter: it names no AI and carries no ethics component. Evidence straddles 1 and 2 and
resolves down.

Why it is urgent rather than merely low: three regulatory instruments now put governance of
automated employment decisions on HR practitioners specifically, and one of them —
the WHS Amendment (Digital Work Systems) Act 2026 — binds in Australia.

### Finding 3 — two points hang on one documentation change

C2 and C4 both stop at 2/3 on the *same* evidence: MGMT90140's assessed competence-
development cycle — ongoing mini-assignments plus a 40% *"Critical reflection on competence
development"*. C2's level 3 wants evidenced strategy adjustment over time; C4's wants the
diagnosis of one's own gaps *together with the plan to close them*. The handbook documents
the diagnosis. It does not say the student must evidence what they changed and why, or state
a plan.

**This is the hand-off to the room.** If the subject already asks for those things, both
items move on a documentation change with no new teaching. Whether it does is a fact about
MGMT90140 that the handbook does not record — and somebody at this table knows it.

### Finding 4 — the workplace provision is real, and optional

BUSA90473 Business Practicum is genuinely level-3 material: student teams placed with a host
organisation, academic mentor and host both in the supervision loop, hurdle in-company
training tasks, a 40-minute presentation to the host company with Q&A, 360-degree feedback.
BUSA90485 and BUSA90525 sit alongside it.

W3 scores 1 because all three sit in the capstone-selective or elective lists. The capstone
slot is a choice among six, three of which involve no workplace at all. **A student can
complete the whole 200-point program without ever entering a workplace** — and the same
optionality is why W1 stops at 2: the one core practitioner artefact, MGMT90016's group
consulting report, is never read by anyone outside the university.

### And why there is no rating

Both coordinates exist, but a position states where a program sits relative to its peers,
and that needs a v4 adaptiveness median that will not exist until the reference cohort is
re-scored. Placing this program against the v3.1 median would rank a v4.1 score against a
different instrument. **The label is withheld rather than estimated.** If the room wants a
band, the honest answer is "not yet, and here is when."

## 6. The questions — this is the session

Four blocks. Each has the questions to ask, what a useful answer sounds like, and where the
scribe records it. Ask the questions as written; they are phrased to invite disagreement.

### Block A — What data is missing? (18 min)

Open by conceding the known gaps first. A room that hears you list your own gaps starts
listing theirs.

**Known and declared already:**

- The three foundation selectives (ACCT90041, ECON90015, FNCE90065) have no captured pages
  — one of the four foundation subjects each student takes is undocumented.
- ENGR90034, a capstone selective, is absent.
- MGMT90267's assessment table names a written activity, a report and a quiz with word
  counts only, so even the AI elective's ethics outcome has no assessment specification
  behind it.
- No QILT field-level figures attach to this program.
- n = 28 is a small alumni sample; three of fifteen titles are medium-confidence mappings.
- No job-advertisement extraction was run; §4's signals are MEDIUM confidence.
- The 150-point entry-point program was not scored.

**Then the questions:**

1. **"The handbook is not the subject guide. What does the subject guide say that the
   handbook doesn't?"** — This is the highest-value question in the session. Ask it about
   MGMT90140 first (Finding 3), then MGMT90280, MGMT90016 and MGMT90010.
   *A useful answer is a document, not a reassurance.*
2. **"For any item you think is scored low — what artefact would prove it? Rubric, task
   brief, moderation record, exemplar, an assessment criteria sheet?"**
3. **"Which of these subjects has changed since the 2026 handbook was published, or changes
   next cycle?"** — the report has a handbook vintage; curriculum has a teaching reality.
4. **"Where does AI actually appear in what students do, whether or not the handbook says
   so?"** — record it, then say plainly that undocumented practice cannot score under R2,
   and ask what it would take to document it.
5. **"Who else holds evidence about this program that we haven't asked?"** — careers
   service, AHRI liaison, the practicum host coordinator, alumni relations, student reps.
6. **"What do you know about where your graduates actually go that our 28-person record
   doesn't show?"**

> **Scribe:** Evidence Request Register (§8.2). Every row needs an owner and a date, or it
> is not a row.

### Block B — What is valid? (15 min)

Work the scorecard item by item, but spend the time on the three the report has *already*
flagged as close calls. Present them as genuine questions; they are.

**The three declared straddles — put each to the room directly:**

1. **W3 straddles 1 and 2.** A required-choice capstone slot is genuinely stronger than a
   free elective. The report resolved down. *"Is a choice among six, where three routes
   contain no workplace, meaningfully different from an elective? A rater who says yes
   scores this program a point higher."*
2. **W1 straddles 1 and 2** on a conjunctive reading. The MGMT90016 consulting report is a
   professional genre named in the level-2 anchor, but the clause requiring criteria drawn
   from professional practice is undocumented. *"Are the marking criteria for that report
   drawn from practice? If they are and it's written down, this is a level-2 evidence
   question, not a judgement call."*
3. **C2 and C4 straddle 2 and 3 on the same evidence.** *"Does MGMT90140 require students
   to evidence what they changed and why, and to state a plan? If yes, both items move."*

**Then the general challenge, for every item:**

- **"Which single score is most wrong, and what's the evidence?"** — force a ranking; a
  room asked "any objections?" gives none.
- **"Where has the instrument counted something once that should count twice, or twice
  that should count once?"** — the report applies one-construct-one-home: group work scored
  in C1 and not again in W1; the consulting report scored in W1 and not in W2; the practicum
  scored in W3 only. Any of those boundaries is arguable.
- **"Is anything in the report factually wrong about the program?"** — subject codes,
  weightings, structure, credit points, who takes what.

> **Scribe:** Item Disposition Sheet (§8.1). Record *agree / dispute / needs evidence*, and
> for disputes, the evidence claimed. Do not record a new score.

### Block C — What is not valid? (10 min)

Now attack the instrument rather than the scores. This block feeds the content-validity
work and is the part a curriculum audience is uniquely qualified to do.

1. **"Does this measure what it claims to measure?"** Sub-scale A claims curriculum
   adaptiveness to an AI-integrated future; sub-scale W claims workplace practice. *"What
   is it missing? What is it scoring that doesn't belong?"*
2. **"The instrument scores documented curriculum intent, not demonstrated graduate
   capability. Is that gap tolerable, or fatal?"** The report declares the inference rests on
   constructive alignment, stated as an assumption. *"Would you accept a score built on that
   assumption?"*
3. **"Is it fair to characterise a program by the exposure of the occupations its graduates
   enter?"** The strongest counter-argument — that a program could be excellent *and*
   feed highly exposed occupations — is exactly why the two coordinates are kept apart.
   Test whether that separation survives contact with the room.
4. **"Is a handbook a legitimate evidence base for any of this?"** Take the answer seriously.
   If it is not, the whole instrument needs a different evidence source, and that is a
   finding worth more than any individual score.
5. **"W3 rewards required placement. Required placement bears unevenly on students who
   can't afford unpaid or off-campus work. Is the item scoring the right thing?"** The
   report declares this and does not recommend moving W3 for exactly this reason.
6. **"What in your professional judgement makes a graduate durable that is nowhere in these
   eight items?"** — open-ended; this is where a new item comes from.

> **Scribe:** Instrument Challenge log (§8.3, type = `construct`). Route to the Panel C
> decision log, not to the report.

### Block D — What questions remain? (5 min)

1. **"What did I not ask that I should have?"**
2. **"If you were going to argue against this report in a program review, what would you
   say?"**
3. **"What would have to be true for you to act on any of this?"**
4. **"Who decides whether anything in the improvement plan happens, and what would they
   need to see?"**
5. **"Who else should be in the room next time?"**

Close by naming the next step and the date (§9). A session that ends without a named
turnaround reads as consultation theatre.

## 9. After the session — what happens to the output

Commit to this in the room, with a date.

| Output type | Where it goes | Result the room sees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence supplied (§8.2) | Re-capture where needed, re-score the affected items against the new evidence | A republished report with a dated correction note naming what changed and why — the same treatment the two existing corrections got |
| Factual errors about the program | Corrected immediately | Corrected report |
| Item disagreements (§8.1) | Recorded as rater-disagreement cases for the [inter-rater reliability study](evidura-inter-rater-reliability-study.md) — disagreement about whether a line satisfies an anchor is a finding there, not noise | Named in the IRR study's disagreement analysis |
| Construct challenges (§8.3, `construct`) | The Panel C decision log in the [v4.1 recommendation](dfva-panelc-v41-recommendation.md), and the content-validity panel that has not yet run | Instrument change, or a written reason why not |
| Improvement-plan reactions | The [improvement plan](../reports/dfva-v4-recommend-mc-mgmthre.md) — particularly P2, whose availability depends on facts about MGMT90140 that only this room holds | Revised plan |

**The commitment to make:** every disputed item is either re-scored against supplied
evidence or answered in writing with the reason it stands. Nothing is left as "noted".

**Do not offer:** a position label, a comparison against another program, or a date for the
content-validity panel or IRR study — none of those are yours to promise in this session.
