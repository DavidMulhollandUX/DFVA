- [L1] Probation and parole officers in Australia are governed by state corrective-services legislation (e.g. Corrections Act 1986 (Vic) and Corrections Regulations 2019 (Vic)), not a national registration scheme; the occupation is not registered with AHPRA.
    source: Victorian Legislation and Parliamentary Counsel — Corrections Act 1986 (Vic) — in force (https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/acts/corrections-act-1986, 2026)
    measured: Confirms Corrections Act 1986 (Vic) is the principal Act governing Victorian correctional facilities and corrective services staff; no national registration body named.
    scope: Victoria state legislation; other states have equivalent corrective-services Acts. Scope limited to legislative governance, not AI-specific rules.
- [L2] Actuarial risk-assessment tools, notably the Level of Service Inventory-Revised (LSI-R), are embedded in Australian corrective services and inform prison placement, therapeutic interventions and parole timing for several thousand offenders each year in Victoria, NSW and the ACT.
    source: Melbourne University Law Review — Algorithmic Risk Assessment in Australian Corrective Services (https://law.unimelb.edu.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0004/5409526/Dancy-491-38.pdf, 2025)
    measured: Legal-scholarly analysis of LSI-R use across Vic/NSW/ACT corrective services; documents that risk scores (not their component factors) inform placement, intervention and parole decisions for thousands annually.
    scope: Vic/NSW/ACT corrective services only; probabilistic actuarial tool, not generative AI. Does not measure probation-officer job displacement, only the algorithmic instrument they operate within.
- [L2] Scholarly critique holds that LSI-R factors affecting risk scores are not published by the agencies that use them, and its application to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander persons may contravene the Guiding Principles for Corrections in Australia (Revised 2018).
    source: Melbourne University Law Review — Algorithmic Risk Assessment in Australian Corrective Services (https://law.unimelb.edu.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0004/5409526/Dancy-491-38.pdf, 2025)
    measured: Argues public scrutiny of LSI-R is sparse, scoring factors opaque, and application to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander persons may breach the 2018 Guiding Principles for Corrections in Australia.
    scope: Equity/transparency critique of an actuarial tool; not a measurement of probation-officer task automation.
