- [L1] General 'Community Health Workers' are not a nationally registered health profession under AHPRA; by contrast Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Health Practitioners are registered with the Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Health Practice Board of Australia (a National Board under AHPRA) from 1 July 2012.
    source: RACGP Working Well / ATSIHPBA — Working well resources: Glossary (Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Health Practitioners) (https://workingwell.racgp.org.au/glossary.htm, 2024)
    measured: States ATSIHPs register with ATSIHPBA (AHPRA sub-board) since 1 July 2012; implies general CHWs are not in the national scheme.
    scope: Registration status only; 'Community Health Workers' as a US-style occupation is not the same as the Australian ATSI health-worker cadre. Scope limited to whether the role is nationally registered.
- [L1] Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Health Workers (distinct from Practitioners) are not registered but are qualified via Certificate III/IV in primary health care and may hold Medicare Provider Numbers; their practice centres on health promotion, education and community engagement.
    source: MJA Insight+ — Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander health workers and practitioners in general practice (https://insightplus.mja.com.au/2025/31/aboriginal-and-torres-strait-islander-health-workers-and-practitioners-in-general-practice, 2025)
    measured: Describes ATSI Health Workers' non-registered status, Cert III/IV qualification, MBS claiming, and community-engagement role.
    scope: ATSI health workers specifically; a culturally-specific cadre within the broad community-health space, not the whole SOC.
- [L2] A 2026 multi-country analysis (The Lancet Primary Care, via the Community Health Impact Coalition) found over half of NGOs piloting AI tools in community health worker programs, yet almost none integrated into national health systems — risking the fragmented trajectory of earlier digital-health innovations.
    source: Community Health Impact Coalition (CHIC) Research Roundup 138 — AI is gaining traction in community health but not yet built into national strategies (https://sbee.link/myj6vxa7fc, 2026)
    measured: Multi-country survey of 28 NGOs across 18 countries operating CHW programs; >50% piloting AI, near-zero national-system integration.
    scope: International (18 countries), not Australia-specific; measures pilot adoption, not Australian labour-market effect. CHIC roundup is a secondary synthesis.
- [L2] A scoping review of AI/algorithmic literacy among health workers (Frontiers in Public Health, 2026) found 12 studies (2021-2025) concentrated in medical and nursing education, with NO studies exploring public-health practice — a gap directly affecting community health workers.
    source: Frontiers in Public Health — AI and algorithmic literacy among health workers: a scoping review through a digital health literacy lens (https://frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1802392/pdf, 2026-04-23)
    measured: Scoping review of 12 studies (2021-2025) on AI literacy among health workers; none in public-health practice; definitions theory-weak.
    scope: Health-worker AI literacy literature gap; identifies absence of evidence for CHWs rather than a measured effect.
- [L3] The 3rd National Policy Roadmap for AI in Healthcare ('Don't Walk. Run.', AAAiH/2026) concludes the Australian healthcare system is unprepared for AI disruption and makes 28 recommendations including workforce upskilling and a national Chief AI Officer training program; it notes NHMRC and MRFF are 'not up to speed on AI'.
    source: Croakey — Australian healthcare system is unprepared for AI disruption: new report (https://croakey.org/australian-healthcare-system-is-unprepared-for-ai-disruption-new-report, 2026)
    measured: Reports AAAiH 3rd National Policy Roadmap: 28 recommendations across leadership, governance, workforce, consumers; health system unprepared.
    scope: Whole healthcare system (policy); CHWs are within scope as part of the workforce but the claim is system-level preparedness, not occupation-specific.
- [L3] A RACGP poll found 40% of Australian GPs were using AI scribes by November 2025 (up from 22% fifteen months earlier), signalling fast AI-augmentation in primary care adjacent to community health work.
    source: Healthcare Australia — Healthcare Workforce Trends 2026: What Australian Health Leaders Need to Know (https://healthcareaustralia.com.au/healthcare-workforce-trends-2026-what-australian-health-leaders-need-to-know, 2026-08-14)
    measured: Reports RACGP poll: 40% of GPs using AI scribes by Nov 2025, up from 22%; dominant model augmentation not replacement.
    scope: GPs (primary care), an adjacent profession; signals augmentation pace, not a measured effect on community health workers specifically.
