Externalities

Costs and benefits that spill beyond the system boundary to actors who did not directly participate in or pay for the assessment.

+ Positive externalities
✓
Improved graduate employability
Students benefit from curriculum that better aligns with labour-market needs, even if they never interact with Evidura directly
✓
Reduced public education waste
Taxpayers fund fewer programs producing graduates for shrinking occupations; capital reallocates to growth areas
✓
Employers receive AI-ready graduates
Industry partners get hires with current, AI-aware skill sets without investing in assessment infrastructure themselves
✓
Sector methodology benchmark
Go8 peer universities gain a reference framework for their own AI-disruption assessments, raising the whole sector's standard
✓
Reusable data infrastructure
The labour-market data pipeline built for DFVA can be repurposed for institutional analytics, strategic planning, research
✓
Policy evidence base
Dept of Education and TEQSA gain aggregated evidence on AI disruption patterns to inform sector-wide policy
✓
Australian HE leadership signal
University of Melbourne demonstrates sector-leading AI-readiness methodology, strengthening brand and international positioning
− Negative externalities
−
Faculty job insecurity
Programs flagged as "not viable" create pressure for departmental restructuring, redundancies, and morale erosion among academic staff
−
Self-fulfilling enrolment prophecy
"At-risk" program perception can drive enrolment drops independent of actual viability, accelerating the decline the tool predicted
−
Humanities / arts devaluation risk
A narrow AI-lens may systematically undervalue programs whose value is not captured by labour-market data alone (critical thinking, civic engagement, culture)
−
Budget diversion
Assessment tool costs divert funding from other academic initiatives, teaching support, or student services
−
Data gaming & Goodhart's Law
Once programs are measured against specific metrics, curriculum design optimises for the metric rather than genuine learning outcomes
−
Reputational risk if methodology is contested
Published assessments invite public scrutiny; if the methodology or data is challenged, institutional credibility is damaged
−
Competitive disadvantage from transparency
If one university openly flags weak programs while competitors do not, it may lose prospective students to opacity
−
Privacy & data dependency risk
Reliance on external labour-market data (LinkedIn, Lightcast) creates a dependency; if data quality degrades or access is revoked, assessments lose credibility
