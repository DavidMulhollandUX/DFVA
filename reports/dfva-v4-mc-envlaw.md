## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** FAIL — The G1 PASS condition requires a documented coherent specialist core with progressive technical or methodological depth via an all-compulsory specialist core, an accredited sequence, or a staged prerequisite chain. The extract documents none of the three. (a) No all-compulsory specialist core: students with a law degree have zero compulsory subjects and 12.5 credit points of free choice from across the Master of Laws, so an eighth of the degree is interchangeable; the one compulsory subject (Fundamentals of the Common Law) applies only to non-common-law entrants and is a generic MLM legal-method subject, not environmental law. (b) No accredited sequence: no professional accreditation is documented anywhere in the extract. (c) No staged prerequisite chain: all prescribed subjects are standalone 12.5-point level-7 intensives with no prerequisite chain, sequencing or capstone. Furthermore, the prescribed lists include generic non-environmental subjects under 'Further subjects related to course' (e.g. Construction Law, Construction Risk). Precedent in 504aa, 507aa, 526aa, and 502cw binds.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — The FAIL condition (recall or scripted responses only) is contradicted by the documented assessment across the extract. Core and prescribed subjects assess 4,000–7,500 word research papers, 3,000–5,000 word take-home examinations, advisory reports, and case-study evaluations. The program documents at graduate attribute level that students must critically analyse problems and develop a range of appropriate solutions through assessment tasks. Subject learning outcomes require evaluating complex trade-offs under high scientific and socio-economic uncertainty (e.g. assessing regulatory efficacy and the precautionary principle in LAWS70100, inter-jurisdictional water conflicts in LAWS70185, carbon pricing vs direct action in LAWS70293, urban sprawl and land use trade-offs in LAWS70349, and toxics/PFAS governance under scientific uncertainty in LAWS90352).

**G1 FAILS.** A disciplinary-foundation failure is reported regardless of the adaptiveness score: the adaptive capabilities are scored on top of a foundation this program is not documented to establish.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **partial** (related program), n = 676, 15 destination titles, 6 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: Environmental Law is a Melbourne Law Masters specialisation; the Master of Laws record (n=676) is the program-family record, as the reconciliation package did for specialisation streams.

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (35 pages) and verified on 2026-08-23: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (10):

- Structural ambiguity across all items: the course structure prescribes a credit volume from subject lists (87.5-100 cp) rather than named compulsory subjects. For law degree holders there are zero compulsory subjects; for non-law degree holders only Fundamentals of the Common Law is compulsory. Every level-2 or level-3 anchor requiring that 'a core unit assesses' or that practice recurs 'across the program' is therefore constrained by the absence of a compulsory specialist core, and subjects from prescribed lists were treated as electives per the scoring-direction rule.
- C1 straddled 1 and 2: assessed group presentation is documented in LAWS90248 AI and Justice (15%) and negotiation simulation strategy in LAWS90114 (15%), which would satisfy level 2 if prescribed subjects counted as core. Resolved down to 1 because LAWS90248 is an elective in 'Further subjects', LAWS90114 is marked 'Not available in 2026', and a student can complete the degree without taking any collaborative unit.
- C2 straddled 0 and 1: the course ILO claims students 'critically reflect on' complex information, but this is reflection on subject doctrine rather than appraisal of the quality of work. Scored 1 because reflection is explicitly claimed in program ILOs; no move to 2 is possible because no peer review, structured critique rubric, portfolio with standards, or marking against exemplars exists in the extract.
- C3: LAWS90248 AI and Justice contains substantive AI governance content (automated decision-making, algorithmic bias, transparency, privacy, and regulatory design) that would read as level 3 material if it were core and assessed. Resolved down to 1 because LAWS90248 is an optional elective in 'Further subjects related to course', and no core unit addresses AI.
- C4 straddled 1 and 3 on self-scoped research papers: 4,000-7,500 word research papers on coordinator-approved topics appear across prescribed subjects. Resolved down to 1 because level 3 requires documented structured progression toward independent learning, whereas the extract documents standalone intensives with no prerequisite chain, no capstone, and the Minor Thesis is explicitly excluded.
- C5 straddled 1 and 2: substantial research papers (4,000-7,500 words) are assessed at 70-85% weight across the prescribed list, but the papers are secondary doctrinal legal scholarship without assessed data collection or empirical methodology selection. Level 3 fails because research papers are not universally required (several subjects set take-home exams) and no viva or methodology defence under scrutiny is documented. Scored 1.
- W1 straddled 0 and 1: class presentations and spoken participation are assessed across most prescribed subjects (LAWS70100, LAWS70185, LAWS70219, LAWS70293, LAWS90115, LAWS90127, LAWS90147, LAWS90168, LAWS90248), but none of these subjects is compulsory for all students. Scored 1 based on program-level graduate attributes and pervasive presentation assessment across the prescribed offerings. Professional genres in electives (Advisory report in LAWS70349, Legal Research Brief in LAWS90352) cannot lift to level 2 because they are electives lacking documented practice criteria.
- W2 straddled 1 and 2: problem-based framing and case-study evaluations appear across subjects, but dominant assessed artefacts (research papers, take-home exams) remain academic. Professional artefacts in electives lack documented practice criteria. Scored 1.
- W3 straddled 1 and 2 on the v4.2 in-practice route: the overview mentions relevance to professionals and practitioners, but the cohort is not documented as already practising professionals, and no assessment is conducted in a student's own practice with real recipients or an outcome measure. Optional internship LAWS70067 caps at level 1.
- G1 FAIL: the program has high specialist topical breadth in environmental law, but the G1 PASS condition requires a documented all-compulsory specialist core, an accredited sequence, or a staged prerequisite chain. None of the three is documented in the extract, matching precedent in 504aa, 507aa, 526aa, and 502cw.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (3):

- Fundamentals of the Common Law (LAWS70217) — the only compulsory subject (for non-common-law entrants) — has no subject or assessment page in the extract, so its assessment design could not be directly inspected.
- International Legal Internship (LAWS70067) has no subject or assessment page in the extract, so its duration, supervision, and assessment design are unknown beyond its listing as an optional subject in the course structure.
- Assessment criteria, rubrics, and marking standards are not published in the handbook extract for any subject, so criterion-referenced appraisal (C2) and practice-derived criteria (W1/W2) could not be tested directly.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
