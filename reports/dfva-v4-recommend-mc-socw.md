## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the preceding scored evidence and market data; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

The Master of Social Work is an AASW-accredited professional entry qualification (225 credit points) meeting Australian Social Work Education and Accreditation Standards (ASWEAS), preparing graduates for clinical, hospital, statutory child protection, and mental health practice (AI Occupational Exposure (AIOE) 86.73). The program achieves a flawless benchmark across workplace practice (**9 / 9** on Sub-scale W: W1: 3/3, W2: 3/3, W3: 3/3, G1 PASS, G2 PASS), anchored by two required supervised field placements — the first documented as 500 hours across SCWK90048 and SCWK90049, with SCWK90050 and SCWK90051 also compulsory in Year 2 — supervised in the placement agency and judged pass/fail against the placement learning and assessment plan. On the adaptive capabilities sub-scale (**8 / 15**), the degree reaches Level 2 on three items (the assessed group presentation in SCWK90063, criterion-referenced peer review and self-assessment in SCWK90056/SCWK90065/SCWK90053, and transfer across the practice areas the core names in SCWK90066/SCWK90057/SCWK90059) and Level 1 on two: no core unit addresses AI capabilities and their limits or ethics (C3), and core research assessment stops at proposals, plans and a scoping review of existing literature (C5). Strategic curriculum headroom focuses on formalizing human-AI clinical workflow charters for intake and documentation (C1), introducing assessed reliance and override logs for AI-assisted risk screening algorithms and diagnostic triage tools (C2), embedding systemic critique of algorithmic bias in welfare systems (Robodebt lessons) and Indigenous data sovereignty under CARE principles (C3), and establishing formal oral viva defense for practice research projects (C5).
