## 2. RECENT JOB AD SIGNALS

> **Confidence: MEDIUM** — directional patterns from documented trend data; live scrape not performed.

**Signal 1 — Public health unit capacity retained post-pandemic**
Victoria's local public health unit network (including the North Eastern Public Health Unit, named among this program's alumni employers) has been sustained rather than wound back after the COVID surge, keeping a steady entry-level intake for public health officers and field response roles. This is the least AI-exposed part of the destination set and the most reliable entry pathway.

**Signal 2 — The Australian Centre for Disease Control creates a new senior demand pool**
The ACDC's establishment has generated epidemiologist, surveillance and preparedness roles at Commonwealth level, appearing among this program's alumni employers. These are high-exposure occupations by task content but in structural shortage by supply — a combination that rewards graduates with defended methodological depth rather than analytic throughput.

**Signal 3 — Evaluation and economic evaluation named as scarce capability**
Health service and NGO ads increasingly separate "program evaluation" from general policy work, and specify economic evaluation or outcome-measurement experience. This maps directly to the Health Program Evaluation and Economic Evaluation elective chain, which sits outside the compulsory core.

**Signal 4 — Climate-health and One Health entering position descriptions**
Roles referencing climate adaptation, planetary health and One Health have moved from research institutes into state health department and NGO advertising. The program's recent subject additions (Climate Change and Health, Planetary and Global Health, Putting One Health into Practice, Sustainability and Healthcare) track this shift closely.

**Signal 5 — Data and analytics literacy assumed, not differentiating**
Public health analyst and officer ads now assume competence with statistical tooling as a baseline rather than listing it as a differentiator. The differentiators cited are causal reasoning, data governance, and the ability to interpret and defend an analysis for a non-technical decision-maker.

**Signal 6 — Indigenous data governance appearing as a named requirement**
Positions in Commonwealth and state health agencies increasingly cite Indigenous data sovereignty and governance frameworks. The program carries a dedicated elective (Indigenous Data Governance in Health) and compulsory Indigenous health content in Foundations, which is well positioned against this signal.

## 3. CURRENT DISCUSSION SIGNALS — MEDIUM CONFIDENCE

> **Confidence: MEDIUM** — pattern-based synthesis of professional discourse; not a dated extraction.

**Theme 1 — "The descriptive cut is free now"**
Practising epidemiologists describe routine descriptive analysis, data cleaning and first-pass literature synthesis as effectively commoditised, with professional value concentrating in study design, confounder identification, and defending an inference to a decision-maker. The recurring framing is that the analyst who only executes is exposed and the analyst who specifies and defends is not.

**Theme 2 — Policy officers as verifiers**
Government public health staff describe a shift from drafting briefs to editing and verifying machine first drafts, with the accountability for what reaches a minister unchanged. The stated risk is unverified fabricated citation entering the evidence chain — a verification discipline that is not currently taught anywhere in this program's compulsory core.

**Theme 3 — Evidence synthesis tooling is outrunning appraisal skill**
Automated systematic-review and evidence-synthesis tools are being adopted faster than critical-appraisal capability is being taught, producing a recognised gap between what practitioners can generate and what they can properly appraise.

**Theme 4 — Community trust is the non-transferable asset**
Health promotion and community practitioners consistently frame co-design, cultural safety and sustained local relationships as the part of the work that no tool touches — corroborated here by measurement, since community and field destinations are the lowest-exposure occupations in this program's set.

**Theme 5 — Preparedness is a standing capability, not a surge**
Post-COVID discourse treats pandemic and emergency preparedness as permanent workforce capability rather than episodic surge, which underwrites sustained demand for the field-facing entry roles.

## 5. CURRICULUM IMPLICATIONS

1. **The methods core is the asset — extend it, do not rebuild it.** Three of six compulsory subjects are research methods spanning quantitative and qualitative traditions. That is the right foundation for producing graduates who can appraise and supervise automated evidence work; the missing piece is teaching them to apply it to machine output.
2. **AI verification belongs in Biostatistics and Epidemiology 1, not in a new elective.** Adding a seventh elective would reach the graduates who least need it. The exposure sits in the destinations every graduate can enter, so the capability must sit in the compulsory core.
3. **The capstone route choice is doing unintended work.** Only the Research Project route reliably produces defended primary research; it is one of three routes available in 2026, which is the single reason Research Methods Rigour scores 2 rather than 3. Making it the default would move both the score and the capability.
4. **Quantitative depth stops at the core boundary.** Epidemiology 2, Regression Methods, Survival Analysis and Infectious Diseases Modelling are all elective, so 100 of 200 points can carry no further quantitative content while graduates enter destinations averaging 89.5 exposure.
5. **Protect and credential the community stream.** The lowest-exposure destinations (contact tracing 64.5, epidemiology support 74.3, health promotion 80.1) are also the ones the program treats least formally as assessed capability. This is a defensible, measurable differentiator.
6. **The recent refresh is real and should be connected.** The new climate-health, One Health, planetary health and Indigenous data governance subjects track live demand signals well; they are not yet linked to assessed systems-framing capability in the core.
