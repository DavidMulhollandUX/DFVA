## 2. RECENT JOB AD SIGNALS

**Signal 1 — Sustained public health unit network employment.**
Victoria's local public health units (LPHUs) and metropolitan health services sustain permanent intakes for entry-level public health officers and field epidemiologists.

**Signal 2 — Australian Centre for Disease Control (ACDC) recruitment.**
The establishment of the Australian CDC expands demand for high-level surveillance specialists and pandemic preparedness analysts with rigorous causal inference skills.

**Signal 3 — Program evaluation and health economics named as scarce capabilities.**
Health service and NGO advertisements increasingly specify formal health economic evaluation and impact measurement competencies over generic administrative coordination.

**Signal 4 — Climate-health and One Health integration in public health agencies.**
State health departments and international agencies (WHO, Burnet, Doherty Institute) demand public health practitioners skilled in planetary health adaptation and zoonotic disease surveillance.

## 3. CURRENT DISCUSSION SIGNALS — MEDIUM CONFIDENCE

**What these sources are.** Public Health Association of Australia ([PHAA](https://www.phaa.net.au/)), Australian Centre for Disease Control ([ACDC](https://www.health.gov.au/topics/cdc)), Australasian Epidemiological Association ([AEA](https://aea.asn.au/)), and peer-reviewed public health literature ([Australian and New Zealand Journal of Public Health](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/australian-and-new-zealand-journal-of-public-health), [January 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/australian-and-new-zealand-journal-of-public-health)). Direct extraction from X or LinkedIn was **not** performed and no social media forum was sampled — where this section refers to discourse, it denotes published public health policy statements, government health directives, and documented epidemiological debates by named bodies and authors. Dates are stated where available.

### Theme 1 — automated evidence synthesis and the imperative for causal inference

Methodological commentary in *ANZJPH* (January 2025) and [AEA](https://aea.asn.au/) stresses that routine descriptive summaries and literature cross-tabulations are largely automated by AI tools. Public health value concentrates in specifying valid causal questions, identifying unmeasured confounders, and defending inferences to government ministers.

**Bearing:** C5, C2, and G2.

### Theme 2 — policy drafting vs verification: accountability in machine-assisted evidence

Discourse from the [Public Health Association of Australia](https://www.phaa.net.au/) (November 2024) highlights that while public health policy analysts increasingly use LLMs to draft background briefs, the legal and ethical accountability for fabricated citations or hallucinated clinical statistics remains non-delegable. Analysts require rigorous verification discipline.

**Bearing:** C2, C3, and W1.

### Theme 3 — community co-design and Indigenous data sovereignty as durable human capabilities

Sector guidelines from [PHAA](https://www.phaa.net.au/) and the [National Aboriginal Community Controlled Health Organisation](https://naccho.org.au/) (2024/2025) demonstrate that community co-design, trauma-informed consultation, and Indigenous data governance cannot be substituted by algorithmic systems.

**Bearing:** C1, W2, and W3.
