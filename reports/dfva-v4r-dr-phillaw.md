# DFVA RESEARCH DEGREE REPORT: Doctor of Philosophy - Law (DR-PHILLAW)

**Instrument:** research-degree report, v4 era. This program carries **no v4 score** — no
Panel C adaptiveness, no Panel A exposure, no position and no gates. Section 1 says why.
The assessment below is carried from the retired v1 instrument as narrative only.

## 1. WHY THIS PROGRAM CARRIES NO v4 SCORE — Basis: stated

Two independent reasons, either of which alone would be sufficient.

**Panel C has nothing to score.** Panel C v4 scores documented *taught curriculum*
structure — assessment design, subject sequencing, stated learning outcomes. A research
degree is examined on an original contribution rather than delivered as coursework, so
there is no curriculum object for the instrument to read. This is a property of the
program, not a gap in the evidence: capturing more handbook pages would not produce a
score.

**Panel A has no destination basis.** Panel A exposure is computed from where a program's
graduates actually go, crosswalked to the published Felten AIOE index. The Panel A resolver
returns no basis for this program at any tier — no own alumni record, no variant, no pooled
or combined cohort, no curated cognate or partial borrow, and no JSA HEO field list. Unlike
the Panel C exclusion this one is an evidence gap rather than a category error, and it could
in principle be closed by data. It has not been, so no exposure figure is published here.

Consequently this report makes **no durability claim on v4 terms** and this program appears
on /reports as a research degree rather than as scored or as pending.

## 2. ASSESSMENT CARRIED FORWARD — Basis: inferred (v1 instrument)

Carried from [the v1 assessment](dfva-dr-phillaw.md) as narrative. The v1 composite, its
per-dimension scores and the retired Irreplaceability bonus are **deliberately omitted** —
they were produced by a different instrument on a different construct, and a single number
beside a program with no v4 score invites exactly the comparison that would be wrong.

### Threshold questions

- **Q1:** Could a well-prompted AI agent produce 80% of this graduate's first-two-year output?  
  **NO** — A well-prompted AI agent could accelerate literature synthesis and drafting, but could not produce an examiner-defensible original doctoral contribution to legal knowledge; the thesis is specifically examined for novelty and sustained argumentative coherence that current AI cannot autonomously generate or defend under questioning.
- **Q2:** Does this program train graduates to design systems, own decisions, or generate original insight?  
  **YES** — This is the explicit purpose of the degree: candidates independently design research, own their methodological and argumentative choices, and generate original insight that must withstand external examination. There is no more direct "own the decision, generate the insight" credential in the university's portfolio.
- **Q3:** Will these graduates be more employable in 5 years than today, given AI trends?  
  **UNCERTAIN** — Graduates' research skills and domain depth will likely become more valuable as AI absorbs routine legal work and demand grows for people who can supervise/critique AI-assisted legal reasoning at an expert level. But the binding constraint on this cohort's five-year employability is the shrinking, casualising academic legal job market and the small number of PhD-tier legal policy/judicial roles — a labour-market structural risk that DFVA's AI-automation lens does not fully capture.

### Analogue graduate profile

The most exposed graduate archetype of ~2021-2022 comparable to entry-level roles this cohort might otherwise compete for is the **junior legal research assistant / paralegal-style literature reviewer** — someone spending early career time producing first-pass case law summaries, citation checking, and literature surveys for senior academics or practitioners. This narrow slice of PhD-adjacent labour (research assistant work, often done by PhD candidates themselves to fund candidature) is now substantially threatened by:

- **Lexis+ AI** and **Westlaw CoCounsel** — automated case law research, summarisation, and citation retrieval
- **vLex Vincent AI** — AI-assisted legal research and document analysis across common law jurisdictions
- **Harvey AI** — legal reasoning and drafting assistance used increasingly in academic and practice research support roles
- **Elicit** and **Scite.ai** — AI literature-review and citation-context tools increasingly used by doctoral researchers across disciplines, including law
- **ChatGPT/Claude-class general LLMs** — used informally by candidates for drafting, summarising, and first-pass argument-stress-testing

Critically, none of these tools threaten the doctoral output itself (the original thesis contribution) — they threaten the paid research-assistant-style work that historically subsidised doctoral candidature, which is a funding/labour-market risk rather than a threat to the credential's value.

### Verdict

The Doctor of Philosophy – Law is a structurally resilient credential against AI automation because its entire purpose — generating original, examiner-defended legal knowledge — sits in the category of work current AI performs worst at. The the v1 composite MODERATE RISK score reflects not automation exposure (which is genuinely low) but structural gaps in AI literacy training (D5), curriculum currency mechanisms (D9), and graduate outcome evidence (D10) that are common to research-only higher degrees and not unique failures of this program. The more consequential risk to this graduate cohort is not displacement by AI but the shrinking academic legal labour market and the erosion of research-assistant-style funding work as AI absorbs that layer — a risk DFVA's automation lens surfaces only indirectly via D10. Melbourne Law School should not redesign the PhD to "compete with AI" (it already doesn't need to); it should instead formalise AI-literacy expectations for candidates who will supervise AI-assisted legal reasoning as academics or senior practitioners, and invest in tracking graduate destination data that reflects the academic-market realities this cohort actually faces.

## 3. MARKET EVIDENCE — Basis: reported

Market intelligence for this program is in [the market report](dfva-market-dr-phillaw.md). **Its §3 discussion-signal section is unsourced** — it predates the sourcing
rule and is grandfathered in `check-report-format.ts`, meaning it warns rather than fails.
Nothing from that section is condensed here, because an unsourced claim does not become
sound by being restated in a second report. The rest of the market report, including its
labour-market and program-profile material, stands.

Note that the market evidence for a research degree answers a different question from the
one it answers for a coursework program. There is no graduate cohort entering an
entry-level labour market, so the market material bears on the standing of the credential
and the fields its holders already work in, not on graduate displaceability.

## 4. LIMITATIONS — Basis: stated

- **No v4 score exists for this program, and none is pending.** Absence here is a
  conclusion, not a backlog item. Do not read it as "not yet assessed".
- **The carried assessment is v1-instrument.** v1 scored a different construct on different
  anchors and is not comparable with Panel C v4 adaptiveness, the workplace sub-scale, or
  any v4 position label. It is reproduced as reasoning, not as a rating.
- **The original v1 scoring treated inapplicable dimensions as N/A** and renormalised over
  the applicable ones. That treatment is itself a judgement about a program the rubric was
  not built for; the underlying numbers are in the v1 report and are not restated here.
- **The DFVA rubric was built to assess training pathways that produce a graduate
  workforce.** These programs do not produce one. The decision-relevant question for a
  research degree is whether the credential remains a durable signal of authority, which is
  what §2 addresses.
- **No destination data.** With no Panel A basis, nothing in this report rests on observed
  graduate destinations for this program.
