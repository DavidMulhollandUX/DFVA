## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — PASS on the accredited-sequence route: the program is accredited by the admitting authority, its award is stated to satisfy the academic requirements for admission to practice across Australia, and it names specific core courses that carry required research training and must be taken at UQ. That is a coherent specialist law core with disciplinary identity, not generic or interchangeable content.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — PASS: assessment requires defended judgement rather than recall or scripted response. Ethics and the Legal Profession states an advanced decision-making outcome on ethical issues in practice and assesses a research essay and reflection alongside the examination; Criminal Law requires students to create solutions and develop law-reform recommendations independently in a 50% research paper; Corporate Law requires application of rules to hypothetical problems in an interactive oral. These are trade-off decisions the student must justify, not scripted answers.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **field** (field grain), n = 0, 13 destination titles, 4 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: field-of-education occupation list (ATO-linked), not this program's own graduates

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (5 pages) and verified on 2026-09-07: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (6):

- C1: the 'Be my Clerk!' interactive oral could be read as stakeholder-facing work (level 2) or as an individual oral examination (level 0). The extract gives the task's name and weighting only, with no documented second party, role allocation or team, so the lower reading was taken.
- C2: an assessed 'Ethical reflection' sits above level 1's unassessed reflection but below level 2's criterion-referenced appraisal of the quality of work, since no peer review, structured critique, portfolio standard or exemplar marking is documented. Ambiguity resolved downward to 1 per the scoring-direction rule.
- C4: the designated research training and the 50% research paper and research assignment could be read as level 3 structured progression toward independent learning, but no capstone is documented, the tasks are not documented as self-scoped, and no assessment identifies the student's own knowledge gaps. Resolved downward to 2.
- C5: the program's designation of research training in four doctrinal courses argues against level 0's 'no research-methods training', but no methods unit, methodology selection or data collection is documented, so level 1 was taken rather than 2.
- W1 and W2: the interactive oral straddles both items and both levels. Under one-construct-one-home, its communicative form is scored in W1 and its task fidelity in W2; in both, R4 blocks scoring the label 'Be my Clerk!' as evidence of professional-genre criteria or an end-to-end professional task, so each was resolved to the lower level.
- W3: Practical Legal Training is named on the program page but is documented as a post-degree admission requirement, not a course, so it does not lift the item off level 0.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (2):

- Program structure: the extract contains the program overview page and four core course pages (LAWS2708, LAWS2709, LAWS3702, LAWS3703) only. The courses-and-requirements list is referenced but not captured, so the full core, the elective set and any capstone are unverified. C1, C3 and W3 level-0 scores and the 'across the program' clauses in C1 level 3, W1 level 3 and W2 level 3 rest on the four documented core courses; a fuller capture could raise them.
- C3: no digital or AI evidence of any kind appears in the extract, so the item is scored 0 on documented absence within the captured pages rather than on a verified survey of the whole core.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
