### Theme 1 — office clerks handle personal and commercial information; the Privacy Act 1988 APPs bind their APP-entity e…

Office clerks handle personal and commercial information; the Privacy Act 1988 APPs bind their APP-entity employers and the Fair Work Act 2009 requires seven-year employee-record retention. No occupation-specific licensing regime governs general office clerks. (Office Clerks, General; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Jacmac Law, Accessing employment records: Privacy Act and Fair Work Act intersection](https://jacmac.com.au/insights/accessing-employment-records) (2025).

Scope: Applies via employer; employee-records exemption; no specific regulator for the occupation.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 2 — 2025 evidence names clerical/secretarial work as the clear automation-exposed group; routine, rules-based o…

2025 evidence names clerical/secretarial work as the clear automation-exposed group; routine, rules-based office tasks are the most readily automated. (Office Clerks, General; lane L2, scholarly and grey-literature studies.)

Sources: [scty.org, AI at Work: The 2025 Evidence](https://scty.org/words/ai-work-2025) (2025-12).

Scope: Synthesis; clerical specifically flagged.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 3 — public relations in Australia is self-regulated

Public relations in Australia is self-regulated. Communication and Public Relations Australia (CPRA, formerly PRIA) is the peak body and offers practitioner accreditation; there is no statutory licensing of PR practitioners. Advertising and marketing communications are additionally subject to self-regulatory codes administered by Ad Standards (AANA). (Public Relations Specialists; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Communication and Public Relations Australia, Communication & Public Relations Australia \| Australia's Peak PR Body](https://www.cpra.org.au/).

Scope: Australian PR practitioners; voluntary accreditation; advertising-code overlay via Ad Standards (AANA).

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 4 — jobs and Skills Australia's Gen AI Capacity Study (Overarching Report 14 August 2025; Final Release 30 Sept…

Jobs and Skills Australia's Gen AI Capacity Study (Overarching Report 14 August 2025; Final Release 30 September 2025) is the first national whole-of-labour-market study of generative AI in Australia and finds Gen AI is more likely to augment human work than replace it; it scores ANZSCO 1.3 occupations across exposure, adoption, adaptation, labour market dynamism and skills. (Public Relations Specialists; lane L2, scholarly and grey-literature studies.)

Sources: [Jobs and Skills Australia, Australia's AI Transition: Jobs, Skills and the Future of Work](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/studies/generative-artificial-intelligence-capacity-study) (2025-08-14).

Scope: Australian occupations nationally; model/account-derived task-level exposure estimates, not employment forecasts.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 5 — editing in Australia is self-regulated

Editing in Australia is self-regulated. The Institute of Professional Editors (IPEd) administers the Accredited Editor scheme and publishes the IPEd standards for editing practice, but there is no statutory licensing of editors. (Editors; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Institute of Professional Editors, Institute of Professional Editors Limited](https://www.iped-editors.org/).

Scope: Australian editors; voluntary professional accreditation (Accredited Editor), not mandatory.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 6 — jSA's Gen AI Transition exposure data places clerical and administrative occupations — and the broader info…

JSA's Gen AI Transition exposure data places clerical and administrative occupations — and the broader information-processing and media/communications groups that include writers, editors and public-relations roles — among the most exposed to automation. The Conversation's analysis of JSA data shows the 20 occupations most at risk of automation are dominated by female-concentrated clerical roles (secretaries, receptionists, bookkeepers, accounting/HR/payroll clerks), while trades and labourer jobs are least exposed. (Editors; lane L2, scholarly and grey-literature studies.)

Sources: [The Conversation, These 3 charts show female-dominated jobs are actually the most exposed to AI](https://theconversation.com/these-3-charts-show-female-dominated-jobs-are-actually-the-most-exposed-to-ai-288103).

Scope: AU occupations; exposure scores are task-level automation potential, not realised displacement; writers/editors/PR sit in adjacent high-exposure information/media groups, not necessarily the top-20 clerical list.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.
