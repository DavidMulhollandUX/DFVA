**Assessment Date:** 2026-08-25 | **Source URL:** https://handbook.unimelb.edu.au/2026/courses/mc-bamktg | **Prompt Version:** DFVA-COPILOT-MARKET-v1

> **Scope note.** MC-BAMKTG is a 300-credit-point dual award — 150 credit points of compulsory
> general management core, 37.5 credit points of compulsory marketing core (Consumer Behaviour,
> Marketing Research, Marketing Strategy), 75 credit points of MBA electives and 37.5 credit
> points of marketing electives. It is a general management degree with a marketing major, not a
> marketing analytics degree, and the destinations below are scoped to that.

## 2. RECENT JOB AD SIGNALS

**Signal 1 — One in three employers report having already replaced at least some entry-level positions with AI.**
The [2026 GMAC Corporate Recruiters Survey](https://poetsandquants.com/2026/06/25/employers-still-want-mbas-but-1-in-3-are-replacing-entry-level-roles-with-ai-gmac-finds/)
(621 recruiters across 39 countries, 53% Global Fortune 500; reported [June 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/06/26/gen-z-entry-level-jobs-replaced-by-ai-new-gmac-recruiters-survey-tech-manufacturing-jobs-most-at-risk/))
records that share. The rungs being removed sit below the general management tier this program
targets, but they are the rungs most graduates enter through.

**Signal 2 — Employer confidence in graduate management education is at its recorded ceiling while the starting salary softens.**
The same survey reports that, for the first time, 100% of recruiters express at least some
confidence in graduate business education, alongside a median MBA starting salary of USD 120,000,
down from USD 125,000 the previous year, and only 52% of recruiters judging graduates to match
previous cohorts on professionalism, down from 61% in 2025.

**Signal 3 — Employers value communication, problem-solving and adaptability now, and AI-tool skill within five years.**
GMAC's 2026 ranking puts communication (64%), problem-solving (62%) and adaptability (60%) at the
top of current hiring priorities, with skill in using AI tools 14th at 50% — but first on the
five-year forecast. The near-term hiring signal and the medium-term one point at different
capabilities, and a two-year degree spans both.

**Signal 4 — Australian demand-side vacancies for this field cluster in banking, retail and services at A$80k–125k.**
The Adzuna AU live-vacancy list in the labour-evidence block below (Commonwealth Bank, Westpac,
NAB, Woolworths Group, EY, Ventia and others) is demand-side advertising, not an alumni
destination record. QILT GOS 2024 reports 92% full-time employment and a median salary of
A$124,000 for postgraduates in this field, with Jobs and Skills Australia rating occupation
demand **Met** rather than in shortage.

**Signal 5 — Destination AI exposure is 91.73, borrowed from the parent MBA record.**
The Panel A figure for this program is measured on the Master of Business Administration alumni
record (n = 422, 15 titles, 9 of medium mapping confidence), not on MC-BAMKTG's own graduates. It
sits above the portfolio median of 90.9. Treat it as an estimate for a related population.

## 3. CURRENT DISCUSSION SIGNALS — MEDIUM CONFIDENCE

**What these sources are.** Australian regulatory and standards instruments — the
[Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2024A00128/asmade),
the [ACCC](https://www.accc.gov.au/), the [AANA](https://aana.com.au/) advertising codes
administered by [Ad Standards](https://adstandards.com.au/) — together with director and
professional-body guidance from the [Australian Institute of Company Directors](https://www.aicd.com.au/)
and the [Australian Marketing Institute](https://ami.org.au/), industry-body technical reporting
from [IAB Australia](https://www.iabaustralia.com.au/), dated Australian trade press
([Mi3](https://www.mi-3.com.au/)), and the Graduate Management Admission Council's employer survey
as reported by [Poets&Quants](https://poetsandquants.com/) and [Fortune](https://fortune.com/).
Direct extraction from X or LinkedIn was **not** performed and no social media platform or
professional forum was sampled — where this section refers to discourse, it means published
positions, codes, guidance and reporting attributable to those named bodies and outlets. Dates are
given where the source carries one.

**Corrections and removals against the previous version of this section.** Three changes. The
previous version attributed the AI-governance debate to LinkedIn and AMI community discussion and
framed it as a professional-identity question for mid-senior marketers; the checkable Australian
material places the duty on the board and the general manager, and theme 1 is rewritten to what
those instruments say. The previous version described Privacy Act reform as enacted and complete
across 2024–2025; the automated-decision transparency obligation it turns on does not start
until 10 December 2026, and theme 6 is scoped to that. The claim that B2B marketing is an
underserved career path for MBA graduates in Australia is **removed** — nothing checkable was
found to support it.

### Theme 1 — AI and data governance is being written as a board and general-management duty, not a technical one

The AICD and the Human Technology Institute at the University of Technology Sydney published
version 2 of [*A Director's Guide to AI Governance*](https://www.aicd.com.au/news-media/research-and-reports/a-directors-guide-to-ai-governance.html)
on [29 June 2026](https://www.aicd.com.au/news-media/research-and-reports/a-directors-guide-to-ai-governance.html),
setting out eight elements of safe and responsible AI governance — oversight and accountability,
strategy and purpose, risk management, system lifecycle management, people and culture,
third-party relationships, transparency and disclosure, and continuous improvement. Allens, the
AICD and the Centre for Business Analytics at Melbourne Business School — this program's own
school — published [*Data Governance Foundations for Boards*](https://www.allens.com.au/insights-news/insights/2025/05/data-governance-for-directors-new-guidance-by-aicd-allens-and-mbs/)
on 14 May 2025, which states that data governance is integral to AI adoption and that directors
must confirm their data policies address AI-specific risk.

This supports a claim about where accountability is being placed. It does not measure how many
Australian boards have adopted either framework.

**Bearing:** C3, W1, and G2.

### Theme 2 — measurement is moving from multi-touch attribution to marketing mix modelling and incrementality

IAB Australia's Ad Effectiveness Council released its
[Market Mix Modelling Landscape Report](https://www.iabaustralia.com.au/resource/market-mix-modelling-landscape-report-2025/)
on [September 2025](https://www.mi-3.com.au/18-09-2025/iab-australia-unveils-market-mix-modelling-landscape-report-marketers-checklist),
profiling twelve MMM vendors active in Australia — Analytic Edge, Analytic Partners, Annalect,
Circana, Gain Theory, Google Meridian, Kantar, Lifesight, Meta's Robyn, Mutinex, Prophet and
Recast — across methodology, validation practice, data requirements and refresh cadence, with an
eight-stage checklist for marketers selecting one. A [December 2025](https://www.iabaustralia.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/IAB-Australia-Market-Mix-Modelling-Landscape-December-2025.pdf)
update added Adobe, Monks and Mortar AI. The methods span classical econometrics through Bayesian
and machine-learning approaches.

A vendor landscape evidences that a supply market exists and that a peak body considers buyer
guidance necessary. It does not measure how many advertisers have switched, and it is not a
finding that multi-touch attribution has been abandoned.

**Bearing:** C5, C2, and G2.

### Theme 3 — the argument marketing leaders are being asked to win is a commercial one, put to a CFO

Australian trade press covering the CMO Awards records marketing leaders framing their case in
finance's terms rather than marketing's: [Mi3, 25 February 2026](https://www.mi-3.com.au/25-02-2026/commercial-marketing-distilled-asahi-growth-chiefs-five-point-plan-get-rapid-runs-board)
reports Asahi's growth chief on defending budget and earning board credibility, and
[Mi3, 17 March 2026](https://www.mi-3.com.au/cmo-awards/17-03-2026/cmos-polymaths-kennards-gm-marketing-gygs-cmo-cross-functional-remit-tussling)
reports marketing leaders describing a cross-functional remit and revenue accountability as the
condition of the job. The underlying constraint is structural: Australian accounting standards
treat brand spend as operating expenditure measured yearly, so long-run brand investment has to be
argued rather than capitalised. The academic counterweight is the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute at the
University of South Australia, whose mental-availability and category-entry-point work is the
standard evidence base marketers cite for long-run brand building.

This supports a claim about what practitioners report being asked for. It is trade-press reporting
of individual practitioners, not survey data on Australian marketing budgets.

**Bearing:** W1, G2, and C5.

### Theme 4 — environmental and sustainability claims moved from reputational risk to enforced law

The AANA [Environmental Claims Code](https://aana.com.au/self-regulation/environmental-claims-code/)
took effect on 1 March 2025 and requires claims to be truthful, substantiated at the time they are
made, specific rather than vague, and — for goals and targets — founded on reasonable grounds with
verifiable data and an implementation plan. The ACCC named greenwashing in its
[2025–26 compliance and enforcement priorities](https://www.accc.gov.au/) in February 2025, and in
[April 2025](https://www.thebrandprotectionblog.com/2025/04/future-proofing-environmental-claims-what-you-need-to-know-now/)
Clorox was penalised AUD 8.25 million over "50% OCEAN PLASTIC" representations. Penalties under
the Australian Consumer Law reach AUD 50 million for a corporation.

**Bearing:** C2, C5, and W2.

### Theme 5 — disclosure obligations attach to the advertiser regardless of who or what made the ad

The ACCC published the findings of its internet sweep of influencer testimonials on
[7 December 2023](https://www.accc.gov.au/about-us/publications/social-media-influencer-testimonials-and-endorsements):
118 influencer accounts and 137 businesses reviewed, with 81% of posts identified as concerning
and needing follow-up, rising to 96% among fashion influencers. Ad Standards states that the codes
it administers are [media-neutral](https://adstandards.com.au/article/new-tech-same-rules/) — they
apply regardless of platform or production method, so AI-generated advertising is assessed under
the same rules, with the requirement that advertising be clearly distinguishable as advertising.
The AANA opened a public review of its Code of Ethics in
[October 2025](https://info.aana.com.au/hubfs/Self-Regulation/20251007%20Code%20of%20Ethics%20Review%20-%20Discussion%20Paper/AANA%20Code%20of%20Ethics%20Review%202025%20Discussion%20Paper.pdf).

The 81% figure describes posts flagged for follow-up in a 2023 sweep, not breaches established. It
is the most-quoted number in this area and it is routinely overstated.

**Bearing:** C3, W1, and W2.

### Theme 6 — automated-decision transparency becomes a statutory obligation on 10 December 2026

The [Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2024A00128/asmade)
inserted APP 1.7, requiring APP entities to disclose in their privacy policy where a computer
program makes, or substantially assists in making, decisions that could reasonably be expected to
significantly affect an individual's rights or interests using their personal information. The
obligation [starts on 10 December 2026](https://jws.com.au/what-we-think/practical-implications-of-new-transparency-requirements-for-automated-decision-making/),
is drafted broadly enough to capture AI systems, rule-based tools and automated assessment
technologies, and carries OAIC infringement and compliance notice powers plus civil penalties.

The obligation is transparency in the privacy policy, not a right to an explanation of an
individual decision, and it is not a general AI law.

**Bearing:** C3, W2, and G2.

### Theme 7 — employers say the durable capabilities are the ones the degree already claims

Across the [2026 GMAC survey](https://poetsandquants.com/2026/06/25/employers-still-want-mbas-but-1-in-3-are-replacing-entry-level-roles-with-ai-gmac-finds/)
([June 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/06/26/gen-z-entry-level-jobs-replaced-by-ai-new-gmac-recruiters-survey-tech-manufacturing-jobs-most-at-risk/))
and the Australian Marketing Institute's competency work, the capabilities named as retained are
judgement, communication and commerciality rather than execution. The AMI launched a Marketers'
Competency Framework and Assessment Tool of 25 competencies across marketing, business and people
domains, reported by [Mi3 on 10 February 2025](https://www.mi-3.com.au/10-02-2025/avoiding-being-one-trick-pony-australian-marketing-institute-unveils-competency),
with commerciality and AI recorded as the skills marketers at every level and seniority most want
to develop.

This is a statement about what employers and a professional body say they value. It is not
evidence that graduates of this program hold those capabilities.

**Bearing:** C2, C4, and W1.

## 5. CURRICULUM IMPLICATIONS

| # | Implication | Dimension | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| CI-1 | AI appears only in marketing electives (Marketing Communications, Product Management); no compulsory subject across the 187.5 credit points of core assesses AI capability, limits or governance, while the governance duty is being written onto the general manager (§3 theme 1) | C3 | Move the AI content already taught in the electives into a compulsory core subject and assess governance on the program's own terrain — accountability for an automated targeting decision, disclosure under APP 1.7, substantiation of a machine-generated claim. For an MBA-structured degree this displaces core credit points and is a program-architecture decision, not a subject edit |
| CI-2 | No core subject assesses appraisal of work quality against criteria; the only peer-judged core item appraises class participation. Themes 2, 4 and 7 all describe judging evidence — a model's output, a claim's substantiation, a synthesis's soundness — as the retained capability | C2 | Add criterion-referenced critique against a published rubric to assessment that already exists: the syndicate deliverables in the general management core and the Consulting Practicum client report. No new content is required |
| CI-3 | Marketing Research is compulsory and collects primary data, but methodology is never defended under scrutiny. Theme 2 describes a measurement market in which methodology choice is the decision that matters and vendors differ on validation practice | C5 | Add a staged supervised methodology defence to the existing Marketing Research field project. This is among the cheaper level-3 steps available to the program because the primary-evidence project already runs |
| CI-4 | The Social Entrepreneur Consulting Practicum places students with a real client and is assessed on a contract and a client report, but professional conduct is not named among the assessed criteria. Theme 3 describes commerciality and board credibility as the condition of marketing leadership | W1 | Document the external client's judgement as assessment against stated conduct and commercial-argument criteria, and require the recommendation to be defended in a CFO's terms |
| CI-5 | Practitioner-format tasks are concentrated in the two capstones and the marketing core; the 150 credit points of general management core are assessed largely by hurdle examinations, quizzes and class-contribution marks, at a time when entry-level execution roles are the ones being automated (§2 signal 1) | W2 | Scaffold practitioner artefacts back through the earlier core stages rather than adding another capstone. This is a coursework redesign across the general management sequence |
| CI-6 | Exposure for this program is borrowed from the parent MBA record rather than measured on its own graduates (§2 signal 5), and the destination evidence in the labour-evidence block is field-of-education level | D10 | Commission graduate destination reporting at role-title granularity for the dual award specifically, so the bifurcation in §1 can be measured rather than inferred |

### Critical evidence gaps to close before institutional use
1. Destination data for the MC-BAMKTG dual award at role-title granularity, to test whether graduates land in the low-substitution leadership families or the high-substitution execution families of §1
2. Australian survey evidence — as opposed to trade-press reporting — on how marketing budgets are argued and approved, to put theme 3 on a measured footing
3. Adoption rates for the AICD/HTI eight-element framework among Australian boards, to establish whether theme 1 describes practice or aspiration
