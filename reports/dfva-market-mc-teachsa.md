## 3. CURRENT DISCUSSION SIGNALS — MEDIUM CONFIDENCE

**What these sources are.** Australian and US government regulatory and policy publications (AITSL, the Australian Government Department of Education, Jobs and Skills Australia, New York State and Colorado education agencies), a national trade-press report (ABC News), a sector policy brief (Responsible AI Australia), a peer-reviewed-methodology labour-exposure dataset (Felten-Raj-Seamans AIOE), and business/trade press covering one company's layoffs (CNBC, Higher Ed Dive). Direct extraction from X or LinkedIn was **not** performed as market discourse, and no professional teaching forum was sampled — the "discourse" in this section is the profession records' L1-L3 lane claims (regulatory documents, government exposure indices, and dated trade press), each independently checked in this session, plus no additional web search was required to fill gaps because the supplied L1-L3 material was sufficient.

### Theme 1 — Australian school systems are moving from broad AI-use principles to enforceable, in-class assessment redesign

The Australian Framework for Generative Artificial Intelligence in Schools, endorsed by Education Ministers and reviewed in June 2025, set six national principles (teaching and learning, wellbeing, transparency, fairness, accountability, privacy/security/safety) for gen-AI use in schools ([Department of Education](https://education.gov.au/schooling/resources/australian-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence-ai-schools), 2025-06). By August 2026 that had translated into binding classroom practice: [ABC News (2026-08-25)](https://abc.net.au/news/2026-08-25/how-education-departments-across-country-are-cracking-down-on-ai/107026976) reports NSW proposing a ban on unsupervised take-home assignments for Years 11-12 after more than 1,000 recorded 2025 cheating cases, alongside NESA's existing 2024 AI-academic-integrity policy and a Victorian primary-school screen-time cap from Term 1 2027. Responsible AI Australia's 2026 education brief adds that schools must run privacy due diligence before procuring AI tools and keep a human in the loop on judgements such as student-achievement marking and report writing ([Responsible AI Australia](https://responsibleaiaustralia.com.au/industries/education), 2026).

This supports a claim about what Australian state systems now require of teachers' assessment practice; it does not support a claim about national employment levels, teacher pay, or the practitioner profession's own attitude toward the changes — none of these three sources measured that.

**Bearing:** W2 (the program's assessment spine is currently a Lesson Plan/pedagogical-content-knowledge report pair, not yet documented as designed to resist unsupervised-AI misuse); C5 (the Capstone's evidence-based inquiry requirement is the program's existing vehicle for teaching process-tracked, non-take-home evidence collection).

### Theme 2 — secondary teaching scores high on AI task-exposure indices, concentrated in planning and admin rather than in-class relational work

The DFVA v31 occupation crosswalk (Felten-AIOE lineage, rescaled 0-100) scores Secondary School Teachers at 91.99/100, with middle-school teaching the highest of the four school-teacher levels at 94.25 (25-2031-L2-01). Jobs and Skills Australia's national Gen AI Capacity Study (August 2025) finds education-professional occupations cluster toward the higher-exposure end nationally because core tasks — lesson planning, resource creation, feedback, assessment drafting — are language- and knowledge-intensive ([Jobs and Skills Australia](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/studies/generative-artificial-intelligence-capacity-study/occupation-data-on-ai-exposure), 2025-08). The related US catch-all occupation "Teachers and Instructors, All Other" separately scores 1.486 on the Felten-Raj-Seamans Language Modeling AIOE extension, the 95th percentile of 774 US SOC occupations ([AIOE-Data](https://github.com/AIOE-Data/AIOE)) — though the underlying profession record flags and corrects an earlier framing that overstated how closely this matches top postsecondary humanities-teaching exposure scores.

These are task-similarity model estimates, not observed employment, wage, or job-loss outcomes, and the AIOE figure is a US SOC dataset with no direct ANZSCO mapping — it corroborates the shape of the exposure story without being additive evidence for the Australian market specifically.

**Bearing:** C3 (capped at Level 1 — ICT content never names generative AI or assesses its capabilities/limits/ethics — is exactly the governance gap this high exposure profile leaves unaddressed); C2 (reflective appraisal of AI tool reliance is not documented as an assessed requirement, short of the Level 3 threshold).

### Theme 3 — adjacent paraprofessional and tutoring markets show diverging exposure, from low-risk teacher aide roles to an already-disrupted homework-help sector

Jobs and Skills Australia scores Education Aides (ANZSCO 4221, containing Teachers' Aides) at 0.31 automation exposure and 0.65 augmentation exposure — the low-automation, medium-augmentation band most of the Australian workforce sits in (bx-L2-1, 25-9041). By contrast, Chegg — an online homework-help and tutoring platform — cut 22% of its workforce in May 2025 and a further 45% (388 roles) in October 2025, attributing the collapse in student traffic to Google's AI Overviews and to students shifting to ChatGPT since late 2022 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/05/12/chegg-to-lay-off-22percent-of-workforce-as-ai-shakes-up-education-tech-industry.html), 2025-05-12; [Higher Ed Dive](https://www.highereddive.com/news/chegg-layoffs-strategic-alternatives-google-ai/804192/), 2025-10-29).

The Chegg cuts are the company's own corporate workforce (product, engineering, support), not a census of contract tutors, and the JSA figure covers an Australian ANZSCO group broader than the narrower US teacher-assistant occupation — neither source supports a precise headcount claim for either occupation.

**Bearing:** W3 (the program's placement-heavy model — 60 placement days plus a hurdle-gated 60-hour WIL Capstone — sits structurally opposite the disintermediated, content-delivery tutoring pathway that Chegg's collapse illustrates); C4 (the Professional Learning Plan's assessed identification of learning needs is the mechanism that would let graduates adapt away from commodity content delivery, the segment showing the clearest disruption).

## 5. CURRICULUM IMPLICATIONS

| # | Implication | Dimension | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| CI-1 | C3 caps at Level 1 (ICT content never names generative AI; nothing assesses AI capabilities/limits/ethics) while both the AU crosswalk (91.99/100) and the related US index (95th percentile) score secondary/related teaching among the most exposed occupations | D3 Technical Currency | Add an assessed AI-capabilities/limits/ethics component that explicitly names generative AI, extending the existing "safe and ethical use of ICT" outcome rather than replacing it |
| CI-2 | State systems (NSW, Victoria) now mandate oral/process-based assessment evidence to counter AI-enabled cheating, but the program's own assessed artefacts (W1's podcast/script, W2's lesson plan/report pair) are not documented as judged against an external professional audience or against this specific policy shift | D2 Workplace Alignment | Extend the Assessment for Graduate Teaching scenario-response component to require candidates to design assessment tasks resistant to unsupervised-AI misuse, evidencing the state policy directly in coursework |

### Critical evidence gaps to close before institutional use:

1. The Adzuna employer and skill data for these occupations are generic placeholders rather than verified, teaching-sector-specific data — a targeted named-employer and skill-text analysis of AU secondary-teaching job ads has not yet been done.
2. The supplied `mc-teachsa` evidence extract does not name the program's actual Learning Area subject specialisations, so §1's family-to-specialisation mapping should be revisited once that detail is available.
3. The only Teacher Assistant (SOC 25-9041) regulatory claims in the supplied record are US-specific (New York, Colorado); no Australian teacher-aide regulatory claim beyond the JSA exposure score was found.
4. The Chegg evidence (Theme 3) is a US-market, corporate-level data point; no Australian tutoring-market employment data was found in the supplied records to corroborate or refute it locally.
