### Theme 1 — in Australia, entry to the teaching profession is regulated nationally through AITSL's Accreditation of ini…

In Australia, entry to the teaching profession is regulated nationally through AITSL's Accreditation of initial teacher education programs in Australia: Standards and Procedures (2025), under which every initial teacher education program must be accredited so its graduates meet the Australian Professional Standards for Teachers at the Graduate career stage. State and territory teacher registration authorities (for example VIT in Victoria and NESA in NSW) then register teachers to practise under their respective Education Acts, so Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Vocational Education requires an accredited teaching qualification and registration to lawfully teach in an Australian school. (Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Vocational Education; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Australian Institute for Teaching and School Leadership (AITSL), Accreditation of initial teacher education programs in Australia: Standards](https://aitsl.edu.au/tools-resources/resource/accreditation-of-initial-teacher-education-programs-in-australia---standards-and-procedures) (2025).

Scope: Australian school teachers (all states/territories); binds who may lawfully teach. Does not cover unregistered paraprofessionals or out-of-school tutoring.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 2 — jobs and Skills Australia's Gen AI Capacity Study (August 2025) scores 358 ANZSCO occupations on augmentati…

Jobs and Skills Australia's Gen AI Capacity Study (August 2025) scores 358 ANZSCO occupations on augmentation and automation exposure using a task-level ILO/Felten framework. It finds most Australian workers face low automation risk, but education-professional occupations cluster toward the higher-exposure end because their core tasks (lesson planning, resource creation, feedback, assessment drafting) are language- and knowledge-intensive and readily supported by generative AI. (Secondary School Teachers, Except Special and Vocational Education; lane L2, scholarly and grey-literature studies.)

Sources: [Jobs and Skills Australia, Our Gen AI Transition: Exposures, Adaptation, Dynamism (Generative AI Capacity Study)](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/studies/generative-artificial-intelligence-capacity-study/occupation-data-on-ai-exposure) (2025-08).

Scope: Australian occupations nationally; model-derived task-level estimates, not employment forecasts.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 3 — in August 2026, state education departments moved to curb student AI cheating in ways that reshape teacher…

In August 2026, state education departments moved to curb student AI cheating in ways that reshape teacher assessment design: NSW proposed banning unsupervised take-home assignments for Years 11-12 (prompted by more than 1,000 recorded cheating cases in 2025) and NESA's 2024 AI-in-schools policy treats unapproved AI use as an academic-integrity breach, while Victoria will impose primary-school screen-time caps from Term 1 2027. Teachers must therefore collect evidence of learning through oral, performance and in-person modes less exposed to AI misuse. (Elementary School Teachers, Except Special Education; lane L3, trade and professional press.)

Sources: [ABC News, How education departments across the country are cracking down on students using AI to cheat](https://abc.net.au/news/2026-08-25/how-education-departments-across-country-are-cracking-down-on-ai/107026976) (2026-08-25).

Scope: Australian state school systems; reporting, not a primary study. NSW/VIC specific examples.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 4 — responsible AI Australia's 2026 education brief notes the national gen-AI schools framework is implemented…

Responsible AI Australia's 2026 education brief notes the national gen-AI schools framework is implemented by states through binding policies, and that schools must run privacy due diligence before procuring AI tools (data storage, model training on inputs, third-party sharing), prohibit entering student or staff personal information into public gen-AI tools, and keep humans in the loop on judgements such as student-achievement marking and report writing. These constraints define the boundaries of AI adoption a teacher can undertake. (Elementary School Teachers, Except Special Education; lane L3, trade and professional press.)

Sources: [Responsible AI Australia, AI Regulation in Australian Education (2026): Schools, Universities, Rules and Updates](https://responsibleaiaustralia.com.au/industries/education) (2026).

Scope: Australian school education; practitioner/policy brief, not a primary study.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 5 — the Australian Framework for Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Schools, endorsed by Education Mini…

The Australian Framework for Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Schools, endorsed by Education Ministers in June 2025 after the National AI in Schools Taskforce review, guides responsible and ethical GenAI use by all school-connected people including school leaders, and makes clear tools must respect privacy, comply with Australian law and protect student data. (Education Administrators, Elementary and Secondary School; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Australian Department of Education, Australian Framework for Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Schools](https://education.gov.au/schooling/resources/australian-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence-ai-schools) (2025-06).

Scope: Australian school leaders, teachers, support staff, students, parents across all sectors.

**Bearing:** C3 and W1 and G1.

### Theme 6 — aITSL's Australian Professional Standard for Principals frames what principals and school leaders are expec…

AITSL's Australian Professional Standard for Principals frames what principals and school leaders are expected to know, understand and do, and is the reference point for how school leaders embed responsible technology use including GenAI into instructional leadership. (Education Administrators, Elementary and Secondary School; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [AITSL, Australian Professional Standard for Principals](https://www.aitsl.edu.au/tools-resources/resource/australian-professional-standard-for-principals).

Scope: Australian school principals and school leaders.

**Bearing:** G1.
