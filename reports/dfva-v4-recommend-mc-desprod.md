## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the scored evidence and market data above; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

The Master of Design and Production (MC-DESPROD) scores 6 / 15 on curriculum
adaptiveness against a comparatively stronger 7 / 9 on workplace practice. The
program's structural strength is Industry Practice Parts 1–2 (W3 = 3/3): two
core, compulsory 200-hour placements with practitioner supervision and a
written host-partner report. Both gates pass — G1 on an all-compulsory,
staged specialist core, and G2 on the graded "Design budgets & schedules"
component, which requires defended trade-off decisions under real resource
constraints.

The adaptive sub-scale is where the program is weakest. C1, C2, C3 and C5
each sit at Level 1: collaboration and reflection appear only as unassessed
outcomes or ungraded-against-criteria activity, and no core unit teaches AI
capabilities, limitations, ethics or governance — the Design Projects
sequence documents digital-tool *operation* only ("a particular emphasis on
the use of graphic software"). C4 alone reaches Level 2, carried by the
Design Projects 3 capstone's cross-context application requirement. The
market report's own gap declaration (§5 implication CI-5) means every action
below rests on adjacent-industry and field-level evidence, not a
program-specific profession record.
