## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** FAIL — Demoted on verification. The PASS anchor requires one of three specific things: 'an all-compulsory specialist core, an accredited sequence, or a staged prerequisite chain.' MC-APLING satisfies none of them cleanly. The compulsory core is just two subjects (LING90039 + one specialisation subject = 25 points), and the rest of the 100-200 point program (75-90%) is drawn from a single shared elective pool (LING90002/3/5/7/8/15/16/20/21/26/28/34/42) that is nearly identical across all three specialisations (English Language, TESOL, Language Testing) — confirmed by comparing the elective lists on the three specialisation pages, which differ only in which one subject is compulsory. No accreditation is mentioned anywhere in the extract. No enforced prerequisite chain is documented — the only sequencing signal is 'It is strongly recommended that students take LING90039... in their first semester,' a recommendation, not a requirement, and it covers one subject, not a chain. The original rationale's framing ('foundation → specialisation-compulsory → capstone') is a real pattern but does not match any of the three PASS exemplars, and the FAIL anchor's negative test ('generic or interchangeable content without disciplinary identity') is close: the specialisation label attaches to only one compulsory subject out of a program that is otherwise a common Applied Linguistics elective pool. PASS was defensible only by treating 'disciplinary identity' (all electives are LING-coded) as sufficient on its own, which is a looser bar than the anchor's disjunctive list actually states.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — PASS anchor: 'Assessments require defended trade-off decisions, or simulations/capstones/live projects with real uncertainty and accountability.' The Minor Thesis capstone requires students to design, develop and carry out their own 'approved research project' and defend it through a hurdle-linked oral presentation, while the alternative LING90041 capstone requires teams to 'analyse, design, and conduct a real-life project' end to end. Both capstone routes place open-ended design and defence responsibility on students rather than recall of scripted material, though the extract does not use the words 'trade-off' or 'uncertainty' explicitly, so this rests on the documented capstone structure rather than an explicit statement.

**G1 FAILS.** A disciplinary-foundation failure is reported regardless of the adaptiveness score: the adaptive capabilities are scored on top of a foundation this program is not documented to establish.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **field** (field grain), n = 0, 7 destination titles, 2 at medium mapping confidence. The seven destination titles were mapped to the published Felten AI Occupational Exposure index (AIOE-2021 variant). Basis note: field-of-education occupation list (ATO-linked), not this program's own graduates

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (45 pages) and verified on 2026-08-31: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (6):

- C1: LING90041 (Projects in Applied Linguistics) is the program's only assessed collaborative-practice unit and is one of two alternative capstone streams (the other being the solo Minor Thesis); Level 3 requires collaborative assessment to recur across the program and to require coordinating people AND tools/AI systems, neither of which is documented, so the item was resolved to Level 2.
- C2: The 'Reflective paper (Individual)' assessed in LING90041 could indicate self-appraisal of quality (Level 1) or criterion-referenced appraisal (Level 2), but the extract does not describe its content or marking criteria; resolved to Level 0.
- C4: The Minor Thesis capstone route ('an approved research project') independently supports Level 3 (a self-scoped research capstone), while the alternative LING90041 route supports only Level 2 (novel real-world application); because the two are alternative, not combined, routes, the item was resolved to Level 2.
- W1: The LING90041 deliverables 'Project brief (Group)', 'Progress report (Group)' and 'Written report (Group)' resemble recognised professional genres (Level 2), but the extract does not confirm an audience beyond the teaching team or practice-derived judging criteria; resolved to Level 1.
- W3: LING90041's framing as a 'real-life project' communicated 'to different stakeholders' could suggest external client/community engagement (Level 2), but no external host, practitioner supervisor, or accountability arrangement is documented in the extract; resolved to Level 0.
- G1: demoted from PASS to FAIL on verification — the compulsory core is two subjects and the remainder of the program is a shared elective pool nearly identical across specialisations, which does not clearly meet any of the anchor's three PASS exemplars (all-compulsory specialist core, accredited sequence, staged prerequisite chain).

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (2):

- LING90018 Sociolinguistics and Language Learning — the compulsory subject for the English Language specialisation — has no overview or assessment page in the extract, so its assessment design could not be verified; scoring instead relied on the universal core subject (LING90039), the other two specialisations' compulsory subjects (LING90009 for Language Testing, LING90012 for TESOL), and the shared capstone options.
- No subject page for any Employability, ESL, or non-compulsory Graduate Elective subject (e.g. MGMT90132 Professional Communication, ESLA90004 Intercultural Professional Communication) appears in the extract, so their potential contribution to W1/W2 could not be assessed; this program was scored only on documented compulsory/capstone (core) evidence.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
