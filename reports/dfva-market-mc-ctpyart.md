## 3. CURRENT DISCUSSION SIGNALS — MEDIUM CONFIDENCE

**What these sources are.** Regulatory and statutory instruments, scholarly and grey-literature studies, drawn from the committed profession records for the occupations in §1, each with its publisher, date and link. Direct extraction from X or LinkedIn was **not** performed and no professional forum was sampled for this report; the profession records declare their own practitioner-discourse sample — practitioner_forums (45 items, National Office Clerks, General Association discourse); practitioner_forums (45 items, National Fine Artists, Including Painters, Sculptors, and Illustrators Association discourse) — which is a declared sample, not a scrape. Themes are the claims as recorded; nothing is paraphrased, and a claim without a source was not carried. Dates are given where the source carries one.

### Theme 1 — office clerks handle personal and commercial information; the Privacy Act 1988 APPs bind their APP-entity e…

Office clerks handle personal and commercial information; the Privacy Act 1988 APPs bind their APP-entity employers and the Fair Work Act 2009 requires seven-year employee-record retention. No occupation-specific licensing regime governs general office clerks. (Office Clerks, General; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Jacmac Law, Accessing employment records: Privacy Act and Fair Work Act intersection](https://jacmac.com.au/insights/accessing-employment-records) (2025).

Scope: Applies via employer; employee-records exemption; no specific regulator for the occupation.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 2 — 2025 evidence names clerical/secretarial work as the clear automation-exposed group; routine, rules-based o…

2025 evidence names clerical/secretarial work as the clear automation-exposed group; routine, rules-based office tasks are the most readily automated. (Office Clerks, General; lane L2, scholarly and grey-literature studies.)

Sources: [scty.org, AI at Work: The 2025 Evidence](https://scty.org/words/ai-work-2025) (2025-12).

Scope: Synthesis; clerical specifically flagged.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 3 — australia's Copyright and Artificial Intelligence Reference Group (CAIRG) confirmed in October 2025 that th…

Australia's Copyright and Artificial Intelligence Reference Group (CAIRG) confirmed in October 2025 that the government is not considering a text-and-data-mining exception; training generative models on fine artists' works without a licence is potentially infringing under the Copyright Act 1968, and the copyright status of AI-generated visual art remains unresolved. (Fine Artists, Including Painters, Sculptors, and Illustrators; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Attorney-General's Department (Australia), Copyright and Artificial Intelligence Reference Group (CAIRG)](https://www.ag.gov.au/rights-and-protections/copyright/copyright-and-artificial-intelligence-reference-group-cairg) (2025-10-28).

Scope: Australian federal copyright policy. Directly affects whether a fine artist's catalogue may be used to train image models and whether AI-produced prints attract copyright.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 4 — the Resale Royalty Right for Visual Artists Act 2009 (Cth) grants eligible visual artists a 5% royalty on c…

The Resale Royalty Right for Visual Artists Act 2009 (Cth) grants eligible visual artists a 5% royalty on commercial resales above $1,000, managed by the Copyright Agency and extended to 26 countries from 31 March 2025; moral rights of attribution and integrity under the Copyright Act 1968 are inalienable. (Fine Artists, Including Painters, Sculptors, and Illustrators; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Aurelian Lawyers & Advisers (Australian art-law update), Art Case Update: Changes in Moral Rights Law, NFTs and Cultural Heritage Practice](https://aurelian.art/stories/art-case-update-september-2025) (2025-09).

Scope: Australian statutory instrument binding the occupation (resale royalties, moral rights). AI-generated pastiches of a fine artist's style complicate attribution/moral-rights enforcement but do not remove the statutory right.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.
