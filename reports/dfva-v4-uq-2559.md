## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — The gate passes on the 'accredited sequence' and 'staged prerequisite chain' routes rather than on breadth. The program is documented as accredited by the Australian Computer Society, and the extract shows technical depth building through a named chain — an introductory software engineering course, then programming in the large, then an algorithms and data structures course that states the earlier course as assumed knowledge. That is disciplinary identity, not interchangeable generic content.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — The gate asks for defended trade-off decisions or projects with real uncertainty and accountability, and both forms are documented. Assessed outcomes require selecting and justifying combinations of data structures and algorithms and analysing operating-system performance trade-offs, and a staged major project is defended in a pass/fail identity-verified interview hurdle. Open-ended studio projects with conflicting constraints add the uncertainty route. This is not recall or scripted response only.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **field** (field grain), n = 0, 14 destination titles, 3 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: field-of-education occupation list (ATO-linked), not this program's own graduates

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (69 pages) and verified on 2026-09-07: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (9):

- Program-wide: the extract does not separate compulsory core from elective and major-specific options. It lists 68 units of study against a 48-unit program and names four majors, so no unit in it is documented as core. Every anchor from level 2 upward is worded 'core unit' or 'core assessment', so this uncertainty touches all eight items; it was resolved down wherever it changed the level, most consequentially in W3.
- C1 (2 vs 3): DECO2801 Human-Centred AI carries the outcome 'Evaluate the role of human-AI collaboration in enhancing decision-making, productivity, and creativity', which is the human-plus-tool coordination level 3 describes, but its assessment tasks are named generically. R2 (level 3 requires assessment evidence, an outcome is never sufficient) resolved it to 2.
- C2 (2 vs 3): 'Reflective Inquiry (AI-Assisted)', five submissions across a semester, is process-focused assessment involving AI and could be read as level 3's 'evidence strategy adjustment over time'. Nothing in the extract documents students justifying a reliance decision or defending AI-output quality, so the lower-level rule applied.
- C3 (2 vs 3): a whole course on Human-Centred AI lists governance structures and ethics among its topics and critique of AI systems among its outcomes, and every one of its tasks is a hurdle or exam. Level 3 asks for assessment that requires the critique or governance, and the task titles document only 'Assignment', an interview and an exam, so the evidence does not reach the anchor.
- C4 (2 vs 3): COSC3500 documents a self-scoped major project with staged milestones and a defence interview, which reads as level 3's 'self-scoped capstone or research project'; against that, it is one course rather than the documented program-level progression the anchor's opening clause requires, and the course actually labelled a capstone (DECO3801) works from a supplied brief. Resolved down.
- W1 (2 vs 3): DECO3801's 'Final Exhibit Demonstration' and COMP3820's real-world clients both point at level 3's external audience, but neither assessment is documented as judged by that audience and no assessed criterion names professional conduct or accountability.
- W2 (2 vs 3): the capstone brief with industry-level deadlines supplies the 'genuine constraint of practice' half of level 3; the 'assessment spine' half fails, because the pattern that actually recurs across the extract's courses is the 40-60% invigilated final exam hurdle.
- W3 (1 vs 2): COMP3820's assessed FHIR project for real-world clients is the 'live client project' level 2 names. It was scored at 1 only because core status is undocumented and the other work-situated courses are explicitly opt-in by application. If a compulsory-core list showed COMP3820 as core, this item would be 2.
- One construct, one home: DECO3801's team capstone was read for coordination in C1 and for task fidelity in W2, but its 'practice-based setting' is simulation, which the W3 construct excludes, so it contributed nothing to W3. COMP3820's client work scored the professional-audience half in W1 and the live-client half in W3, not both in one item.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
