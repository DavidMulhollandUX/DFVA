## 3. CURRENT DISCUSSION SIGNALS — MEDIUM CONFIDENCE

**What these sources are.** A peer-reviewed journal study (Emerald), a Brookings Institution research brief applying published academic exposure indices, a professional body's own global surveys (RICS) as reported in trade press (Construction Dive, Specification Online) and directly (RICS), a professional-body interview with a named practitioner (Association for Project Management), and a single self-identified practitioner's blog post. Direct extraction from X or LinkedIn was **not** performed and no professional forum was sampled — where this section refers to discourse, it denotes these named, dated publications, not a scrape of social platforms. All figures are reported plainly with the scope the source actually measured.

### Theme 1 — AI adoption in construction is accelerating industry-wide but remains uneven and largely pilot-stage

[Construction Dive](https://www.constructiondive.com/news/builders-ai-survey-adoption-gap-construction/761632/) (2025-10-01) reported that a RICS global survey of more than 2,200 built-environment professionals found 45% of organisations had no AI implementation at all, and only around 1.5% used AI across multiple processes, even though respondents rated AI highly for cost estimating, scheduling and risk tasks. A follow-up RICS survey reported by [Specification Online](https://specificationonline.co.uk/articles/2026-08-18/rics/ai-use-surges-across-property-and-construction-industries-but-firms-struggle-to-move-beyond-pilot-studies) (2026-08-18) found regular AI use in construction rose from 12% in 2025 to 19% in 2026, alongside a rise in security and privacy concern from 22% to 30%.

Both figures are global, sector-wide RICS survey results spanning construction broadly; they are not Australia-specific and not disaggregated by occupation. They support a claim about the pace of organisational AI adoption in the sector generally, not a claim about task-level automation of any single role in this program's job families.

**Bearing:** C3 and W2.

### Theme 2 — quantity take-off and cost-estimating tasks are the parts of quantity-surveying practice most exposed to AI

A [2026 peer-reviewed survey](https://www.emerald.com/jedt/article/24/1/171/1307366/The-impact-of-artificial-intelligence-on-the) (Journal of Engineering, Design and Technology, 2026-02-03) of 77 quantity surveyors in Sarawak, Malaysia found the two AI impacts rated most severe were "maximise work efficiency to improve productivity" and "improve quantity take-off from drawings," with professionals' skill and knowledge for communication and contractual issues the strongest predictor of AI adoption. A [practicing chartered quantity surveyor's own blog](https://aiconstructionhub.blogspot.com/2026/05/will-ai-replace-quantity-surveyors.html) (2026-05-17) makes the same distinction from the practitioner side: administrative tasks such as summarising documents, assisting with quantity calculations and drafting letters are the ones AI automates, while professional judgement in negotiation and site-specific decisions stays irreplaceable.

The survey is a self-reported sample of 77 Malaysian professionals, not Australian and not a measured automation outcome; the blog is one self-identified practitioner's account, not a representative view. Together they scope a directional claim — quantity take-off and administrative drafting are the AI-exposed sub-tasks within QS practice — not a measured displacement rate.

**Bearing:** C2, C3, and W2.

### Theme 3 — construction management sits in a higher AI-exposure occupational band, but disciplined human control at the start and end of a process is the practitioner-reported route to durable practice

[Brookings Metro](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-ai-durability-of-built-environment-careers/) (2026-03-12) applied the Felten-Raj-Seamans AI Occupational Exposure index and the IMF's AI-complementarity (theta) score to 148 US built-environment occupations and placed "construction managers" in the higher-exposure group of 33 occupations. In an [interview with the Association for Project Management](https://www.apm.org.uk/news/the-big-interview-james-garner/) (2026-03-17), James Garner — a RICS-qualified quantity surveyor now Gleeds' Global Head of Data and Insights & Analytics — argued firms should not use AI as a simple search tool, and that a disciplined "bookending" approach, keeping human control at the start and end of a process, could produce 20-50% efficiency gains provided firms build genuine AI literacy and address data-privacy and IP risk first.

Brookings' finding is a US SOC-based categorical placement, not a numeric score for the Australian ANZSCO occupation, and Garner's efficiency-gain figures are his own projection for his own firm's method, not a measured or cross-sectional result.

**Bearing:** C2 and G2.

### Theme 4 — practitioners report both concern and confidence about AI's effect on their own role, with no resolved consensus

[RICS's Q2 2025 skills survey](https://www.rics.org/news-insights/artificial-intelligence-in-construction-report) (2025-09-12) of more than 2,000 built-environment professionals worldwide found 44% of project managers and 38% of quantity surveying/construction professionals concerned about AI's impact on their own role, and 48% and 41% respectively felt overwhelmed by the pace of technology change — while 67-69% in both groups agreed AI would help them deliver greater value in future.

This is a prompted, self-selected RICS-membership survey response from a global sample, not evidence of what practitioners raise unprompted in forums or Australian discourse specifically. It supports a claim about surveyed sentiment, not about observed workplace outcomes.

**Bearing:** W1.

## 5. CURRICULUM IMPLICATIONS

### Critical evidence gaps to close before institutional use:

1. No Australia-specific AI-exposure or task-automation study for the ANZSCO construction-project-manager or quantity-surveyor occupations was retrievable — Jobs and Skills Australia occupation pages and the Productivity Commission's AI paper both failed to extract across repeated attempts, per the profession record's search log.
2. No direct numeric AIOE or theta score for this occupation was retrieved; the Brookings finding is a categorical group placement (higher-exposure group of 33), not an occupation-specific index value.
3. No claim in the profession record covers the property-valuation job family (Job Family 4) — that half of this double degree has no sourced AI-exposure evidence in this report.
4. The Adzuna job-ad snapshot does not disaggregate quantity surveying or contract administration from the "construction project manager" query, even though §3's sources identify quantity take-off as the more AI-exposed sub-function.
5. No Australian trade press, forum or social-media discussion of AI's effect on this occupation was found despite repeated searches — all discourse signals sourced above are global, UK or Malaysian in origin. Factiva (premium trade press) was unavailable for this research window (no interactive OpenAthens session).
