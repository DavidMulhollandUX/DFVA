## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the scored evidence and market data above; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

The Master of Art Curatorship provides strong disciplinary and professional grounding for museum and gallery practice. G1 passes on a coherent specialist sequence across art conservation, exhibition management, and museum history, and G2 passes on authentic curatorial trade-offs under real-world exhibition constraints and unwaiverable capstones. C4 (3/3) reaches the instrument ceiling through mandatory self-scoped research capstones across all three completion streams (minor thesis, practical exhibition project, or industry placement).

However, the program operates under moderate exposure (76.58) and holds addressable headroom across both sub-scales: **9 / 15 on Adaptiveness** and **5 / 9 on Workplace**. The primary areas for development are C2 (1/3) and C3 (1/3): evaluative judgement is unguided by published criteria or reliance/override logs, and digital/AI literacy is confined to elective media writing without core assessment of digital collection management (TMS/EMu) or algorithmic metadata ethics. On workplace practice, W3 (1/3) reflects that the 150-hour industry placement (ACUR90012) is an elective capstone option rather than a mandatory requirement for all candidates, while W1 (2/3) and W2 (2/3) rely on internal academic assessment rather than external industry panels.
