## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — PASS anchor: a coherent specialist core with progressive technical depth. COMP1100 is documented as the first of three core computer science courses on programming, and the structure stages that sequence — programming and discrete mathematics in year 1 with COMP1600 Foundations of Computing, software construction and computer architecture in year 2, then five 3000/4000-level computing courses in year 3. A mathematics prerequisite gates entry. This is a disciplinary identity, not interchangeable generic content, so the FAIL condition does not hold.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — PASS anchor: assessed simulation work carrying design trade-off decisions. COMP2300's assessment is 40% practical assignments against a documented requirement to design and implement a processor in a digital simulator and to evaluate trade-offs between designs and account for the implications of machine-level choices; COMP1100's 30% assignments require determining which algorithmic approach fits a problem from a named set of alternatives. The FAIL condition — recall or scripted responses only — is contradicted by these design and selection tasks, even though half of the core marks sit in final exams.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **field** (field grain), n = 0, 14 destination titles, 3 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: field-of-education occupation list (ATO-linked), not this program's own graduates

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (20 pages) and verified on 2026-09-07: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (6):

- W1 straddled levels 0 and 1: MATH1005 assesses 'Workshop contributions: 10%', which could be spoken participation in front of peers and staff (level 1) or graded attendance and written work (level 0). The extract does not describe the mode, so R1 and the resolve-down rule fix it at 0.
- C1 straddled levels 1 and 2: three core units in the study options — COMP1110 Structured Programming, COMP2100 Software Construction and COMP2420 — carry no assessment evidence in the extract (the first two pages are empty, COMP2420 has no page), and software-construction units commonly assess group work. With no documented collaborative core assessment the resolve-down rule fixes C1 at 1.
- C5 straddled levels 0 and 1: STAT1008 'Quantitative Research Methods' appears in the first-year elective list, which could read as an available introductory methods unit (level 1), but it is not core and its page carries no outcomes or assessment, so the score resolves down to 0.
- C3 straddled levels 1 and 2: CILO 1 claims solutions demonstrating understanding of ethical and social context, and the elective COMP2400 assesses privacy and security implications. Both are outside the assessed core (R2 bars the outcome statement; COMP2400 is an elective), so C3 stays at 1.
- W2 versus C2: COMP2300's 'Evaluate tradeoffs of different designs and ideas' could read as appraisal of quality. Under one-construct-one-home, appraisal belongs to C2 and task fidelity to W2; the trade-off evidence is used for the G2 gate and for W2's task description, not to raise C2, because no criterion-referenced appraisal of work is documented.
- G2 is the weakest of the two gate calls: the trade-off language sits in COMP2300 learning outcomes rather than in an assessment task description, and the assessment table names only 'Practical Assignments'. It passes on the documented simulator design work, not on the outcome statement alone.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (2):

- Three units named in the core study options carry no content in the extract — COMP1110 Structured Programming and COMP2100 Software Construction have empty 'Overview:', 'Learning Outcomes:' and 'Assessment Tasks:' sections, and COMP2420 is listed in year 2 with no course page at all. No item could be scored from them, so C1, C2, W1 and W2 rest on the four core units that do carry assessment tables (COMP1100, COMP1600, COMP2300, MATH1005).
- No unit outline in the extract mentions generative AI in any form, so C3 is scored on general digital-tool and ethics evidence only; the extract carries no evidence bearing on AI governance, bias, accountability or transparency.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
