## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the preceding scored evidence and market data; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

Two items sit at the floor. C3 (digital and AI literacy, including governance) and W3 (work-situated learning) each score 0 of 3 — no digital or AI content is documented anywhere in the core, and no placement, practicum or supervised-practice component appears in the course structure. Four items sit one level above that floor: C1 (distributed cognition and relational capability), C5 (inquiry and evidence generation), W1 (professional communication and conduct) and W2 (authentic task design) each score 1 of 3, with assessed evidence that reaches only part of the level-2 anchor. C2 (hybrid metacognition and evaluative judgement) and C4 (life-long learning and transfer) each score 2 of 3, one step short of the maximum. Both gates pass: the course documents an all-compulsory rehabilitation-science core (G1) and defended trade-off decisions under real contextual constraint (G2).

This program's destinations rest on a field-of-education basis rather than its own graduates, and the field's occupations carry their own external standards. ASORC's self-regulation model (§3 Theme 3) and NHMRC's and NATA's regulatory and accreditation regimes (§3 Themes 1-2) each set a competence or governance expectation the course does not yet assess against. 2025 evidence names the clerical and administrative segment of this program's own job-family map as the clearest automation-exposed group (§3 Theme 6) — a signal bearing on how routine work is assessed within the program, not on the program's overall exposure figure.
