## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — PASS condition: a coherent specialist core. Two thirds of the degree (96 of 144 UOC) is a disciplinary component in which every student must complete at least one named specialist major of 60 to 78 UOC, declared before Stage 2 courses begin, which is a staged specialist sequence rather than interchangeable generic content. The FAIL condition, generic content without disciplinary identity, is contradicted by the compulsory major even though the program describes itself as a generalist degree and the program-level compulsory core is only 6 UOC of science-skills and portfolio units.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** FAIL — The PASS condition requires assessments demanding defended trade-off decisions, or simulations, capstones or live projects with real uncertainty and accountability. Nothing in the required core documents that: the compulsory core is 6 UOC of an introductory skills unit and two zero-credit professional-development elements, the compulsory capstone SCIF3010 is a fully online career portfolio, and CILO 2's problem-solving claim is an outcome statement, not an assessment (R1, R2). The live projects with genuine uncertainty and client accountability (SCIF3000, the placement suite) are elective options whose enrolment is not guaranteed, so no student is required to meet the PASS condition.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **field** (field grain), n = 0, 14 destination titles, 0 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: field-of-education occupation list (ATO-linked), not this program's own graduates

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (47 pages) and verified on 2026-09-07: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (7):

- C1 straddled levels 1 and 2. SCIF1000 is a compulsory unit whose outcomes name a team-based project brief with evaluation of personal and peer performance, which reads as level 2 assessed collaborative practice; but its assessment table records only 'In-person - Standard (usually weekly or fortnightly): N/A%' with no task named, so assessment of the team project is not documented. Resolved DOWN to 1 under R2 and the never-resolve-upward rule.
- C2 straddled levels 1 and 2. The SCIF3010 portfolio evidences attainment against Program Learning Outcomes and Graduate Attributes rather than appraising the quality of a piece of work against exemplars. It was scored 2 rather than 1 because level 1's own condition, that no core unit assesses appraisal against criteria, is contradicted: the portfolio is a compulsory, graded, graduation-gating assessment built against a named standard set. It was held at 2, not 3, because no reliance or override decision is documented.
- C4 and C5 straddled levels 1 and 2 through the Employability Experiences block. Every student must complete 12 UOC from that list and many entries are research projects with data collection or novel-problem application, but the same list can be satisfied with conventional taught units (MATH3856, PHYS2116, GEOS2291, BIOS6671, AVIA3012), so no student is required to conduct an inquiry or tackle a documented novel problem. Resolved DOWN to 1 in both items.
- W1 straddled levels 1 and 2. The SCIF3010 digital portfolio is addressed to potential employers, which reads as a professional genre with an audience beyond the teaching team, but level 2 also requires judgement against criteria drawn from professional practice, which the extract does not document (the grading basis is Satisfactory/Unsatisfactory against program outcomes). Resolved DOWN to 1.
- SCIF3010 evidence straddled C2, W1 and W2. Under 'one construct, one home' its criterion-referenced appraisal is scored in C2; its employer-facing genre is discussed in W1 only as the ambiguity that was resolved down, and it is not scored in W2.
- The SCIF1000 outcome line about a team project with peer evaluation straddles C1 (collaboration) and C2 (appraisal of quality). It is cited once, in C1; the C2 score rests on SCIF3010 instead.
- G2 has no clean evidence in either direction: the PASS condition is unevidenced in the required core, while the stated FAIL condition (recall or scripted responses only) is also not directly evidenced, because the extract publishes no assessment task descriptions at all. Recorded as FAIL because a gate passes on documented evidence and none is present in the compulsory core.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (2):

- Assessment task detail is absent throughout the extract. Every course page renders its assessment table as a delivery mode with 'N/A%' and 'Hurdle: False' and no task description, weight or due point, so no item could be evidenced at level 3, which requires quoting the assessment task, hurdle or placement requirement (R2).
- The disciplinary spine is absent. Students must complete a named major of 60 to 78 UOC, but the extract contains no course pages for any major, so C1 to C5 and W1 to W2 could be scored only against the 6 UOC compulsory core and the Employability Experiences option list. Major-level assessment could raise several items and cannot be judged from this capture.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
