## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **combined** (both components), n = 631, 29 destination titles, 12 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: components: Master of Advanced Nursing Practice (exact) ∪ Master of Public Health (exact)

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (64 pages) and verified on 2026-08-29: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (5):

- C4 and C5: the Research pathway's NURS90164/NURS90165 Independent Study Project satisfies C4's Level 3 ('self-scoped capstone or research project') and C5's Level 2 ('design and conduct... with data collection'), but is required only on the Research pathway, one of the program's four elective pathways -- not documented as true of the program as a whole, so the lower, universally-guaranteed level was taken for both items; C5's own Level 3 anchor additionally disqualifies this route by name ('REQUIRED, not one route among several').
- W1, W2 and W3 could each score higher under the Professional pathway (POPH90286/POPH90287 Professional Practice: an extended supervised, assessed placement with 'Workplace supervisor assessment of the student's completion of the project' as a hurdle) and the Collaborative Global Leadership pathway's 'two-week fieldwork integrated learning experience'; W1 and W2 also higher under the Professional/CGL-only POPH90218 Public Health Leadership and Policy ('Policy Advocacy Pitch + Campaign Materials'; 'Systems Mapping'). None of these units are core to the Research or Coursework pathways, so the lower level guaranteed program-wide was taken for all three items.
- W2: Applied Program Development's 'Health program proposal document', set against 'public health scenarios drawn from Australian and international case-studies', is realistic enough to arguably satisfy Level 2 ('reproduces a professional task end to end'), but the extract never states that its marking criteria are drawn from practice, so Level 1 was taken.
- C5: the compulsory core-selective and core research-methods units (NURS90059, POPH90302, POPH90231) all assess a written proposal/protocol/design rather than actually conducting research and collecting data, which is Level 1 by the anchor's own wording ('introductory methods unit; literature-review assessment').
- C4 (verify pass): demoted from Level 2 to Level 1 -- the sole Level-2 evidence line came from a course Overview sentence, not from an assessed task, and no other core-across-all-pathways unit supplied qualifying assessed evidence.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (3):

- The Collaborative Global Leadership pathway's two capstone subjects ('Collaborative Global Leadership Part 1' and 'Collaborative Global Leadership Part 2') have no subject page, learning outcomes, or assessment breakdown in the extract beyond the one-sentence course-overview description of a 'two-week fieldwork integrated learning experience' -- their detailed assessment design could not be scored.
- The subject page fetched at the POPH90239 ('Professional Practice - S') URL returns content for an unrelated subject, Economics of Financial Markets (ECON30024), so POPH90239's own content and assessment (referenced as a discontinued alternative in the Professional pathway's capstone table, marked 'No longer available') could not be verified from this extract.
- NURS90072 Advanced Nursing Practice in Context documents a learning contract, self-directed scope-of-practice project and practice portfolio directly relevant to C4 and W2, but the subject is marked 'Not available in 2026', so it could not be used as current core/elective evidence.

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
