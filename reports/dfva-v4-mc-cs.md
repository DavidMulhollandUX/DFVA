### C1 — Distributed cognition & relational capability: 1/3

Level 2 requires that at least one *core* unit assess collaborative practice. The only
unconditionally compulsory Year 1 subject is COMP90044 Research Methods, and every one of
its assessments is individual — an individual literature review, an individual research
design assignment, an individual oral presentation, individual workshop exercises. The
compulsory Year 2 research project has no assessment page in the extract.

Assessed group work exists, but only in subjects a student may or may not take.
COMP90049 assesses *"A group-based (group of 2-3) research project essay"* and GEOM90008
assesses a *"Group project – data management design proposal"*; both sit in the
Foundational band, which requires *"Between 25 and 37.5 credit points of Year 1
Foundational Computer Science subjects"* chosen from six — three of which assess only
individually. The band is completable with no assessed collaboration at all. Teamwork
otherwise appears as a subject-level outcome (*"ILO 5. Demonstrate professional skills in
ethics and sustainability, communication, and teamwork."*), which rule R2 caps at level 1.

Level 3 is not reached on any reading: nothing in the extract assesses the division of
work between people **and** tools or AI systems.

### C2 — Hybrid metacognition & evaluative judgement: 2/3

The level 2 anchor lists structured critique as an exemplar, and the compulsory
COMP90044 supplies it: *"An individual critical literature review of 2000 words"* (40%)
and *"An individual research methods evaluation and design assignment of 2000 words"*
(40%), both tied to the assessed outcome *"Apply rigour and skepticism in the analysis,
interpretation and evaluation of research findings"*. That is appraisal of the quality of
research work, assessed, in a unit every student takes [[4]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0220-3).

Level 3 is not met: no assessment requires students to document and justify a decision to
rely on or override a tool, source or collaborator, to defend a judgement of AI-output
quality, or to evidence strategy adjustment over time. The nearest process-focused
artefact — GEOM90008's *"individually written 1-page reflection on the group project"* —
is in a choosable subject and reflects on a group project rather than on a reliance
decision.

### C3 — Digital & AI literacy, including governance: 1/3

Level 1 matches literally. Artificial intelligence is named in the course outcome as one
area of *"Specialist knowledge in (at least) one of artificial intelligence,
cybersecurity, human-computer interaction, programming languages & distributed systems,
and spatial information science"*, and the AI-bearing subjects sit in the elective bands
(*"Artificial Intelligence electives"*, within *"Between 50 and 62.5 credit points of
Year 1 elective subjects"*) or in the choosable Foundational band. COMP90054 does carry
*"Ethics in AI planning"* and an assessed outcome requiring students to *"critically
evaluate the strengths, weaknesses, and ethical consequences of different approaches"* —
which would satisfy level 2 if it were compulsory. It is not. The sole compulsory Year 1
subject contains no AI or digital-tool content at all.

Level 3 is unreachable: the extract contains no generative AI, AI governance,
accountability, transparency or data-governance content in any assessment.

This is the finding worth pausing on. A computer science masters can carry the whole AI
curriculum of a leading university in bands a graduate may bypass entirely, and the item
that measures whether *every* graduate meets AI capability and limits scores 1 — the same
score as the Master of Public Health, whose core contains no AI content whatsoever.

### C4 — Life-long learning & transfer: 3/3

The only level-3 score in either of the two worked-example reports, and it is soundly
evidenced. The level-3 anchor names a self-scoped capstone or research project documented
as structured progression toward independent learning, and the handbook documents exactly
that as required curriculum rather than as an outcome statement: *"100 credit points of
Year 2 compulsory research project (in four parts)"*, described as *"a year-long research
project in a field of choice"* — half the degree. The progression is scaffolded and the
scaffold is assessed: *"COMP90044 Research Methods should be taken in the semester
immediately prior to the semester you commence your Research Project"*, and that subject
assesses the outcome *"Survey relevant literature, identify gaps and formulate productive
research questions"* through a 40% literature review and a 40% design assignment. The
course-level statement of *"Research maturity, including the ability to independently
carry out a research survey, and plan, execute, interpret and report"* is corroborated by
required credit-bearing structure, not left as a claim.

Level 2 would understate this: the requirement is not one assessment on a novel problem
but a half-degree self-scoped project.

### C5 — Inquiry & evidence generation: 2/3

Level 2 is fully documented. COMP90044 assesses methodology selection and design
(*"An individual research methods evaluation and design assignment"*, 40%; assessed
outcome *"Conduct effective research design and planning"*), and the compulsory four-part
project requires students to execute an inquiry.

Level 3 additionally requires methodology defended under scrutiny — viva, defence, or
staged supervised review — and that is not documented. COMP90078–COMP90081 have no
subject or assessment pages in the extract, so no defence is quotable, and the four-part
staging appears only as enrolment sequencing (*"Students studying full-time must take
Part 1 and 2 concurrently, and Part 3 and 4 concurrently."*). The only assessed
methodological defence in the extract is a 5-minute individual oral presentation worth
15%, which is too thin to carry "under scrutiny" alone. Evidence straddles 2 and 3; the
lower level is taken. **This is a capture gap as much as a curriculum finding** — if the
project subjects document a supervised review, C5 moves to 3 with no curriculum change.

### W1 — Professional communication & conduct: 1/3

The level 1 anchor is met precisely. Communication and professional conduct appear in the
course-level outcomes — *"Verbal and written communication skills that enable them to make
a meaningful contribution to our changing society"* and *"Professional ethics and
responsibility towards the profession and the community"* — and core assessment adds a
spoken component in COMP90044's *"individual oral presentation relating to the research
methods evaluation and design assignment"*. That presentation is delivered to the teaching
team and peers.

Level 2 requires a recognised professional genre **or** an audience beyond the teaching
team, judged against criteria drawn from professional practice. The compulsory
assessments are a 2000-word literature review, a 2000-word research design assignment, an
oral presentation and workshop exercises — all academic genres addressed to the marker.
No client brief, consultancy advice, pitch, industry panel or public-facing artefact is
documented anywhere in the core.

Under rule R2, and for the reason Barrie's graduate-attribute work makes explicit —
institutions assert generic attributes and do not measure them [33] — the stated course
outcomes and the Melbourne graduate attributes cannot themselves raise this score. HESF
cl. 1.4.2(b) asks for generic skills *applied in the discipline's context*, and cl. 1.4.3
asks that assessment be capable of confirming the outcomes are achieved [[19]](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2021L00488); the first is
claimed here and the second is not evidenced for communication.

### W2 — Authentic task design: 1/3

The compulsory COMP90044 assessments are contextualised by a research problem but
academic in artefact and in stated criteria — level 1. The genuinely practitioner-shaped
tasks in the extract are real and worth naming: GEOM90008 runs a staged
design-proposal-then-realisation of a spatial database (*"Group project – realisation of
the spatial database previously designed in the project design proposal, assessed via a
report of a maximum of 15 pages (per group) and a set of database queries proving the
realised implementation"*), and INFO90004 assesses an *"EXPERT EVALUATION"* producing a
heuristic evaluation and cognitive walkthrough of an interactive technology. Both are
close to what practitioners produce. Both sit in the choosable Foundational band, and
neither documents criteria drawn from professional practice.

The compulsory research project could plausibly be an end-to-end research-practice task,
but its assessment page is absent, so neither artefact nor criteria can be quoted.

Rule R4 was load-bearing here. COMP90049's outcome text reads *"Design, implement, and
evaluate machine learning systems for real-world problems"*. Under an earlier reading that
phrase would have looked like evidence of authenticity; it is a label, in a choosable
subject, and it earned nothing [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634).

### W3 — Work-situated learning: 0/3

Level 0, and unambiguously. The complete statement of requirements is *"100 credit points
of Year 1 subjects"* — one compulsory subject, a Foundational band and electives — plus
*"100 credit points of Year 2 compulsory research project (in four parts)"*. No placement,
practicum, internship, industry-hosted component or community-based project appears in any
of it, and no practitioner supervision or accountability to an external host is documented
anywhere in the 200 credit points.

Two things this score does **not** say. It does not say the program is weak: the research
project is a genuine high-impact practice in Kuh's sense [27], and it is what earns C4 its
3. And it does not predict employment: work-integrated learning is not evidenced to raise
employment rates [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z), and this program's destinations are strong. What the 0 records is
that no graduate of this program is required to have worked inside a professional
community before they are certified as ready to join one — the pre-professional identity
formation that placement uniquely supplies [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551) is absent by design, not by oversight.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel D metadata.** Exact-name JIR alumni match, n = 41, 15 destination titles, all 15
inherited from the prior crosswalk vintage (0 newly mapped). n = 41 is a small sample;
the exposure value should be read with that in mind.

**Scoring basis.** The 2026 handbook as captured for this cycle: course page, structure,
attributes, the compulsory subject and the Foundational-band and elective subject pages
with their assessment pages. Both sub-scales were scored from this one extract. Each
evidence line quoted above was verified verbatim against the captured text, and each
level-boundary judgement was subjected to an adversarial review.

**A capture gap that bounds two items.** COMP90078–COMP90081, the four-part compulsory
research project, have no subject or assessment pages in the extract. That project is
half the degree. It is the documented basis for C4 = 3, and its absence is what holds C5
at 2 and W2 at 1. Two of the eight items in this assessment would be re-examined on a
capture that includes those pages, and the honest reading is that this program's scores
are bounded below on C5 and W2 by what was captured, not only by what is taught.

**Construct-boundary check.** COMP90044's oral presentation was scored in W1 as
communication to an audience, and cited in C5 only as the question of methodological
defence — not counted twice. GEOM90008's group work was considered under C1
(collaboration), not W2; its 1-page reflection under C2 (appraisal), not W2. No placement
evidence exists, so the C1/C4/W3 boundary did not arise.

**Four ambiguities are recorded** in the evidence file, all resolved downward under the
never-resolve-upward rule: C1 (the meaning of "core unit" in a band structure), C3
(COMP90054's ethics content in a choosable band), C5 (the undocumented project defence)
and W2 (the undocumented project artefact). C1 and C3 turn on the same structural
question — whether a required *band* is a core unit — which the content-validity panel
should settle, because it is a general question about band-structured degrees and not a
fact about this program.

**Standing v4.1 limitations.**

- Scores describe documented curriculum intent, not demonstrated graduate capability.
  The inference from documentation to capability rests on constructive alignment,
  which is stated here as an assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; assessed data
  governance counts toward C3 level 3 where it is taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al.
  2025, Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Physical and perceptual capability (the third of Frey and Osborne's engineering
  bottlenecks [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is not scored.
- A W3 score of 0 describes curriculum design, not student welfare or employability;
  required placements carry their own equity costs [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report)[[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634), and work-integrated learning is
  not evidenced to raise employment rates [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z).
- W2 is scored against computer science's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so comparing
  this program's W2 with the Master of Public Health's is weaker than comparing either
  with another program in its own discipline. The W3 comparison, which rests on the
  presence or absence of a documented placement, is the more robust of the two.
- W3 excludes simulation by design; simulation scores in W2. The two sub-scores are read
  together and never merged.
- The validation program that would answer the critique in Woods, Lyons et al.
  [[18]](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08969-3) — a content-validity panel, competency-framework crosswalks, and an
  inter-rater reliability study on this instrument — has not yet been carried out. The W
  items have had no external review at all. These scores are a single-rater application
  of a draft instrument.
