### 1. PROGRAM PROFILE
The Master of Public Health is the University's generalist professional-entry public health degree: 200 credit points comprising a 75-point compulsory core (Foundations of Public Health, Biostatistics, Epidemiology 1, Qualitative Research in Public Health, Applied Program Development, Public Health Leadership and Policy), a compulsory 25-point capstone chosen from four routes (Research Project, Professional Practice placement, Public Health in Practice, Collaborative Global Leadership), and 100 credit points of electives spanning epidemiology, health economics, global and Indigenous health, environmental and planetary health, and health systems. Entry points at 100, 150 and 200 points plus an intercalated MD–MPH route mean the compulsory share varies substantially by cohort.  
Typical early-career roles include public health officer, health promotion officer, policy officer, research assistant, data analyst, and epidemiology or contact-tracing support officer; mid-career destinations include epidemiologist, health economist, health program evaluator and senior policy officer.

### 2. AUTOMATION EXPOSURE PROFILE
Measured exposure is **89.46** on the Felten AI Occupational Exposure index (0–100, rescaled over the published population), the mean across 15 alumni destination titles drawn from this program's own graduate-destination record (n = 562). That sits **1.44 points below the placed-portfolio median of 90.9** — nominally "low exposure", but well inside the band where the position is not firm.

The internal spread matters more than the mean. Entry-stage destinations average **84.77**, but that average hides a wide split: the desk-analytic entry roles are among the most exposed occupations in the index (Research Analyst 97.60, Policy Officer 97.41, Data Analyst 96.55, Research Assistant 92.30), while the field-facing roles are among the least (Contact Tracing Support Officer 64.49, Epidemiology Support Officer 74.33, Public Health Officer and Health Promotion Officer 80.08). Mid- and senior-stage destinations are uniformly high (Epidemiologist 97.82, Health Economist 97.05, Health Program Evaluator 95.24).

The implication is that this program's exposure profile is bimodal, not moderate: graduates who land in the analytic and policy stream face near-ceiling task overlap, and graduates who land in community and field practice face roughly a third less. The mean of 89.46 describes neither group. Exposure here means **task overlap with AI capability, not projected job loss** — several of the most exposed destinations (epidemiologist, health economist) are also in structural shortage.

### 5. THREE THRESHOLD QUESTIONS
- **Q1:** Could a well-prompted AI agent produce 80% of this graduate's first-two-year output?  
  **UNCERTAIN** — it depends entirely on which entry stream the graduate lands in, and the program does not determine that. For the analytic and policy stream (data analyst, research analyst, policy officer, research assistant — four of nine entry destinations, all above 92 on the exposure index), a substantial share of first-two-year output is literature synthesis, descriptive analysis, and first-draft briefings that current tools produce competently. For the field stream (health promotion, community engagement, contact tracing), it is not close.
- **Q2:** Does this program train graduates to design systems, own decisions, or generate original insight?  
  **YES** — Applied Program Development and the compulsory capstone require students to design, implement and evaluate a public health program or investigation with, in the handbook's words, "a high level of personal autonomy and accountability", and the methods core equips them to generate primary evidence rather than only synthesise it.
- **Q3:** Will these graduates be more employable in 5 years than today, given AI trends?  
  **UNCERTAIN** — the demand side is strong and structurally growing (pandemic preparedness, climate and planetary health, ageing, One Health, and a public health workforce rebuilt post-COVID), but the entry rung graduates actually use is the most exposed part of the destination set, and the program currently sends them into it with no AI literacy in the compulsory core.

### 6. ANALOGUE GRADUATE PROFILE
Most threatened entry-level profile: the **"desk-analytic public health officer"** whose first years consist of literature reviews, descriptive epidemiological summaries, routine data cleaning and cross-tabulation, evidence briefs, and first-draft policy submissions.  
Threatening tool classes: general reasoning and research assistants, automated evidence-synthesis and systematic-review tools, statistical copilots that produce and interpret descriptive analyses, and briefing and submission drafting tools now embedded in government workflow.

### 7. VERDICT
The Master of Public Health is a methodologically serious program — half its compulsory core is research methods, spanning both quantitative and qualitative traditions, which is rare in a professional masters — attached to a destination set that is more AI-exposed than the program's own position reading suggests. It scores as low-exposure only by 1.44 points, and its measured mean conceals a bimodal split in which the analytic stream sits at the ceiling of the index and the field stream a third below it.

The binding constraint is not method and not domain: it is that the compulsory core contains no AI literacy at all while the fastest-automating destinations are precisely the analytic and policy roles most graduates enter. A degree that teaches confounding, study design and qualitative rigour has exactly the right raw material to produce graduates who can supervise and audit automated evidence work — it simply does not currently teach them to do it. The path to RESILIENT runs through embedding AI verification and governance in the methods core, hardening the systems and evaluation content that already exists into assessed core capability, and making the research capstone the default rather than one of four options.

### 9. THE REDESIGNED GRADUATE PROFILE
A resilient MPH graduate in 2027 is not a desk-analytic officer who produces the evidence brief. They are a **public health evidence owner** who can commission and audit automated synthesis, tell a confounded association from a causal one and say why, verify what a model retrieved or fabricated before it reaches a minister's brief, design and defend a study or program evaluation under real constraint, and hold the community relationships through which any of it becomes practice.

This profile combines: (1) the quantitative and qualitative methods core the program already teaches well, (2) practical AI verification and governance applied to health evidence, (3) systems framing across determinants with explicit failure-mode reasoning, and (4) the community engagement and Indigenous-health practice that remains distinctively human and is the least exposed part of the destination set. Their value moves from "produce the analysis" to "own the evidence claim, orchestrate human and machine work, and stand behind what goes to the public".
