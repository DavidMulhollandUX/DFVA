### Theme 1 — australian copyright law (Copyright Act 1968) protects human-authored original works; the Government's Copy…

Australian copyright law (Copyright Act 1968) protects human-authored original works; the Government's Copyright and AI Reference Group (CAIRG, est. Dec 2023) ran 2024 consultations and the Attorney-General confirmed (Oct 2025) the government is 'not considering a text and data mining exception,' leaving the copyright status of AI-generated design outputs unresolved. (Commercial and Industrial Designers; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Attorney-General's Department (Australian Government), Copyright and Artificial Intelligence Reference Group (CAIRG)](https://www.ag.gov.au/rights-and-protections/copyright/copyright-and-artificial-intelligence-reference-group-cairg) (2025-10).

Scope: Framework-level: affects all creators of copyright works incl. industrial designers' original designs; does not by itself mandate any change to how industrial designers practise.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 2 — the Design Institute of Australia (DIA) is the national professional body for designers; its voluntary DIA…

The Design Institute of Australia (DIA) is the national professional body for designers; its voluntary DIA Accredited Designer™ scheme (introduced 2004, refreshed May 2024) is aligned to the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) and ANZSCO Skill Level 1 and requires the DIA Code of Conduct and Professional Indemnity Insurance — industrial designers are accredited voluntarily, not via statutory registration. (Commercial and Industrial Designers; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Design Institute of Australia, DIA Accredited Designer](https://design.org.au/accreditation) (2024-05).

Scope: Voluntary professional self-regulation for DIA members; does not evidence a statutory registration/licensing regime that legally binds all Australian industrial designers.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 3 — safe Work Australia published guidance (23 July 2026) that AI and digital technologies are a work health an…

Safe Work Australia published guidance (23 July 2026) that AI and digital technologies are a work health and safety matter requiring the normal WHS risk-management process, and NSW's WHS Amendment (Digital Work Systems) Act 2026 is the first law imposing express WHS duties on employers using AI, automation and digital platforms - binding construction managers who introduce site cameras, scheduling tools or AI assistants. (Construction Managers and Quantity Surveyors; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [The Good Builder (reporting Safe Work Australia guidance and NSW WHS Amendment Act 2026), AI Workplace Safety Guidance: What Builders Should](https://thegoodbuilder.com.au/safe-work-australia-publishes-ai-and-digital-technology-safety-guidance-signalling-where-national-rules-are-headed) (2026); [OSIAT (Australian WHS Legislative Updates), Australian WHS Legislative Updates](https://osiat.com.au/australian-whs-legislative-updates) (2026-07-23).

Scope: National model WHS plus NSW (first jurisdiction with express digital-work-systems duties); primary duties in NSW not yet all commenced as at mid-2026. Binds PCBUs, including construction managers.

**Bearing:** C3 and W1 and G1.

### Theme 4 — state builder licensing (NSW Fair Trading, Victorian Building Authority, QBCC) holds the licensed builder r…

State builder licensing (NSW Fair Trading, Victorian Building Authority, QBCC) holds the licensed builder responsible for the accuracy of representations and quote claims, including AI-generated content - so a construction manager cannot delegate accuracy to a generative tool. (Construction Managers and Quantity Surveyors; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Mindiam (AI for Trades and Construction in Australia), AI for Trades and Construction in Australia](https://mindiam.com/industries/trades-construction) (2026).

Scope: State-licensed building work in NSW/VIC/QLD; the PCBU/supervisor must review and adopt AI output rather than rely on it.

**Bearing:** C3 and W1.

### Theme 5 — iCT occupations in Australia, including Web Developers (ANZSCO unit group 2612, Multimedia Specialists and…

ICT occupations in Australia, including Web Developers (ANZSCO unit group 2612, Multimedia Specialists and Web Developers), are not subject to statutory licensure. The Australian Computer Society (ACS) is the peak professional body for the ICT sector, the authorised assessing authority for ICT skilled migration, and sets voluntary professional standards through its Certified Professional (CP) / Certified Technologist (CT) scheme. (Web Developers; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Australian Computer Society, ACS — The Professional Association for Australia's ICT sector](https://www.acs.org.au/); [Australian Computer Society, Get ACS Certified](https://www.acs.org.au/professionalrecognition/certification-landing-page.html); [Australian Computer Society, IT occupations and ANZSCO codes](https://www.acs.org.au/msa/information-for-applicants/occupations-anzsco-codes/information-technology.html).

Scope: Australian ICT occupations. ACS standards are voluntary; migration skills assessment is mandatory only for visa purposes, not for domestic employment of Web Developers.

**Bearing:** C3 and G2.

### Theme 6 — jobs and Skills Australia's Generative AI Capacity Study (August 2025) finds generative AI is more likely t…

Jobs and Skills Australia's Generative AI Capacity Study (August 2025) finds generative AI is more likely to augment than automate work: augmentation outweighs automation, and the highest automation potential is concentrated in routine roles. At task-category grain, Technical Development & Engineering tasks show high augmentability (~0.69 on the 0–1 scale). (Web Developers; lane L2, scholarly and grey-literature studies.)

Sources: [Jobs and Skills Australia, Our Gen AI Transition – Exposure](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/studies/generative-artificial-intelligence-capacity-study/our-gen-ai-transition-exposure) (2025-08); [Jobs and Skills Australia, Our Gen AI Transition: Exposures, Adaptation, Dynamism (occupation data)](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/studies/generative-artificial-intelligence-capacity-study/occupation-data-on-ai-exposure) (2025-08).

Scope: Australian labour market; scores are model-derived task-level exposure estimates, not employment-change forecasts. Per-occupation ICT unit scores sit in the high-augmentation band but this claim reports the study-level finding and the task-category figure.

**Bearing:** C4 and W2 and G2.
