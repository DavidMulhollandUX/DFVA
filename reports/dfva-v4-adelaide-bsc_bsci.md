## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — PASS on the staged prerequisite chain route. Completion requires at least one major of at least 24 units and at least 24 units of Level III Science, and every listed major documents an all-compulsory Level I to Level II to Level III sequence with disciplinary identity — for example the Chemistry major's Chemistry IA and IB, then Organic Chemistry II and Physical and Inorganic Chemistry II, then Level III Inorganic, Organic and Physical Chemistry. That is progressive technical depth in a named discipline, not generic or interchangeable content. The caveat, recorded in ambiguities, is that the depth lives entirely in the major: the program-wide core is 3 units and the degree is explicitly student-designed.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** FAIL — FAIL because the PASS condition is undocumented. The gate passes on assessments requiring defended trade-off decisions, or simulations, capstones or live projects with real uncertainty and accountability. The extract contains no assessment description for any course in the Bachelor of Science structure, and no capstone or live project is required. The nearest evidence is PLO 3.2 and PLO 1.1, which claim designing and planning an investigation and reasoning about contestable knowledge, but R1 and R2 bar an outcome statement from carrying a gate that is defined on assessments. Scored FAIL rather than PASS under the never-resolve-upward rule; the extract does not positively show recall-only assessment either, and that limit is recorded in notScoreable.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **field** (field grain), n = 0, 14 destination titles, 0 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: field-of-education occupation list (ATO-linked), not this program's own graduates

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (5 pages) and verified on 2026-09-07: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (6):

- C1, C2, C4 and W1 all straddle level 0 and level 1: the capability is stated in a program learning outcome, and the extract documents no assessment anywhere, so neither the level-0 nor the level-1 anchor is fully satisfied. Resolved to 1 in each case by R2, which fixes outcome-only evidence at 1 uniformly.
- C5 straddles 1 and 2: PLO 3.2 to 3.4 claim designing an investigation and collecting, recording and interpreting data, which would read as level 2 if assessed, and named methods courses are compulsory within majors. No assessment task is documented, so resolved DOWN to 1 under the never-resolve-upward rule and R2.
- W1 straddles 1 and 2 on PLO 4.1's phrase about communicating to a range of audiences: an audience beyond the teaching team would be level 2 if a task documented it. No task does, so resolved DOWN to 1.
- W3 straddles 1 and 2: Defence Industry Internship (STEM 3500) is compulsory within the Nuclear and Radiation Physics major, which would be a core placement for students on that route. The major is invitation-only, security-clearance and GPA gated, and is one of 21 majors, so it is not core to the program. Resolved DOWN to 1.
- G1 straddles PASS and FAIL: the program-level core is 3 units and the overview describes a student-designed degree, which reads toward the FAIL side (interchangeable content), but the mandatory major carries an all-compulsory staged Level I to III sequence with clear disciplinary identity. Resolved to PASS on the major requirement, with the depth located in the major rather than the program spine.
- The extract appends four subject pages with assessment tables — BIOL1002, BIOL3010, HLTH3009 and HLTH3010 — whose codes appear nowhere in the Bachelor of Science program rules in the same extract (HLTH3009 and HLTH3010 render as outdoor-leadership courses, not the PUB HLTH 3009 and PUB HLTH 3010 listed as Bioinformatics major options). They were treated as not belonging to this program under R3 and used for no score.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (3):

- W2 — authentic task design: the extract documents no assessment task for any course in the Bachelor of Science structure, so neither the level-0 nor the level-1 anchor can be positively evidenced. Scored 0 as the floor and flagged here rather than inferred upward.
- G2 — decision-making under uncertainty: the gate is defined on assessment features, and the extract carries no assessment description for any program course. FAIL is recorded because the PASS condition is undocumented, not because recall-only assessment was observed.
- Assessment evidence generally: the program calendar pages captured here list unit values, course titles and completion rules only. Every C and W item that depends on assessment (the level-2 and level-3 anchors throughout) is capped by this absence, which is why no item scores above 1.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
