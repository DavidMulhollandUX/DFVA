## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the preceding scored evidence and market data; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

The Master of Global Competition and Consumer Law carries measured destination exposure of 94.83, among the highest in this field-of-education basis, and both regression gates fail: G1 because only the 25-credit-point capstone is unambiguously compulsory against 75 points of prescribed electives, and G2 because the cited hypothetical-exam format is the standard shape of nearly every elective in the program, not documented evidence of defended trade-offs under real uncertainty. Adaptiveness sits at 6/15 and workplace practice at 1/9. The program is built almost entirely on doctrinal exam and coordinator-approved research-paper assessment.

The market evidence in reports/dfva-market-mc-gcclaw.md bears most directly on the program's two largest destination families — Lawyers (Signal 3, §2, 4,820 postings; Themes 5 and 6, §3) and Accountants and Auditors (Signal 1, §2, 6,240 postings; Themes 1 and 2, §3) — both of which document AI-governance and reliance-and-override obligations that the current elective-heavy structure does not assess anywhere in the core. The interventions below prioritise the items those two families warrant most strongly before addressing the more thinly warranted work-situated learning gap.
