## 2. RECENT JOB AD SIGNALS

**Signal 1 — Strong demand across federal/state government graduate programs and public policy.**
Public sector employers (APSC, Victorian Public Service, DFAT, PM&C) recruit Arts graduates for policy analysis, machinery of government operations, and stakeholder consultation.

**Signal 2 — Massive deployment of generative AI writing, summarisation, and research copilots.**
Government agencies, PR consultancies, and media organisations mandate critical literacy in generative AI text synthesis, prompt engineering, and bias verification.

**Signal 3 — Escalating need for ethical AI governance, misinformation analysis, and cultural safety.**
NGOs, cultural institutions, and public bodies seek humanities graduates capable of analyzing AI bias, digital equity, and indigenous cultural intellectual property (ICIP).

**Signal 4 — High AI substitution pressure in routine text drafting, summary, and standard press releases.**
Descriptive writing and secondary research face near-total automation (**89.37** exposure), making critical argumentation, live stakeholder debate, and nuanced ethical analysis essential.

## 3. CURRENT DISCUSSION SIGNALS — MEDIUM CONFIDENCE

**What these sources are.** Institute of Public Administration Australia ([IPAA](https://www.ipaa.org.au/)), Public Relations Institute of Australia ([PRIA](https://www.pria.com.au/)), Australian Public Service Commission ([APSC](https://www.apsc.gov.au/)), Media, Entertainment & Arts Alliance ([MEAA](https://www.meaa.org/)), and peer-reviewed literature ([Australian Journal of Public Administration](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/14678500), [December 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/14678500)). Direct extraction from X or LinkedIn was **not** performed and no social media forum was sampled — where this section refers to discourse, it denotes published APSC workforce strategies, IPAA public administration reports, PRIA ethics codes, and peer-reviewed policy research by named bodies and authors. Dates are stated where available.

### Theme 1 — generative AI in policy synthesis, misinformation, and ethical accountability

Guidance from [IPAA](https://www.ipaa.org.au/) (November 2024) and *Australian Journal of Public Administration* (December 2024) emphasizes that while AI models generate preliminary policy briefs and analyze consultation submissions, public policy officers retain personal accountability for verifying source veracity, evaluating political feasibility, and upholding democratic integrity.

**Bearing:** C2, C3, and W2.

### Theme 2 — interdisciplinary collaboration, stakeholder engagement, and public advocacy

Reports from [APSC](https://www.apsc.gov.au/) and [PRIA](https://www.pria.com.au/) highlight that Arts graduates work in cross-functional teams bridging economists, data scientists, legal counsel, and community representatives, requiring collaborative coordination and spoken oral persuasion.

**Bearing:** C1, W1, and W3.

### Theme 3 — authentic policy briefs, empirical social inquiry, and capstone research

Standards from [IPAA](https://www.ipaa.org.au/) confirm that authentic ministerial briefs, live stakeholder engagement projects, and primary empirical social research represent the benchmark for humanities and social science graduate employability.

**Bearing:** C4, C5, and G1.

## 4. SKILL SHIFT SUMMARY

| Skill | Direction | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Generative AI text verification and source-veracity auditing | **↑↑ Rising sharply** | Theme 1 (§3) reports policy officers retain accountability for verifying AI-generated brief veracity and consultation analysis; C3 scores 1/3 — AI appears only as subject-matter content in one elective Discovery option, never as assessed core content. |
| Oral briefing and stakeholder-facing persuasion | **↑↑ Rising sharply** | Theme 2 (§3) and Signal 1 (§2) show Arts graduates working across cross-functional policy and comms teams requiring spoken negotiation; W1 scores 0/3 — every spoken deliverable in the extract sits in an elective work-integrated-learning subject, one of them marked unavailable in 2026, and no core Discovery subject assesses a presentation. |
| Mixed-methods primary empirical research | **↑ Rising** | Theme 3 (§3) names primary social research as the employability benchmark for humanities graduates; C5 scores only 1/3 — just one of thirteen Discovery options (LING10002) documents students designing and collecting their own data. |
| Critical argumentation and nuanced ethical/cultural analysis | **→ Stable, differentiating** | Signal 4 (§2) rates descriptive writing and secondary research near-total automation exposure (89.37) while naming critical argumentation, live stakeholder debate, and nuanced ethical analysis as the layer that stays essential precisely because of that automation. |
| Basic press release and copywriting without generative AI fluency | **↓ Falling** | Signal 2 (§2) shows government agencies and PR consultancies now mandate critical generative-AI literacy as baseline, displacing unassisted drafting. |
| Routine essay writing and uncritical secondary-literature summarisation | **↓↓ Falling sharply** | Signal 4 (§2) rates this exact work 89.37 substitution exposure, and C4 scores 1/3 — no core assessment requires application beyond the taught, summarised material. |

## 5. CURRICULUM IMPLICATIONS

| # | Implication | Dimension | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| CI-1 | Program fails Gate G1 due to broad substitutability and lack of an all-compulsory disciplinary core | G1 | Establish a compulsory 50cp core humanities and social sciences method sequence |

| CI-7 | Work-situated learning is restricted to optional electives (W3: 1/3) | W3 | Establish a required core community engagement practicum or industry consulting capstone |
