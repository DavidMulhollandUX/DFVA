## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — The program documents a coherent specialist core with progressive depth on all three of the gate's alternative routes. The core is large and compulsory (225 of 300 points, or 125 of 200), and the core list is almost entirely discipline-specific property subjects (valuation, property law, development, securitisation, property economics). It is staged, with Year 1 core separated from Year 2 and 3 core in the 300-point entry, and it carries a prerequisite chain from Property Research and Analysis into the research subject. It is also an accredited sequence, recognised by two property-profession bodies. Nothing here reads as generic or interchangeable content.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — Core assessment requires defended trade-off decisions under genuine uncertainty rather than recall or scripted response. Property Investment and Finance assesses four successive case analyses each framed as a decision — portfolio repositioning, portfolio construction, an investment decision on a proposed development, and a choice among financing options — and Property Development assesses development appraisal against viability, risk and return, with the outcome expressed as evaluating the viability of proposals. The program's own outcome statement frames property projects as evaluated in terms of risk allocations and viability, and the assessed cases operationalise that. This clears the v1 D4 >= 2 equivalent.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **exact** (measured), n = 65. Index variant AIOE-2021; crosswalk `data/aioe/v31_extension_crosswalk.csv`.

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (38 pages) and verified on 2026-08-15: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (6):

- C2 straddled 1 and 2. The Mock Court Room cross-examination is documented assessment in a core unit and is a hurdle, but the handbook does not state the criteria it is judged against, so 'criterion-referenced' is inferred from the moot-court form rather than quoted. Scored 2 on the anchor's own exemplar 'structured critique'; a reviewer who requires explicit criteria would score 1.
- C4 and C5 both draw on the ABPL90363/ABPL90362 research sequence. Under one-construct-one-home, the self-scoped topic and the documented progression toward autonomous work score in C4 (transfer and self-directed learning); the data collection, methods and supervised review score in C5 (inquiry and evidence generation). No line is used in both items.
- C5 straddled 2 and 3. A substantial research project is compulsory (one of two selectives must be taken, and both are research subjects) and it carries staged supervised review, which satisfies the level-3 scrutiny clause. But the level-3 primary-evidence clause is contradicted by the handbook's own wording, 'primary or secondary'. The scoring direction resolved this to 2.
- W2 straddled 2 and 3. Professional-artefact tasks recur across at least four core subjects, and 'full professional standard' supplies the profession's own standard of performance that level 3's second clause wants. The first clause failed: the assessment spine cannot be demonstrated when several core subjects are examination- or essay-dominated and the 300-point Year 1 stage is missing from the extract. Resolved downward to 2.
- W3 straddled 1 and 2. The Industry Based Research subject has every feature of a level-2 placement except the one the anchor makes decisive — it is not a core unit. It is a quota-limited selective alternative to a desk-based thesis. Resolved downward to 1.
- W1 level 3 would have been reachable if the host-organisation oral presentation in Industry Based Research counted as delivery to an external practitioner audience. It was excluded from W1 under one-construct-one-home, since placement evidence scores in W3, and it is in any case not core.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (3):

- The 300-point entry's Year 1 core subjects (ABPL90290, ABPL90274, ABPL90275, ACCT90041, ABPL90130, ABPL90029, FNCE90060, ECON90015) appear in the course structure but none of their subject or assessment pages are in the extract. The first stage of the 300-point program is therefore unscored, which specifically bounds W2's 'at least one per stage' test and any claim of progressive sequencing in W1. All items were scored on the core shared by both entry points.
- Advanced Property Analysis (ABPL90011, core) lists 'Assignment one' and 'Assignment two' with word counts only and no task description, so its two 25% assessments could not be characterised for C1, C2, W1 or W2.
- No gen-AI content of any kind appears in the extract — no core unit, elective or program-level outcome mentions artificial intelligence — so C3's levels 2 and 3 had no evidence to weigh rather than evidence that failed.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
