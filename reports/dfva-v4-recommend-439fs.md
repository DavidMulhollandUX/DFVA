## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the scored evidence and market data above; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

The Master of Food Science prepares graduates for technical and leadership careers in food technology, quality assurance, product development, and food regulatory affairs across Australia's major FMCG and agricultural processing sectors. G1 passes on a coherent specialist core spanning eight compulsory subjects (100 credit points) in food chemistry, processing, microbiology, and safety, supported by an assessed research capstone. G2 passes on defended food processing choices, HACCP risk identification, and gated capstone research proposals under uncertainty.

Under moderate exposure (**79.43**, well below the portfolio median), the program exhibits moderate headroom on Adaptiveness (**8 / 15**) and Workplace Practice (**4 / 9**). In adaptiveness, C2 (1/3) and C3 (1/3) are constrained: core units teach statistical analysis in R but contain zero content on AI predictive modeling, machine-assisted formulation, or digital food safety systems, while evaluative judgement is asserted in generic skills without assessed peer review or AI reliance logs. In workplace practice, W1 (1/3) and W3 (1/3) are restricted because spoken presentations are delivered solely to internal academic markers, and industry internships sit exclusively in the elective pool (Option 50–75 credit points).
