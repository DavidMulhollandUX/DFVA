## 2. RECENT JOB AD SIGNALS
> **Confidence: MEDIUM** — based on industry reporting and trade press (McKinsey, Screen Australia) and global studio signals; no live 90-day scrape of Seek/ LinkedIn was conducted for this field, and Australian-specific hiring volumes are **not** asserted here.

**Signal 1 — post-production and VFX roles are the first to feel task-level compression.**
McKinsey ([What AI could mean for film and TV production](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/technology-media-and-telecommunications/our-insights/what-ai-could-mean-for-film-and-tv-production-and-the-industrys-future), 2025) reports leaders seeing 5–10% productivity gains in specific use cases this year, concentrated in development and pre-production, with post-production (dubbing, localisation, footage clipping, VFX) expected to follow. It estimates AI could influence **~20% of original content spend** within five years and redistribute up to **US$60 billion** of annual revenue.

**Signal 2 — Australian public funders now collect AI-usage disclosure.**
[Screen Australia's AI Transparency Statement](https://www.screenaustralia.gov.au/corporate-documents/policies/ai-transparency-statement/) (2025) updated all funding application forms to capture AI usage in applications and in projects seeking agency funding — a governance signal that production roles now carry an AI-disclosure and rights-clearance expectation.

**Signal 3 — generative video tools reached production footage in 2025.**
Netflix's first original series with AI-generated final footage was noted on its Q2 2025 earnings call (reported by S&P Global Market Intelligence Kagan, July 2025); text-to-video tools (Sora, Runway) are now cited in the academic literature as capable of scene and camera-movement generation from prompts ([ScienceDirect, digital transformation of the film industry](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0308596125001181), 2025).

## 3. CURRENT DISCUSSION SIGNALS — MEDIUM CONFIDENCE

**What these sources are.** McKinsey industry-analysis reporting, the SAG-AFTRA AI policy timeline and agreements, Screen Australia's funding-transparency statement, and the peer-reviewed film-industry digital-transformation literature. Direct extraction from X or LinkedIn was **not** performed and no practitioner forum was sampled — the themes rest on named industry bodies, trade and academic sources, not on inferred practitioner discourse. Dates are given where the source carries one. Claims about Australian graduate hiring volumes are **not** sourced here and have been omitted rather than restated.

### Theme 1 — production workflows are being reshaped from development through post, post-production first

McKinsey ([What AI could mean for film and TV production](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/technology-media-and-telecommunications/our-insights/what-ai-could-mean-for-film-and-tv-production-and-the-industrys-future), 2025) finds leaders experimenting across development and pre-production now, with physical production and post-production expected to absorb AI over the next five years; post schedules could shorten once tools reach "professional-grade resolution and consistency." Banijay's partnership with Moments Lab for AI video discovery (Banijay press release, 30 September 2025) illustrates footage-library search and clipping already in production.

**Bearing:** C2 and C3. The compressible work is task execution in post; the resilient skill is evaluative judgement about when AI output meets premium standards.

### Theme 2 — performers' digital replicas are now governed by consent-based union agreements

The [SAG-AFTRA AI policy timeline](https://www.sagaftra.org/contracts-industry-resources/member-resources/artificial-intelligence) shows the union ratifying an Animation Agreement with voice-replica protections (March 2024), introducing California Assembly Bill CA 2602 on digital replicas (February 2024), and reaching a 2025 Interactive Media Agreement ratified **95.04% to 4.96%**; in October 2025 it secured opt-in-only voice/likeness use in OpenAI's Sora 2. These are binding labour constraints on how any produced content may use a performer's identity.

**Bearing:** C3 and W3. Any curriculum that places students into hosted production must teach replica-consent and rights-clearance as a workplace competency, not an elective aside.

### Theme 3 — generative video is reaching production footage but below premium quality

Netflix's Q2 2025 earnings call (S&P Global Market Intelligence Kagan, July 2025) is cited by McKinsey as the first original series with AI-generated final footage; [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0308596125001181) (2025) notes Sora and Runway generate scenes and camera movement from text prompts. McKinsey concurrently reports studio executives judging generated output not yet at a quality level to drive meaningful disruption to premium formats.

**Bearing:** C4 and C5. Tool fluency (prompt-shaped previs, gen-video literacy) is rising; the irreducible skill remains judging reliability and designing validation.

**Confidence restated:** MEDIUM. Theatrical/streaming production discourse is well documented by McKinsey and SAG-AFTRA, but Australian employer hiring volumes and local role redesign are not sourced here and should not be inferred from the global signals.

## 4. SKILL SHIFT SUMMARY

### Declining Demand
- Manual footage search, tagging, and routine subclip assembly
- Unassisted rotoscoping, wire removal, and basic cleanup tasks in VFX
- Routine translation and subtitle drafting without automated assist

### Rising Demand
- Evaluative judgement and directorial quality curation over generative AI video/previs outputs
- Rights management, performer digital-replica consent workflows, and AI usage disclosure compliance
- Virtual production integration (LED volume, real-time camera tracking)
- Multidisciplinary crew communication and on-set technical coordination under live shooting constraints
