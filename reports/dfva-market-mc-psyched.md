### Theme 1 — ahpra and the National Boards (including the Psychology Board of Australia) published principles for the sa…

Ahpra and the National Boards (including the Psychology Board of Australia) published principles for the safe use of AI in healthcare (August 2024), placing responsibility on the registered practitioner for verifying AI-generated content, maintaining contemporaneous records, and obtaining patient consent before AI-assisted documentation. The Psychology Board's refreshed Code of Conduct (2025) and Professional Competencies for Psychologists (effective 1 December 2025) embed digital-competence expectations for AI use. (Psychologists, All Other; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Ahpra / Psychology Board of Australia, Applying your code of conduct to evolving technologies](https://psychologyboard.gov.au/News/2024-08-22-AI-in-healthcare-code-of-conduct.aspx) (2024-08-22).

Scope: Australian registered psychologists; regulatory guidance (not primary legislation). Binds registered practitioners, not unregistered roles.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 2 — a 2026 narrative review of AI in psychological practice reports unintended consequences for psychologists t…

A 2026 narrative review of AI in psychological practice reports unintended consequences for psychologists themselves: cognitive offloading and automation bias can erode diagnostic reasoning and clinical judgement, while moral deskilling, technostress, data dependence and vendor lock-in threaten practitioner wellbeing and professional sovereignty. The authors frame the central risk as a 'competence paradox' - mistaking fluent tool use for understanding. (Psychologists, All Other; lane L2, scholarly and grey-literature studies.)

Sources: [Frontiers in Psychology, The unintended negative consequences of artificial intelligence use for psychologists](https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1729050/full) (2026).

Scope: International (mostly US/EU) practitioner-level evidence; directional, not Australia-specific counts.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.
