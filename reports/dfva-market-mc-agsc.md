### Theme 1 — the peak professional body, Ag Institute Australia (Australian Institute of Agricultural Science and Techno…

The peak professional body, Ag Institute Australia (Australian Institute of Agricultural Science and Technology), administers the Certified Practising Agriculturalist (CPAg) national accreditation scheme for agricultural, horticultural and natural-resource professionals. (Agricultural, Plant, and Horticultural Scientists; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Ag Institute Australia, Ag Institute Australia — membership and accreditation](https://aginstitute.com.au) (2026).

Scope: Voluntary professional accreditation; not a statutory registration requirement to practise.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 2 — plant breeders' rights in Australia are governed by the Plant Breeder's Rights Act 1994 (Cth), administered…

Plant breeders' rights in Australia are governed by the Plant Breeder's Rights Act 1994 (Cth), administered by IP Australia, granting exclusive commercial rights over new plant varieties — the core IP regime for plant scientists developing cultivars. (Agricultural, Plant, and Horticultural Scientists; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Legislation.gov.au, Plant Breeder's Rights Act 1994 (Cth)](https://legislation.gov.au/C2004A04783/2024-10-14/2024-10-14/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html) (2024-10-14).

Scope: Binds plant variety IP and breeding work; not a claim about field agronomy employment generally.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 3 — environmental impact assessment in Australia is governed by the Environment Protection and Biodiversity Con…

Environmental impact assessment in Australia is governed by the Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999 (EPBC Act), administered by DCCEEW. Actions likely to significantly impact a Matter of National Environmental Significance require Commonwealth referral, assessment and approval; environmental scientists prepare the referrals, field surveys and assessment reports the process depends on. The Act is being reformed through 2026 (National Environmental Standards; a new federal EPA). (Environmental Scientists and Specialists, Including Health; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [DCCEEW, Environmental Impact Assessment Client Service Charter](https://dcceew.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/eia-client-service-charter.pdf) (1999).

Scope: Commonwealth environmental approvals; state/territory systems run parallel EIA. Binds consultants preparing EPBC referrals/assessments.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 4 — jobs and Skills Australia's Generative AI Capacity Study (August 2025) scores 358 ANZSCO occupations on aug…

Jobs and Skills Australia's Generative AI Capacity Study (August 2025) scores 358 ANZSCO occupations on augmentability and automatability using an ILO/Felten-derived task-level framework. It finds that augmentation outweighs automation across the workforce: 79% of Australian workers face low or very low automation risk, while routine clerical and administrative roles carry the highest automation exposure. The study reports professionals and managers (ANZSCO skill levels 1-2) see the highest augmentation potential. (Environmental Scientists and Specialists, Including Health; lane L2, scholarly and grey-literature studies.)

Sources: [Jobs and Skills Australia, Australia's AI Transition: Jobs, Skills and the Future of Work (Generative AI Capacity Study)](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/studies/generative-artificial-intelligence-capacity-study) (2025-08).

Scope: Australian occupations nationally; model-derived task-level estimates.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 5 — animal scientists working with genetically modified organisms are bound by the Gene Technology Act 2000 (Ct…

Animal scientists working with genetically modified organisms are bound by the Gene Technology Act 2000 (Cth) and overseen by the Office of the Gene Technology Regulator (OGTR); institutions must hold OGTR accreditation and run Institutional Biosafety Committees. (Animal Scientists; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Office of the Gene Technology Regulator, Legislative documents (Gene Technology Act 2000)](https://ogtr.gov.au/about-ogtr/legislative-documents) (2025-02-26).

Scope: Binds GMO/gene-editing research work by animal scientists; does not cover conventional animal husbandry or non-GMO field trials.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 6 — animal scientists in research involving animals or imported biologicals must comply with the Biosecurity Ac…

Animal scientists in research involving animals or imported biologicals must comply with the Biosecurity Act 2015 (Cth) and state biosecurity/animal-welfare legislation, administered through institutional biosafety and animal-ethics committees. (Animal Scientists; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Legislation.gov.au, Biosecurity Act 2015 (Cth) No. 61, 2015](https://legislation.gov.au/C2015A00061/2025-01-01/2025-01-01/text/original/pdf) (2025-01-01).

Scope: Applies to research settings using animals/biologicals; not a claim about commercial farm employment of animal scientists.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.
