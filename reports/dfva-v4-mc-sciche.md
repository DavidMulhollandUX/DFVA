## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — The gate's PASS condition — 'a coherent specialist core with progressive technical or methodological depth… or a staged prerequisite chain' — is documented twice over. A compulsory 125-credit-point Chemistry research project forms a staged chain of four (or five) parts in which progression is conditional on successful completion of the earlier component, and on top of that every student must take 37.5–62.5 credit points from a closed list of advanced CHEM-coded discipline subjects. The content is unmistakably disciplinary rather than generic or interchangeable.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — The gate passes on the capstone route: a compulsory, self-scoped, four-semester research project that the student must plan and execute independently and submit for formal examination carries genuine methodological uncertainty and personal accountability for the result — this is not recall or scripted response. Discipline coursework reinforces it with outcomes requiring plausible syntheses to be proposed and defensible risk assessments to be produced from evidence. Noted against the PASS: the project's own assessment page is absent from the extract, so the gate rests on the course-structure requirement and the course-level outcome rather than a task description.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **cognate** (cognate program), n = 318. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: Reconciliation package tier cognate (discipline-matched bachelor major).

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (35 pages) and verified on 2026-08-15: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (7):

- C5 straddled 2 and 3. The required substantial primary-evidence project satisfies the first half of anchor 3, and the clause 'Students may only progress through Chemistry Sciences Research Project subjects upon successful completion of the earlier project component' read against supervisor negotiation could be argued as 'staged supervised review'. But no viva, defence or methodology-scrutiny task is quotable — the Chemistry Research Project subject pages are absent from the extract — so the scoring-direction rule (never resolve ambiguity upward) fixed it at 2.
- C2 straddled 1 and 2. 'oral presentation of a literature article' plus the outcome 'Investigate peer-reviewed literature to determine the strengths, limitations, and relevance' could read as structured critique. Resolved to 1 because no criteria, exemplars or peer-review process are documented, the assessment entries carrying those outcomes are undescribed written assignments, and the subjects concerned are menu selections rather than required units (R2).
- C1 considered for level 1 on the graduate attribute 'Be able to initiate and implement constructive change in their communities, including professions and workplaces.' Rejected: the line names communities and workplaces, not collaboration, teamwork or stakeholder work, and level 1 requires collaboration to appear in outcomes. Scored 0.
- C3 considered for level 0 (strictly, no required unit and no program-level outcome carries digital or AI content). Not taken, because anchor 1 literally describes the observed state — digital and AI content present only as option-list selections and as tool operation — and levels 0 and 1 describe different facts rather than different intensities of the same fact.
- W2 straddled 1 and 2 on the research project: a 125-credit-point bench project arguably reproduces the criterion situation of a research chemist end to end. Resolved to 1 under R4, which forbids scoring up from an unevidenced label or inference — the extract documents no task description, artefact or criteria for any research project subject.
- C4 and C5 both draw on the same required research project. Scored in the item whose construct names the feature: structured progression toward independent, self-scoped learning is C4's level-3 anchor; the generation of primary evidence and its methodological defence is C5's. The project's staged-progression clause is cited in both because it evidences progression in C4 and was weighed and rejected as methodology defence in C5.
- W3 vs C1/C4: the internship was scored only in W3 per the one-construct-one-home rule, and contributed nothing to C1 or C4.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (6):

- No subject page or assessment table for ANY Chemistry Research Project subject (CHEM90023–CHEM90038, CHEM90056–CHEM90063) is present in the extract, although the project is the only compulsory component and 125 of the program's 200 credit points. Every coursework-derived score is therefore based on the modal assessment pattern documented across the discipline subjects supplied.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
