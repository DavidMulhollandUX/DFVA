## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — PASS on both the accredited-sequence and staged-prerequisite-chain routes of the anchor. The course is externally accredited by APHEA, the rules require course core units plus specialisation units to 96 points, and the extract documents a staged chain of specialist public health prerequisites: Biostatistics II requires Biostatistics I, and the Practicum requires successful completion of six named PUBH units. Content is specialist public health, not generic or interchangeable.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — PASS: assessment goes beyond recall and scripted response to defended trade-off decisions. PUBH5752 Health Systems and Economics - a documented required completion before the practicum - is built on cost-effectiveness evaluation integrated into resource allocation and decision making and priority setting, assessed by assignment; PUBH5743 requires students to identify uncertainties in the evidence and debate options for managing an environmental or occupational health issue, assessed by major assignment and interactive oral assessment; PUBH5763 requires applying evidence-based principles to leadership in times of uncertainty or crisis.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **field** (field grain), n = 0, 11 destination titles, 0 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: field-of-education occupation list (ATO-linked), not this program's own graduates

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (37 pages) and verified on 2026-09-07: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (9):

- The extract never enumerates the core unit set. The course structure page prints the group headings ('Take all units (24 points):', 'Group A Coursework', 'Group B Dissertation', 'Group C Practicum') with no unit list beneath them, and the 'Program Structure & Prescribed Units of Study' list of 36 units is contaminated with Juris Doctor units (LAWS4101 Foundations of Law and Lawyering, LAWS4102 Criminal Law, LAWS4103 Contract, LAWS4104 Property Law, LAWS4106 Torts, LAWS5174 Legal Internship), so it cannot be read as the MPH core. Core membership was therefore taken only where the handbook documents a unit as a required completion - the six PUBH units named as Practicum prerequisites - and every item whose level-2 or level-3 evidence sat in a unit outside that set was resolved to the lower level.
- C1 straddled 1 and 2: PUBH5763 Health Leadership and Management assesses a 'group assessment' and PUBH5721 Public Health Field Trip a 'group report', either of which would satisfy the level-2 anchor if core. Neither is documented as required (the field trip is capped at an approved quota of 20 and has alternative prerequisites), so the lower level was taken.
- C2 straddled 1 and 2 on the same core question: the critique task sits in PUBH5805, which the handbook documents as a required completion before the Practicum. That documentation was treated as sufficient for core membership, so 2 was taken; had PUBH5805 been read as specialisation-only the item would score 1.
- C4 straddled 2 and 3: the dissertation is a self-scoped research project ('Students are encouraged to choose their own topics'), which reads on the level-3 anchor, but it is one of three routes and eligibility-restricted, so the lower level was taken.
- C5 straddled 2 and 3: the dissertation generates primary evidence under supervision with a proposal hurdle and seminar presentation, but the level-3 anchor requires it be REQUIRED and not one route among several. The handbook states the course can be taken as coursework only, so the lower level was taken. A second ambiguity within level 2: PUBH5805 assesses a research proposal that plans data collection rather than a documented executed collection, which was not read up.
- W1 straddled 1 and 2: PUBH5754's 'project proposal and presentation' and PUBH5705's 'program evaluation proposal' could be professional genres, but the extract documents neither an audience beyond the teaching team nor criteria drawn from professional practice for either, and R4 forbids reading the label up. The lower level was taken.
- W2 straddled 1 and 2 in the other direction from W1: what the level-2 anchor supplies here is the artefact and the realistic problem (a health promotion program proposal on a priority health issue; an evaluation plan for a specific location, population and program), which are practitioner artefacts rather than level 1's academic ones, so 2 was taken; the undocumented third element - criteria drawn from practice - is also why no level-3 reading was entertained.
- W3 straddled 1, 2 and 3. Against 3: the practicum is not required of the cohort. Against 1: it is far more than an optional internship - 24 points, a full semester, minimum 26 hours per week, two supervisors, assessed by workplace assessment and portfolio, with all items required to pass. Level 2's 'stands alone' clause fits the single-unit, single-route structure, so 2 was taken. The other work-situated options in the extract (SVLG5001 McCusker Centre for Citizenship Internship, awarded by competitive application; PUBH5721 Public Health Field Trip, quota 20 and a $3,000 incidental fee) are level-1 optional activity and did not lift the score.
- C1, W1 and W3 all had a claim on the Practicum's teamwork, communication and professional-conduct outcomes. Under one-construct-one-home the placement evidence was scored in W3 only.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
