## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — The program documents a coherent specialist core with a staged prerequisite chain, which is the gate's PASS condition. Thirty-six of the seventy-two credit points are compulsory public health core units spanning epidemiology, biostatistics, disease prevention and health promotion, critical challenges, qualitative research methods and health policy and health economics, followed by a required capstone experience whose units carry the whole core as prerequisite or corequisite (the capstone unit requires 30 credit points from the core, and later electives chain off core units, for example Advanced Statistical Modelling on PUBH5217). This is not generic or interchangeable content.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — The gate passes on the capstone or live-project route rather than on assessed trade-off decisions. Every candidate must complete 6-12 credit points of capstone experience, and each of the three routes is a project carrying real uncertainty and accountability rather than recall: the placement is a supervised workplace project of relevance and benefit to a host organisation, assessed by proposal, presentation and report, and the research project is 240-300 hours of independent work assessed by proposal and a manuscript for publication. The compulsory core alone would not pass, since the only captured core assessment set is exam and quiz based.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **field** (field grain), n = 0, 11 destination titles, 0 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: field-of-education occupation list (ATO-linked), not this program's own graduates

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (37 pages) and verified on 2026-09-08: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (7):

- C2 straddled 1 and 2. PUBH5010 is a compulsory core unit whose outcomes are entirely criterion-referenced appraisal of research quality and whose assessment is 100% exam, quiz and written assessment, which is consistent with level 2; but no assessment task is documented as a peer review, structured critique or marking against exemplars, so R2 and the resolve-downward rule give 1.
- C4 straddled 1 and 2. Every candidate must complete a capstone experience, and each route plausibly applies methods to a new problem, which would read as level 2; but the current capstone unit page (PUBH5134) is not in the extract, the research-project route is coordinator-scoped and merit-gated, and the placement route belongs to W3, so no required core assessment on novel problems is documented. Resolved down to 1.
- C5 straddled 1 and 2. The research project route documents a designed inquiry with a supervisor and an assessed proposal and manuscript, which would read as level 2; but it is one of three routes, restricted by weighted average and place availability, and its own description permits a literature review or secondary dataset analysis. Resolved down to 1.
- W1 straddled 0 and 1. If the placement is treated as outside the core, because it is optional and merit-gated, no core spoken or professional-genre assessment survives and the item reads as level 0; the assessed Q&A and presentation sits inside the required capstone experience block, and communication appears in outcomes, so level 1 is the fitting anchor rather than an upward resolution. Recorded because the reading turns on whether an optional capstone route counts as core.
- W1 and W3 share the PUBH5040 evidence. The one-construct-one-home rule names only C1 and C4 as barred from placement evidence, so the placement's structure and supervision are scored in W3 and its communication outcome and spoken task in W1; W1 does not credit the workplace setting itself.
- W2 straddled 1 and 2. The manuscript-for-publication route produces the artefact a public health researcher would produce, which would read as level 2; it is one of three capstone routes and merit-gated, so it is not core assessment. Resolved down to 1. Per R4 the word authentic in PUBH5034 LO1 and PUBH5040 LO1 was not scored.
- W3 straddled 1 and 2. The placement has every feature level 2 names (a real workplace, practitioner supervision, assessment) except the one that decides the level: no student is required to take it, it substitutes for the capstone unit, it is listed among the Public Health electives, and it is gated on a 75% weighted average with limited places. Resolved down to 1.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (2):

- Core-unit assessment evidence is largely absent from the extract. Of the six compulsory core units, only PUBH5010 carries a captured assessment table; PUBH5018 is captured but marked no longer offered and lists no assessment tasks, and no page at all was captured for FMHU5002, PUBH5033, PUBH5036, FMHU5003 or HPOL5000. The current capstone unit PUBH5134 is likewise uncaptured (the captured PUBH5034 is the retired unit, with no assessment tasks). C1, C2, W1 and W2 are therefore scored on one core unit's assessment set plus the capstone-experience units, and their scores are floors set by what the extract documents, not findings that the missing units assess nothing.
- No program-level learning outcomes or graduate attributes appear anywhere in the extract, so every level-1 anchor that turns on an outcome claim was tested against unit-level outcomes only.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
