## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the preceding scored evidence and market data; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

The Bachelor of Commerce holds a coherent specialist core in accounting and finance and passes both gates: G1 on an all-compulsory sequence running level I to level III in every major, tied to professional accreditation with CPA Australia, CA ANZ, the CFA Institute and the AMI; G2 on ECON1012's statistical decision-making under uncertainty and a required capstone in every major, though the gate's own rationale notes the pass rests on that outcome and the capstone requirement rather than on a documented defended trade-off task. Adaptiveness sits at 5/15 and workplace practice at 2/9, both close to the instrument floor. W1 (0/3) is the largest single gap: no documented core assessment reaches beyond an academic genre addressed to the marker, even though the destination occupations in the market report sit inside regimes that bind professional communication and conduct — audit standards and the Privacy Act. C1, C2, C3, C4, C5 and W2 each sit at 1/3: collaboration, evaluative judgement, digital and AI literacy, transfer, inquiry and authentic task design are all named in program-level outcomes but not yet assessed in the two documented core courses, ECON1008 and ECON1012. W3 (1/3) reflects two internship options sitting alongside two other capstone alternatives, so no core unit yet guarantees supervised workplace practice for every graduate.
