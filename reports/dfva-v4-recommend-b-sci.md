## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the preceding scored evidence and market data; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

Seven of the eight items sit at level 1 or below their achievable next step, and the
cause is structural rather than pedagogical. B-SCI mandates "12.5 credit points of
Level 1 Compulsory subjects" — SCIE10005 alone — and delegates the remaining 225
credit points of science to a major the student chooses. Every level-2 and level-3
anchor in the instrument requires *core* placement, so a first-semester, pass/fail,
12.5-point subject is currently carrying the entire scored weight of the degree. The
capabilities the market is asking for are all present in the elective menu — Artificial
Intelligence, Machine Learning, Science Research Project, Science and Technology
Internship, a named agricultural capstone — and none of them is required of anybody.

That produces a specific and awkward pattern: the program *states* almost
everything and *requires* almost none of it. Course outcomes claim graduates are "adept
with a range of technologies", apply knowledge "across a range of fields", and
communicate "to expert and non-expert audiences" — but rule R2 scores a stated
capability that is not assessed at level 1, which is exactly where C3, C4 and W1 sit.
Gate G2 fails for the same reason: the capstone and project subjects that would satisfy
it exist, as electives. The single highest-leverage instrument in this plan is not a
subject redesign, it is a course rule — a statement of what every major's core must
contain. Nine of the twelve interventions below ride on that one instrument.

One gate passes and one fails. The failing gate is not a separate problem from the
scores — it fails for the same reason C4, C5 and W2 sit at 1, and P4 is the intervention
that addresses both. The passing gate is the constraint that shapes how every other
lever must be implemented.

The only compulsory subject assesses five module quizzes, two 800-word reports and a group presentation, all satisfactory-completion hurdles on a pass/fail subject, with no documented trade-off to defend and no accountability beyond the teaching team. Capstone-type subjects capable of satisfying the gate appear only as electives — AGRI30038 Professional Practice for Agriculture is described as a capstone, but it is one option on the science subject menu. | This gate is a regression check, so it cannot be "passed" by a lever alone — but P4, P8 and P9 are the changes most likely to clear it, because a required, defended, primary-evidence project producing a practitioner artefact is exactly the PASS condition's "simulations/capstones/live projects with real uncertainty and accountability". Two cautions. First, the FAIL is recorded against the documented required curriculum; the majors pages were not captured, and a required capstone inside a major would change the result before any intervention is made — which is why the documentation action precedes P4. Second, a required project that is scoped by the supervisor and marked satisfactory/unsatisfactory would satisfy C5 level 2 and still leave this gate failing; the defended trade-off is the gate's object, not the project's existence. | [16] |

If implemented and documented in the handbook, the interventions correspond to the
following changes. The two sub-scales are tracked separately and are never added.

| P6 | C2 | 2 | 3 | +1 | 11 | 4 |
| P7 | C3 | 2 | 3 | +1 | 12 | 4 |
| P8 | W2 | 1 | 2 | +1 | 12 | 5 |

A plan that moved adaptiveness to 15 and left workplace practice at 6 has done
something different from a plan that moved both, and a single total would hide which.

Read the sequence, not the endpoint. P1, P2 and the SCIE10005 half of P3 are subject-level
edits inside one existing 12.5-point subject and are achievable in a single teaching
cycle; on their own they take adaptiveness to 8 and workplace practice to 4. Everything
from P4 onward waits on a course-rule change across 23 majors, two of them externally
accredited, and none of it is a twelve-month proposition. The three second steps on the
workplace sub-scale are deliberately not planned: W1 level 3 requires professional
communication assessed repeatedly and progressively across the program with a real
external audience, W2 level 3 requires authentic tasks as the program's assessment
spine, and W3 level 3 requires an extended required placement sequence — each is a
capacity commitment across the whole cohort that the available evidence does not
warrant recommending, and the placement one carries a documented equity cost
[[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report).

Six categories of change would not move any score, and are recorded so that effort is
not directed toward them.

| Change | Why it does not move a score | Governing rule | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revising the course outcomes or generic skills to name AI literacy, transfer, inquiry or audience-matched communication | This is the exact failure that produced three of the four scores of 1. The outcomes already claim graduates are "adept with a range of technologies" (C3), apply knowledge "in a range of novel contexts" (C4), and communicate "to expert and non-expert audiences" (W1) — each scores level 1 because nothing assesses it. Adding more such language raises nothing. | Scoring rule R2 | [[8]](https://doi.org/10.1257/pandp.20181019)[33] |
| Describing the existing climate-change report, the paper evaluation or the group project as "authentic", "real-world" or "industry-relevant" | Rule R4 scores documented task features only. A W2 step requires a change to the task, the artefact produced, or the criteria applied — which is why P8 changes the output form and the standard it is judged against, not its description. The subject's framing language about tackling real challenges is already present and already scores 1. | Scoring rule R4 | [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634)[[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676) |
| Adding AI, research-project, capstone or internship options to the elective Science Discipline subject list | They are already there — COMP30024, COMP30027, NEUR30006, SCIE30001, SCIE30002, AGRI30038 — and the program scores 1 on C3, C4, C5 and W3 *with* them. Every level-2 and level-3 anchor on those items requires core placement or the word REQUIRED. Adding a further elective changes an already-satisfied condition. | C3, C4, C5, W3 anchors at levels 2 and 3 | [[1]](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/guides-resources/resources/corporate-publications/assuring-quality-learning-gen-ai-integrated-future-role-adaptive-capabilities)[26] |
| Publishing each major's required core assessment in the handbook | Not a curriculum change: it changes what is scoreable, not what is required. It belongs first in the sequence all the same. The captured extract stops at the course rules and the elective menu, so if a major's core already requires an inquiry project or a capstone, capture alone would raise C5, C4, W2 or gate G2 with no redesign at all — and a course rule written before that is known may mandate what several majors already do. | Evidence capture, not intervention | [17] |
| Extending the SCIE10005 group project or adding more group work in first year | C1 already scores 2 on that task. Level 3 requires that assessed collaborative practice *recurs across the program* and that coordination across people and tools is itself assessed. More collaboration inside the one compulsory first-semester subject satisfies neither limb — which is why P11 places the second task in each major's Level 3 core. | C1 level 3, both limbs | [[5]](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjx022) |
| Citing placement or internship volume as evidence of graduate employment | Work-integrated learning was not found to raise full-time employment rates [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z). P10 is justified on skill development, employment relevance and professional identity formed through participation in a community of practice [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551), and must account for the equity cost of unpaid and relocation-dependent placement [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report) — a live constraint for a 23-major cohort with regional and field-based destinations. | W3 honesty rule | [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z)[[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551)[[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report) |

Re-scoring after implementation follows the harness without modification: capture the
new handbook vintage — including the majors pages this time — score the eight items,
verify adversarially, and publish the change. Because the instrument is unchanged,
v4-to-v4 comparison is valid; comparison with v3.1 values is not
[17][[18]](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08969-3).
