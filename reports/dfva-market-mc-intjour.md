### Theme 1 — public relations in Australia is self-regulated

Public relations in Australia is self-regulated. Communication and Public Relations Australia (CPRA, formerly PRIA) is the peak body and offers practitioner accreditation; there is no statutory licensing of PR practitioners. Advertising and marketing communications are additionally subject to self-regulatory codes administered by Ad Standards (AANA). (Public Relations Specialists; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Communication and Public Relations Australia, Communication & Public Relations Australia \| Australia's Peak PR Body](https://www.cpra.org.au/).

Scope: Australian PR practitioners; voluntary accreditation; advertising-code overlay via Ad Standards (AANA).

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 2 — jobs and Skills Australia's Gen AI Capacity Study (Overarching Report 14 August 2025; Final Release 30 Sept…

Jobs and Skills Australia's Gen AI Capacity Study (Overarching Report 14 August 2025; Final Release 30 September 2025) is the first national whole-of-labour-market study of generative AI in Australia and finds Gen AI is more likely to augment human work than replace it; it scores ANZSCO 1.3 occupations across exposure, adoption, adaptation, labour market dynamism and skills. (Public Relations Specialists; lane L2, scholarly and grey-literature studies.)

Sources: [Jobs and Skills Australia, Australia's AI Transition: Jobs, Skills and the Future of Work](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/studies/generative-artificial-intelligence-capacity-study) (2025-08-14).

Scope: Australian occupations nationally; model/account-derived task-level exposure estimates, not employment forecasts.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 3 — in Australia the rights of authors are governed by the Copyright Act 1968 (Cth); the Australian Society of…

In Australia the rights of authors are governed by the Copyright Act 1968 (Cth); the Australian Society of Authors (ASA), established 1963, is the peak national body representing authors and illustrators and advocates on copyright reform. Writing is not a licensed or registered occupation. (Writers and Authors; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Australian Society of Authors, Copyright](https://www.asauthors.org.au/advocacy/copyright/).

Scope: Australian authors; copyright framework, not a licensing/registration regime (writing is an unlicensed occupation).

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 4 — jSA's Gen AI Transition exposure data places clerical and administrative occupations — and the broader info…

JSA's Gen AI Transition exposure data places clerical and administrative occupations — and the broader information-processing and media/communications groups that include writers, editors and public-relations roles — among the most exposed to automation. The Conversation's analysis of JSA data shows the 20 occupations most at risk of automation are dominated by female-concentrated clerical roles (secretaries, receptionists, bookkeepers, accounting/HR/payroll clerks), while trades and labourer jobs are least exposed. (Writers and Authors; lane L2, scholarly and grey-literature studies.)

Sources: [The Conversation, These 3 charts show female-dominated jobs are actually the most exposed to AI](https://theconversation.com/these-3-charts-show-female-dominated-jobs-are-actually-the-most-exposed-to-ai-288103).

Scope: AU occupations; exposure scores are task-level automation potential, not realised displacement; writers/editors/PR sit in adjacent high-exposure information/media groups, not necessarily the top-20 clerical list.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 5 — office clerks handle personal and commercial information; the Privacy Act 1988 APPs bind their APP-entity e…

Office clerks handle personal and commercial information; the Privacy Act 1988 APPs bind their APP-entity employers and the Fair Work Act 2009 requires seven-year employee-record retention. No occupation-specific licensing regime governs general office clerks. (Office Clerks, General; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Jacmac Law, Accessing employment records: Privacy Act and Fair Work Act intersection](https://jacmac.com.au/insights/accessing-employment-records) (2025).

Scope: Applies via employer; employee-records exemption; no specific regulator for the occupation.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 6 — 2025 evidence names clerical/secretarial work as the clear automation-exposed group; routine, rules-based o…

2025 evidence names clerical/secretarial work as the clear automation-exposed group; routine, rules-based office tasks are the most readily automated. (Office Clerks, General; lane L2, scholarly and grey-literature studies.)

Sources: [scty.org, AI at Work: The 2025 Evidence](https://scty.org/words/ai-work-2025) (2025-12).

Scope: Synthesis; clerical specifically flagged.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

## 5. CURRICULUM IMPLICATIONS

| # | Implication | Dimension | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| CI-1 | Persistent demand for public relations, communications and stakeholder-engagement skill (Signal 1, §2) alongside a market that expects generative AI to augment this work rather than replace it (Theme 2, §3) favours assessed, practitioner-judged communication over classroom-only criteria. | W1 | Add a practitioner-judged component, such as an industry-panel review or client brief, to one core communication assessment (cost: sourcing and briefing panel practitioners each teaching period). |
| CI-2 | Writers and Authors sit among the occupations JSA's exposure data ranks most automation-exposed (Theme 4, §3), even as ad volume for the "writing" keyword continues (Signal 2, §2), which argues for pairing writing practice with assessed evaluation of AI-assisted output rather than leaving AI content to an elective. | C3 | Move one AI-literacy or governance topic from the elective list into a core subject's assessed content (cost: core-subject redesign and, if needed, a unit-of-study proposal). |
| CI-3 | The volume of "stakeholder engagement" and "media relations" postings (Signal 1, §2) against a self-regulated profession with no statutory licensing (Theme 1, §3) means employers are relying on the curriculum, not a licensing body, to certify collaborative and stakeholder-facing capability, which argues for assessing that capability directly rather than stating it only as a graduate outcome. | C1 | Add an individually accountable group or stakeholder-facing task to one core subject's assessment table (cost: redesigning group-marking criteria and moderation). |
| CI-4 | If generative AI augments rather than replaces public relations and communications work (Theme 2, §3), graduates will increasingly need to judge the quality of AI-assisted output rather than only produce it, which the curriculum's outcomes-level reflection statements do not yet assess. | C2 | Add a criterion-referenced peer-review or exemplar-marking task to one core subject (cost: developing marking exemplars and a shared rubric). |
