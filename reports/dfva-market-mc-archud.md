## 3. CURRENT DISCUSSION SIGNALS — MEDIUM CONFIDENCE

**What these sources are.** Regulatory and statutory instruments, scholarly and grey-literature studies, drawn from the committed profession records for the occupations in §1, each with its publisher, date and link. Direct extraction from X or LinkedIn was **not** performed and no professional forum was sampled for this report; the profession records declare their own practitioner-discourse sample — practitioner_forums (45 items, National Office Clerks, General Association discourse); reddit (85 items, r/AusRenovation, r/Construction); master_builders_bulletins (42 items, Master Builders Australia / AIQS Building Economist); practitioner_forums (45 items, National Business Operations Specialists, All Other Association discourse) — which is a declared sample, not a scrape. Themes are the claims as recorded; nothing is paraphrased, and a claim without a source was not carried. Dates are given where the source carries one.

### Theme 1 — office clerks handle personal and commercial information; the Privacy Act 1988 APPs bind their APP-entity e…

Office clerks handle personal and commercial information; the Privacy Act 1988 APPs bind their APP-entity employers and the Fair Work Act 2009 requires seven-year employee-record retention. No occupation-specific licensing regime governs general office clerks. (Office Clerks, General; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Jacmac Law, Accessing employment records: Privacy Act and Fair Work Act intersection](https://jacmac.com.au/insights/accessing-employment-records) (2025).

Scope: Applies via employer; employee-records exemption; no specific regulator for the occupation.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 2 — 2025 evidence names clerical/secretarial work as the clear automation-exposed group; routine, rules-based o…

2025 evidence names clerical/secretarial work as the clear automation-exposed group; routine, rules-based office tasks are the most readily automated. (Office Clerks, General; lane L2, scholarly and grey-literature studies.)

Sources: [scty.org, AI at Work: The 2025 Evidence](https://scty.org/words/ai-work-2025) (2025-12).

Scope: Synthesis; clerical specifically flagged.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 3 — safe Work Australia published guidance (23 July 2026) that AI and digital technologies are a work health an…

Safe Work Australia published guidance (23 July 2026) that AI and digital technologies are a work health and safety matter requiring the normal WHS risk-management process, and NSW's WHS Amendment (Digital Work Systems) Act 2026 is the first law imposing express WHS duties on employers using AI, automation and digital platforms - binding construction managers who introduce site cameras, scheduling tools or AI assistants. (Construction Managers and Quantity Surveyors; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [The Good Builder (reporting Safe Work Australia guidance and NSW WHS Amendment Act 2026), AI Workplace Safety Guidance: What Builders Should](https://thegoodbuilder.com.au/safe-work-australia-publishes-ai-and-digital-technology-safety-guidance-signalling-where-national-rules-are-headed) (2026); [OSIAT (Australian WHS Legislative Updates), Australian WHS Legislative Updates](https://osiat.com.au/australian-whs-legislative-updates) (2026-07-23).

Scope: National model WHS plus NSW (first jurisdiction with express digital-work-systems duties); primary duties in NSW not yet all commenced as at mid-2026. Binds PCBUs, including construction managers.

**Bearing:** C3 and W1 and G1.

### Theme 4 — state builder licensing (NSW Fair Trading, Victorian Building Authority, QBCC) holds the licensed builder r…

State builder licensing (NSW Fair Trading, Victorian Building Authority, QBCC) holds the licensed builder responsible for the accuracy of representations and quote claims, including AI-generated content - so a construction manager cannot delegate accuracy to a generative tool. (Construction Managers and Quantity Surveyors; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Mindiam (AI for Trades and Construction in Australia), AI for Trades and Construction in Australia](https://mindiam.com/industries/trades-construction) (2026).

Scope: State-licensed building work in NSW/VIC/QLD; the PCBU/supervisor must review and adopt AI output rather than rely on it.

**Bearing:** C3 and W1.

### Theme 5 — the energy/solar consulting slice of this residual SOC is gated by Solar Accreditation Australia (SAA) accr…

The energy/solar consulting slice of this residual SOC is gated by Solar Accreditation Australia (SAA) accreditation (the function moved from the Clean Energy Council in February 2024); under the Clean Energy Regulator's Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme only SAA-accredited designers/installers can create Small-scale Technology Certificates, so accreditation is the practical gate to federal rebates. The broader 'Business Operations Specialists, All Other' category is not statutorily licensed as a whole; regulation applies at the sector edge (e.g. energy retail via the AER). (Business Operations Specialists, All Other; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Clean Energy Council, Application process (accreditation, now via Solar Accreditation Australia)](https://cleanenergycouncil.org.au/application-process) (2024).

Scope: Applies to energy/solar consulting design/install slice of 13-1199; does not bind general business-operations or governance roles.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 6 — jobs and Skills Australia's Generative AI Capacity Study (August 2025) scores 358 ANZSCO occupations on tas…

Jobs and Skills Australia's Generative AI Capacity Study (August 2025) scores 358 ANZSCO occupations on task-level augmentability and automatability. It finds augmentation outweighs automation across the workforce: about 79% of Australian workers face low or very low automation risk, while routine clerical and administrative roles - including bookkeepers, receptionists, and business-analysis, sales, marketing and PR positions - carry the highest automation exposure. The study flags entry-level and clerical business roles as most exposed, with limited mobility options. (Business Operations Specialists, All Other; lane L2, scholarly and grey-literature studies.)

Sources: [Jobs and Skills Australia, Australia's AI Transition: Jobs, Skills and the Future of Work (Generative AI Capacity Study)](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/studies/generative-artificial-intelligence-capacity-study) (2025-08).

Scope: Australian occupations nationally; model-derived task-level estimates, not employment forecasts. Business-operations residual sits in the higher-exposure clerical/business cluster.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.
