## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the preceding scored evidence and market data; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

Master of Data Science scores 11/15 on adaptive capabilities and 3/9 on workplace practice, on a field-of-education exposure estimate of 81.84 (estimated, field-tier basis — the destinations are field 020199 Computer Science, n.e.c., not this program's own graduates). Both regression gates pass: G1 on the all-compulsory, staged core (foundations, statistics, data taming, cyber security) and G2 on the compulsory research project's defended Proposal-then-Report structure. Every intervention below extends that existing core and research project rather than replacing them.

Headroom concentrates in workplace practice: W1, W2 and W3 all sit at level 1, each for the same shape of reason — outcomes claim the capability but no core assessment reaches it, or the one candidate task (the elective internship, the labelled "Professional Simulation") is either optional or undocumented. Among adaptive items, C1 sits at level 1; C2 and C3 sit one level below ceiling on the same pattern — assessed critique exists but stops short of process-justification (C2) or AI-specific governance (C3). C4 and C5 are already at ceiling on the compulsory research project and carry no action.

The market report grounds three levers. CI-1 and Theme 2 note that the ACS Code of Professional Conduct binds a graduate's sign-off on any generated model or analysis, market-warranting an evaluative-judgement step on C2 and a governance step on C3. CI-3 and the destination map note that retail and clerical roles sit inside this field's own outcomes with no external credential to distinguish a graduate from them, market-warranting a professional-form artefact on W2. And the Tech Council of Australia's 2026 update, showing employer experience expectations rising from 70% to 85% while ad volumes roughly halved, market-warrants moving the elective internship into the core on W3 — justified by skill development and professional identity, never by employment rates.
