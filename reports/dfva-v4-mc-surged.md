## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — The handbook documents a coherent specialist core with progressive methodological depth: four compulsory subjects plus a compulsory research-methods subject, all specific to surgical education, followed by a staged prerequisite chain requiring at least 75 points of coursework including Research Methods before the minor thesis may commence. Depth is progressive and explicitly cross-referenced — the curriculum-design assessment states it escalates the skills assessed in Subject 2 to the program level, and the capstone integrates learning from the earlier Graduate Certificate and Diploma stages. The content is not generic or interchangeable: it is anchored on the specific discipline of surgical education and its clinical settings.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — Assessments require defended trade-off decisions rather than recall or scripted responses. The capstone requires justification for curriculum decisions across learning outcomes, lesson plans, assessment and evaluation strategies and governance; MEDS90007's reflection requires an evidence-based rationale for the selection of objectives and methods and how they will be changed; MEDS90011 requires selection and defence of research methods within a theoretical framework and schedule; and MEDS90009's assessment essay is framed around genuinely consequential uncertainty, with the handbook stating that assessment in surgical practice carries high stakes for the trainee and for patients. The program-level outcome likewise names the exercise of professional judgment.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **partial** (related program), n = 611. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: Reconciliation package tier partial.

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (33 pages) and verified on 2026-08-16: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (10):

- C1 (1 vs 2): the capstone MEDS90033 assesses genuine stakeholder work (a needs assessment of stakeholders, 'Written report on needs assessment', 20%), which would satisfy the level-2 anchor's 'client or stakeholder work' clause. It is not scored as level 2 because the handbook lists only four subjects under the heading 'Core Subjects' and places the capstone under the 'Coursework option', so Research-pathway students never take it. Resolved downward per the never-resolve-upward rule. If the pathway-required capstone were treated as core, C1 would be 2.
- C1 (1 vs 2, second route): the MEDS90006 hurdle requires contributing to a discussion board and sharing educational philosophies with other students, which is assessed peer interaction but not a group project with individual accountability, client work, or interprofessional activity. Treated as insufficient for level 2.
- C2 (2 vs 3): MEDS90007 assesses a teaching plan (40%) and then a reflection on that plan as implemented (35%), requiring students to state why elements need development and how they will make those improvements. This is arguably the level-3 route 'evidence strategy adjustment over time (process-focused assessment)'. Resolved downward because the adjustment is documented once, retrospectively, in a single essay rather than tracked across iterations, and because no reliance-decision or AI-output-appraisal element exists anywhere in the extract.
- W1 (1 vs 2) and the W1/W2 boundary: the session teaching plan, the program/curriculum design essay and the capstone curriculum document are plausibly 'recognised professional genres' for a surgical educator, which would satisfy W1 level 2. Per 'one construct, one home' they are scored in W2, whose construct names the form of the result and the fidelity of the task; W1 keeps audience and professional conduct, and no core assessment documents an audience beyond the tutorial group and teaching team. Note also that the handbook labels these design tasks 'Essay', an academic genre wrapper, which reinforces the downward resolution.
- W2 (2 vs 3): the strongest constraint-of-practice evidence (stakeholder-scoped conditions; faculty, settings, equipment and resource specification) sits in the pathway-conditional capstone. Level 3 is nevertheless taken because the clause is independently satisfied from compulsory core evidence — the MEDS90007 plan is delivered in workplace-based activity to real trainees and must include a measure of outcome for those recipients, and MEDS90011 requires a scheduled research proposal. The spine clause is also met from core alone (MEDS90007 to MEDS90009 to MEDS90011, plus a final-year capstone or thesis).
- W3 cohort-practising status: the 'Entry and participation requirements' page is not captured in the extract (navigation heading only). Practising status was established from the course overview ('designed to support surgeons', 'busy professionals undertaking studies in conjunction with clinical commitments') and assessment text ('aligned with their current practice', 'their own teaching'). Accepted as documented cohort evidence; had it been rejected, ambiguity-down on the placement route alone would have yielded 1.
- W3 double-home risk: the MEDS90007 teaching-plan and reflection essays belong to the assessment spine homed in W2. Resolved by one-construct-one-home: W3 scores only the own-practice participation they evidence (required workplace delivery to real recipients with an outcome measure, and the assessed reflection on the implemented session); the authentic-artefact fidelity of plan/curriculum/proposal remains W2 and was not re-counted.
- W3 (2 vs 3): 'Students are encouraged to design curriculum aligned with their actual practice' is encouraged, not required, and is artefact design; MEDS90033's stakeholder needs assessment is required only on the Coursework pathway. No required sequence of assessed own-practice activity exists for the whole cohort, and the recipient outcome measure approaches but does not document accountability to workplace stakeholders as an assessed criterion — resolved downward to 2.
- [superseded by v4.2 re-score 2026-08-16; retained for the audit trail] W3 (0 vs 1 vs 2): none of the three anchors fits cleanly. Level 0 is excluded because workplace-based activity is documented in a compulsory core subject; level 2 is excluded because no placement, practitioner supervision or host accountability appears anywhere; level 1's exemplars (elective, optional, unassessed) fit imperfectly because the activity is core and feeds assessment. Scored 1 as the lower of the two live readings. The underlying mismatch is cohort-structural: the anchors assume a pre-professional cohort placed into a host workplace, whereas this cohort are practising surgeons learning within their own existing practice, which the program does not supervise.
- Elective availability: MEDS90015 Simulation in Surgical Education, MEDS90016 Teaching Surgical Science, MEDS90017 Educational Leadership in Surgery and MEDS90018 Assessment and Evaluation in Surgery are all marked 'Not available in 2026'. Their assessments (including the MEDS90016 hurdle 'design and deliver a teaching session' and the MEDS90017 formal debate) were therefore not used as scoring evidence for the 2026 offering, and in any case are elective, not core.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (2):

- The AI-specific clauses of C1 level 3 ('coordinating work across people AND tools/AI systems') and C3 levels 2-3 (core units addressing AI capabilities and limitations/ethics; critique or governance of AI systems) could not be tested: the extract contains no occurrence of artificial intelligence, gen AI, machine learning or any equivalent in any course page, subject overview, learning outcome, generic-skills list or assessment description. Both items were still scoreable on their non-AI evidence (collaboration for C1, digital/e-learning for C3), so neither is left unscored — but the absence, not a judgement about AI content, is what caps them.
- Assessment detail for MEDS90030 Minor Thesis Part 2 is not published in its own right; the page states only that assessment refers back to MEDS90029 Minor Thesis Part 1, whose combined assessment table was used for both.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
