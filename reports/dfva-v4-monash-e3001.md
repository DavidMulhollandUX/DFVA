## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — PASS on the coherent specialist core with progressive depth: 144 of 192 credit points are core engineering studies, a compulsory common first year of named engineering units precedes a nominated branch, a minimum of 72 points must sit at level 3 or above, and the sequence is written against external accreditation - the Engineers Australia competency standards and, for Malaysian offerings, EAC and BEM requirements. This is not generic or interchangeable content.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — PASS: core assessment requires defended trade-off decisions under acknowledged uncertainty rather than recall. ENG1011 makes the simplifications and assumptions of an analysis the explicit object of scrutiny and requires students to justify a finalised design against key variables, assumptions and system boundaries in an assessed project; ENG1014 emphasises the assumptions, complexities and uncertainties of numerical models; and the course requires constraints from economics, safety, environment and professional standards to be synthesised into professional judgements.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **field** (field grain), n = 0, 14 destination titles, 0 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: field-of-education occupation list (ATO-linked), not this program's own graduates

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (31 pages) and verified on 2026-09-07: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (10):

- Extract coverage: the capture contains the E3001 course pages, the common-first-year core units (ENG1005, ENG1011, ENG1012, ENG1013, ENG1014), the two CPD units (ENG0001, ENG0002) and the first-year breadth-elective list. The Parts C, D and E specialisation unit pages (AEROENG04, CIVILENG03, MECHENG03 and the rest) are linked but not captured, so no assessment evidence exists for years 2 to 4. Every ceiling that requires recurrence or progression across the program - C1 level 3, C4 level 3, W1 level 3, W2 level 3 - is therefore unquotable and was refused under R3, not judged absent.
- C1 between 2 and 3: assessed teamwork recurs across three core first-year units, which reads toward the recurrence half of level 3, but no assessment documents allocation of work across people AND tools or AI systems. Resolved down by the lower-level rule and by R3 on program-wide recurrence.
- C2 between 1 and 2: the compulsory CPD ePortfolio collects assessed written skill reflections and the CPD requirement is framed against the Engineers Australia competency standards, which could read as a portfolio with standards (level 2). The extract names no criteria, standards or exemplars applied to the ePortfolio itself and gives it no weighting, so the lower level stands.
- C3 between 1 and 2: ENG1013 assesses ethical considerations of data collection and ENG1014 requires evaluation of numerical uncertainty, so a purely digital reading of level 2 (capabilities plus limits, assessed) is arguable. The level-2 anchor names AI, and no core unit mentions AI, so the lower level stands.
- C4 between 1 and 2: the design projects are the taught context of their own units, so the transfer claim could be read as outcome-level only (level 1). The explicit cross-disciplinary scrutiny in ENG1011 and the given-scenario design task in ENG1013 carried it to 2; this is the least secure of the C-scale scores.
- C5 between 1 and 2: ENG1011 has students build and test a component and compare the outcome against the analysis, which is assessed primary data collection, and its methods content covers simplifications, assumptions, constraints, boundary conditions and precision, which resembles methodology selection. The procedure is prescribed by the unit and no assessment row names an investigation, so the lower level stands.
- W1 between 1 and 2: the professional genres (pitch, professional engineering documentation, review meetings, demonstrations) appear in learning outcomes and unit overviews while the assessment rows are generically titled (Project submission, Interview, Project). Scored 2 because the ENG1012 outcome ties the genre and the Engineers Australia Code of Ethics conduct criteria to the assessed project itself rather than to a standalone attribute statement; a stricter R2 reading would give 1.
- W2 between 2 and 3: three of the five common-first-year core units (ENG1011, ENG1012, ENG1013) are assessed design projects, which within the captured year looks like a spine, and limit state design is the profession's own standard of performance. Level 3 requires the spine across stages, which the missing specialisation pages make unquotable, so the lower level stands.
- W3 between 2 and 3: ENG0002 alone would support level 3 (12 weeks of supervised industry field work, half the mark awarded by the industry supervisor), but it is one of two alternative routes to the CPD requirement and the alternative documents neither practitioner supervision nor host accountability. Scored on the weaker required guarantee.
- One construct, one home: the ENG0002 industry-supervisor judgement is real practitioner judgement of professional conduct and could read into W1 level 3; it is scored only in W3. The CPD ePortfolio reflection is cited in C2 for the reflection construct only, not as work-situated learning.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
