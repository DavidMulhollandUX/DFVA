## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — The program documents a coherent, discipline-specific specialist core — two compulsory subjects plus five discipline-compulsory subjects (The Multinational, Global Corporate Governance, Cross Cultural Management and Teamwork, Managing in Asia, Strategic Management, Global Value Chains) plus a compulsory capstone — all specific to international business, satisfying the 'all-compulsory specialist core' branch of the anchor.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **field** (field grain), n = 0, 13 destination titles, 3 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: field-of-education occupation list (ATO-linked), not this program's own graduates

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (38 pages) and verified on 2026-08-29: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (7):

- C1: no assessed coordination of people AND tools/AI systems is documented anywhere in the core (the only AI subject, MGMT90267, is an elective) — level 3 not credited; scored at level 2.
- C2: criterion-referenced peer appraisal (360-degree feedback, peer review) appears only inside optional capstone-selective subjects (BUSA90473, BUSA90485) that can be substituted with a non-appraisal option (MGMT90148); resolved down from a possible level 2 to level 1.
- C3: MGMT90280 (core) references 'emerging technologies' generically without naming AI; not counted as core AI content, so C3 stays at level 1 on the elective-only evidence (MGMT90267).
- C4: BUSA90525's assessed self-directed goal-setting/reflection would support level 3, but BUSA90525 is one optional choice among several capstone-selective subjects, not a guaranteed core unit; resolved down to level 2 on the guaranteed-core simulation/consulting evidence.
- C5: program learning outcomes claim evidence-based research technique and analytical tools, but no core unit's assessment table shows a research-methods or inquiry-design task (R2 requires assessment evidence); scored 0 despite the outcome claim. The practicum subjects' 'in-depth research' is applied consulting research, not methodology-selection-plus-data-collection training, and is also a non-guaranteed selective option.
- W1: placement subjects (BUSA90473, BUSA90485) show assessed presentation to a real host-company audience with professional-conduct criteria, which would satisfy level 3, but they are optional alternatives within the capstone-selective slot (MGMT90148 is a non-placement, academic-audience alternative); resolved down to level 1 on the guaranteed core.
- W3: placement/internship subjects (BUSA90473, BUSA90485, BUSA90525) document substantial supervised, assessed workplace learning that would score 2-3 if mandatory, but placement is one of several options for the required capstone-selective credit, with a non-placement alternative (MGMT90148) equally available; resolved down to level 1, treating work-situated learning as an optional/elective route rather than a guaranteed core requirement.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
