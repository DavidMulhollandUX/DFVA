## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — PASS on the accredited-sequence and staged-chain routes of the anchor. The 192-unit award is built on a compulsory specialist LAWS core whose units are individually accredited against Law Admissions Consultative Committee Prescribed Academic Areas of Knowledge, and the sequence is staged: LAWS2202 builds on prior public law study, LAWS2248 builds on skills acquired in compulsory courses earlier in the degree, and LAWS2207 is designed to be taken towards the end of the degree. That is disciplinary identity with progressive depth, not interchangeable generic content.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — PASS: the FAIL anchor is recall or scripted responses only, and the documented curriculum is the opposite. Core units require generating legally defensible conclusions, and three core units (LAWS1205, LAWS2201, LAWS2205) require predicting how unresolved or ambiguous questions of doctrine would be resolved by the courts, with LAWS2205 listing prediction on unresolved doctrinal questions among its learning activities. Recorded caveat: the gate wording asks for assessments requiring defended trade-off decisions, and no core unit's assessment tasks are specified in the extract, so the PASS rests on documented outcomes and learning activities rather than on a quoted assessment task.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (7):

- C3 straddled levels 0 and 1: the extract contains no AI content of any kind, which points at level 0, but level 0 requires no digital content in any core unit or program-level outcome and CILO 3 plus the LAWS1201 outcome put legal databases and online sources in the core. Level 1's 'tool operation/training' clause is the exact match, so 1 stands rather than 0.
- C5 straddled levels 1 and 2: the LAWS1205 outcome 'plan and execute a public law research project' reads toward designing and conducting an inquiry, but it is an outcome statement and LAWS2248's own outcome stops at developing a plan, with no data collection and no assessment specification. Resolved DOWN to 1 under the lower-level rule and R2.
- W1 straddled levels 0 and 1: no spoken or professional-genre communication is documented as assessed, which fits level 0, but level 1's first clause (communication and professional conduct in outcomes and graduate attributes) is squarely met. R2's uniform rule - a capability in outcomes but never assessed scores 1 everywhere - resolved it to 1.
- W2 straddled levels 1 and 2: LAWS2207's 'analyse a brief of evidence ... draw a conclusion about the prospects of proof' and LAWS1206's live workshops on giving legal advice describe practitioner tasks, but neither is documented as an assessment and no criteria drawn from practice are stated. Resolved DOWN to 1.
- W3 straddled levels 0 and 1: the 'Law elective' and 'Law Capstone' slots are unnamed in the extract and could in principle contain a clinic or internship, but nothing in the extract documents one. Resolved DOWN to 0 under the lower-level rule rather than inferring an elective clinic.
- C4 straddled levels 1 and 3 on the capstone route: the structure lists a Year 4 'Law Capstone' but the extract carries no capstone course page, so there is no documented evidence that it is self-scoped or assessed. Scored at 1 on the outcomes-only route.
- G2 straddled PASS and FAIL: the gate names assessments, and no assessment task is specified anywhere in the extract. PASSed on the documented outcomes and learning activities requiring defensible conclusions on unresolved questions, which excludes the FAIL condition of recall or scripted responses only.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (4):

- Assessment specification is absent program-wide: all 13 course pages in the extract carry the same ANU placeholder in place of assessment tasks ('The proposed means of assessment for this course will provide students with at least two pieces of assessment ...'), naming no task type, no weighting breakdown and no criteria. Every level-2 and level-3 anchor across C1-C5, W1 and W2 requires assessment evidence, so those discriminations are unscoreable from this extract and each item was held at the highest anchor the documented outcomes and learning activities support.
- Three core units have entirely empty pages in the extract - LAWS2204 Property, LAWS2244 Litigation and Dispute Management and LAWS2249 Legal Theory each show blank Overview, Learning Outcomes, Assessment Tasks and Workload fields - so their contribution to every item is unknown. LAWS2244 in particular is the litigation and dispute unit where advocacy or simulated-practice assessment would most plausibly sit for W1 and W2.
- The Year 4 'Law Capstone' requirement has no course page in the extract, so its scope, supervision and assessment are undocumented; C4 level 3 and C5 levels 2-3 could not be evaluated against it.
- Elective slots are listed generically as 'Law elective' or 'Law elective or non-Law later year elective' with no elective catalogue in the extract, so the presence or absence of a clinical, AI-law or internship elective could not be established (bears on C3 level 1 and W3 level 1).

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
