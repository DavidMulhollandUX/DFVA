## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** FAIL — The program is explicitly generalist: marketed to learners 'from across diverse fields of study and work' and to early-career professionals 'including those from a non-cognate background', offering a 'broad foundation in business and economic studies'. The Discipline Core menu spans disconnected generic-management topics without a specialist technical/methodological sequence, and Foundation is a 4-of-6 selection, not a fixed core. No prerequisite chain or accreditation is documented. Matches the FAIL anchor: generic/interchangeable content without disciplinary identity. Caveat: subject-level 'Eligibility and requirements' sub-pages, which might list prerequisites, were not captured in this extract.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** FAIL — No assessment in the extract requires a defended trade-off decision, and no simulation/capstone/live project is documented with real uncertainty and accountability. MGMT90239's outcomes mention decision-making under uncertainty, but its assessment is weekly multiple-choice quizzes, a discussion reflection, and an exam. MGMT90267's assessment page is missing from the extract (see notScoreable). The capstone's Business Improvement Case carries no documented real stakes or external accountability.

**G1 FAILS.** A disciplinary-foundation failure is reported regardless of the adaptiveness score: the adaptive capabilities are scored on top of a foundation this program is not documented to establish.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **partial** (related program), n = 655, 13 destination titles, 7 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: Generalist business master; Master of Management family record.

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (35 pages) and verified on 2026-08-29: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (6):

- C1: BUSA90530's Syndicate assignment secures Level 2 as an effectively-compulsory Discipline Core unit (6 of 7 listed options are available in 2026, exactly matching the 75cp requirement), but the anchor's 'individual accountability' language is not explicitly documented for any of the group tasks cited — treated as satisfying the broader 'group projects' example rather than resolved upward.
- C2: ePortfolios and reflective tasks are assessed at substantial weight (60-70% in some units), which could imply criterion-referenced appraisal (Level 2), but no assessment text documents explicit standards, exemplars, or peer review — held at Level 1 per the lower-level rule.
- C3: MGMT90267's outcomes describe AI-limitations/ethics content consistent with Level 2, but its assessment page in the extract is mismatched (Master of Commerce (Economics) content appears instead), so the 'assessed' half of the anchor is unconfirmed — held at Level 1.
- C4: MGMT90271's 'Business Improvement Case' plausibly applies frameworks to a novel/unfamiliar (student-specific) context, which could satisfy Level 2's 'cross-context projects', but this is not stated explicitly in the extract — held at Level 1.
- W2: ACCT90040's group business case and MGMT90271's Business Improvement Case could be read as an assessment spine (Level 3) spanning Foundation and Capstone, but ACCT90040 sits in the 4-of-6 Foundation selection and is not guaranteed for every student, and no task documents a genuine constraint of practice — held at Level 2.
- G1: the 'Eligibility and requirements' subject sub-pages, which might document prerequisites, were not captured in this extract, so a staged prerequisite chain cannot be fully excluded even though the documented program framing supports FAIL.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (2):

- MGMT90267 (Artificial Intelligence in Organisations) assessment page: the extract under the 'mgmt90267/assessment' source header is actually Master of Commerce (Economics) overview content, not MGMT90267's assessment table — no assessment evidence for this subject is available, affecting C1 (level-3 tool+people coordination), C3 (level-2/3 'assessed' confirmation), and G2.
- MKTG90038 (Value Creation Strategies) assessment page: the extract under the 'mktg90038/assessment' source header is actually Applied Ecology (ECOL30005) content, not MKTG90038's assessment table — no assessment evidence for this Foundation-option subject is available in this extract.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
