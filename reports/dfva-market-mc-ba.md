## 2. RECENT JOB AD SIGNALS

**Signal 1 — AI fluency now a baseline expectation across consulting and corporate strategy.**
Tier 1 and boutique consultancies (McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Deloitte Strategy) require MBA candidates who can deploy and critically audit generative AI tools in market benchmarking and client synthesis.

**Signal 2 — Escalating demand for AI transformation change leaders.**
ASX100 companies recruit managers who combine organizational psychology with digital literacy to manage workforce resistance during AI automation rollouts.

**Signal 3 — Product management roles demand hybrid business-technical fluency.**
Tech firms and corporate digital divisions require product managers proficient in data instrumentation, API fundamentals, and AI product roadmapping.

**Signal 4 — Premium on executive presence and board-level persuasion.**
As routine data synthesis is commoditised, employers place the highest premium on interpersonal persuasion, negotiation, and high-stakes executive communication.

## 3. CURRENT DISCUSSION SIGNALS — MEDIUM CONFIDENCE

**What these sources are.** Australian Institute of Management ([AIM](https://aim.com.au/)), Business Council of Australia ([BCA](https://www.bca.com.au/)), Association to Advance Collegiate Schools of Business ([AACSB](https://www.aacsb.edu/)), and peer-reviewed executive management scholarship ([Journal of Management](https://journals.sagepub.com/home/jom), [February 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/home/jom)). Direct extraction from X or LinkedIn was **not** performed and no social media forum was sampled — where this section refers to discourse, it denotes published executive leadership reports, AACSB accreditation standards, and documented technological reviews by named bodies and authors. Dates are stated where available.

### Theme 1 — generative AI in management consulting: commoditisation of slides vs executive framing

Whitepapers from [AIM](https://aim.com.au/) (November 2024) and *Journal of Management* (February 2025) underline that while LLMs produce first-pass market sizing and slide decks in seconds, automated outputs frequently hallucinate market dynamics and lack strategic context. MBA graduates must possess sharp evaluative judgement to critique and override AI analysis before presenting to boards.

**Bearing:** C2, C3, and W2.

### Theme 2 — high-touch relational leadership and stakeholder coalition building

Reports from [BCA](https://www.bca.com.au/) (2024/2025) emphasize that executive leadership during technological disruption is primarily relational: aligning executive stakeholders, leading cross-functional teams, and resolving interpersonal conflicts. These human capabilities are entirely insulated from AI automation.

**Bearing:** C1, W1, and W3.

### Theme 3 — evidence-based managerial decision-making under uncertainty

Executive education frameworks from [AACSB](https://www.aacsb.edu/) (2024/2025) demand that future corporate leaders generate and defend primary empirical evidence rather than relying on unvalidated heuristics or unverified AI summaries.

**Bearing:** C5, C4, and G1.

D3 (technical and quantitative depth) scores only 2/3, with sustainability currently elective rather than core (CI-3). |

| Technical and data literacy for product-management pathways (SQL, API, analytics platforms) | **↑ Rising** | Signal 2 (§2): product manager ads increasingly require SQL proficiency and analytics-platform experience, and Theme 4 (§3) names a supply-demand mismatch where MBA candidates lack technical depth. ## 5. CURRICULUM IMPLICATIONS

| # | Implication | Dimension | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| CI-1 | Core coursework covers quantitative data analytics (C3: 1/3) but leaves GenAI and AI strategy in the elective pool | C3 | Move AI strategy concepts into the compulsory core; introduce an assessed module on AI governance and commercial risk |
| CI-2 | Core assessments produce syndicate case presentations without assessed peer critique or AI reliance logs (C2: 1/3) | C2 | Attach an assessed AI reliance and override log to core strategy consulting projects |
| CI-3 | Core syndicate tasks assess group work but lack explicit human-and-AI collaboration protocols (C1: 2/3) | C1 | In the Immersive Business Experience, require student teams to document a protocol allocating tasks between consultants and AI synthesis tools |
| CI-4 | Immersive Business Experience connects with live clients (W1: 2/3, W2: 2/3) but lacks assessed professional conduct criteria | W1, W2 | Formalize assessed professional conduct rubrics and embed practitioner deliverables across all core modules |
