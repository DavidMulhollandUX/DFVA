## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — The program documents a fully compulsory, staged specialist core (100 credit points each in Year 1 and Year 2, plus a compulsory Year 3 capstone) covering electronics, control, signal processing, communications and power engineering, and is professionally accredited by Engineers Australia — an accredited sequence with progressive technical depth.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — Core subjects are explicitly built around decision-making under real uncertainty (Probability and Random Models; Control Systems' framing of feedback design under modelling and environmental uncertainty), and core assessment requires defended trade-off/design decisions (ELEN30013's ILO on identifying and justifying implementation tradeoffs; ENGR90051's assessed requirement to 'justify design decisions throughout the design process').

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **field** (field grain), n = 0, 20 destination titles, 2 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: field-of-education occupation list (ATO-linked), not this program's own graduates

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (35 pages) and verified on 2026-08-29: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (5):

- C2: ENGR90051's core-assessed 'Peer feedback + evaluation' and 'Reflection' components straddle Level 1 (assessed but no stated criteria) and Level 2 (criterion-referenced appraisal); no explicit standards/criteria are quoted in the extract, so resolved DOWN to Level 1 per the lower-level rule.
- C4: The compulsory Year 3 Engineering Capstone Project (ENGR90037/ENGR90038) plausibly satisfies Level 3's self-scoped-capstone route, but its own subject overview/assessment pages are absent from this extract, so Level 3 was not inferred; resolved DOWN to Level 2 using documented novel-problem project evidence elsewhere in the core.
- C5: Assessed lab-based empirical data collection (ELEN30011) sits between Level 0 and Level 2 — data collection is assessed, but student-driven 'methodology selection' is not documented (experiments read as staff-designed); resolved DOWN to Level 1.
- W2: Realistic, end-to-end core design tasks recur across at least two years (a possible Level 3 'assessment spine'), but no instance in the extract documents a genuine constraint of practice (ambiguous/externally-supplied problem, real resource/time limits, or a consequential external audience); resolved DOWN to Level 2.
- W3: The Engineering Practice Hurdle is mandatory (unlike a pure elective), which pulls toward Level 2, but its three satisfying routes are all non-core and/or non-credit-bearing (STEP, an unpaid internship, or the Group-B-elective ENGR90033), so no core unit meets Level 2's placement-with-assessment requirement; resolved DOWN to Level 1.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (2):

- The Year 3 Engineering Capstone Project subjects (ENGR90037 Engineering Capstone Project Part 1, ENGR90038 Engineering Capstone Project Part 2) are named in the course-structure table but their own overview/assessment pages are not present in this extract, so capstone-specific evidence could not be scored for C4-Level 3, C5, or W2-Level 3.
- Specialisation core subjects (e.g. for the 'artificial intelligence' specialisation named in the course Overview) are not included in this extract — the course-structure page states 'Please see the specialisation subjects in the respective specialisation page' — so any AI-governance content specific to that specialisation route could not be assessed for C3.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
