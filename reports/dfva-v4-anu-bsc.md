## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** FAIL — The PASS anchor needs a documented coherent specialist core with progressive technical or methodological depth by one of three routes — an all-compulsory specialist core, an accredited sequence, or a staged prerequisite chain. The handbook denies the first (no specific course must be taken), denies the third (no formal program prerequisites, and first-year science courses assume little specific knowledge), and documents no accreditation. What is documented is a 312-course prescribed pool spanning anthropology, economics, finance, management, sociology and computing alongside the sciences, with up to ten courses permitted from another ANU College — the FAIL descriptor's interchangeable content. The requirement to take 2000- and 3000-level courses from a BSc major supplies level progression, but the major's own compulsory courses are outside this extract, so the specialist core is not documented here.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** FAIL — The PASS anchor requires that assessments require defended trade-off decisions, or simulations, capstones or live projects with real uncertainty and accountability. No assessment in this program is required of any student, so nothing is documented that assessments require. Tasks of the right shape do exist in the pool — COMP3500 has students defend engineering decisions at project reviews before stakeholders — but that course is restricted-entry and optional. Scored down per the rule that ambiguity is never resolved upward; the FAIL descriptor 'recall or scripted responses only' does not describe the pool, but the PASS condition is not documented.

**G1 FAILS.** A disciplinary-foundation failure is reported regardless of the adaptiveness score: the adaptive capabilities are scored on top of a foundation this program is not documented to establish.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **field** (field grain), n = 0, 14 destination titles, 0 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: field-of-education occupation list (ATO-linked), not this program's own graduates

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (313 pages) and verified on 2026-09-07: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (5):

- Every item straddles level 1 and level 2 for the same reason: the construct is assessed somewhere in the 312-course prescribed pool but no course in the program is compulsory, so nothing qualifies as core assessment. Resolved down to 1 in all eight items by the rule that ambiguity is never resolved upward, and by R1 (score what the handbook documents, not what a graduate plausibly does).
- C1 versus W1: assessed group work (ARCH3042, COMP2120) and audience-facing communication (ENVS3028 policy brief, SCOM2016 press release) both sit in the same elective pool. Coordination evidence was scored in C1 and audience/genre evidence in W1 per the one-construct-one-home rule; neither reaches level 2 in either item.
- C2 versus W2: peer review and peer feedback (BIOL3109, BIOL3206) are appraisal of quality and were scored in C2 only, never as task fidelity in W2.
- G1: the requirement for four 2000-level and four 3000-level courses from a BSc major is a documented level progression and a real argument for PASS. It was resolved to FAIL because the extract contains no major's compulsory-course list, the program states no course must be taken, and it states there are no formal program prerequisites — so none of the three PASS routes is documented in this extract.
- G2: the pool contains capstones and simulations with genuine uncertainty (COMP3500, ENVS3020, BIOL3188), so the FAIL descriptor fits the evidence poorly; the gate was failed because the PASS condition requires assessments that are required, and this program requires none.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (1):

- No item could be scored at level 2 or above on core-assessment grounds: the extract carries the program's prescribed 312-course pool but not the compulsory-course list of any individual BSc major, which the handbook says is where compulsory courses, if any, live. Recapturing the major requirement pages would be needed to test whether any of C1-C5, W1-W3 or G1 clears its level-2/PASS bar.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
