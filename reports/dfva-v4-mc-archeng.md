## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — The program documents dual professional accreditation (Engineers Australia, Architects Registration Board of Victoria, ASIIN-EUR-ACE) and a staged, prerequisite-linked technical sequence: the geotechnical capstone explicitly builds on Engineering Mathematics, Fluid Mechanics and Earth Processes for Engineering, and Structural Theory and Design is described as underpinning further specialised structural-design study. This is a coherent specialist core with progressive technical depth, not generic or interchangeable content.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** FAIL — Demoted from PASS on adversarial review: all three cited evidenceLines are Overview-section or Intended-Learning-Outcome prose (CVEN30010, Sustainable Infrastructure Engineering, ENGR90051), not Assessment-page text, despite the criterion requiring assessments with defended trade-off decisions. CVEN30010's Assessment page separately describes a 50%-weighted hurdle design report addressing 'identifying potential solutions, evaluation and analysis', which plausibly still supports PASS, but was not cited, so the gate does not stand as evidenced.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **partial** (related program), n = 669, 30 destination titles, 11 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: Architectural engineering spans both records.

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (35 pages) and verified on 2026-08-29: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (7):

- C1: group-collaboration evidence is solid for Level 2, but no task documents coordinating people AND tools/AI systems as an assessed construct, so Level 3 was not applied.
- C2: Earth Processes for Engineering's peer-review survey is used to allocate individual marks for group work; the extract does not specify whether it is a criterion-referenced appraisal of peers' work quality or a contribution-only survey. Matched to the Level 2 anchor's literal 'peer review' example rather than resolved upward.
- W1: guest critics (Studio A) and jury panels (thesis) give an audience beyond the teaching team, recurring at more than one program stage, which could support Level 3, but the extract never states the panels are external practitioners or that professional conduct/accountability is an explicit assessed criterion — resolved to Level 2.
- W2: realistic, practice-constrained tasks (Fisherman Bend industry report, National Construction Code compliance, a stated 'real-world project') recur across pathway-core, core, and capstone stages, which could support an assessment-spine Level 3 reading, but the extract does not state that each is judged against criteria drawn from practice — resolved to Level 2.
- The final 225-point 'Core' subject list is presented with 'Please select' choice prompts, distinct from the 100-point Architecture/Engineering Pathway Core lists that match the handbook's stated '8 subject sequence.' Evidence drawn from subjects in the 225-point Core list (e.g. Engineering Site Characterisation) may not be taken by every student, which weighs against reading such evidence as recurring 'across the program' for any item's Level 3 anchor.
- C5 was demoted from 3 to 2 in adversarial review: 3 of the 4 originally-cited evidenceLines sit under ABPL90390's Overview 'Intended learning outcomes' heading rather than its Assessment page, matching the R2-prohibited outcomes-not-assessment pattern. The Assessment-page interim proposal presentation and mid-project report hurdle plausibly substantiate the hypothesis/methodology claim at a stronger level, but were not cited and so were not credited here; a fresh capture/scoring pass would be needed to re-examine Level 3.
- G2 was demoted from PASS to FAIL in adversarial review: all three cited evidenceLines are Overview-section or Intended-Learning-Outcome prose (CVEN30010, Sustainable Infrastructure Engineering, ENGR90051), not Assessment-page text, despite G2's criterion reading 'Assessments require defended trade-off decisions...'. CVEN30010's Assessment page separately describes a 50%-weighted hurdle design report addressing 'identifying potential solutions, evaluation and analysis,' which plausibly still supports PASS, but was not cited and so the gate does not stand as evidenced; a fresh capture/scoring pass with corrected citations would be needed to re-examine this gate.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (1):

- W3: several core-list subjects have no overview or assessment page in this extract (ABPL90140 Architectural Practice; ABPL90437–90439 Design Studio C/D/E; CVEN90045, CVEN90049, CVEN90050, CVEN90051, CVEN90058, CVEN90060; ABPL90117, ABPL90118, ABPL90424; CVEN90075), so the level-0 absence finding reflects only the subjects captured in this extract, not confirmed knowledge of the full course structure.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
