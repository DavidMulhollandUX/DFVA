## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — PASS condition: a coherent specialist core with progressive technical or methodological depth via a staged prerequisite chain. The eight courses are prescribed by the chosen engineering major and are documented as satisfying the first year requirements of the Bachelor of Engineering (Honours), and prerequisite chains are explicit - Physics 1B requires a pass in Physics 1A or Higher Physics 1A, Computer Systems Fundamentals assumes the prior C programming course - with a pass required in seven discipline-specific courses. This is not generic interchangeable content: the core carries engineering design and engineering-specific mathematics and physics. The depth is one foundation year only, which the rationale records but which does not meet the FAIL condition.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — PASS condition: a live project with real uncertainty rather than recall or scripted responses. The core design course documents a ten-week team project on a poorly specified, open-ended problem in which solutions must be generated and evaluated against competing budget, time, resource, environmental and social constraints - a documented trade-off decision, not a scripted response. The gate rests on the course description rather than an assessment task listing, because the extract carries no assessment descriptions for any course.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **field** (field grain), n = 0, 14 destination titles, 0 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: field-of-education occupation list (ATO-linked), not this program's own graduates

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (23 pages) and verified on 2026-09-07: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (7):

- C1 straddled 1 and 2: the core design course documents a ten-week assigned team project, which reads as a group project, but the level 2 anchor also asks for individual accountability, client or stakeholder work, and the extract lists that course's assessment only as undescribed modality labels. Resolved down to 1 by the never-resolve-upward rule and by R1, which scores documented curriculum evidence rather than what the course plausibly assesses.
- C4 straddled 1 and 2: a design project that changes from year to year suggests application to an unfamiliar problem, but no assessment task is documented for it, so the transfer claim rests on a course description. Resolved down to 1 by the never-resolve-upward rule.
- C5 straddled 1 and 2: Higher Physics 1B carries a design-and-plan-an-experimental-investigation outcome, which would support level 2, but that course sits in the additional-courses list rather than the core and its assessment is undescribed. Resolved down to 1.
- W2 straddled 1 and 2: the open-ended design problem with budget, time, resource, environmental and social constraints has genuine practice fidelity, but no artefact specification and no criteria drawn from practice are documented, and R4 forbids scoring up on the program's real-life situations wording. Resolved down to 1.
- W1 straddled 1 and 2 on the engineering design report, which is a professional genre, but it appears only as a learning outcome with no audience beyond the teaching team, so R2 and the never-resolve-upward rule put it at 1.
- C1 versus W1 on the design course's communication evidence: coordination inside the team scores in C1 and communication of the work scores in W1, per the one-construct-one-home rule; the design report is scored only in W1.
- W2 versus W3 on the design-and-build prototype: simulation-like coursework is W2 by construct, so it is not scored in W3.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (2):

- Level 3 on every item: the extract records each course's assessment only as an undescribed modality label (for example Multimodal - Standard (usually weekly or fortnightly): N/A% (Hurdle: None) and In-person - Standard (usually weekly or fortnightly): N/A% (Hurdle: None)) with no task description, weight or hurdle, so the assessment evidence R2 requires for a level 3 is absent program-wide rather than judged and rejected.
- C3 AI content: the extract contains no reference to artificial intelligence, generative AI or AI governance in any course or program outcome, so the AI half of the construct has no evidence either way and the score rests solely on the documented digital-tool operation.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
