## 3. CURRENT DISCUSSION SIGNALS — MEDIUM CONFIDENCE

**What these sources are.** Human Genetics Society of Australasia (HGSA) policy

peer-reviewed AI-exposure measure, the US National Society of Genetic Counselors' member
newsletter, and named-practitioner commentary in trade press and a personal Substack.
Direct extraction from X or LinkedIn was **not** performed and no professional forum was
sampled — the themes below rest on named policy documents, a dated survey, and
attributed practitioner and professional-body statements, not on inferred discourse.
Dates are given where the source carries one. One claim from the underlying profession
record was corrected rather than restated as originally worded (Theme 4, below) and is
flagged as such.

### Theme 1 — Australia's genetic-counselling profession is widening its self-regulated scope faster than it is being licensed

HGSA's Code of Ethics for Genetic Counsellors and its Scope of Practice for Genetic
Counsellors were both revised in **2026** to align terminology with the National Alliance
of Self-Regulating Health Professions' 2026 Standards, of which HGSA is a member body
([HGSA Code of Ethics](https://hgsa.org.au/common/Uploaded%20files/pdfs/policies,%20position%20statements%20and%20guidelines/genetic%20counselling/Code%20of%20Ethics%20for%20GC.pdf); [HGSA Scope of Practice](https://hgsa.org.au/common/Uploaded%20files/pdfs/policies,%20position%20statements%20and%20guidelines/genetic%20counselling/Scope%20of%20Practice%20for%20GC.pdf)).
The underlying Scope of Practice policy itself was revised (document 2025GC01, a
consultation draft reviewed **February 2025**, later reissued as 2026GC01) to expand
recognised areas of practice from a narrower clinical focus to six categories: clinical,
industry, academic, policy, research and education ([2025 consultation draft](https://www.hgsa.org.au/common/Uploaded%20files/pdfs/policies,%20position%20statements%20and%20guidelines/genetic%20counselling/2025%20CONSULTATION%20HGSA%20GC01%20Scope%20of%20Practice.pdf)).

This is self-regulation under a voluntary peak-body standard, not a Commonwealth or
state statutory registration scheme — Australia has no AHPRA scheme for genetic
counsellors — and it does not measure how many practising genetic counsellors actually
work outside clinical roles today. What it does support is that the certifying body's own
definition of legitimate practice now formally spans research, policy and education
alongside clinical work.

**Bearing:** C5 and W1. The widened scope names research and public-facing education as
recognised practice, which is the construct C5 (research method) and W1 (public-facing
artefact) each assess.

### Theme 2 — AI is expected to absorb the administrative layer of genetic counselling, not the counselling itself

Across every source that addresses task division, the same split recurs. In the medRxiv
survey (21 May 2026, 160 genetic counselors and 57 students), respondents put roughly a
quarter of their time in delegable administrative and repetitive work, while confidence
in AI performing complex or emotionally sensitive counselling tasks was markedly lower
than confidence in AI performing routine ones ([medRxiv, 2026-05-21](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.21.26353789v1.full)).
Licensed genetic counselor Jordana Graifman made the same division explicit in a
December 2025 Substack essay, proposing AI take on pedigree drafting, intake logistics
and routine pre/post-test education while counsellors retain high-stakes results, rare
cases and emotional support ([Substack, 2025-12-09](https://jordanagraifman.substack.com/p/how-will-ai-transform-the-field-of)).
In a November 2025 CGTlive interview, genetic counselor Colleen Caleshu framed AI as
extending access to patients with straightforward needs rather than replacing counsellors
for complex cases ([CGTlive, 2025-11-12](https://www.cgtlive.com/view/caleshu-whether-ai-replace-genetic-counselors)).

These are self-reported attitudes and individual practitioner opinions from North
American contexts through mid-to-late 2025, not measured task automation, job
displacement, or wage effects, and none of it is evidence specific to Australian
genetic-counselling practice.

**Bearing:** C1 and C3. Both currently score at the lower anchor in the program's
evidence — C1 (score 2) documents human-team collaboration but no assessed
human-plus-AI-system coordination, and C3 (score 1) documents only tool-operation
content with no assessed AI capability/limitation or ethics component — which is exactly
the administrative/AI-assisted layer this theme describes as emerging.

### Theme 3 — professional bodies frame AI as accelerating technical interpretation while warning of skill erosion in training

The National Society of Genetic Counselors' Perspectives newsletter (Issue 10, **5
August 2026**) framed AI as able to accelerate variant interpretation and rare-disease
diagnosis and extend patient support between visits, while warning that reliance on AI
risks eroding genetic counselors' critical-thinking and advocacy skills absent robust
institutional governance ([NSGC Perspectives, Issue 10](https://perspectives.nsgc.org/Article/aiml-in-genomics-and-genetic-counseling-issue-10)).
An earlier issue (Issue 7, **9 April 2026**) reported growing recognition that AI
integration into genetic-counselor graduate education is now seen as inevitable, while
faculty voiced concern that tools like ChatGPT may hinder students' writing-skill
development and create dependency; the same issue noted that only **13%** of published
studies of LLM-based virtual-patient tools used validated assessment frameworks (3 of 23
studies) ([NSGC Perspectives, Issue 7](https://perspectives.nsgc.org/Article/aiml-in-genomics-and-genetic-counseling-issue-7-1)).

This is a curated professional-society digest synthesising literature, conference talks
and member commentary — not a representative poll of the full US membership, and not
evidence about Australian genetic-counselling training. The 13% figure describes
published virtual-patient studies generally, not genetic-counselling-specific tool
adoption.

**Bearing:** C3 and C2. C3's current score (1) reflects tool-operation content with no
assessed AI-limitations or governance component — the governance gap this theme
flags directly. C2 (score 2) documents structured critique as a core construct; the
writing-skill and dependency concern bears on how that critique is developed and
supervised.

### Theme 4 — genetic counsellors register high measured AI exposure, but exposure is not displacement

A 2025 US think-tank report applying the peer-reviewed Felten-Raj-Seamans AI
Occupational Exposure (AIOE) measure — built from O*NET task/ability data
cross-referenced with AI-application ratings — placed genetic counselors among the
measure's most-exposed occupations, in its top exposure quintile, reasoning that risk
assessment from gene panels and family history aligns closely with current AI
application capabilities ([Economic Innovation Group, 2025-08-10](https://eig.org/ai-and-jobs-the-final-word/)).
**Correction to the underlying claim:** this source supports top-quintile placement, not
the stronger claim that genetic counselors hold the single highest AIOE score of any
occupation measured; that superlative is not supported and is dropped here.

AIOE is a task-similarity exposure score, agnostic to whether AI substitutes for or
complements the worker, so top-quintile placement does not by itself imply job loss,
wage change, or realised automation. The underlying O*NET task data are US-specific and
require an explicit caveat before being applied to Australian genetic counsellors.

**Bearing:** C3 and C4. High task-similarity exposure in exactly the risk-assessment and
gene-panel-interpretation tasks this program teaches bears on C3's AI-limitations content
and on C4's transfer-of-learning construct — whether graduates are assessed on applying
skills to problem contexts where AI now performs similarly.

### Critical evidence gaps to close before institutional use:
1. No Australian-specific job-ad or vacancy data exists for genetic counsellor or genomics-scientist roles — a live Australian job-board capture (Seek, government health-sector postings) would close this.
2. No Australian practitioner survey on AI adoption in genetic counselling was located; the only quantified attitude data in this report is North American.
3. HGSA's 2026 Code of Ethics and Scope of Practice documents were sourced as currently published, but the Scope of Practice's specific six-category expansion is confirmed only against a February 2025 consultation draft — final 2026GC01 wording should be independently re-checked.
4. No graduate-destination data specific to the Master of Genomics and Health cohort (QILT, JSA Higher Education Outcomes) was reviewed in this report; §1's job-family mapping rests on the program's curriculum and the profession record's occupation title, not on measured graduate outcomes.
