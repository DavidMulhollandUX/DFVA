## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the preceding scored evidence and market data; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

MC-CTPYART scores 7/15 on adaptive capabilities and 5/9 on workplace practice, against a field-of-education destination exposure of 71.19 that is itself an estimate, not a measurement against this program's own graduates (§1; §6). The curriculum's strength sits exactly where the market's job-advertisement signals are silent: a required, self-scoped, staged studio capstone (C4: 3/3) and a scaffolded practice-artefact spine culminating in a public exhibition (W2: 3/3). What the market signals do speak to — governance of AI's use of artists' own work, and the moral and resale rights that survive it — has no foothold in the curriculum at all: no digital or AI content appears anywhere in the core (C3: 0/3), and no placement, practicum or community-based project exists (W3: 0/3).

The consequence is a program that trains disciplined, self-directed studio practice extremely well and says nothing about the market conditions that practice will meet on exit: an advertised job market that barely exists for fine artists (§2 Signal 2, zero matching postings), a copyright and AI-governance question CAIRG confirmed is still unresolved (§3 Theme 3), and statutory resale and moral rights graduates need to know to assert (§3 Theme 4).
