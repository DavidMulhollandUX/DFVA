## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the scored evidence and market data above; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

The Master of Psychiatry serves as a premier academic qualification for medical practitioners and psychiatric trainees completing RANZCP Fellowship training. G1 passes on a coherent specialist sequence aligned to the RANZCP Competency-Based Fellowship across six compulsory core subjects and four selectives. G2 passes on defended diagnostic formulations under uncertainty in Consultation-Liaison case studies and workplace-based clinical assessments.

However, operating near the portfolio median exposure (**90.74**), the program exhibits considerable headroom across both sub-scales: **5 / 15 on Adaptiveness** and **4 / 9 on Workplace**. The primary deficit on adaptiveness is C3 (0/3), where the core contains zero digital or AI content despite rapid clinical adoption of ambient AI transcription platforms (Heidi Health, Lyrebird). C1 (1/3), C2 (1/3), and C4 (1/3) reflect that core tasks are heavily academic (essays, literature reviews, RANZCP exam-format papers) with collaborative, peer review, and transfer skills asserted in generic outcomes rather than assessed in core rubrics. On workplace practice, W1 (1/3) and W2 (1/3) are restricted because case studies are submitted as academic essays rather than authentic psychiatric consultation notes or tribunal reports, while W3 (2/3) reflects that workplace hurdles offer an unverified peer logbook alternative without mandatory supervisor sign-off.
