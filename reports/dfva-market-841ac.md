## 2. RECENT JOB AD SIGNALS

**Signal 1 — Critical workforce shortages across public community dental and rural clinics.**
Public health networks (Dental Health Services Victoria, Victorian Aboriginal Health Service) and regional health services recruit oral health therapists to address massive waiting lists.

**Signal 2 — Rapid adoption of digital intraoral scanning, 3D printing, and chairside imaging.**
Modern dental practices mandate proficiency in digital impression systems (iTero, TRIOS), digital radiography with AI-assisted caries detection, and cloud dental management software.

**Signal 3 — Expansion of dual-scope adult dental therapy practice under updated DBA guidelines.**
Dental Board of Australia scope changes allow oral health therapists with appropriate education to treat patients of all ages, driving hiring demand for adult restorative therapy.

**Signal 4 — Very low AI substitution pressure in physical clinical operative care and patient empathy.**
Direct manual dexterity in operative cavity preparation, periodontal debridement, local anaesthesia administration, and paediatric patient management are fundamentally immune to automation (**40.23** exposure).

## 3. CURRENT DISCUSSION SIGNALS — MEDIUM CONFIDENCE

**What these sources are.** Australian Dental Council ([ADC](https://www.adc.org.au/)), Dental Board of Australia ([DBA](https://www.dentalboard.gov.au/)), Dental Hygienists Association of Australia ([DHAA](https://dhaa.info/)), Australian Dental and Oral Health Therapists’ Association ([ADOHTA](https://www.adohta.net.au/)), and peer-reviewed literature ([Australian Dental Journal](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/18347819), [December 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/18347819)). Direct extraction from X or LinkedIn was **not** performed and no social media forum was sampled — where this section refers to discourse, it denotes published ADC professional competencies, DBA codes of conduct, ADOHTA practice standards, and peer-reviewed dental research by named bodies and authors. Dates are stated where available.

### Theme 1 — digital intraoral imaging, AI caries detection, and diagnostic accountability

Guidance from the [ADC](https://www.adc.org.au/) (November 2024) and *Australian Dental Journal* (December 2024) emphasizes that while AI diagnostic software highlights interproximal caries and bone loss on bitewing radiographs, registered oral health therapists retain personal statutory liability for clinical diagnosis and treatment planning.

**Bearing:** C2, C3, and W2.

### Theme 2 — interprofessional dental team collaboration and four-handed dentistry

Reports from [ADOHTA](https://www.adohta.net.au/) and [DHAA](https://dhaa.info/) highlight that oral health therapists work in complex multi-operator clinical environments alongside dentists, dental prosthetists, and dental assistants, requiring advanced collaborative communication and operator-assistant ergonomics.

**Bearing:** C1, W1, and W3.

### Theme 3 — evidence-based prevention, health promotion, and self-scoped clinical inquiry

Standards from the [ADC](https://www.adc.org.au/) confirm that structured clinical portfolios, self-scoped literature reviews, and primary oral health promotion interventions represent the gold standard for clinical training.

**Bearing:** C4, C5, and G1.
