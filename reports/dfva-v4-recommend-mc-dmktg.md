## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the preceding scored evidence and market data; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

This program's curriculum scores lowest on hybrid metacognition and evaluative judgement (C2) and digital and artificial intelligence (AI) literacy including governance (C3), both at the entry level, and on work-situated learning (W3), whose only evidence is a restricted, optional internship. The remaining five items — distributed cognition and relational capability (C1), life-long learning and transfer (C4), inquiry and evidence generation (C5), professional communication and conduct (W1) and authentic task design (W2) — sit one level below their ceiling, each with an existing core assessment the interventions below extend rather than replace. Market evidence for these gaps concentrates on three shifts: AI adoption in market research (95% of researchers already use or are testing AI tools, market report §3 Theme 2), sales-management regulatory obligations under the Australian Consumer Law (ACL) and the Australian Financial Services (AFS) licensing regime (§3 Themes 3 to 4), and clerical-task automation exposure (§3 Theme 6) — none of which the core curriculum currently documents assessing against.
