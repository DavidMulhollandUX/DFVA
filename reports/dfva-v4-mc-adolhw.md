## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — The course structure documents an all-compulsory specialist core for each specialisation ('Six core subjects' for both Research and Leadership specialisations), every one of which is adolescent-health-specific in name and content (Young People in Context, Professional Practice in Context, SocioEnvironmentalContext of Adolescents, Health Promotion and Young People, plus a specialisation-specific research or leadership sequence). This is a coherent, compulsory, discipline-specific core, satisfying G1's PASS condition.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — POPH90268 (Research-specialisation core) requires students to independently justify and defend methodological choices ('Justified appropriate methods for data analysis') in a hurdled, supervised research proposal before proceeding to the thesis, with real (not scripted) uncertainty about outcome. POPH90311 (Leadership-specialisation core) requires students to 'Plan, implement and evaluate a stakeholder engagement strategy on a chosen topic of concern or interest' -- an open-ended, live, accountability-bearing task rather than recall or scripted response. Both specialisation cores document defended trade-off decisions or live-project uncertainty, satisfying G2's PASS condition.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (7):

- C1: elective POPH90169 (Adolescent Sexuality and Sexual Health) has genuine group-assessed work ('Group written assignment... Students will be assessed as a group'), but as an elective it does not count toward a core-unit-only construct; C1 is scored from core evidence alone at Level 1.
- C2: PAED90007's 5x reflective-writing pieces are assessed (10%), which could be read as self-assessment 'assessed against criteria', but no criterion-referenced rubric for appraising quality of work is documented in the extract, so the lower Level 1 was taken rather than Level 2.
- C3: the course-level generic-skills bullet 'Skills in information technology' is the only IT-adjacent mention anywhere in the extract; it does not describe assessed content, an elective, or tool-operation training, so the lower Level 0 was taken rather than Level 1.
- C4: demoted on review from 3 to 2. The self-scoped-capstone evidence (POPH90268 Minor Thesis) is documented for the Research-specialisation core only; the Leadership-specialisation core substitutes WELF90013 (subject page not captured in this extract) and POPH90311, so it is unconfirmed whether an equivalent self-scoped/knowledge-gap route exists on that branch. Per the instrument's global ambiguity rule (resolve ambiguity to the lower level, regardless of whether an individual anchor restates it), this now matches C5's treatment of the identical Research-only-versus-unconfirmed-Leadership ambiguity.
- C5: POPH90268 in isolation would support Level 3 (substantial required primary-evidence project, methodology defended in a supervised proposal), but per the anchor's own text ('REQUIRED, not one route among several'), because the Leadership specialisation does not document an equivalent required primary-research project, the item was capped at Level 2.
- W2: whether PAED90007's 'Case Study including psychosocial assessment' and POPH90173's health-promotion 'Project Proposal' constitute the literal end-to-end artefact a practitioner would produce, versus an academic write-up framed around one, is not fully explicit in the extract; the lower reading (Level 2, not 3) was taken, and no genuine constraint of practice was found to support Level 3 regardless.
- W3: POPH90311 (Leadership core) references the student's 'own understanding of workplace situations' and 'the realities of their own position', approaching the v4.2 own-practice route, but its assessment (Report, Reflective Practice Paper, oral presentations on stakeholder advocacy) is not documented as activity in the student's own practice with real recipients and a documented outcome measure, so it was not counted, and W3 remains at Level 1 on the strength of the elective-only POPH90170.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
