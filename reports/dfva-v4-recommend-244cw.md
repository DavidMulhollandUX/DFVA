# DFVA v4 IMPROVEMENT PLAN: Master of Public Health (244CW)

**Instrument:** DFVA 4.0-draft — Panel C v4 on the TEQSA adaptive capabilities [[1]](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/guides-resources/resources/corporate-publications/assuring-quality-learning-gen-ai-integrated-future-role-adaptive-capabilities)
**Assessment date:** 2026-08-13 · **Derived from:** the verified panelCv4 scoring + reports/dfva-market-244cw.md
**Position basis:** Destination AI Exposure 89.46 (measured) × Curriculum Adaptiveness 9/15 (v4 draft) — no v1 composite, no position label until the v4 migration cycle

## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the scored evidence and market data above; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

The score concentrates in a specific and unusual shape: four of five items sit
at 2/3 with both gates held, and one item — C3, Digital & AI literacy including
governance — sits at 1/3. The program's disciplinary machinery is sound: a
compulsory 75-point core, three assessed methods subjects, recurring team
assessment, criterion-referenced appraisal, and a required capstone. What the
handbook does not document, anywhere, is AI-era capability: no core unit
addresses AI capabilities and limits, no assessment asks a student to justify
reliance on a machine-assisted output, and no team task names a role for AI
tools.

**C3 is the binding constraint**, for two compounding reasons. First, it is the
only item two anchor levels from ceiling. Second, the market evidence points at
exactly this gap: graduates enter destinations averaging 89.46 exposure while
"AI verification, provenance and disclosure" is the skill-shift row rising
sharply *and* absent from the compulsory core. Four of the five levers below
are one-anchor-level moves on existing assessment infrastructure; none
requires a new subject.

## 2. SCORE-TO-ACTION MAP — Basis: inferred

**C3 · Digital & AI literacy, including governance — 1 → 2.**
Next anchor: *"Core units address AI capabilities AND limitations/ethics; use
with limits discussed and assessed."* Action: add an AI-capabilities-and-limits
component with assessed verification exercises inside Biostatistics
(POPH90013) and Epidemiology 1 (POPH90014) — the two core units where machine
output already appears as statistical tooling. This is the operational-to-
critical move the TEQSA framework requires [[1]](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/guides-resources/resources/corporate-publications/assuring-quality-learning-gen-ai-integrated-future-role-adaptive-capabilities),
at the competency grain defined by the AI-literacy frameworks [[11]](https://doi.org/10.1145/3313831.3376727)[[12]](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000391105),
and it must live in the core rather than an elective — the Lodge et al.
framework's Principle 6 warns directly against tool-level skills of "limited
future utility" [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/).

**C2 · Hybrid metacognition & evaluative judgement — 2 → 3.**
Next anchor: *"Assessment requires students to document and justify reliance
decisions — when they relied on or overrode a tool, source or collaborator;
defend judgements of AI-output quality; or evidence strategy adjustment over
time (process-focused assessment)."* Action: extend one existing appraisal
assessment — the POPH90231 critical reflection or the POPH90218 reflective
leadership piece — to require a documented reliance log with justification.
This is evaluative judgement operationalised [[4]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0220-3)
and the process-focused rebalancing TEQSA's assessment-reform series has asked
for since 2023 [[3]](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-09/assessment-reform-age-artificial-intelligence-discussion-paper.pdf)[[1]](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/guides-resources/resources/corporate-publications/assuring-quality-learning-gen-ai-integrated-future-role-adaptive-capabilities).

**C1 · Distributed cognition & relational capability — 2 → 3.**
Next anchor: *"Assessed collaborative practice recurs across the program AND
at least one assessment requires coordinating work across people and tools/AI
systems."* Action: the recurrence half is already satisfied; specify the
human–AI coordination half inside one existing team assessment (the POPH90274
group program-development task is the natural host): documented role and tool
allocation — who drafts with what, who verifies, who signs off. Distributed
cognition is precisely this architecture of thinking across people and tools
[[1]](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/guides-resources/resources/corporate-publications/assuring-quality-learning-gen-ai-integrated-future-role-adaptive-capabilities),
and the labour market pays for the combination of social and analytical skill
[[5]](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjx022).

**C4 · Life-long learning & transfer — 2 → 3.**
Next anchor: *"The program documents structured progression toward independent
learning: self-scoped capstone or research project, assessed identification of
one's own knowledge gaps, or work-integrated learning requiring performance in
a context not taught."* Action: the capability exists on two of three capstone
routes (hurdle Learning Plan; self-scoped research project); the block is
documentation — publish the Public Health in Practice route's assessment
detail in the handbook, and extend the assessed learning-plan requirement to
all capstone routes so the transfer claim holds for every graduate
[[9]](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/13398/chapter/2)[[10]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10459-025-10413-y).

**C5 · Inquiry & evidence generation — 2 → 3.**
Next anchor: *"A substantial project generating primary evidence is REQUIRED
(not one route among several), with methodology defended under scrutiny."*
Action: make a primary-evidence element required in every capstone route —
either by making the Research Project the default, or by adding a defended
primary-evidence component to the Professional Practice and Public Health in
Practice routes. This is the largest governance decision in the plan
[14][15].

## 3. MARKET ALIGNMENT — Basis: reported → inferred

Each lever answers named market evidence (confidence as stated in the market
report: exposure values HIGH, signals and skill shifts MEDIUM):

- **P1 (C3) ← "AI verification, provenance and disclosure ↑↑ rising sharply"**
  and discussion theme 2, *policy officers as verifiers* — government
  destinations sit at 97.4 exposure and the stated professional risk is
  unverified machine output entering the evidence chain.
- **P2 (C2) ← discussion theme 3**, *evidence-synthesis tooling is outrunning
  appraisal skill* — the recognised gap between what practitioners can
  generate and what they can properly appraise is an evaluative-judgement gap
  [[4]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0220-3).
- **P3 (C1) ← skill-shift rows "community co-design and cultural safety ↑"
  and "report and brief drafting fluency ↓"** — the retained work is
  coordinated, accountable, relational; the delegated work is solo drafting
  [[5]](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjx022).
- **P4 (C4) ← signal 3**, *evaluation named as scarce capability*, plus the
  program's own recent additions (climate-health, One Health) — transfer into
  novel contexts is the market's description of the scarce evaluator.
- **P5 (C5) ← skill-shift row "causal inference and confounder reasoning ↑↑"**
  and discussion theme 1, *"the descriptive cut is free now"* — the defended
  primary inference is the retained human task, and it is exactly what a
  required primary-evidence capstone assesses.

## 5. GATE GUARDRAILS — Basis: scored

Any redesign is checked against the gates, which currently PASS and must not
be traded away for adaptiveness points:

- **G1 · Disciplinary foundation.** The 75-point compulsory core plus required
  capstone is the epistemic foundation the capabilities are built on
  [[1]](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/guides-resources/resources/corporate-publications/assuring-quality-learning-gen-ai-integrated-future-role-adaptive-capabilities);
  a redesign that dilutes the specialist core to make room for AI content
  breaks the gate regardless of its C3 effect — which is why P4 embeds inside
  existing methods subjects rather than adding units. Technical depth is the
  precondition, not the adaptiveness claim
  [[6]](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjaa021).
- **G2 · Decision-making under uncertainty.** The ethical-dilemma exam and
  prioritisation assessments carry this gate [16]; P3's reliance-log extension
  must add to, not replace, defended trade-off assessment.

## 6. WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE SCORE — Basis: inferred

Explicit anchor deltas, if implemented and documented in the handbook:

- P4 moves C3 1→2 (+1); P6 subsequently moves C3 2→3 (+1).
- P3 moves C2 2→3 (+1). P2 moves C1 2→3 (+1). P1 moves C4 2→3 (+1).
- P5 moves C5 2→3 (+1). Ceiling on this plan: adaptiveness 9 → 15, with
  realistic 12-month reach 9 → 12 (P1, P2, P4).

What would **not** move a score, stated so it is not attempted:

- **Outcome-statement edits.** Rule R2 caps outcomes-without-assessment at
  level 1 everywhere; rewording ILOs changes nothing the instrument can see
  [[8]](https://doi.org/10.1257/pandp.20181019).
- **New electives.** C3's anchors require the *core*: an elective reaches the
  graduates who least need it, and the exposure sits in destinations every
  graduate can enter.
- **A new AI subject that displaces core methods content** — it would risk G1
  while duplicating what P4 achieves inside existing subjects.

Re-scoring after implementation follows the harness unchanged: scrape the new
handbook vintage, score, adversarially verify, and publish the delta — the
same instrument, so v4-to-v4 comparison is valid, unlike v3.1-to-v4
[[17]][[18]](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12909-026-08969-3).
