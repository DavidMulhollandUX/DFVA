## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — PASS on the anchor's all-compulsory specialist core and accredited sequence, with progressive technical depth. The core is 66 units of named civil engineering courses compulsory at each of Levels I to IV, the program is accredited by Engineers Australia with graduates qualifying for professional membership, and depth is staged - Structural Mechanics precedes Reinforced Concrete Design and Structural Steel Design, and Civil Engineering Hydraulics precedes Advanced Civil Engineering Hydraulics. Content is disciplinary, not interchangeable.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — PASS on the anchor's capstone-with-real-uncertainty route and its defended trade-off route. The required capstone assesses engineering solutions to complex, open ended problems through reports and two oral defences, and the Level III core Reinforced Concrete Design assesses design decisions informed by environmental impact through a 40% Design Project - a defended trade-off rather than recall. Recognising uncertainty and the limits of engineering approaches is an explicit outcome of the Level II core hydraulics course.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **field** (field grain), n = 0, 14 destination titles, 0 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: field-of-education occupation list (ATO-linked), not this program's own graduates

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (73 pages) and verified on 2026-09-07: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (6):

- C1 straddled 1 and 2: the capstone's assessed items are reports and oral defences, and the extract does not state that collaboration itself is marked, only that the project is done in small groups. Resolved to 2 because the level-2 anchor names 'group projects' as the qualifying form and the group project's deliverables carry documented assessment weights; the level-1 anchor's 'no core unit assesses it' would be false here.
- C4 straddled 2 and 3: a required two-semester honours research project preceded by a dedicated Level III research-methods course reads as structured progression toward independent learning, but the level-3 exemplar is a SELF-SCOPED capstone and the extract states supervisors provide the project offerings. Resolved DOWN to 2 under the lower-level rule.
- W1 straddled 1 and 2: design reports and design documentation produced against AS3600, AS4100 and the Australian Rainfall and Runoff national guidelines are arguably recognised professional genres. Resolved DOWN to 1 because the extract documents neither the assessment criteria for those reports nor any audience beyond the teaching team, and because the fidelity of the design task is scored in W2 under the one-construct-one-home rule.
- Course-page identity for the Level IV core: program rules list CIVILENG 4050 Design Practice as the sole Level IV core, and the extract carries two candidate pages - Civil and Environmental Design Project (ENGIX403, an industry-led capstone design with Concept Design and Design Project tasks worth 65%, an Interview worth 30% and a Portfolio worth 5%) and Design Practice (ENGM2015, a mechanical design introduction with an empty assessment list). Neither is confirmable as CIVILENG 4050, so ENGIX403 was not used to lift C2 (its Portfolio), C4 (its self-directed-learning outcome), W1 (its diverse-audience communication) or W2 above the level its other evidence already supports.
- W3 straddled 1 and 2: the 8-week placement is compulsory, unlike the level-1 anchor's elective or optional framing, but level 2 additionally requires practitioner supervision and assessment, and the 0-unit enrolment shell documents neither. Resolved DOWN to 1.
- C5: the capstone's project styles range across design-build-test, industry-focused and research-orientated work, so not every student's project is research-shaped. Scored 3 regardless because every listed style generates primary evidence (theoretical analyses, simulations and experiments) and the defence and staged Part A review apply to all of them.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (3):

- ENG 3100 Engineering Internship UG - no course page was captured for the 0-unit placement shell, so its supervision arrangements, assessment (if any) and reflective requirements could not be scored; W3 was scored on the program-rules text alone.
- CIVILENG 3006 Geotechnical Engineering (Level III core) - no matching course page appears in the extract, so its assessment contributed to no item.
- Assessment tables are empty for a large share of the core: Introduction to Infrastructure, Engineering Mechanics - Statics, Introduction to Engineering, Programming (Matlab and Excel), Mathematics IA, Strength of Materials, Civil Engineering Hydraulics, Introduction to Geo-engineering, Transportation Engineering & Survey, Statistics & Numerical Methods II, Computer Analysis of Structures and Structural Dynamics, Advanced Civil Engineering Hydraulics, Systems Engineering and Industry Practice, and Research Methods and Project Management. Every level-2 and level-3 judgement above therefore rests only on the subset of core courses whose assessment weights were captured, and C1, C2 and W1 in particular could move up if those tables were captured.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
