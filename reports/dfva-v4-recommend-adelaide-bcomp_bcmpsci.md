## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the preceding scored evidence and market data; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

This document argues from evidence; it is not itself evidence. Adelaide's Bachelor of Computer Science passes both gates: G1, on the 36 units of compulsory computer science core staged across Level I to Level III with a required third-year major, and G2, on the trade-off decisions already assessed in core Computer Systems, Software Engineering & Project, and the project-management course. Neither gate constrains the redesign below; both need only to be preserved, not created.

AI exposure rests on a field-of-education basis (83.3, field grain, estimated) — the destinations are the whole 0201 Computer Science field's, not this program's own graduates — so it is sound to say the field is highly exposed and no more. The program holds broad headroom on both sub-scales: 11 of 15 on Adaptiveness (currently 4/15) and 6 of 9 on Workplace (currently 3/9). C5 (0/3) is the single largest gap — no research-methods training appears in the core at all — followed by C1 through C4 and W1 through W3, each sitting at level 1: collaboration, evaluative judgement, AI literacy, transfer, professional communication, authentic task design and work-situated learning are each claimed in the program's stated outcomes but not documented as assessed in a compulsory unit.
