## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — PASS on the anchor's 'staged prerequisite chain' and specialist-core routes: a 192-unit honours award with a compulsory five-course interdisciplinary engineering design core numbered and sequenced from ENGN1211 to ENGN3301 into a year-4 capstone, technical courses that state they build directly on their year-1 predecessors, mathematics prerequisites at admission, and a required ENGN major. Content is disciplinary and non-interchangeable, not generic.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — PASS: core assessment requires defended trade-off decisions and carries real uncertainty. ENGN2301 requires navigating constraints, balancing multiple design factors and constructing justifications from data; ENGN2219 assesses evaluation of design trade-offs; the capstone requires critically evaluating alternative options to justify solutions on an open-ended project. This is not recall or scripted response.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **field** (field grain), n = 0, 14 destination titles, 0 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: field-of-education occupation list (ATO-linked), not this program's own graduates

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (19 pages) and verified on 2026-09-07: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (7):

- C1 straddled 2 and 3: assessed teamwork recurs across every stage (level-3 first clause satisfied), but no assessment documents allocation of work between people and tools/AI systems; the lower-level rule and the 'one construct, one home' rule (COMP1730's GenAI work is individual) held it at 2.
- C2 straddled 2 and 3: reflection is assessed in five core units and a logbook is assessed in ENGN2301, which is process-focused in form, but the extract never states that any reflection requires documenting or justifying reliance on a tool, source or collaborator; resolved down to 2.
- C3 straddled 1 and 2: ENGN3301 states it explores the use and risks of AI tools in professional engineering, which reads as level-2 capabilities-plus-limits content, but no assessment task in that course names AI, and COMP1730's AI content is prompt-engineering tool operation, which the item caps at level 1; resolved down to 1 under R2.
- C5 straddled 2 and 3: ENGN2301's 'Project Presentation and Interview' is defence-shaped scrutiny of a data-generating project, but the substantial year-4 project is one of two routes (ENGN4300 OR ENGN4350) and no viva or staged supervised methodological review is documented; resolved down to 2.
- W1 straddled 2 and 3: professional-genre communication is assessed repeatedly and progressively and the capstone includes a public showcase, but professional conduct appears as a learning outcome rather than as a stated assessment criterion, which R2 and the Barrie caution in the item's migration note forbid scoring; resolved down to 2.
- W3 straddled 1 and 2: the capstone is core and assessed and involves external project hosts (level-2 'live client project' shape), but the extract documents simulation of an engineering firm rather than placement in a workplace, and no practitioner supervision or host accountability; resolved down to 1. Scored above 0 because host-facing project work does appear in the structure.
- Seven core or alternative courses have empty Overview, Learning Outcomes and Assessment Tasks fields in the extract (ENGN1217, ENGN1218, ENGN2217, ENGN2228, ENGN4350, MATH1013, MATH1014), so every item was scored from the documented courses only; the missing pages could only raise scores, never lower them, so the lower-level rule applies to them as well.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
