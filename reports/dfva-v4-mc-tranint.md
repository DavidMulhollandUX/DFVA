**C1 Distributed cognition & relational capability — 2/3**

> This subject engages students in real-world translation projects with actual clients. They will work in project teams and develop the necessary processes and professional skills to produce translations of publication quality.
>
> Supervised translation projects (various tasks and deadlines throughout the semester)
>
> Work effectively and productively in a group situation

**C2 Hybrid metacognition & evaluative judgement — 2/3**

> Using the standards for 'Certified Translator' from Australia's National Accreditation Authority for Translators and Interpreters (NAATI) as a framework, students will undertake regular practical tasks and sharpen their ability to review translations, identify errors, and compare and evaluate translation choices.
>
> Translation and peer reviews

**C3 Digital & AI literacy, including governance — 2/3**

> Students will be trained to make effective and ethical use of various AI technologies for intercultural communication tasks including translation, language teaching, transnational marketing communications, and game localisation.
>
> Through practical tasks and interactive activities, students will learn to critically assess the output of AI in intercultural communication tasks, and to adopt different paradigms and strategies for human-AI collaboration.
>
> Understand the ethics of translation and interpreting in the digital age and resolve ethical dilemmas in their professional practice.
>
> Assignments on AI-enhanced intercultural communication

**C4 Life-long learning & transfer — 2/3**

>

>
> Thesis Draft: A full draft of the thesis is to be submitted approximately 2 weeks prior to the final thesis submission date.

## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — The program documents a coherent specialist translation/interpreting core (six compulsory subjects, 75 points, all discipline-specific: theory and practice, Australian-context and Chinese-context translation, bidirectional translation, AI/intercultural communication) and is an accredited sequence, endorsed by NAATI at multiple certification levels — the anchor's 'accredited sequence' route to PASS.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — Capstone options carry real uncertainty and accountability: TRAN90022 has students deliver actual client translation projects under real deadlines, and TRAN90010 places students in an industry partner's workplace for ~340 hours with an external evaluation report — both are live-project/placement routes with genuine stakes, meeting the anchor's 'simulations/capstones/live projects with real uncertainty and accountability' condition.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (8):

- C1: Level-2 evidence (TRAN90022 team-based client project) comes from one option within Capstone Stream 1; students choosing other capstone combinations (e.g. TRAN90021+TRAN90027, or the Stream 2 thesis) show no comparably explicit assessed team-collaboration evidence. Resolved by scoring the documented Level-2 instance and not crediting Level 3, since no core evidence shows recurrence or assessed coordination of people-plus-tools/AI.
- C2: TRAN90011's overview claims students 'critically assess the output of AI', which gestures toward Level 3 (defending judgements of AI-output quality), but the graded assessment-item descriptions do not themselves specify this as a criterion. Resolved to Level 2 per R2 (assessment evidence required, not overview/outcome text).
- C3: TRAN90011's overview names 'legal and ethical risks incurred by AI use' as course content, but the assessment-item descriptions do not name bias, accountability, transparency, regulation or data governance as graded criteria. Resolved to Level 2 per R2/R3.
- C4: Level-3 evidence (the Stream 2 thesis) is route-dependent — Stream 1 capstone students are not shown completing a self-scoped project. Scored 3 because the C4 Level-3 anchor, unlike C5's, has no explicit 'required, not one route among several' exclusion.
- C5: The same thesis evidence that satisfies Level 2 (inquiry with methodology, assessed) is explicitly barred from Level 3 by the anchor's own 'REQUIRED (not one route among several)' qualifier, since Stream 2 is an alternative to Stream 1. Resolved to Level 2.
- W1: TRAN90010's practitioner-graded 'Evaluation report from industry partner' is a single strong instance toward Level 3, but 'repeatedly and progressively across the program' is not documented — compulsory-core presentations (TRAN90007, TRAN90008, TRAN90009) are addressed to the teaching team, not a professional/external audience. Resolved to Level 2.
- W2: Level-3 evidence combines guaranteed compulsory-core scaffolding (TRAN90007's NAATI-framework tasks) with a capstone instance carrying genuine practice constraints (TRAN90022's real clients/deadlines, or TRAN90010's placement); Stream 2 (thesis) students may not encounter that specific constraint. Scored 3 on the strength that three of four capstone routes carry it and the anchor's own phrasing does not require universal coverage.
- W3: Placement evidence (TRAN90010, TRAN90027) is optional rather than required — the capstone can be completed entirely on-campus (TRAN90021+TRAN90022) or via the Stream 2 thesis with no workplace placement. Resolved to Level 2 since Level 3 requires the placement to be 'in the core' (required).

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
