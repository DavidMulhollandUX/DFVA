## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the preceding scored evidence and market data; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

Both gates pass, and the adaptive sub-scale is closer to its floor than its
ceiling: three items (C3, C4, C5) sit at Level 1, and the strongest adaptive
item (C1, C2) sits at Level 2. Workplace practice is stronger — W3 is already
at the maximum, carried by the compulsory, year-long, host-supervised
internship — but W1 and W2 both sit one level short.

The gap has a shared shape. C3, C4 and C5 all fail for the same reason: the
program's strongest candidate evidence for the next level exists, but is
reserved for another item (the internship's cross-context work supplies W3,
not C4) or is optional rather than universal (the Health Research stream
supplies C5's Level 3 anchor in full, but only for students who elect it, not
for the whole compulsory core). Every lever below is either extending an
existing assessed task to a second construct, or making an already-adequate
optional route compulsory — none proposes new content from outside the
handbook evidence.

never added.

A plan that moved workplace practice to its ceiling and left adaptiveness one
item short (C4, capped at Level 2 by the available market evidence) has done
something different from a plan that moved both to ceiling, and a single
total would hide which. Workplace practice reaches its full 9/9 ceiling; the
adaptive sub-scale reaches 14/15 — C4 has no market-warranted lever to Level
3 in the sourced material, so this plan does not propose one.

A realistic twelve-month outcome from P1, P2 and P3 — all inside existing
subjects, none requiring an external agreement or a course-structure decision
— is an adaptiveness of 10 and workplace practice unchanged at 7. P7 depends
on securing external-panel capacity and P8 on a course-structure decision, so
both sit beyond that horizon.

Five categories of change would not move any score, and are recorded so that
effort is not directed toward them.

| Change | Why it does not move a score | Governing rule | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revising program-level outcomes to name AI capability, transfer or self-directed learning more explicitly | This is exactly the failure already scored: "Identify future learning needs..." and "Skills in self-directed learning" already sit in program outcomes and HLTH90011's generic skills, and C4 scores 1, not 2, because none of it is tied to an assessed criterion. Rule R2 scores a capability that is stated but not assessed at Level 1. | Scoring rule R2 | [[8]](https://doi.org/10.1257/pandp.20181019)[33] |
| Describing the existing GENE90025/GENE90026 software-and-database content, or the WELF90004/WELF90005 role plays, as "AI-literate" or "authentic" without changing the task | Rule R4 scores documented task features only. P1, P4 and P6 change the task, the artefact produced, or the criteria applied — relabelling the current tool-operation content would not. | Scoring rule R4 | [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634)[[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676) |
| Adding AI-governance or transfer content as an elective, optional workshop, or unassessed module | The C3 and C4 anchors at Levels 2–3 require core units and core *assessment*. An elective would not reach the core-assessed bar even if the program had a general-elective structure — the specialist core is entirely compulsory by G1's own evidence. | C3 and C4 anchors, levels 2–3 | [[1]](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/guides-resources/resources/corporate-publications/assuring-quality-learning-gen-ai-integrated-future-role-adaptive-capabilities)[13] |

capture the new handbook vintage, score the eight items, verify adversarially,
