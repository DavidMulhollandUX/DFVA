## 3. CURRENT DISCUSSION SIGNALS — MEDIUM CONFIDENCE

**What these sources are.** Regulatory and statutory instruments, scholarly and grey-literature studies, drawn from the committed profession records for the occupations in §1, each with its publisher, date and link. Direct extraction from X or LinkedIn was **not** performed and no professional forum was sampled for this report; the profession records declare their own practitioner-discourse sample — reddit (115 items, r/Accounting, r/AusFinance); cpa_caanz_bulletins (62 items, In The Black, Acuity Magazine); practitioner_forums (45 items, National Personal Financial Advisors Association discourse); practitioner_forums (45 items, National Bookkeeping, Accounting, and Auditing Clerks Association discourse) — which is a declared sample, not a scrape. Themes are the claims as recorded; nothing is paraphrased, and a claim without a source was not carried. Dates are given where the source carries one.

### Theme 1 — aPESB professional standards require auditors to verify automated ledger anomaly detection algorithms and m…

APESB professional standards require auditors to verify automated ledger anomaly detection algorithms and maintain documented audit trial logs for all AI-generated financial estimates. (Accountants and Auditors; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Accounting Professional & Ethical Standards Board (APESB), APES 110 Code of Ethics for Professional Accountants — Technology and AI Provisio](https://apesb.org.au/standards-guidance/apes-110-code-of-ethics/) (2024-12-01).

Scope: All registered company auditors and public practice accountants in Australia.

**Bearing:** C2 and C3 and G1.

### Theme 2 — mandatory Australian Sustainability Reporting Standards (ASRS) create an acute shortage of accounting gradu…

Mandatory Australian Sustainability Reporting Standards (ASRS) create an acute shortage of accounting graduates skilled in multi-capital greenhouse gas assurance and climate risk governance. (Accountants and Auditors; lane L2, scholarly and grey-literature studies.)

Sources: [Accounting & Finance, Climate Reporting Mandates and the Changing Competency Profile of Australian Auditors](https://doi.org/10.1111/acfi.13289) (2025-05-18).

Scope: ASX-listed reporting entities and registered audit firms.

**Bearing:** C4 and C5 and W2.

### Theme 3 — personal financial advisers ('relevant providers') are among the most regulated occupations in Australia: u…

Personal financial advisers ('relevant providers') are among the most regulated occupations in Australia: under the professional standards regime (Corporations Act 2001) they must meet an approved qualifications standard, pass the financial adviser exam, complete a professional year (new entrants), and undertake 40 hours/year CPD, governed by the Financial Planners and Advisers Code of Ethics 2019. Advisers must act in the client's best interest (Corporations Act s961B) and operate under an AFSL. The reforms raised the education bar, with all existing advisers required to meet the qualifications standard by 1 January 2026. (Personal Financial Advisors; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [ASIC, Professional standards (financial advisers)](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/financial-services/financial-advice/professional-standards/) (2026).

Scope: Applies to personal advice to retail clients on relevant financial products; does not cover general advice or non-licensed guidance.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 4 — jobs and Skills Australia's Generative AI Capacity Study (August 2025) scores 358 ANZSCO occupations on tas…

Jobs and Skills Australia's Generative AI Capacity Study (August 2025) scores 358 ANZSCO occupations on task-level augmentability and automatability. It finds augmentation outweighs automation across the workforce: about 79% of Australian workers face low or very low automation risk, while routine clerical and administrative roles - including bookkeepers, receptionists, and business-analysis, sales, marketing and PR positions - carry the highest automation exposure. The study flags entry-level and clerical business roles as most exposed, with limited mobility options. (Personal Financial Advisors; lane L2, scholarly and grey-literature studies.)

Sources: [Jobs and Skills Australia, Australia's AI Transition: Jobs, Skills and the Future of Work (Generative AI Capacity Study)](https://www.jobsandskills.gov.au/studies/generative-artificial-intelligence-capacity-study) (2025-08).

Scope: Australian occupations nationally; model-derived task-level estimates. Adviser roles are professional, high-augmentation per JSA, with robo-advice as the automation edge.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 5 — the Tax Practitioners Board Code of Professional Conduct (Division 30, Tax Agent Services Act 2009) binds r…

The Tax Practitioners Board Code of Professional Conduct (Division 30, Tax Agent Services Act 2009) binds registered tax agents and BAS agents, a category that includes bookkeepers who provide tax/BAS services; it sets competence, confidentiality and ethical-conduct standards. (Bookkeeping, Accounting, and Auditing Clerks; lane L1, regulatory and statutory instruments.)

Sources: [Tax Practitioners Board, TPB(GS) 01/2010 Code of Professional Conduct](https://tpb.gov.au/tpb-gs-01-2010-code-professional-conduct) (2010 (current to 2026-04-30)).

Scope: Registered tax/BAS agents only. Bookkeepers not providing tax/BAS services fall outside TPB but remain subject to employer obligations.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

### Theme 6 — seven major 2025 studies (ILO, WEF, Anthropic, OpenAI, academic RCTs) converge: GenAI augmentation is real…

Seven major 2025 studies (ILO, WEF, Anthropic, OpenAI, academic RCTs) converge: GenAI augmentation is real and large in cognitive service work, while automation risk concentrates in clerical and secretarial work. (Bookkeeping, Accounting, and Auditing Clerks; lane L2, scholarly and grey-literature studies.)

Sources: [scty.org, AI at Work: The 2025 Evidence](https://scty.org/words/ai-work-2025) (2025-12).

Scope: Synthesis of multiple studies; clerical/secretarial named as the clear automation-exposed group.

**Bearing:** not stated in the profession record.

## 5. CURRICULUM IMPLICATIONS

| # | Implication | Dimension | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| CI-1 | APESB standards now require auditors to verify automated anomaly-detection algorithms and keep documented audit trail logs for AI-generated financial estimates (Theme 1, §3), a governance-of-AI skill distinct from AI tool operation. If the curriculum's AI-related core content covers technique and implementation without an assessed governance, limitations or audit-trail component, it undershoots what the largest destination family (Accountants and Auditors, Signal 1, §2, 6,240 postings) increasingly expects. | C3 | Add an assessed task in an existing core AI/analytics subject requiring students to document and justify an automated system's output, rather than only build or apply it (cost: rubric redesign and additional marking criteria in that subject). |
| CI-2 | Mandatory Australian Sustainability Reporting Standards create an acute shortage of graduates skilled in multi-capital greenhouse gas assurance and climate risk governance (Theme 2, §3), a domain the curriculum's quantitative core was not designed around. This is falsifiable against whether a core or capstone assessment already requires evidence generation in a novel, externally defined domain. | C5 | Offer an ASRS-assurance option within the existing research-report component, alongside the current topic range (cost: sourcing a supervisor with sustainability-assurance expertise). |
| CI-3 | Personal financial advisers operate under one of Australia's most regulated professional-conduct regimes — an approved qualifications standard, a professional year, mandatory CPD and a best-interest duty under the Corporations Act (Theme 3, §3). If no core assessment is delivered to, or judged against criteria drawn from, a real external audience, the curriculum understates the conduct-facing communication this destination family expects on entry. | W1 | Add a client-facing or practitioner-judged communication task to an existing core assessment, such as a mock advice engagement reviewed by an industry panel (cost: coordinating practitioner reviewers and added marking load). |
| CI-4 | The Jobs and Skills Australia capacity study and a seven-study synthesis both find automation risk concentrates in clerical and bookkeeping work while cognitive service work is augmented rather than replaced (Theme 4 and Theme 6, §3). This implies resilience in this curriculum depends on assessed tasks that require coordinating people and AI systems together, not on tool proficiency alone. | C1 | Extend an existing core group assessment to require a documented allocation of work between team members and AI tools, rather than teamwork alone (cost: rubric redesign for that assessment). |
