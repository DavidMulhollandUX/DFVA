## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** FAIL — DEMOTED on verify pass: the submitted rationale claimed 'an explicit staged prerequisite chain between core subjects', but a full-text search of the extract finds exactly ONE prerequisite relationship in the entire 2,245-line document — ECOM90013 (Econometrics 3) as a concurrent prerequisite for ECOM90026 (Econometrics Research Report Part 1) — and both subjects are alternative Capstone options, not core subjects. One link is not a 'chain'. The other two PASS routes are also unsupported: the core is not all-compulsory (one of the six core slots is selected from ECOM90003/ECOM90004/ECON90033, and the Capstone slot is selected from ECOM90026/ECOM90013), and no accreditation is mentioned anywhere. PASS may still be defensible on the gate's primary criterion — a documented foundational-to-advanced core sequence (Data Wrangling/Basic Econometrics → Econometrics 2/Advanced Data Analysis/Forecasting → Capstone) — but that is not the ground the original rationale cited, so as justified it is not supported by the evidence quoted. Recorded as FAIL pending re-grounding in subject-sequence progression rather than the unsupported 'chain' claim.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** FAIL — DEMOTED on verify pass: two of the three original evidenceLines ('Diagnose model misspecification...', 'Implement, compare, and critique...') are Intended Learning Outcomes, not assessment-task descriptions, and G2's own anchor requires assessments to require defended trade-off decisions. Neither outcome is tied by the extract to a specifically named assessed task (ECOM90001's and ECOM90025's assessment tables list only generic report/quiz/exam titles, none naming misspecification diagnosis or framework critique as the graded task). The third citation (the Capstone-project sentence, tied to the graded 25%-weighted 'Capstone Project Submission' in ECOM90002/90004, built on self-collected real-world data) is genuine assessment evidence and on its own could support PASS via the gate's second disjunct (capstones with real uncertainty and accountability), but that narrower ground was not what the original rationale argued. Recorded as FAIL pending re-grounding solely in the Capstone-project evidence.

**G1 FAILS.** A disciplinary-foundation failure is reported regardless of the adaptiveness score: the adaptive capabilities are scored on top of a foundation this program is not documented to establish.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **cognate** (cognate program), n = 30, 15 destination titles, 6 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: Econometrics/economics discipline.

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (34 pages) and verified on 2026-08-29: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (5):

- C1: BUSA90520's group assessments state students 'should contribute equally' but document no explicit individual-accountability mechanism (e.g. peer assessment); scored at level 2 on the collaborative-practice clause alone, since level 3 is excluded regardless (no assessment documents coordinating work across people AND tools/AI systems).
- C3: 'computer software'/statistical-software content in core units is digital-tool literacy rather than explicitly AI-labelled content; scored at level 1 under the item's broader 'digital tools' construct and the Migration note capping tool-operation content at 1, rather than level 0.
- C4/C5: ECOM90002's assessment items are literally named 'Capstone Project Proposal/Progress/Submission' with self-formulated hypotheses and real-world data collection, which reads toward level 3 on both C4 and C5. Resolved to level 2 on both because the program's actual designated Capstone subjects (Year 2) are not present in this extract to confirm a defended/viva component (C5) or a documented structured progression to independent learning (C4) — per R3/scoring-direction, ambiguity resolved to the lower level.
- G1: verify pass demoted PASS to FAIL — the cited 'staged prerequisite chain' is a single link between two alternative Capstone options, not a chain across core subjects. A PASS may still be reachable via the foundational-to-advanced core-sequence criterion, but that requires a fresh rationale grounded in subject sequencing, not a re-score of the same evidence.
- G2: verify pass demoted PASS to FAIL — two of three evidence lines are outcome statements, not assessment-task descriptions, and are not tied to a named graded task. The remaining Capstone-project evidence line is genuine assessment evidence and may support PASS on narrower grounds (capstone-only), but that requires a fresh rationale, not a re-score of the same evidence.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (4):

- Program-level 'Attributes, outcomes and skills' page (mc-aecoenh/attributes-outcomes-skills) returned mismatched content — the text captured under that URL is an unrelated subject, Australian Architecture (ABPL90089), not this program's graduate attributes — so no program-level outcomes statement was available as evidence for any item.
- The subjects/ecom90004 and subjects/ecom90004/assessment URLs returned mismatched content (a duplicate Econometrics 2 overview and the Data Wrangling and Visualisation overview respectively, not Time Series Analysis and Forecasting), so ECOM90004 could not be used as evidence for any item.
- None of the program's actual required Capstone subjects (ECOM90026 Econometrics Research Report Part 1, ECOM90027 Part 2, ECOM90013 Econometrics 3) or the alternative Year-2 core options (ECOM90008 Microeconometrics, ECOM90011 Financial Econometrics, ECOM90012 Modelling the Australian Macroeconomy) have subject or assessment pages in this extract. This limits full confirmation of C4 and C5 level-3 evidence (a self-scoped capstone with documented progression, and methodology defended under scrutiny), since that is where such evidence would most plausibly appear.
- subjects/econ30024/assessment (Economics of Financial Markets, an elective) has no captured page, and subjects/econ90034/assessment returned mismatched content (Time Series Analysis and Forecasting assessment instead of Economics of Finance assessment); neither subject is core, so this does not affect any scored item.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
