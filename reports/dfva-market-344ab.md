## 2. RECENT JOB AD SIGNALS

**Signal 1 — Victorian and Commonwealth Public Service demand for strategic policy advisors.**
Departments (DPC, DTP, DEPW, APS Prime Minister & Cabinet) seek policy practitioners with strong briefing, cabinet submission, and regulatory reform capabilities.

**Signal 2 — Adoption of AI in policy research synthesis and automated drafting.**
Public sector agencies and consultancies deploy generative AI for background literature scanning and first-pass brief drafting, raising expectations for policy officers who can audit algorithmic summaries against primary administrative data and statutory constraints.

**Signal 3 — Algorithmic decision-making and public sector AI governance mandates.**
The Australian Digital Transformation Agency (DTA) and state integrity bodies mandate stringent AI governance frameworks for automated government service delivery, creating acute demand for policy analysts with digital regulation expertise.

**Signal 4 — Demand for rigorous quantitative policy evaluation and causal inference.**
Treasury and finance departments increasingly require microeconomic policy evaluation and data analytics to justify budget expenditures.

## 3. CURRENT DISCUSSION SIGNALS — MEDIUM CONFIDENCE

**What these sources are.** Institute of Public Administration Australia ([IPAA](https://www.ipaa.org.au/)), Australian Public Service Commission ([APSC](https://www.apsc.gov.au/)), Australia and New Zealand School of Government ([ANZSOG](https://anzsog.edu.au/)), and peer-reviewed public policy scholarship ([Australian Journal of Public Administration](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/14678500), [February 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/14678500)). Direct extraction from X or LinkedIn was **not** performed and no social media forum was sampled — where this section refers to discourse, it denotes published APS reform reports, ANZSOG executive monographs, and documented technological reviews by named bodies and authors. Dates are stated where available.

### Theme 1 — generative AI in public administration: evidence integrity and administrative law

Reports from [ANZSOG](https://anzsog.edu.au/) (November 2024) and *AJPA* (February 2025) emphasize that while LLMs accelerate policy brief drafting, uncritical reliance poses severe risks of statutory misinterpretation and automated bias under administrative law. Public managers must possess robust evaluative judgement to audit machine-generated advice against administrative law principles.

**Bearing:** C2, C3, and W2.

### Theme 2 — political navigation, co-design, and cross-sector stakeholder consensus

Discourse across [IPAA](https://www.ipaa.org.au/) and the [APSC](https://www.apsc.gov.au/) stresses that the most durable public policy capabilities are political acumen, collaborative inter-agency negotiation, and community co-design. These relational and deliberative capabilities are entirely resilient against automated analysis.

**Bearing:** C1, W1, and W3.

### Theme 3 — evidence-informed policy evaluation and defended reform inquiry

Monographs from [ANZSOG](https://anzsog.edu.au/) (2024/2025) underline the necessity for policy leaders to generate primary empirical evidence and defend policy proposals under live ministerial or parliamentary scrutiny.

**Bearing:** C5, C4, and G1.

## 4. SKILL SHIFT SUMMARY

### Declining Demand
- Routine descriptive literature summaries without strategic synthesis
- Passive administrative record collation without policy framing
- Unchecked reliance on template policy briefing notes

### Rising Demand
- Evaluative verification and governance of AI-assisted policy drafts
- Multi-agency collaborative negotiation and genuine community co-design
- Authentic cabinet submission drafting and live ministerial briefing under time pressure
- Defended program evaluation methodologies and econometric impact analysis
