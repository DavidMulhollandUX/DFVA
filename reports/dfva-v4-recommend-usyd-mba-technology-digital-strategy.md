## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the preceding scored evidence and market data; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

This MBA (Technology and Digital Strategy) sits on an estimated field-of-education exposure basis of 88.71 because it has no destination record of its own. Its curriculum instrument mirrors that gap in documentation rather than a gap in substance: G1 passes on a staged, all-compulsory DMBA core with a documented co-requisite chain, but G2 fails because no core unit page documents an assessment task at all, leaving every defended-decision claim unevidenced. That same absence of published assessment detail holds five items (C1, C2, C5, W2, W3) at the instrument floor and the remaining three (C3, C4, W1) at level 1 on outcome language alone. Market evidence names concrete, dated demand this program is positioned to meet: an APESB duty to verify automated ledger anomaly-detection output (Theme 1), an ASRS-driven shortage of assurance-methodology graduates (Theme 2), a field whose own entry destinations concentrate in the clerical and retail occupations a 2025 synthesis names as the clearest automation-exposed group (Theme 4), and a Privacy Act obligation that follows graduates into every APP-bound employer (Theme 3). Closing any of these gaps needs assessed evidence to reach the handbook — an edited outcome statement moves a level-1 anchor that is itself outcome-defined, but nothing beyond that.
