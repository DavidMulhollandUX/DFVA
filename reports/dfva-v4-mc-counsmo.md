## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — The program documents an all-compulsory specialist core ('125 credit points of Compulsory subjects') with a staged prerequisite chain: the Residential subject 'provides preparation for placements and needs to be completed before Placement A', and Placement B is gated ('Students must have completed the Counselling Placement A to be eligible for subject') — a coherent, progressively deepening counselling-specific sequence from theory/skills/ethics/assessment through residential, dual placement, and capstone.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — Core assessment repeatedly requires defended, scenario-based decisions under pass/fail hurdles ('Case study/Scenario based oral assessment' with 'Hurdle requirement: Students must achieve a Pass for this hurdle'), and the required placement sequence places students in live, uncertain practice with real clients and real-time simulation ('Practical experience... extending their development of counselling skills with real-world experience and the varied clients they will work within professional practice'; 'Simulated 15 minute counselling session during residential experience week').

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **field** (field grain), n = 0, 11 destination titles, 5 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: field-of-education occupation list (ATO-linked), not this program's own graduates

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (31 pages) and verified on 2026-08-29: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (7):

- C1: WELF90018's 'Written peer evaluation' (30%) and Placement's descriptive 'liaising with other professional practitioners' could suggest Level 2 collaborative/interprofessional practice; resolved to Level 1 — peer evaluation is homed in C2 (appraisal of quality) per the one-construct-one-home rule, and the placement liaison language is not a quoted assessed criterion.
- C2: WELF90018's six-weekly reflective journal and placement's supervisor-guided reflection gesture toward Level 3's 'evidence strategy adjustment over time', but neither is quoted as a graded, criterion-referenced justification of reliance/strategy decisions; resolved to Level 2.
- C4: The Practice Innovation capstone's suggested project list ('client-facing tool, training module, policy submission, service enhancement plan, business case') is somewhat bounded rather than fully open-scoped; the Level 3 score rests primarily on the independently unambiguous WELF90023 professional-development-plan evidence. Verification demoted this item to Level 2 — see rationale.
- C5: The Research Capstone pathway shows Level-3-caliber content (methodology defended via a hurdle-linked oral defence), but the capstone is 'comprised of a practice innovation or research capstone pathway' — an OR choice — and the Level 3 anchor explicitly excludes 'one route among several'; resolved to Level 2. Verification demoted this item to Level 1 — see rationale.
- W1/W3: Both draw on the Counselling Placement A/B sequence. W1 is scored from the audience/practitioner-judgement and professional-conduct clauses (supervisor observation, police-check compliance); W3 is scored from the duration/structure/accountability clauses (210 required hours, prerequisite sequencing, structured reflection) — distinct constructs per instrument design, not double-counted from the same evidence.
- W1/W2: Both draw on placement text. W1 uses the audience/practitioner-judgement clauses; W2 uses the task-content and constraint-of-practice clauses (real clients, fixed weekly structure) — kept to separate homes. Verification demoted W1 to Level 2 — see rationale.
- Verification pass 2026-08-30: C4, C5 and W1 were demoted from the submitted scores on adversarial review; see each item's rationale for the specific reasoning.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
