## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **field** (field grain), n = 0, 11 destination titles, 1 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: field-of-education occupation list (ATO-linked), not this program's own graduates

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (36 pages) and verified on 2026-08-31: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (5):

- C1: team-graded assessment (INFO90002 database-design assignment; ISYS90069 group project) exists only in elective subjects, not the compulsory core, so scored at Level 1 (outcomes-only) rather than Level 2 per the 'core unit' requirement.
- C2: the graduate-attribute phrase about being 'critical and creative thinkers, with an aptitude for continued self-directed learning' could be read as reflection/self-assessment language (Level 1), but is generic boilerplate not tied to an assessed appraisal task; resolved to Level 0, the lower level.
- C4/C5: the compulsory capstone Research Project (Options 1 and 2) plausibly satisfies the Level 3 anchors for both items (self-scoped required project; primary evidence with defended methodology), but no dedicated overview/assessment page for POPH90288/POPH90289/POPH90149 was captured in the extract. On adversarial review this evidence gap was found insufficient even for the Level 2 anchor claimed in the first-pass score, so both items were demoted to Level 1, matching R2's requirement for quoted assessment evidence and the identical gap already resolved downward on W2.
- W2: the same capstone Research Project could satisfy the Level 2 'professional task end to end' anchor, but lacking assessment-task detail in the extract, W2 was resolved to Level 1 based on the health-context framing of the compulsory coursework subjects instead.
- G2: PASS relies partly on the compulsory capstone Research Project as a project with real uncertainty and accountability, but as with C4/C5/W2 its specific assessment tasks are not documented in this extract; the statistical-inference interpretation content in Probability & Inference in Biostatistics was treated as sufficient independent support for PASS.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (2):

- MAST90099 (Advanced Regression) is compulsory but has no subject overview/ILO/assessment page in this extract — only its title and credit points appear in the course-structure table — so its specific contribution to any item could not be verified.
- The compulsory capstone subjects (POPH90288 Biostatistics Research Project Part 1, POPH90289 Part 2, POPH90149 Biostatistics Research Project - S) have no dedicated overview/ILO/assessment page in this extract — only titles, study periods and credit points in the course-structure table — so C4/C5 (now scored at Level 1), W2, and G2's capstone-based PASS support could not be fully confirmed against quoted assessment evidence.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
