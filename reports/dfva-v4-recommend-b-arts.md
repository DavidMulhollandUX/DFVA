## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the scored evidence and market data above; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

The Bachelor of Arts (B-ARTS) is the University of Melbourne's flagship 300-point undergraduate liberal arts degree encompassing over thirty disciplines across the humanities, social sciences, and languages. The program passes Gate G2 on contested essay argumentation and take-home exams weighing competing sociological and philosophical claims, but fails Gate G1 due to the absence of an all-compulsory specialist core, a 125-point cap on any single discipline, 50 points of compulsory Breadth, and thirty substitutable major options without a shared methodological spine.

Operating in a highly exposed, text-dense knowledge domain (**89.37**, near the top of the portfolio), the program faces substantial automation exposure while exhibiting baseline adaptive capabilities and minimal assessed workplace practice (**Adaptiveness: 5 / 15**, **Workplace Practice: 2 / 9**). Core assessment contains zero assessed spoken genres or presentations across the compulsory Discovery pool (**W1: 0 / 3**). Furthermore, collaborative practice is unassessed in core subjects (**C1: 1 / 3**), peer appraisal occurs only in selected menu options (**C2: 1 / 3**), AI literacy is limited to library searches (**C3: 1 / 3**), essays apply knowledge to taught material without novel transfer (**C4: 1 / 3**), empirical inquiry is elective (**C5: 1 / 3**), case studies use academic essays (**W2: 1 / 3**), and internships sit as optional electives (**W3: 1 / 3**).
