## 2. RECENT JOB AD SIGNALS

**Signal 1 — Rapid expansion of MBS-funded genomic testing driving clinical demand.**
Public hospital Familial Cancer Clinics (Peter MacCallum Cancer Centre, Royal Melbourne Hospital, Alfred Health) and state clinical genetics services experience surges in hereditary cancer and rare disease referrals following Medicare item additions.

**Signal 2 — Normalisation of hybrid and telehealth genetic counselling.**
Hospital networks and regional genetics services mandate telehealth competency to service rural and remote populations across Australia and New Zealand.

**Signal 3 — Scale-up of national reproductive carrier screening.**
The implementation of national reproductive carrier screening programs creates sustained demand for genetic counsellors in prenatal clinics and IVF networks.

**Signal 4 — Statutory licensure and interpersonal counselling moat.**
Human Genetics Society of Australasia (HGSA) certification and empathetic psychosocial communication insulate clinical genetic counselling from AI automation, even as algorithmic tools automate first-pass variant filtering.

## 3. CURRENT DISCUSSION SIGNALS — MEDIUM CONFIDENCE

**What these sources are.** Human Genetics Society of Australasia ([HGSA](https://www.hgsa.org.au/)), Board of Censors in Genetic Counselling ([HGSA Board of Censors](https://www.hgsa.org.au/genetic-counselling)), Australian Genomics Health Alliance ([Australian Genomics](https://www.australiangenomics.org.au/)), and peer-reviewed literature ([Journal of Genetic Counseling](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/15733599), [May 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/15733599)). Direct extraction from X or LinkedIn was **not** performed and no social media forum was sampled — where this section refers to discourse, it denotes published HGSA Professional Competency Standards for Genetic Counsellors, Board of Censors Part 1/2 certification guidelines, Australian Genomics clinical translation reports, and peer-reviewed studies by named bodies and authors. Dates are stated where available.

### Theme 1 — algorithmic variant prioritization, VUS classification, and clinical accountability

Guidelines from the [HGSA](https://www.hgsa.org.au/) (December 2024) and the *Journal of Genetic Counseling* (May 2025) underline that while AI-assisted bioinformatics pipelines (Fabric Genomics, Franklin by Genoox) accelerate variant annotation, variants of uncertain significance (VUS) and secondary findings require non-delegable clinical interpretation. Genetic counsellors carry professional accountability for translating complex genomic uncertainties into actionable, empathetic patient communication.

**Bearing:** C2, C3, and W2.

### Theme 2 — multidisciplinary team integration and cross-cultural communication

Position papers from [Australian Genomics](https://www.australiangenomics.org.au/) emphasize that clinical genetic counsellors act as pivotal communicators within multidisciplinary teams (clinical geneticists, oncologists, pathologists, bioinformaticians), requiring advanced collaborative protocols and culturally safe counselling practices.

**Bearing:** C1, W1, and W3.

### Theme 3 — self-directed empirical clinical research and methodology defence

Accreditation reviews from the [HGSA Board of Censors](https://www.hgsa.org.au/genetic-counselling) highlight that candidate counsellors must design and execute original empirical research, submitting formal monographs and defending methodology under viva scrutiny.

**Bearing:** C4, C5, and G1.
