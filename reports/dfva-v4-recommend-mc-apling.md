## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the scored evidence and market data above; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

The Master of Applied Linguistics (MC-APLING) carries a low adaptive score (6 / 15) and a low workplace score (3 / 9) against a field-estimated AI exposure of 88.72, above the field-basis median of 83.21. Gate G2 passes on the capstone's design-and-defend structure (Minor Thesis or LING90041 team project), but Gate G1 fails: the compulsory core is only two subjects, and 75–90% of the 100–200 point program is drawn from a shared elective pool that differs across the English Language, TESOL and Language Testing specialisations only in which single subject is compulsory. Every documented core-assessed activity — collaboration, the Reflective paper, and the "real-life project" framing — sits inside a single unit, LING90041 Projects in Applied Linguistics, one of two alternative capstone streams; nothing recurs across the program, and the alternative Minor Thesis stream carries none of it.

C2 and C3 both score 0/3: no core assessment appraises the quality of work (self, peer, or a tool's), and no core unit or program-level outcome names AI or digital content anywhere. W3 also scores 0/3: neither capstone route places students in a real workplace or professional-community setting with practitioner supervision. Against these gaps, the market report finds institutional GenAI adoption in postsecondary administration rising sharply (§3 Theme 1), "AI" as the single most common skill keyword in the program's own academic-administration destination job ads (§2 Signal 4), and all four destination job families — curriculum coordination, ESL/adult-literacy teaching, postsecondary language teaching, and postsecondary/ELICOS academic administration — workplace-embedded professional roles rather than research positions (§1). No market evidence in the report ties directly to C5 (inquiry & evidence generation) for any of the four destination families; that item's gap is carried below for completeness, with no intervention proposed against it, in the same manner the market report itself treats G1 as a structural rather than market-driven finding (§5 CI-5).
