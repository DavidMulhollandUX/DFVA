## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — PASS condition: a coherent specialist core with progressive technical or methodological depth. Twenty-four units of core courses are all compulsory, spanning computer science foundations, mathematical foundations, statistics, data taming and cyber security, and are followed by a compulsory 12-unit research project. The foundations sequence is staged A then B, and the mathematical foundations unit is documented as providing the basis for further study in data science, statistical machine learning and cybersecurity - a methodological chain rather than interchangeable generic content.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — PASS condition: assessments require defended trade-off decisions, or capstones with real uncertainty and accountability. The compulsory research project is an empirical investigation the student plans and conducts, with a Proposal and a weighted Oral Defence at both stages - a capstone whose methodology is defended under scrutiny rather than recalled. The core ethics unit adds a Report with Oral Defence against an outcome requiring recommended strategies, policies or resolutions for contested ethical and legal challenges, which is a defended trade-off rather than a scripted response.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **field** (field grain), n = 0, 12 destination titles, 4 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: field-of-education occupation list (ATO-linked), not this program's own graduates

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (33 pages) and verified on 2026-09-07: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (8):

- C1 straddled levels 1 and 2: the core Project Management Fundamentals unit states a team-skills outcome, but none of its named assessment tasks (Oral Defence, Critical Analysis, Case Study) is documented as group work with individual accountability. Resolved DOWN to 1 by the lower-level rule and R2.
- C2 straddled levels 1 and 2: 'Critical Analysis' is a named, weighted core assessment task and matches the level-2 exemplar 'structured critique', but the extract does not document the criteria or exemplars it is judged against. Scored 2 on the strength of the named task plus the research project's assessed appraisal of literature; recorded here because a stricter reading of 'criterion-referenced' would give 1.
- C3 straddled levels 2 and 3: the assessed core ethics unit covers privacy, data protection and surveillance, which is data governance, but the critique is framed around information technology generally rather than AI systems in the discipline's own context, and no assessment task names bias, accountability, transparency or regulation. Resolved DOWN to 2.
- W1 straddled levels 1 and 2: 'Professional Simulation: 20%%' is a core assessment task whose name suggests a professional genre, but the extract documents neither the artefact nor criteria drawn from professional practice, and no audience beyond the teaching team appears anywhere. Resolved DOWN to 1 by R4 and the lower-level rule.
- W2 straddled levels 1 and 2 on the same 'Professional Simulation' task, for the same reason. Resolved DOWN to 1.
- One-construct-one-home: the STEM Internship evidence was scored only in W3, never in C1 or C4; the Oral Defence tasks were scored for defended methodology in C5 and for audience in W1, with the C4 claim resting on the Proposal and independent-working evidence rather than the defence.
- Capture-fidelity ambiguity: the core course listed in the program rules as 'COMP SCI 7212 / Human and Ethical Factors in Computer Science' is represented in the extract by a page titled 'Ethical and Legal Challenges in Technology (COMP2022)', and the page captured for 'Concepts in Cyber Security (COMP5067)' carries an overview and outcomes about machine learning in organisations rather than cyber security. Both were scored as they stand in the extract, which is the only permitted source, but every score resting on them is exposed to that mismatch.
- The line 'A research project to the value of 12 units' contains a non-breaking space and was therefore not quotable byte-for-byte; the equivalent requirement sentence 'To satisfy the requirements for Research Project students must complete courses to the value of 12 units.' is quoted instead.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (4):

- Assessment evidence is absent for five of the eight core courses: Foundations of Computer Science A, Foundations of Computer Science B, Concepts in Cyber Security, Mathematical Foundations of Data Science and Data Taming each end with an empty 'Assessment Tasks:' line. Every item's ceiling here is set by the three core units that do publish assessment (the ethics unit, Project Management Fundamentals and the two research-project stages).
- The core course 'STATS 7022 / Data Science PG' appears in the program rules but no subject page for it was captured, so none of its assessment could be scored.
- Mathematical Foundations of Data Science publishes an empty 'Learning Outcomes:' section as well as an empty assessment section, so it contributed no item evidence beyond G1.
- No workload or hurdle information is usable: every captured subject page reports 'Workload: N/A' and every assessment task reports 'Hurdle: False', so no hurdle-requirement evidence was available to any item.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
