## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — PASS condition: a coherent specialist core with progressive technical depth — an accredited sequence and an all-compulsory specialist core. Every major lists an all-compulsory course set that runs level I to level III within the discipline (introductory accounting through corporate accounting and auditing; business finance through portfolio theory and topics in corporate finance), and the program is built for professional accreditation with CPA Australia, CA ANZ, the CFA Institute and the AMI. This is not generic or interchangeable content.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — FAIL condition is recall or scripted responses only, and that does not hold: the documented core assessment is critical analysis, case studies and problem-solving tasks, and the core statistics course requires students to weigh statistical decisions against biases, errors and the consequences of inappropriate methods — inference from a sample is decision-making under uncertainty. A capstone is a qualification requirement for every major, with live-project and internship options. The evidence sits close to the bar because no assessment task descriptor in the extract names a defended trade-off, so the pass rests on the ECON1012 outcome and the required capstone rather than on a documented trade-off task.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **field** (field grain), n = 0, 13 destination titles, 1 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: field-of-education occupation list (ATO-linked), not this program's own graduates

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (3 pages) and verified on 2026-09-07: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (7):

- C2 straddled 1 and 2: the ECON1008 outcome 'Evaluate and present a national economic issue or a personal financial issue based on a predetermined set of criteria' reads as criterion-referenced appraisal, but it appraises an issue rather than the quality of work and is an outcome, not a listed assessment task. Resolved DOWN to 1 by the lower-level rule and R2.
- C4 straddled 1 and 2: ECON1012 lists two 'Case Study' assessments, which could be read as the anchor's 'case variation', but the extract never documents the cases as novel or unfamiliar. Resolved DOWN to 1 by the lower-level rule.
- C5 straddled 1 and 2: ECON1012 outcomes cover selecting suitable statistical techniques and data collection is named in the course overview, but no assessment task is documented as a student-designed inquiry. Resolved DOWN to 1 by the lower-level rule.
- W1 straddled 0 and 1: communication is in program outcome 5 and 'present' appears in an ECON1008 outcome, satisfying the first clause of level 1, but no listed assessment task is a presentation, so the second clause fails. Resolved DOWN to 0 by the lower-level rule and R2.
- W3 straddled 1 and 2: the capstone block is compulsory and contains two internship courses, so a student may complete a workplace placement inside the core, but the internships are alternatives to eChallenge and Tech eChallenge rather than required. Resolved DOWN to 1 by the lower-level rule.
- W2 versus W3: the internship capstone options could be read as authentic task design, but per 'one construct, one home' workplace immersion is scored in W3 only.
- The extract's ECON1008 course page is titled 'Introductory Economics and Personal Finance (ECON1008)' while the program rules list ECON 1008 as 'Data Analytics I', and the ECON1012 page is titled 'Data Analytics' while the rules list ECON 1012 as 'Principles of Economics I'. The two course pages were still treated as core-course evidence because both codes appear in the compulsory course lists.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (1):

- The extract contains only two core course pages (ECON1008 and ECON1012). No page is present for ENTREP 1011, any ACCTING, CORPFIN, COMMLAW or MARKETNG course, or any capstone option (ABLEINT 3001, BUSINESS 3002, ENTREP 3900, ENTREP 3901, MARKETNG 3004), so level-2 and level-3 assessment evidence in the disciplinary core and the capstone could not be checked for C1, C2, C4, C5, W1, W2 or W3. Every score above rests on the program rules page plus those two course pages; a fuller capture could raise C4, W1, W2 or W3.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
