## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — PASS condition: the program documents a coherent specialist core with progressive technical and methodological depth across all three routes. First, 175 of the 200 credit points (87.5%) are in an all-compulsory specialist industrial engineering core. Second, the program is professionally accredited by Engineers Australia. Third, the core follows a staged prerequisite and design spine from first-year foundational manufacturing, design, simulation, and probability subjects (MCEN90054, MCEN90055, MCEN90058, MCEN90053, MCEN90059, ENGR90051) through advanced digital transformation and lifecycle engineering (MCEN90056, MCEN90060, MAST90014) to the compulsory 25-point Engineering Capstone Project Parts 1 & 2 (ENGR90037/ENGR90038). Content is thoroughly disciplinary and not generic.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — PASS condition: assessments require defended trade-off decisions, open-ended problem solving, and capstone projects with real uncertainty and accountability. In ENGR90037/ENGR90038, students undertake a substantial year-long project addressing complex client or explorative research challenges, defended via a 30% hurdle oral examination, 30% hurdle final report, and continuous assessment. In MCEN90054, students synthesize solutions to open-ended and vaguely defined design tasks, balancing customer requirements, manufacturing feasibility, cost, and environmental concerns, defended in two hurdled VIVA presentations. In ENGR90051, students navigate trade-offs among stakeholders, sustainability, safety, and technical constraints. In MCEN90060, students evaluate triple bottom line trade-offs through LCA and eco-design reports and oral exams. This far exceeds recall or scripted responses.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **partial** (related program), n = 113, 26 destination titles, 15 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: dfva_jir_map: mechanical engineering / engineering management family.

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (35 pages) and verified on 2026-08-23: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (9):

- C1 straddled levels 2 and 3: collaborative practice is assessed across nearly all core units (ENGR90051, ENGR90037/8, MCEN90054, MCEN90053, MCEN90056, MCEN90057, MCEN90058, MAST90014, MGMT90026, ENGM90011), satisfying recurrence, but level 3 additionally requires documented role and tool allocation or assessed division of work between people and AI/systems. Because no assessment description explicitly assesses human-system work division, resolved DOWN to 2 per the never-resolve-upward rule.
- C2 straddled levels 1 and 2: ENGR90051 includes an explicit 15% 'Peer feedback + evaluation (individual)' assessment alongside longitudinal project logs and reflection, and the degree features an e-portfolio system against accreditation standards, satisfying level 2's criterion-referenced appraisal/peer review anchor. Level 3 was considered due to the longitudinal project logs, but no documented tool reliance/override decisions or defence of AI-output quality are present, so the score remains at 2.
- C3 straddled levels 0 and 1: digital systems, automation, IIoT, discrete event simulation, and digital transformation are heavily featured in the core (MCEN90056, MCEN90057, MCEN90053, MAST90014), but artificial intelligence capabilities/limitations/ethics are not assessed in core units, capping C3 at 1.
- C4 straddled levels 2 and 3: the program documents a strong design progression through ENGR90051, MCEN90054 and the year-long ENGR90037/8 capstone. Level 3 requires a self-scoped capstone/research project or assessed identification of one's own knowledge gaps with a plan to close them. In ENGR90037, capstones are based on approved topics, industrial client tasks, or academic research adjuncts rather than student self-scoped questions, and the e-portfolio is an attainment record rather than an assessed gap-closing plan. Resolved DOWN to 2.
- C5 straddled levels 2 and 3: the 25-point capstone ENGR90037/8 is compulsory and defended via an individual oral examination hurdle (30%), oral assessment (10%), and lay-audience presentation (10%), alongside hurdled VIVA presentations in MCEN90054. However, because ENGR90037 accommodates an industrial client design route ('synthesis of practical solutions to complex technical problems') alongside explorative research, primary empirical evidence generation is not strictly mandatory for all project routes. Resolved DOWN to 2 per the scoring-direction rule.
- W1 straddled levels 2 and 3: ENGR90037 assesses a non-technical presentation to a lay audience (10%) and professional engineering practice assignments (20%), and MCEN90060 assesses a poster submission (10%), satisfying level 2. Level 3 was not awarded because delivery to or judgement by a real external practitioner panel with explicit assessed professional conduct criteria is not documented for all students.
- W2 straddled levels 2 and 3: practitioner tasks form the core assessment spine across Year 1 and Year 2 (ENGR90051, MCEN90054, MCEN90053, MCEN90060, ENGR90037/8) with genuine constraints of practice (open-ended vaguely defined tasks, physical prototyping/testing, and external industrial client briefs). Both limbs of level 3 are satisfied on documented task features.
- W3: work-situated learning is available through elective ENGR90033 or uncredited/STEP pathways to satisfy the Engineering Practice Hurdle Requirement, but no workplace placement is a compulsory core unit, placing the item squarely at level 1.
- One-construct-one-home allocations: ENGR90051 peer feedback and evaluation was scored under C2 (evaluative judgement) rather than W2; ENGR90037 lay-audience presentation was scored under W1 (audience/genre) and oral examination under C5 (methodological defence/scrutiny), not double counted; MCEN90054 VIVA presentations were scored under C5 (defence under scrutiny) with task fidelity scored in W2.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
