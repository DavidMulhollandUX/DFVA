## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the preceding scored evidence and market data; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

The Bachelor of Fine Arts (Production) (B-FAPRO) is a 300-credit-point specialist production degree in the Faculty of Fine Arts and Music, of which 237.5 credit points are compulsory production subjects chained by level-1 to level-2 to level-3 prerequisites. Its strength is where the work is real: the core Production Practice 1 to 3 sequence places every student in design, technical and management roles on live VCA productions, closes with a required 120-hour industry internship supervised by a host manager or mentor and reports back to the placement coordinator through the workplace supervisor (W3 = 3/3). Both gates pass on that same spine — G1 on the compulsory core, G2 on the senior-role live-production and negotiated-independent-project routes of the Production Practice 3 capstone. The destination field is exposed at **72.81** on the AI Occupational Exposure (AIOE) index, a field basis drawn from the Jobs and Skills Australia Higher Education Outcomes (JSA HEO) occupation list for Drama and Theatre Studies rather than from this program's own graduates, and sits at or below the field median of 83.21.

Headroom is wide and, unusually, concentrated in what is documented rather than what is done. Adaptiveness is **6/15** because four of the five adaptive items sit at level 1. C1 was demoted on verification: the paired presentation in Artefact and Performance 2 documents two students presenting together, not that collaboration is a judged criterion. C2 rests on a graded Reflective essay in Production Practice 1 that articulates insights from experience but is not framed as appraisal against stated criteria or exemplars. C3 has computer-aided drafting and other digital representation techniques in Production Studio 1 but no unit that addresses AI capabilities, limitations or governance — the terms do not appear in the extract. C5 has academic literacy and a library research skills hurdle in Artefact and Performance 1, and no inquiry with methodology selection or data collection. On the workplace sub-scale (**6/9**), W2 was demoted from 3 to 1 because every original evidence line described the VCA-production placements, which the instrument houses in W3; the Production Studio units' practical projects are the remaining candidate W2 evidence and the extract does not describe their artefact type or judging criteria. W1 sits at 2 because the professional-genre and external-judgement evidence (curriculum vitae and artist biography, the workplace supervisor's hurdle and report) lives entirely in the final-year internship subject rather than progressively across the program.

The market report's curriculum implication CI-3, which reads W3 as 1/3 and proposes an external placement, and its C2 and W2 figures predate the verified scoring; W3 is at maximum and no intervention is proposed for it. Where the market report carries no direct signal for an item (C5, C4, W1) the table says so rather than borrowing one.
