## 1. POSITION — Basis: measured × scored

**Destination AI Exposure: 91.73.** Destination titles are crosswalked to the published Felten AIOE index and rescaled 0–100 by the Panel A procedure used for every other program; 9 of 15 titles carry medium mapping confidence. Basis: measured on the parent program's alumni record — Master of Business Administration (n = 422, 15 titles). The portfolio median is 90.9, so this program sits **above** it.

**Curriculum Adaptiveness: 8 / 15.** Scored on Panel C v4.2 sub-scale A [[1]](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/guides-resources/resources/corporate-publications/assuring-quality-learning-gen-ai-integrated-future-role-adaptive-capabilities) from 2026 handbook evidence: anchors are declarative statements about documented curriculum evidence, level 3 requires assessment evidence, and ambiguous evidence resolves to the lower level.

**Workplace Practice: 6 / 9.** Scored on sub-scale W [[19]](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2021L00488) in the same pass, from the same extract. The position axis remains adaptiveness alone; the workplace sub-score is reported beside it, not folded into the axis, and the two are never added together.

**Position: High exposure · low adaptiveness.** Assigned against the v4 medians (exposure 90.9, adaptiveness 9), computed from all 34 reference-cohort programs re-scored on this instrument. This is the deterministic median rule the site applies, not a probabilistic stability class.

## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — The program documents a coherent, all-compulsory specialist core with staged progression: 150 credit points of compulsory general management core plus 37.5 credit points of compulsory marketing core, sequenced General Management 1 (September) → 2 (January) → 3 (Summer Term) and terminating in named capstone subjects for each award. Content is disciplinary rather than interchangeable — accounting, finance, economics, strategy, consumer behaviour, marketing research, marketing strategy — with the later subjects explicitly integrating the earlier ones.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — The compulsory capstone Business in Complex Environments is a firm-management simulation in which students must respond to information arriving during play, revise operating plans, diagnose the results of their own decisions and defend them — decision-making under genuine uncertainty with accountability, assessed including a hurdle individual competition. The Consulting Practicum adds a live client engagement with negotiated terms. This is well beyond recall or scripted response.

**What changed in this revision.** §3 of the market report was re-sourced on 2026-08-25 and this
section rewritten from it. Two claims were corrected: the AI-governance question was previously
framed as a professional-identity debate among marketers attributed to LinkedIn and AMI community
discussion, and now rests on the AICD/HTI and Allens/AICD/MBS instruments that place the duty on
the board; and Privacy Act reform was previously described as enacted and complete across
2024–2025, where the automated-decision obligation starts on 10 December 2026. One claim was
removed for want of any checkable source — that B2B marketing is an underserved career path for
MBA graduates in Australia. Two quantified job-ad figures that the previous version of this
section carried, a 34% year-on-year rise in performance-marketing roles requiring AI proficiency

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **variant** (measured · parent record), n = 422, 15 destination titles, 9 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; crosswalk `data/aioe/v31_extension_crosswalk.csv`. Basis note: variant of "Master of Business Administration"

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (35 pages) and verified on 2026-08-23: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (8):

- W1 (closest call): evidence straddled 2 and 3. Two of level 3's three conjuncts hold — repeated, progressive professional-communication assessment via PEP 1/2/3 syndicate presentations, and delivery to a real external client in the Consulting Practicum. The third conjunct, professional conduct or accountability explicitly among the assessed criteria, is not quotable: 'Contribution to syndicate' and the attendance hurdles are stated as requirements without criteria. Resolved to 2 by the lower-level rule and R4 (no credit for professional framing absent documented task/criteria features).
- C2: straddled 1 and 2. 'peer and instructor evaluation of contribution to class learning' is peer judgement inside core assessment, but it appraises participation rather than the quality of work and no criteria, standards or exemplars are documented. Resolved to 1. Separately, the genuinely criterion-relevant item ('In-class experiential activities, lecturer, peer and self-assessed', Leadership and Change) sits in a marketing ELECTIVE and therefore cannot satisfy a core-assessment anchor.
- C4: straddled 2 and 3. The compulsory Marketing Research field project starts from student 'problem definition', which reads toward a self-scoped research project, but both capstones are externally scoped (simulation exercise; client brief) and no assessment documents a plan to close self-identified knowledge gaps. Resolved to 2.
- C5: straddled 2 and 3. A required core subject does generate primary evidence (survey design and implementation, qualitative and quantitative techniques), but level 3's defence-under-scrutiny requirement has no quotable support — the 'final report and presentation' appears in the subject description, and the assessment table lists only a syndicate project and a hurdle examination. Resolved to 2.
- C1: recurrence of assessed collaborative work is overwhelming and would satisfy the first half of level 3, but the second half (an assessment coordinating work across people AND tools/AI systems, with role and tool allocation documented) has no supporting line. Resolved to 2.
- W2: the level-3 'genuine constraint of practice' clause is satisfied (externally supplied client brief) but the 'assessment spine' clause is not, since the 150-credit-point general management core is assessed mainly by hurdle examinations, quizzes and class contribution. A conjunction with one half unmet resolves to 2.
- Boundary handling: only compulsory subjects (General Management 1–3, Business in Complex Environments, Social Entrepreneur Consulting Practicum, Consumer Behaviour, Marketing Research, Marketing Strategy) were treated as core. Brand Management, Leadership and Change, Marketing Communications, Negotiations, Product Management, Digital Product Management and the MBA electives were excluded from all level-2/3 core-assessment anchors. Business in Asia is listed among core subjects but marked 'Not available in 2026', so no anchor was rested on it.
- Cross-item placement: the client-facing Consulting Practicum evidence is used for its communication genres in W1, its task fidelity in W2 and its workplace membership in W3, each on the distinct construct the item names; the CompXm/Business in Complex Environments simulation is scored in W2 and G2 and deliberately kept out of W3, which excludes simulation.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
