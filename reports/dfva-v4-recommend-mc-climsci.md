## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the preceding scored evidence and market data; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

MC-CLIMSCI sits at the v4 cohort median on adaptive capabilities (9/15) with a low workplace sub-score (3/9), and one structural feature explains most of both: the degree ends in a 25-point project that a student takes as **either** ATOC90019 (an on-campus academic research project) **or** ATOC90018 (a Climate Science Industry Project with an industry partner charter). Two items turn on that single fork. C5 cannot reach 3 because primary-evidence generation is not unconditionally required of everyone; W3 cannot reach 2 because a student can complete the degree without ever entering a workplace. Neither is a gap in provision — the program *has* both the research training and the industry placement — it is a gap in what is guaranteed.

That has a consequence worth stating plainly, because it is the one real trade-off in this plan. Requiring the research route lifts C5 to 3 and leaves W3 at 1. Requiring the industry route lifts W3 to 2 and, unless the industry project is also specified to generate its own data, may leave C5 where it is. **P5 is written to avoid the trade rather than take a side**: specify primary evidence in both routes, then make workplace immersion the default. A program that instead picks one route should expect one of the two items to stay put.

The rest of the plan is cheaper than that. Core ATOC90002 Climate Science for Decision-Making already sits exactly at the science–policy interface the market is hiring for, and assesses nothing in a non-academic genre; ATOC90004 already runs a group presentation and cohort paper discussion without published appraisal criteria. P1, P2 and P3 are all single-task changes inside subjects that already run, and they move three of the five two-level gaps.
