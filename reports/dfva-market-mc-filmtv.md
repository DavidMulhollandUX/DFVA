## 5. CURRICULUM IMPLICATIONS

| # | Implication | Dimension | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| CI-1 | Theme 2 (§3) names clerical and secretarial work as the clearest automation-exposed group and identifies routine, rules-based office tasks as the most readily automated; §4 accordingly marks administration, clerical, data entry and office administration as falling or falling sharply. Graduates who take entry-level administrative or production-coordination work face automation pressure on exactly the tasks recurring in the job-advertisement sample (§2, Signal 1, 122 postings). | C4 | Option: add a module distinguishing durable, judgement-based work from the falling clerical-task skills named in §4, so students can name what to build past entry-level administrative work (cost: syllabus content development, no new contact hours). |
| CI-2 | Theme 1 (§3) establishes that anyone handling personal or commercial information as an employee of a Privacy Act 1988 entity is bound by the Australian Privacy Principles, and that the Fair Work Act 2009 requires seven-year employee-record retention; this regulatory context applies to any graduate who takes on the administrative or clerical work recurring in the job-advertisement sample (§2, Signal 1). | W1 | Option: reference the Privacy Act 1988 and Fair Work Act 2009 record-handling obligation named in Theme 1 (§3) in a professional-practice unit's conduct content (cost: minor syllabus update, no new contact hours). |
| CI-3 | §4 marks data entry as falling sharply, the direction most strongly supported by Theme 2's (§3) description of routine, rules-based office tasks as most readily automated; the job-advertisement sample (§2, Signal 1, 122 postings) still recurs on this keyword today, so the fall is a forward exposure signal, not a current-vacancy one. | C3 | Option: make the distinction between current administrative-task demand and forward automation exposure explicit in any unit that teaches office or production-coordination software, rather than teaching current tool use as a stable skill (cost: instructor time to update unit framing, no new contact hours). |
| CI-4 | §4's pattern — four of five recurring keywords (administration, clerical, data entry, office administration) marked falling or falling sharply, with only reception stable — indicates the administrative skillset in the job-advertisement sample (§2, Signal 1) is broadly contracting, not just individual tasks, making entry-level administrative work a weaker basis for authentic assessed tasks than a stable occupation would be. | W2 | Option: if any authentic or work-integrated assessment currently uses general office-administration tasks as its authentic context, review it against the falling skills in §4 and substitute a task closer to the program's own discipline (cost: task redesign for the affected unit). |
