## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the preceding scored evidence and market data; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

This program's Panel C v4.2 scores are uniformly thin: seven of eight items sit at level 1, naming the capability in program or unit outcomes without a core assessment that tests it, and Life-long learning & transfer reaches level 2 only because the compulsory final-year capstone synthesises prior learning through case-variation problem questions. Both gates pass — the staged, prerequisite-gated 102-credit-point core carries G1, and the defended trade-off decisions the capstone and problem-question assessments require carry G2 — so a redesign has room to add assessed capability without weakening either floor.

The market evidence sharpens where that redesign should land. The profession's own regulator makes human oversight of AI-generated legal submissions and discovery synthesis non-delegable (§3 Theme 1), and the same guidance treats generative-AI drafting as subject to the paralegal-supervision framework (§3 Theme 4) — verification and governance of AI output, not tool operation, is the disciplinary floor this program's outcomes name but never assess. Automated contract analytics and discovery review are compressing the entry-level document-review volume that has trained junior lawyers' inquiry and investigative skill (§3 Theme 2), and statutory interpretation, legal advocacy and fiduciary risk management are recorded as rising keywords while contract drafting falls (§4 Skill shift summary) — a shift toward judgement under uncertainty that the program's existing placement and clinic units could carry, if they sat in the core rather than the 42 credit points of electives.
