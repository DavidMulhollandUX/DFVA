## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the preceding scored evidence and market data; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

The Bachelor of Biomedicine (B-BMED) is a 300-point undergraduate specialist degree offered by the Faculty of Medicine, Dentistry and Health Sciences (MDHS) and the Faculty of Science, serving as the flagship pathway into medical, dental, and health-science careers. The program passes Gate G1 on an extensive compulsory specialist core (125 points across all year levels, from BIOM10001 through BIOM30001) with a strict prerequisite chain, but fails Gate G2 because core assessments are dominated by tests, quizzes, lab reports, and written exams lacking assessed trade-off decisions or live project accountability under clinical/diagnostic uncertainty.

Operating in a low-exposure, physically grounded biomedical research and clinical domain (**44.92**, well below the portfolio median of 90.9), the program demonstrates strong disciplinary depth and foundational adaptive capabilities alongside significant workplace headroom (**Adaptiveness: 7 / 15**, **Workplace Practice: 3 / 9**). It features recurring assessed collaborative proposals and presentations (**C1: 2 / 3**) and graded anonymous peer review (**C2: 2 / 3**). However, AI in drug discovery and computational biology is confined to electives (**C3: 1 / 3**), independent research capstones are elective (**C4: 1 / 3**), laboratory practicals use prescribed protocols without methodology selection (**C5: 1 / 3**), presentations are delivered to internal peers/staff without external practitioner judging (**W1: 1 / 3**), case-based tasks use academic exam criteria (**W2: 1 / 3**), and laboratory internships sit as optional electives (**W3: 1 / 3**).
