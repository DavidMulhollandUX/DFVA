## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the preceding scored evidence and market data; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

The Bachelor of Fine Arts (Screenwriting) scores 7/15 on adaptive capabilities and 5/9 on workplace practice, on an estimated field basis (the Jobs and Skills Australia Higher Education Outcomes (JSA HEO) field list, not this program's own graduates). Both gates pass: G1 on an all-compulsory, level-staged specialist core with an explicit prerequisite chain, and G2 on FLTV30030's capstone mentorship and FLTV30028's Industry Placement route. C5 (0/3) is the single largest gap: the program has no research-methods unit and no assessed methodology selection or primary-evidence generation in its core, and the one available inquiry route is optional rather than required.

C3 (1/3) and W3 (1/3) are the next-largest gaps. FLTV20011 documents digital-tool operation but no AI-specific capability, limits or governance content; the Industry Placement route in FLTV30028 exists only as one of two alternative assessment routes, framed as an 'Optional Placement.' The remaining five items (C1, C2, C4, W1, W2) each sit one level below the instrument ceiling, largely on documentation gaps in assessment items that pair with an existing outcome (an industry panel, a self-directed project, a professional-script artefact) rather than missing content.
