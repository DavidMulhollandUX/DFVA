**Position: High exposure · low adaptiveness.** Assigned against the v4 medians (exposure 90.9, adaptiveness 9), computed from all 34 reference-cohort programs re-scored on this instrument. This is the deterministic median rule the site applies, not a probabilistic stability class.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **combined** (both components), n = 226, 29 destination titles, 13 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: components: Master of Construction Management (exact) ∪ Master of Property (exact)

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (36 pages) and verified on 2026-08-31: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (5):

- W1: Property Markets and Valuations' overview names the graded 'individual presentation' as a 'client presentation of the report,' which would support Level 2 (professional genre) if confirmed, but the assessment table itself gives no audience detail — resolved to Level 1, the lower level, per the scoring-direction rule.
- W2: the authentic-task pattern (real professional artefacts, judged against practice/professional-standard criteria) recurs across most first-year core subjects and includes one instance measured against the profession's own standard (ASMM in Cost Management) — the shape of Level 3 evidence — but the extract has no capstone or later-stage subject to confirm the pattern continues as the program's assessment spine, so it is scored at Level 2, the level the extract directly supports.
- W3: the course overview states professional experience 'can be an integral part of the program,' phrasing consistent with an elective placement (Level 1), but no placement/practicum subject is named or allocated points in the documented course structure — resolved to Level 0, the lower level.
- Construct overlap: Planning Law's two assignments (site assessment; planning-application critical review) and Construction of Buildings' real-building report each straddle two constructs — case-by-case transfer (C4) or structured critique (C2) versus task authenticity (W2). Each was scored once, in the construct its anchor names most specifically, to avoid double counting.
- Data quality: the captured page for ABPL90130's subject overview (SOURCE: .../subjects/abpl90130) contains unrelated content from a different subject (3-D Echocardiography & New Technologies, MEDI90046); both captured pages for ABPL90335 — Contract Management, a first-year core option — contain unrelated Food Science content (Sensory Evaluation FOOD90028; Food Packaging Materials and Processes FOOD90031). This is apparent scrape cross-contamination, not genuine curriculum content. No mismatched text was used as evidence; ABPL90130's correctly-captured assessment page was used, but ABPL90335 contributes no evidence anywhere in this score.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (2):

- Second-year specialisation subjects (Building / Cost Management / Project Management streams), the Master of Property's post-first-year subjects, and any capstone, thesis or placement subject are not included in this extract. C4 Level 3 (self-scoped capstone / assessed knowledge-gap identification), C5 above Level 0 (research methodology, if taught in a later-stage core subject), W2 Level 3 (program-wide confirmation of the assessment spine) and W3 above Level 0 (a placement/practicum subject, if the structure allocates one) cannot be confirmed or ruled out beyond what is scored here.
- ABPL90335 (Contract Management), listed as a first-year core subject, has no usable content in the extract — both its captured pages contain unrelated Food Science subject matter (see the data-quality ambiguity note above).

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
