## 1. POSITION — Basis: measured × scored

**Destination AI Exposure: 90.11.** Destination titles are mapped to the published Felten AI Occupational Exposure (AIOE) index and rescaled 0–100 by the Panel A procedure used for every other program; 12 of 30 titles carry medium mapping confidence. Basis: union of both components' alumni records — Master of Architecture ∪ Master of Construction Management (n = 782, 30 titles). The portfolio median is 90.9, so this program sits **at or below** it.

**Workplace Practice: 4 / 9.** Scored on sub-scale W [[19]](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2021L00488) in the same pass, from the same extract. The position axis remains adaptiveness alone; the workplace sub-score is reported beside it, not folded into the axis, and the two are never added together.

**Position: Low exposure · low adaptiveness.** Assigned against the v4 medians (exposure 90.9, adaptiveness 9), computed from all 34 reference-cohort programs re-scored on this instrument. This is the deterministic median rule the site applies, not a probabilistic stability class.

**C1 Distributed cognition & relational capability — 2/3**

> Class group presentation, outlining the ESD report that outlines overall environmental system strategies for the delivery of environmentally sound healthy building with zero energy operation (equivalent to 300 words per student, 600 words per presentation from a two-student group)
>
> Assignment 1 – Group assignment (2-3 students) focusing on measurement of quantities for (i) Masonry Trade and (ii) Finishes Trade, (1250 words equivalent per student)
>
> Essay and class presentation focussing on issues facing construction management in the future. Essay due in week 7 and presentations (in pairs) held in weeks 8 to 12
>
> One group assignment equivalent to 1000 words per student

**C2 Hybrid metacognition & evaluative judgement — 1/3**

> The remainder of the final grade is reserved for explicit evidence of reflective thinking in the notebook.
>
> Evaluate their work in relation to other exemplary spatial compositions;

**C3 Digital & AI literacy, including governance — 1/3**

> make basic use of digital design programs.
>
> Drafting software (e.g. McNeel Rhino, AutoCAD)
>
> RiB CostX Educational ver 6.9
>
> The subject also examines alliancing and partnering contracts, performance-based contracts as well as contract management in the context of BIM-based projects.

**C4 Life-long learning & transfer — 2/3**

> Each project will run for four weeks, changing in scale from the familiar objects of the home to the unfamiliar and the urban, introducing students to an array of processes in design thinking, methods and scope.
>
> Once the above subjects have been completed, students are required to choose one of the following construction specialisations:  Building, Cost Management or Project Management.  Information about these specialisations, including the remaining course structure, can be accessed from here.

**C5 Inquiry & evidence generation — 1/3**

> Research essay
>
> An essay (inclusive of references) based on oral presentation
>
> Research through the selection of architectural case studies and regular use of the library.
>
> Students can take a research subject which may enable progression to further studies as a PhD candidate.

**W1 Professional communication & conduct — 2/3**

> The final project submission and presentation constitutes a hurdle requirement and students must present their final work to a panel of guest critics at the end of the semester.
>
> Communicate their designs and the disciplinary context in a clear and professional manner.
>
> Generate design and contractual documentation that clearly conveys information to both specialist and non-specialist audiences and that enables a design project to be realised.

>
> Assignment 2 , is an exercise to document and report comprehensively on a selected residential building under construction

## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — The program documents an all-compulsory specialist core plus staged professional-accreditation requirements (ARBV, AIQS, AIB, RICS, CIOB) and an entry structure staged by prior major, evidencing a coherent, progressively structured disciplinary core.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — Assessed tasks require defended trade-off and risk decisions under real uncertainty: Contract Management's assessed work on variations/delays/conflict-dispute, construction-systems units requiring evaluation of alternative construction methods against time/cost/quality, and the design studios' hurdle-gated presentation to an external guest-critic panel.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **combined** (both components), n = 782, 30 destination titles, 12 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: components: Master of Architecture (exact) ∪ Master of Construction Management (exact)

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (34 pages) and verified on 2026-08-29: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities:** none recorded. A block with no boundary note on a program with placement, capstone-route choice or peer assessment has probably not had the boundary check run.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
