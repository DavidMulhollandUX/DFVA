## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — The program documents a coherent specialist core with progressive methodological depth: 50 points of stream-specific level-9 Earth Sciences core (with 25 of those points required to correspond to the thesis topic, tying coursework to the research specialisation) plus a 125-point Research Project sequenced Pt1–Pt5 with an explicit staged progression gate. This is a disciplinary identity with a staged chain, not generic or interchangeable content.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** PASS — Assessment goes well beyond recall or scripted response. ATOC90002 assesses participation in a negotiation simulation in which students defend a national position under competing policy options, against outcomes explicitly about uncertainty in decision-making; and the required 125-point original research project is a capstone with genuine uncertainty and accountability, gated by semesterly hurdle reviews and a thesis judged at publishable standard.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **cognate** (cognate program), n = 46. Index variant AIOE-2021; crosswalk `data/aioe/v31_extension_crosswalk.csv`. Basis note: Reconciliation package tier cognate.

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2025 handbook capture (31 pages) and verified on 2026-08-15: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (8):

- C1 straddled 2 and 3: ATOC90010's team assignments are solved in teams of two to three AND 'involve computations with MATLAB', which reads as coordinating work across people and a tool. Resolved down because the division of work between people and systems is not itself assessed and no role/tool allocation is documented — the level-3 anchor requires that, not merely tool use inside a team task.
- C2 straddled 1 and 2: several core-menu subjects carry appraisal-shaped outcomes (criticising active research, assessing climate models and their uncertainty, judging the strengths and limitations of statistical approaches). Resolved down under R2 — every one is an intended learning outcome and none of the matching assessment entries documents criterion-referenced appraisal.
- C3 straddled 1 and 2: ERTH90026 documents that climate models are 'imperfect but invaluable tools' and ATOC90015 outcomes concern the limitations of assimilation schemes, which resembles 'capabilities AND limitations'. Resolved down because the level-2 anchor names AI, the systems in question are numerical physical models rather than AI systems, and the only AI-governance subject is an unavailable elective.
- C4 and C5 both draw on the same 125-point Research Project, which the one-construct-one-home rule flags. Kept in both because each anchor names that route independently and the facets are disjoint: C4 scores self-scoping plus the staged Pt1–Pt5 progression gate toward independent learning; C5 scores the required generation of original primary evidence and the semesterly hurdle reviews as staged supervised methodological scrutiny.
- W1 straddled 1 and 2: the ATOC90002 UNFCCC negotiation role-play is a professional/diplomatic genre in the discipline's policy-facing criterion situation. Resolved down — the audience is the class, no external audience or practitioner judge is documented, and no professional-conduct or accountability criterion appears among the assessed criteria (R4 bars scoring the framing rather than the documented features).
- W2 straddled 2 and 3: the required 125-point project runs from first semester to completion at 'publishable quality', which is arguably a spine carrying the profession's own standard of performance. Resolved down because the practitioner-artefact tasks (structural map, geophysical interpretation report, field report) all sit in selection menus rather than being required of every student, so a documented spine cannot be established for the program as a whole.
- Structural caveat affecting every item: apart from the Research Project, this program has no compulsory named subject — both stream cores and the Professional Skills component are selection menus ('Students must take 50 points of the following', 'Students must take 12.5 to 25 points of the following subjects'). Menu-listed subjects were treated as core units for the anchors that say 'at least one core unit', but not as evidence of program-wide recurrence for any level-3 anchor.
- Extract coverage: subject pages are supplied for the Atmospheric Science core and four Geology core subjects only. Several Geology core-menu subjects (e.g. Ore Reserve Estimation, Exploration Field Skills, Sedimentary Basins and Resource Analysis, Remote Sensing) and all Professional Skills subjects have no page in the extract, so their assessment design could not be inspected. Scores reflect the supplied text only.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
