## 1. DIAGNOSTIC SUMMARY — Basis: inferred

*This plan argues from the preceding scored evidence and market data; it is
interpretation, not observation.*

The Bachelor of Advanced Computing and Bachelor of Commerce combines an 84-credit-point compulsory computing core, a required 48-credit-point computing major and 24 credit points of Commerce core with a staged prerequisite chain — G1 passes on that specialist sequence. G2 fails: the documented extract carries no assessment at all (every unit's 'Assessment Tasks:' field is empty), so no defended trade-off decision or live-project accountability is recorded to satisfy the anchor.

Panel C splits 3/15 on Adaptiveness and 4/9 on Workplace practice, both capped by the same gap: nothing in the extract is assessed. C1, C2, W2 and W3 sit at 0/3 — the C1 anchor is bare (no collaboration construct in outcomes, and the only stakeholder wording belongs to W1's communication construct instead), C2 and W2 have no appraisal or authentic-task evidence of any kind, and W3 has no work-situated learning beyond an international-exchange mention. C3, C4, C5 and W1 sit at 1/3: each has the right construct named in a generic outcome statement, but not one is assessed, so R2's uniform rule holds every one of them at the outcomes-only level. The market evidence below — stakeholder-engagement and requirements-analysis demand, ACS-code accountability for AI-generated work, no statutory ICT licence, and PwC's finding that exposed junior roles need senior-level human-intensive skills — supports moving each item toward its next anchor, but the assessment gap, not the market, is what currently caps every score.
