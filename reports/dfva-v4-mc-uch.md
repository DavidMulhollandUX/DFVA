## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** FAIL — PASS: the program documents a coherent, staged heritage-specific structure rather than generic or interchangeable content — a compulsory Core (both subjects specific to heritage theory, policy and global practice), a compulsory Pathway component, and a Selective list confined to heritage/architecture-specific subjects (measured drawings and digital heritage, architectural conservation in Asia, urban and landscape heritage, applied heritage conservation techniques), giving disciplinary depth and progression within urban and cultural heritage. DEMOTED to FAIL by verification pass: the PASS rests on descriptive About-page marketing copy ('The core subjects... examine contemporary and theoretical approaches...') plus the four bare credit-point headings, not on documented 'progressive technical or methodological depth' or a 'staged prerequisite chain' — no prerequisite structure appears anywhere in the extract. Only 25 of 100 credit points (the two Core subjects) are genuinely compulsory and heritage-specific; the Pathway (25cp) is a choice of two unrelated formats (research thesis vs. internship); the Selective list (25cp) is heritage-confined as claimed, but the Elective list (25cp, unconstrained choice) pulls in subjects with no disciplinary tie to urban/cultural heritage — Marketing the Arts, Cultural Festivals and Special Events, Twenty-first Century Architecture, Planning Theory and History, Construction History, RESPECT, Sustainable Collections. That is a quarter of the degree open to generic/interchangeable content, which cuts against 'all-compulsory specialist core' and toward the FAIL anchor ('generic or interchangeable content without disciplinary identity') for that portion. G1 is treated as, at minimum, a much weaker PASS than argued, defaulted to FAIL given the evidentiary gap on progression/depth.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** FAIL — PASS: the compulsory Internship Pathway option places students in a live project with real accountability — students 'develop and complete a specific project in discussion with the host organisation,' supervised jointly by the Subject Coordinator and a host-organisation representative, assessed via a project deliverable, a reflective journal and a coordinator-meeting hurdle. This is a live project with real uncertainty and accountability to an external party, satisfying the anchor. Rests on the Internship route specifically (see ambiguities). DEMOTED to FAIL by verification pass: the PASS is earned entirely by the internship's live host-organisation project — one of two compulsory Pathway options. The rationale's own ambiguities note admits 'the alternative Minor Thesis route does not evidence comparable real-world uncertainty/accountability in the extract,' meaning roughly half the cohort (by design, a free compulsory choice) has no documented basis for this gate. A program-level gate that only half of all graduates' documented curriculum satisfies is exactly the kind of ambiguous-between-outcomes case the scoring-direction rule addresses ('when the evidence is ambiguous... take the LOWER level... never resolve ambiguity upward'); the same reasoning was used elsewhere in this scoring to withhold level-3 credit on W1–W3 for single-route, non-universal evidence. For internal consistency and per the default-to-refute instruction, G2 does not stand as a clean program-wide PASS on internship-only evidence.

**G1 FAILS.** A disciplinary-foundation failure is reported regardless of the adaptiveness score: the adaptive capabilities are scored on top of a foundation this program is not documented to establish.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **partial** (related program), n = 130, 15 destination titles, 6 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: Urban and cultural heritage sits in the planning program family.

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (35 pages) and verified on 2026-08-30: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (5):

- C2: the assessed reflective journals in ABPL90282 and the Internship (ABPL90360) are graded reflection but not documented as criterion-referenced appraisal of quality (peer review, critique against standards) — could read as Level 2; resolved to Level 1 per the lower-level rule.
- C5: both Core subjects pair an assessed Research Proposal with an assessed Research Report/Essay, which could read as Level 2 ('methodology selection and data collection'), but the extract never distinguishes a data-collection step from literature/archival synthesis — resolved to Level 1 per the lower-level rule.
- Pathway subjects (ABPL90360 Internship, ABPL90382 Minor Thesis) are a compulsory choice between two alternatives, not identical curriculum for every student. C4, W1, W2 and W3 each draw their strongest evidence from one Pathway option (Minor Thesis for C4; Internship for W1, W2, W3) — scores reflect what the documented course structure makes available, not a claim that every graduate takes the same route.
- G2 rests on the optional Internship Pathway route; the alternative Minor Thesis route does not evidence comparable real-world uncertainty/accountability in the extract.
- Verification pass (2026-08-29/30): G1 and G2 demoted PASS→FAIL, and C4 demoted 3→2, for internal inconsistency between how single-route/non-universal evidence was treated for C4 and G2 versus W1–W3 in the same scoring pass. See per-item rationale for full reasoning; a fresh capture and scoring pass would be needed to argue any of these back upward.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
