## 3. GATES — Basis: scored

- **G1 Disciplinary foundation:** PASS — The award requirements document a coherent specialist core with progressive technical depth: 84 credit points of compulsory computing core units, a required 48-credit-point computing major from a named specialist list (Computer Science, Software Development, Information Systems, Computational Data Science, Cybersecurity), at least 24 credit points of 4000-level or higher IT electives, plus 24 credit points of Commerce core and a Commerce major. A staged prerequisite chain is stated as a progression rule requiring all units at a given level to be passed or concurrently taken before enrolling at a higher level, which is the anchor's 'staged prerequisite chain' route to PASS.
- **G2 Decision-making under uncertainty:** FAIL — The gate PASSes only on assessments requiring defended trade-off decisions, or simulations, capstones or live projects with real uncertainty and accountability. The extract documents no assessment at all: every unit's 'Assessment Tasks:' field is empty, and the unit entries carry only a templated four-line outcome set. With no assessment evidence in the extract, the PASS condition cannot be satisfied, so the gate is recorded FAIL; see notScoreable for the caveat that this reflects an extract with no assessment data rather than documented recall-only assessment.

## 6. EVIDENCE CONFIDENCE & LIMITATIONS — Basis: reported

**Panel A metadata.** Basis tier **field** (field grain), n = 0, 14 destination titles, 3 at medium mapping confidence. Index variant AIOE-2021; destination titles mapped to the published Felten index. Basis note: field-of-education occupation list (ATO-linked), not this program's own graduates

**Panel C provenance.** Scored from the 2026 handbook capture (1 pages) and verified on 2026-09-07: adversarial refutation of every level-3 score and every gate PASS (run), verbatim evidence check (run), and the construct-boundary pass.

**Ambiguities and construct-boundary notes** (6):

- C1 0 vs 1: the outcome 'Communicate evidence-based findings effectively to technical and professional stakeholders' mentions stakeholders, which could be read as stakeholder work in the outcomes (level 1). Resolved to 0 by the one-construct-one-home rule — audience-directed communication is W1's construct — and by the scoring-direction rule.
- C3 1 vs 2: 'Apply ethical frameworks and professional standards in practice' appears in every unit's outcomes and could be read as ethics content. Resolved down to 1 because it is generic rather than AI-specific, and R2 bars an unassessed outcome from reaching level 2.
- C4 1 vs 3: the Honours pathway's 24 credit points of research-related units resembles level 3's self-scoped research project. Resolved to 1 because Honours is optional and merit-gated (WAM of at least 70), so it is not documented structured progression for the cohort, and no assessment evidence exists (R2).
- C5 1 vs 2: research thesis and industrial research project units are documented, which could suggest an assessed designed inquiry. Resolved down to 1 because those units sit in the 4000-level elective pool or the Honours pathway rather than the compulsory core, and no assessment task is documented for any unit.
- W2 0 vs 1: 'resolve complex real-world challenges' could be read as scenario framing. Resolved to 0 under R4 (never score up on a 'real-world' label) and the scoring-direction rule, since no task features are documented.
- W3 0 vs 1: the unit titled 'major industrial research project' could be read as elective work-situated learning (level 1). Resolved to 0 by the scoring-direction rule because nothing documents a workplace setting, practitioner supervision, host accountability or assessment for it.

**Not scoreable from the handbook** (4):

- Assessment evidence is absent from the entire extract: all six units in the unit-of-study tables show an empty 'Assessment Tasks:' field followed by 'Workload: 150 hours'. Every level-2 and level-3 anchor across C1-C5 and W1-W3 requires assessment evidence, so no item in this record could be scored above 1, and the scores reflect documented absence of assessment data rather than positive evidence of decontextualised assessment.
- The compulsory core is not enumerated: the resolutions reference '84 credit points of core units as specified in Table A' and '24 credit points of core units of study as set out in Table A for the Bachelor of Commerce', but Table A itself is not included in the extract. The six units shown (INFO1110, COMP2123, COMP2922, INFO4914, INFO4999, INFO4913) are a partial list weighted toward Honours and research units, and their learning outcomes are identical boilerplate with the unit title substituted, so they carry no unit-specific curriculum evidence.
- G2 could not be positively evidenced. With no assessment tasks documented anywhere in the extract, neither the PASS condition (defended trade-off decisions, simulations, capstones or live projects with real uncertainty) nor the FAIL condition (recall or scripted responses only) is directly observable; it is recorded FAIL because the gate's PASS requires documented assessment evidence that the extract does not contain.
- No Commerce-side unit or assessment evidence appears in the extract at all, despite the Commerce degree contributing 24 credit points of core units and a 48-credit-point major to the award; the Commerce professional accreditation page is listed in the site navigation but its content is not captured.

**Standing v4 limitations.**

- Scores describe **documented curriculum intent**, not demonstrated graduate
  capability; the extrapolation warrant is constructive alignment, stated as an
  assumption (Kane [17]).
- Indigenous data governance is not scored as a distinct construct; C3 level 3
  counts it where taught (CARE Principles; Lodge et al. 2025 Principle 4 [[2]](https://www.acses.edu.au/publication/australian-framework-for-artificial-intelligence-in-higher-education/)).
- Perception/manipulation (Frey & Osborne's third bottleneck [[7]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2016.08.019)) is unscored.
- A high W3 score describes curriculum design, not student welfare: required
  placements can disadvantage students who cannot afford unpaid work or
  relocation (Accord [[30]](https://www.education.gov.au/australian-universities-accord/resources/final-report); Fawns et al. [[24]](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2024.2404634) on ableism and elitism).
- W2 is scored against each discipline's own criterion situation [[22]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02504676), so
  cross-disciplinary W2 comparison is weaker than within-discipline comparison.
- W3 excludes simulation by design (simulation scores in W2), so programs whose
  professional norm is high-fidelity simulation may score low on W3 for a
  defensible pedagogic choice. Read the two sub-scores together.
- The authentic-assessment employability evidence rests largely on student
  self-report [[25]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stueduc.2021.101030); W2's warrant is documented design fidelity plus the
  assessment-drives-learning mechanism [26], not demonstrated employment effects.
- Work-integrated learning is NOT claimed to raise employment rates — Jackson &
  Collings [[28]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10734-017-0216-z) found it does not. W3's warrant is skill development, employment
  relevance and professional identity formation [[29]](https://doi.org/10.1080/07294360.2016.1139551).
