This program offers an integrated, cross-disciplinary perspective combining broad and rigorous training in the foundations of politics, philosophy and economics. You will study insights from all 3 disciplines and explore current and future global challenges. This program will equip you with versatile skills to address complex and challenging issues drawing on an historical, social, political and economic knowledge base.
You may choose electives from all 3 disciplines, or opt to specialise in one discipline, earning a major in politics, philosophy or economics.
All students graduate with Honours at the completion of the 4 year degree.
For students who commenced in 2023 or later, the following courses contribute to the research training required by the Program Design Procedure: PPES1101, PPES2101, PPES3101 and PPES4100. These courses must be taken at UQ.

Subject: Politics, Philosophy, Economics 2: Foundations of Policy Problems (PPES2101)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course will contain several modules focusing on an integrated PPE approach towards addressing some of the world's 'wicked problems'. The course will be taught in lecture-seminar mode, using a range of policy documents and online materials. Where possible, eminent scholars from across UQ and beyond may assist as visiting experts from time to time.¿

- Explain the basis for diverse perspectives on social, economic and environmental problems.
- Analyse how public policy decisions are made on important complex issues.
- Critically analyse and evaluate selected policy problems and solutions and suggest evidence-informed improvements.

Subject: Politics, Philosophy, Economics 3: Advanced Analysis of Complex Problems (PPES3101)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course will contain two or three modules focusing on an integrated PPE approach towards addressing some of the world's 'wicked problems' taught in seminar mode or online. The modules will be taught by leading academics from across UQ's school, faculties and institutes or by eminent international academics or visitors, acting as a means of facilitating their engagement with UQ teaching and learning in an intensive period.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse complex economic and social systems by identifying key agents, interactions, and mechanisms that generate aggregate outcomes, using major frameworks from the history of economic thought.
- Compare and critically assess alternative analytical frameworks developed by influential economic thinkers, with particular attention to how they explain coordination, instability, growth, and change.
- Evaluate the role of models, abstraction, and formalisation in economic analysis, recognising both their explanatory strengths and their limitations in the study of complex systems.
- Construct clear and coherent analytical arguments in both written and oral form, drawing on appropriate concepts and evidence from the course material.
- Apply historical and counterfactual reasoning to explore how alternative institutional or historical trajectories could have shaped economic systems differently, and to reflect on the normative implications of different analytical approaches.
Assessment Tasks:
- Group Analytical Exercise Team or group-based In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Individual In-Class Essay In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Group Uchronia Presentations Team or group-based In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Politics, Philosophy, Economics 4: Toward Social and Economic Reform (PPES4100)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: PPES4100 Politics, Philosophy, Economics 4: Toward Social and Economic Reform applies a real-life, employability-focused lens that will enable you to draw on and apply the practical and professional skills you have gained during your PPE journey. With a particular emphasis on equality and inequality - a core, substantive area of dispute in resolving complex policy problems - you will engage in a collaborative strategic thinking exercise to address an area of critical uncertainty. This inquiry requires you to think deeply about the life experiences of those affected by inequality to provide thoughtful and creative solutions. You will need to be self-regulating in your research and project development as you integrate knowledge and skills from the three disciplines to create an evidence-based solution to a national concern.
Learning Outcomes:
- Integrate knowledge and skills from the three disciplines to work individually and collaboratively to investigate factors underlying inequalities in relation to a chosen problem. A1; A4
- Develop effective methods and strategies for communicating with different audiences, including academic, professional, and community groups. B2; B5; B6
- Generate an evidence-based solution to a chosen problem of inequality, drawing on research skills commensurate with Honours level. C4
- Justify a preferred solution to the problem of inequality being investigated. C5; D3
- Adopt a theoretically informed, evidence-based approach to analysing, explaining, and evaluating alternative solutions. D4; D5; D6
- Demonstrate insights into different ethical perspectives on the moral issues associated with inequalities for the purpose of grounding the preferred solution on solid moral principles and reasoning. E1; E5
Assessment Tasks:
- Participation Team or group-based In-person: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Argument Map: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Scenario Planning report Team or group-based: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Presentation of group scenario plan: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Position paper and policy brief Team or group-based: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Seminar 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: Professional Practicum (PPES4999)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course aims to provide penultimate or final year students with the opportunity to apply conceptual knowledge to the real world via a professional placement. As such, this course strongly links discipline learnings, personal employability skills and attributes developed by students across the duration of their studies with the challenges and expectations of working in industry. In doing so, students have the opportunity to gain valuable work experience and to connect this experience to the development of a workplace ready skill-set. Successful completion of the course will provide students with the employability skills required for transition to the world of work. Further information about how to apply for PPES4999 can be found on the Faculty's work-integrated learning website .
Learning Outcomes:
- Articulate key workplace practices, processes and challenges within an organisation.
- Visually and verbally communicate your employability skill-set and personal brand using workplace appropriate language.
- Reflect on your own learnings and application of your personal employability attributes in the workplace.
- Demonstrate your ability to evaluate your own work performance.
- Apply the professional rigour expected in industry practice.
Assessment Tasks:
- Video Pitch- Your Brand: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Reflections: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Professional Development Plan: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- In-class Workshop Discussion Team or group-based In-person Online: 30% best 2 of 3% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Placement 120 Hours/ Semester Workshop 12 Hours/ Semester Tutorial 20 Hours/ Semester
