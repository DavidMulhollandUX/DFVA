## Master of Legal Studies
##### L6013
##### 72 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
This expert graduate course provides a thorough theoretical and practical grounding in one or more areas of law. It is suitable for graduates interested in developing or enhancing specialist careers with knowledge of the law.

You investigate contemporary issues in law, practice and scholarship, and evaluate complex issues relevant to the field of theoretical, international, and interdisciplinary perspectives. The course enhances your capacity to undertake independent research, and includes options for a pathway to doctoral studies.

### Structure
The course is structured in three parts. Part A Foundation studies and Part B Specialist studies, and Part C Specified elective studies.

Part A Foundation studies

These studies enable you to gain fundamental knowledge of legal principles and concepts, and develop core skills in legal research and writing.

Part B Specialist studies

These studies enable you to develop specialised knowledge and advanced skills in a particular area of law that suit your interests, skills and career goals. You will be able to focus on sources of information relevant to your specialisation and the application of research methods and specialist discipline knowledge and skills necessary to support law-related work. Depending on your interests and motivation, you can choose a program of study in which you plan and execute a major research-based project with a high level of personal autonomy and accountability.

Part C: Specified elective studies

Units are chosen from the law specialisations to meet the remaining credit points required for the course. Students may also apply for the MON5750 Monash Innovation Guarantee. Refer to the Notes section for further information.

Master's entry points

Depending on prior qualifications you may receive entry level credit (a form of block credit) which determines your point of entry to the course:

  * If you admitted at entry level 1 you must complete 72 credit points, comprising all of Parts A, B, and C.
  * If you are admitted at entry level 2 you must complete 48 credit points, comprising all of Parts A, B, and C.

### Requirements
The course comprises 72 points structured in three parts, Part A Introductory knowledge and core electives (6 points) and Part B Extending specialist knowledge (30 points) and Part C Research knowledge (36 points).

Units are 6 points unless otherwise stated.

Part A Foundation studies (6 points)

You must complete either:

LAW5000 Australian legal reasoning and methods or LAW5081 Australian legal process and research.

Part B: Specialist studies (30 points)

Complete one of the specialisations below.

Note that if you do not want to complete a specialisation you must compete 12 points from the list of research-integrated electives in any specialisation and a further 18 points from units in any specialisation.

  * Commercial and corporate law

  * Criminal law and forensics

  * Dispute resolution

  * Global society and human rights

  * Health law and community

  * Labour and employment law

  * Public sector governance and regulation

  * Technology and innovation

  * Transnational economic regulation

Not all elective units are offered in each semester or year.

Part C: Specified elective studies (12 or 36 points)

You must complete any remaining credit points from units in any law specialisation. Students may also apply for the MON5750 Monash Innovation Guarantee. Refer to the Notes section for further information.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Apply with integrity appropriate research principles and methods to plan, carry out and report on an original project.
**2.**
Demonstrate mastery of theoretical knowledge and recent developments in a range of areas of law practice.
**3.**
Apply theories, knowledge and skills to different areas of law or to new situations in law or legal scholarship.
**4.**
Explain and justify arguments, methods, and conclusions to professional and non-professional audiences.
**5.**
Design, evaluate, implement, analyse and theorise developments that contribute to global professional practice or scholarship.
**6.**
Demonstrate high level personal autonomy and judgement.

### Progression to further studies
Successful completion of this course may provide a pathway to a graduate research degree. If you wish to progress to a graduate research degree you are advised to take LAW5083 or LAW5084 Minor thesis.

Subject: Australian legal reasoning and methods (LAW5000)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The unit provides students who are new to the study of Australian law with advanced knowledge and understanding of the Australian legal system, its underlying principles and concepts (including international and comparative contexts), principles of justice and approaches to ethical legal practice; and advanced practical and professional skills to undertake legal research, interpret legislation and judicial decision, analyse factual and policy issues, demonstrate professional judgment and scholarship to formulate and evaluate solutions to legal problems, collaborate and communicate effectively.
Learning Outcomes:
- Generate appropriate responses to legal problems and communicate them in a form appropriate for legal practice or scholarship; and
- Exercise professional judgment in responding to ethical issues that arise in legal practice or the administration of justice.
- Design and implement an efficient research strategy which requires you to synthesise relevant legal, factual and policy issues;
- Apply professional judgment and critical thinking skills to interpret legal conclusions and legislation, evaluate alternative constructions and justify a considered opinion;
- Demonstrate knowledge of the foundations of the Australian Legal System and the principles and norms that underpin the system.
Assessment Tasks:
- Written assignment: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- In-class assessment: 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Written Assessment: 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Take home exam: 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Class participation: 10% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Students enrolled in this unit will be provided with 24 contact hours of workshops, plus 6 hours of library tutorials and 6 hours of general tutorials per teaching period. You will be expected to do reading set for class, and to undertake additional research and reading applicable to a 6 credit point unit.

Subject: Australian legal process and research (LAW5081)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit is designed to provide international students and students with prior qualifications from overseas, in particular those from civil law countries, with a general understanding of the operation of the Australian legal system and common law systems more generally, including the structure and content of cases and the main principles of statutory interpretation, and with the legal research and communication skills necessary for postgraduate legal research and writing
Learning Outcomes:
- Use cognitive, technical and creative skills to generate and evaluate at an abstract level complex ideas and concepts relevant to case law, statutes, and advanced legal research.
- Investigate, analyse and synthesise complex legal information, problems, concepts and theories in relation to the operation of the Australian legal systems;
- Conduct research into the common law systems more generally, including the structure and content of cases based on knowledge of appropriate research principles and methods; and
- Apply knowledge and understanding of recent developments in relation to the rules of precedent and application of statutes to new situations in professional practice and/or for further learning with creativity and initiative;
Assessment Tasks:
- Discussion board participation: 10% (Hurdle: None)
- Research assignment: 45% (Hurdle: None)
- In class exercise: 45% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Students enrolled in this unit will be provided with 36 contact hours of workshops per teaching period. Students will be expected to do reading set for class, and to undertake additional research and reading applicable to a 6 credit point unit.

Subject: Extended research (LAW5083)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: This unit involves completion of a 12,000-15,000 words (maximum) thesis. The topic is designed by the student in conjunction with a staff member and approved by the Board of Examiners. Students work under supervision but with considerable independence. The unit is suitable for students seeking a pathway to doctoral studies.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop their capacity to analyse critically, reflect on and synthesise information, problems, policies, concepts and theories, evidenced by their ability to develop and sustain a convincing 'thesis' throughout their research;
- Acquire an advanced knowledge of legal research principles and methods, evidenced by their ability to locate, update and apply the law in their chosen area of research;
- Make a contribution to knowledge in their area of research.
- Demonstrate advanced written communication skills; and
- Demonstrate an advanced and integrated understanding of a complex body of knowledge in an area of law;
- Develop a high level of personal autonomy and accountability in planning and executing a substantial piece of research on a particular legal issue;
Assessment Tasks:
- Minor thesis: 100% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Students enrolled in the minor thesis will be provided with appropriate levels of supervision to meet the needs of the student and expectations of the university. Students will be expected to do reading and research applicable to a 12 credit point unit.

Subject: Monash Innovation Guarantee (MON5750)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The MIG is an intensive unit designed to equip you with the holistic skills and mindsets required to lead and implement viable innovation solutions in industry. You will work on a purpose-driven solution to a real-world challenge facing a commercial or social enterprise, a government or UN agency, or a start-up. Industry and academic experts will coach and guide you and your interdisciplinary team through a three-week innovation process involving analysis, ideation, design, testing, validation, and pitching. During the program, you will build your employability skills and broaden your professional networks. The MIG provides a solutions-focused experience to transform your capacity to drive positive change in the workplace through multi-stakeholder collaboration and innovation. It provides a basis for understanding how to intervene in contemporary global issues facing industry, and the implications for society and your career.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply innovation methods and mindsets to design and implement viable and purpose-driven solutions;
- Lead and collaborate to integrate and leverage interdisciplinary and multi-stakeholder knowledge to approach real-world issues;
- Reflect upon your role in the project, in the context of your emerging professional identity, and create a career and leadership plan.
- Analyse an industry challenge, considering relevant stakeholders and systems;
- Communicate effectively to industry partners and stakeholders from different sectors;
Assessment Tasks:
- Professional Reflection: 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Weekly Professional Development Online Modules: 15% (Hurdle: None)
- Innovation Design Sprint Prototype Presentations: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Industry Innovation Proposal: 35% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 144 hours, made up of intensive workshops and personal learning
