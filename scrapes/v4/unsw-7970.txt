## Description
As our world becomes increasingly complex and uncertain, so too does our need for understanding, support and hope. The Graduate Certificate in Counselling at UNSW is a fully online program designed to introduce you to the essential foundations of counselling practice.  Through four core courses, you’ll gain the knowledge and confidence to support others with empathy and insight.  Whether you're looking to enhance your current role or explore a new career path, this Certificate offers a flexible, accessible entry point. As a student at one of Australia’s top-ranked universities, you’ll benefit from a supportive and inspiring learning environment. The Graduate Certificate also provides a clear pathway into our dynamic and fully accredited Graduate Diploma and Master of Counselling, to continue your studies and progress to become an accredited counsellor.

## Program Learning Outcomes (CILOs)
On successful completion of this program, students will be able to:

1. Apply foundational knowledges to inform counselling and the therapeutic relationship across diverse client contexts
2. Demonstrate counselling skills and reflective and culturally responsive practice
3. Explain ethical and professional standards and practices in counselling

## Career Opportunities & Employment Areas
Practice Lead, Mental Health Coordinator, Youth Worker, Support Worker

## Program Structure & Degree Requirements
Students must complete 24 Units of Credit (UOC) structured across the following academic components:

### Core Courses (24 UOC)
You must take 24 UOC of the following courses.

- **ZZCO5001**: Foundational Counselling Skills [6 UOC]
- **ZZCO5002**: Ethics in Counselling Practice [6 UOC]
- **ZZCO5003**: Mental Health, Trauma and Resilience [6 UOC]
- **ZZCO5004**: Counselling in a Diverse World [6 UOC]

## Progression & Academic Rules
If you successfully complete the requirements for this program you will be eligible for admission to either the Graduate Diploma of Counselling or the Master of Counselling with full credit for courses completed.

## Admission Requirements

Advanced standing or RPL is not permitted for this program.

A Bachelor degree or higher-level qualification in a cognate discipline*

OR

A Bachelor degree or higher-level qualification in any discipline plus 2 years relevant work experience**

OR

Applicants without a Bachelor qualification may be considered for entry to a Graduate Certificate based on a minimum of six years relevant professional experience**

*Cognate Disciplines may include:

**Relevant Work Experience:

- Applicants should demonstrate relevant experience reflecting professional readiness, that may include supportive work, effective communication, facilitation or mentoring. Part-time roles and internships undertaken during undergraduate studies will not be counted.

Subject: Foundational Counselling Skills (ZZCO5001)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course introduces you to the foundations of the therapeutic relationship and use of self in counselling. The course orients you to a humanistic, person-centred approach to counselling. Utilising the online environment, you will build your capacity to use counselling skills in digital contexts. You will apply First Nations knowledges (such as deep listening) while fostering reflective practice and critical self-awareness, through experiential and reflective learning. You will engage with experiential learning in small groups and roleplays to develop foundational counselling skills and practices, including the use of digital and online counselling.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate understanding of relevant therapeutic approaches
- Articulate the components of the therapeutic relationship
- Demonstrate core counselling skills
- Critically appraise your counselling skills

Subject: Ethics in Counselling Practice (ZZCO5002)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course introduces you to the ethical foundations and professional standards, informed by the Australian Counselling Association (ACA) that guide your development as a counsellor and your counselling practice. You will explore ethical theories and decision-making frameworks while emphasising reflexivity as central to professional identity. You will consider key issues of ethical practice, including boundaries, authenticity, and cultural responsiveness in the therapeutic relationship. You will be introduced to supervision as a site of ethical and reflective practice and critically consider emerging issues such as digital ethics (including use of AI) in counselling.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the use of supervision in ethical and reflective counselling practice
- Evaluate the role of professional standards in counselling
- Demonstrate an understanding of ethics, boundaries, authenticity, and cultural responsiveness in the therapeutic relationship
- Critically reflect on relevant ethical issues and theoretical approaches in counselling practice

Subject: Mental Health, Trauma and Resilience (ZZCO5003)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course introduces you to a biopsychosocial spiritual cultural perspective on human development and health over the lifespan. You will analyse contemporary approaches to mental health, trauma and resilience, across diverse populations. You will utilise a range of interactive practice skills to develop skills and knowledges in trauma informed care, risk and counselling responses to mental health issues. Your reflections on your experiential learning build your skills and capacity for effective counselling practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse contemporary understandings of mental health, trauma, and resilience
- Articulate an understanding of trauma-informed and responsive care
- Explain key concepts of human development and relational processes across the lifespan

Subject: Counselling in a Diverse World (ZZCO5004)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course engages you in critical exploration of counselling practice within diverse personal, social, geographical and cultural contexts. Emphasis is placed on culturally responsive and socially just counselling practice, informed by First Nations knowledges, cultural humility, intersectionality, and emancipatory frameworks. The course highlights the role of humanistic and narrative traditions—including existential and meaning-making perspectives—in supporting clients’ stories and lived experiences. You will engage with online and digital counselling technologies to promote inclusivity. You will develop skills to engage with clients from a wide range of backgrounds and experiences including neurodiversity.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate integrative counselling strategies to promote inclusive, culturally responsive and socially just practice
- Evaluate the role of digital technologies in counselling, including accessibility and inclusivity
- Critically reflect on how social and cultural diversities shape counselling practices
- Articulate how First Nations knowledges and relevant frameworks inform inclusive and socially just counselling practices
