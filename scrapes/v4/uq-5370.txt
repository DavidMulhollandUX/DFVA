## Career Opportunities & Employment Outcomes
Both in Australia and overseas, career opportunities for graduates are excellent, as demand in the interaction design area continues to grow. The Graduate Diploma in Interaction Design provides the opportunity for graduates to understand and solve usability problems which will enable graduates to work in across-discipline areas and apply their skills to any specialist discipline area. Graduates will find employment in industry, business, government, defence, health, education, media and other areas.

Subject: Advanced Human-Computer Interaction (DECO6500)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: DECO6500 Advanced Human-Computer Interaction explores how human experience is shaped within socio-technical systems, and how HCI practitioners can responsibly analyse, design, evaluate, and intervene in systems where technological agency shifts and challenges human self-determination. The course extends foundational HCI theories and methods to examine the relationships between people, technologies, institutions, data, culture, and society. Students will develop the critical, ethical, and practical judgement needed to understand complex technological systems and shape them in ways that support human agency, inclusion, accountability, and social impact.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse human needs, values, behaviours, and experiences within complex socio-technical contexts using appropriate contextual inquiry, qualitative, and quantitative research methods.
- Synthesise HCI theories, socio-technical perspectives, and research evidence to explain how technologies, people, organisations, infrastructures, policies, cultures, and wider systems shape each other.
- Develop and justify socio-technical systems maps that identify actors, boundaries, relationships, feedback loops, tensions, leverage points, and broader conditions shaping human experience and technological practice
- Design, prototype, and evaluate technology-based interventions that respond to socio-technical problems, considering usability, accessibility, inclusion, implementation constraints, stakeholder needs, and human experience.
- Critically evaluate technological interventions across multiple system layers, including stakeholder relationships, organisational workflows, technical and data infrastructures, ethical concerns, governance, equity, cultural safety, sustainability, and unintended consequences.
- Justify the strategic selection and responsible integration of diverse knowledge sources, including formal research, practitioner knowledge, crowdsourced documentation, and generative systems, within complex problem-solving workflows, demonstrating intellectual accountability for the synthesised outcome
- Establish, enact, evaluate and adapt accountable collaborative practices to support effective teamwork, shared decision-making and the achievement of collective outcomes.
Assessment Tasks:
- A1 Socio-technical Systems Map (Individual): 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- A2 Collaborative Evaluation Inquiry (Group) Team or group-based: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- A3 Layered Systemic Evaluation (Individual) Hurdle Identity Verified: 40%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week Applied Class 3 Hours/ Week
