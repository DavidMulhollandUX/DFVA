## Diploma in Cybersecurity - Melbourne (Bundoora)
##### PD004
##### 120 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
La Trobe’s Diploma of Cybersecurity is designed to be a supported pathway into your dream Cybersecurity degree or set you up to enter the industry in an entry-level role.

Build a strong foundation in core IT subjects –IT Fundamentals,  Programming and Cybersecurity – that’ll prepare you for your next degree. You’ll study alongside Bachelor’s degree students from day one and get a taste of university life.

You’ll learn:

  * Programming  * Learn how to design and implement software solutions using a range of popular programming languages such as C, Java and Python.

  * Information technology fundamentals  * Gain insight into how computers function across both hardware and software systems. Learn how data storage and retrieval, communications, networking, security and information systems are critical to our everyday lives.

  * Cybersecurity  * Equip yourself with the knowledge to defend against cyber threats and attacks. Cover topics including information security, the ethical and legal implications of online information systems, and dynamic practices for incident analysis and response.

  * Cryptography and Security  * Gain insight into the operation and the use of various cryptographic protocols to enhance system security and access controls. You will also study the performance of various crypto protocols.

On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Explore the effectiveness of selected security frameworks in protecting businesses from emerging cyber threats.
**2.**
Justify the selection of appropriate hardware and software technologies to solve information technology problems
**3.**
Apply foundational programming skills to solve everyday information technology tasks
**4.**
Identify the different uses of cryptography in establishing data security, privacy protection and access control

Subject: CYBERSECURITY IN PRACTICE (CSE1CPR)

Subject: INTRODUCTION TO CYBERSECURITY (CSE1ICB)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In the modern Internet era, industries and organisations need to be prepared to defend against cyber threats and attacks. Stakeholders should be familiar with basic principles and best practices of cybersecurity to protect their businesses and personal information. In this subject, these principles and strategies for future cyber security are explored. Key topics include: information security; ethical and legal practices; mitigating cyber vulnerabilities; and the process of incident response and analysis. The subject introduces the broad discipline of cybersecurity and outlines how to ensure the privacy, reliability, confidentiality and integrity of information systems and mitigate against cyber threats and risks.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply and compare approaches to cyber risk management in addressing real-world digital security challenges.
- Demonstrate foundation skills in identifying malware, securing applications, systems, and networks
- Analyse emerging cybersecurity practices, regulations, and standards, and evaluate their implications for digital security.
- Work efficiently and effectively as a member of a team to investigate possible solutions such as cryptography to enhance data security, and web security, and digital forensics against cyber threats

Subject: OBJECT-ORIENTED PROGRAMMING FUNDAMENTALS (CSE1OOF)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: The Object-Oriented (OO) paradigm is a significant influence in software development in the computer industry. It divides a system into objects that exist in the model of the application domain. In this subject, students will be introduced to OO concepts, terminologies, syntax and programming using Java. Topics covered include classes and objects, inheritance, object attributes and methods, overloaded methods, the use of the Unified Modelling Language (UML) for representing these concepts, primitive data types, flow of control, basic input/output, arrays, Java library class, and exception handling. Some software engineering principles are included: program design, algorithm for solving a problem and version control. This subject is designed as inclusive as possible for undergraduate/diploma students.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply the object-oriented method to designing and implementing a computer program for solving a real-life problem.
- Relate the principles behind the use of the abstract data type (ADT) to localise and minimise the effects of modifications through object-oriented approach
- Justify the capabilities of the OO model to share and reuse code with OO techniques, reducing the time required to develop an application
- Compare the traditional method of analysis/design/implementation and the object-oriented approach, with the OO model’s basic construct being the object, which combines both data structure and behaviour.

Subject: NETWORK ENGINEERING FUNDAMENTALS (CSE2NEF)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, students are introduced to the foundational concepts of computer networking. The subject covers the OSI reference model and its associated protocols to provide a layered understanding of network communication. Students will explore the lifecycle of a data packet across a network and learn the principles of IP addressing. The subject also includes hands-on experience with basic network device configuration and an introduction to static routing, enabling students to build and manage simple network topologies.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the overall OSI model and how layers interact with adjacent layers in the model.
- Apply appropriate network physical standards for different network scenarios.
- Identify functions of each OSI layer and apply this knowledge to build a Network.
- Describe TCP/IP based network protocol suite and compare with OSI model.
- Describe the evolution of Local Area Network (LAN technology) with focus on Ethernet.

Subject: CRYPTOGRAPHY AND SECURITY (MAT1003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject provides a strong foundation for cryptography and coding, which are fundamental for mastering cybersecurity. Technical skills in mathematics will be developed, enabling the introduction of a range of common cryptographic techniques, including public and private key encryption, hashing, and cryptanalysis.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and summarise a range of cryptographic techniques, including private key systems such as AES (Advanced Encryption System), and public key systems such as RSA (Rivest, Shamir, Adleman).
- Explain basic mathematical principles and constructions underlying cryptographic methods and employ these to solve simple problems.
- Identify and describe different contexts of application of cryptographic techniques in communication and cybersecurity, such as authentication and secure transmission.

Subject: DATA-BASED CRITICAL THINKING (STA1DCT)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject helps the student evaluate data-based evidence encountered in everyday life. It provides the fundamental numeracy skills required by business people, scientists, lawyers, nurses, journalists, social scientists, teachers and other professionals who need to evaluate data-based arguments, whether found in newspapers, television or on-line websites. This is achieved by a combination of studying newsworthy topics introduced in lectures, laboratory classes which encourage engagement with others and on-line quizzes that assess numeracy skills. The four themes covered in this subject are gathering useful data, turning data into information, probability and from data to decision making.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critique data-based conclusions that are reported in the media and similar outlets
- Interpret/derive simple numeric and graphical statistical summary measures of data.
- Calculate probabilities in a variety of scenarios that may be used for informed decision making.
- Identify and discuss common misconceptions of probability that can lead to poor decisions
