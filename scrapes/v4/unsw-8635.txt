## Description
The Master of Pre-Doctoral Business Studies is a pathway program for students wishing to undertake a PhD degree in the UNSW Business School – it is the first component of the UNSW Business School’s Doctoral Program. The Business School’s Doctoral Program is an elite, boutique program and rivals the best Doctoral Programs world-wide. The Master of Pre-Doctoral Business Studies is specifically designed to allow Business School Doctoral students to undertake up to two successive years of advanced coursework during their Doctoral studies.

The Master of Pre-Doctoral Business Studies is the first year of the Doctoral Program and constitutes one year of advanced coursework, including a research project. The degree has three core courses, providing a broadening of background in business research for all Doctoral students. Students also undertake a specialisation, ensuring they have completed appropriate field-specific preparatory advanced coursework for their chosen PhD degree. Entry into the PhD component of the Doctoral program requires a minimum overall average of 75 in the Master of Pre-Doctoral Business Studies and a minimum score of 85 in the Research Project Seminar.

See UNSW Higher Degree Research Learning Outcomes.

## Progression & Academic Rules
A pass average is required to remain in the Masters degree.

## Admission Requirements
**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
The minimum requirements for admission are:

- The equivalent of a three-year undergraduate degree plus a postgraduate degree in a relevant discipline from UNSW with an overall average grade of at least 75 (Distinction), OR the equivalent of multiple undergraduate degrees (eg a double degree) from UNSW with at least one in a relevant discipline and an overall average grade of at least 75 (Distinction), OR the equivalent of a four-year degree of Bachelor with first or upper second class Honours from UNSW in a relevant discipline.

- For international students, a high degree of English language proficiency (IELTS 7.0 overall with at least 6.5 in each band)

- For applicants of the Economics specialisation, results for the GRE unless waived by the Specialisation Convenor.

- For applicants of the Marketing specialisation, results for the GMAT unless waived by the Specialisation Convenor.

Admission will ultimately be at the discretion of the UNSW Business School and will rely on the Business School having appropriate research supervision capacity and resources available for an applicant’s research interests.

**Limitations on Recognition of Prior Learning**:
All students will be required to undertake the three Core Courses, regardless of prior learning. If students have previously undertaken courses specified by their specialisation, or those courses are unavailable, they will be given the option of taking relevant elective courses instead, subject to the approval of the Associate Dean Higher Degree Research.

Subject: Foundations of Business Research (COMM8100)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course covers the foundations of business research. The course has three parts. The first part of the course covers the intellectual foundations of research - i.e., thesis strategy, topic choice, humanities of science, theory building, academic writing, and research methods. The second part covers qualitative research design. The third and final part covers statistical methods - i.e., probability models, estimation, hypothesis testing, and regression analysis. The course is only for Business School students enrolled in the MPDBS and PhD programs.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate ability to analyse qualitative data.
- Demonstrate oral and written communication skills appropriate for a research context.
- Demonstrate understanding of quantitative research methods.
- Understand problems and solutions associated with humanities of science, thesis strategy, topic choice, theory building, academic writing and reviewing, and research design options.

Subject: Statistical Methods for Business Research (COMM8101)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course covers the foundations of statistical methods for business research and builds on COMM8100 Foundations of Business Research, offered in the first Term. Students will complete topics in quantitative research methods such as experimental design and analysis, survey design and analysis, linear and multiple regressions, mediators and moderator, and conditional mediation.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically analyse and review research papers and assess their strengths and weaknesses.
- Recognise distinct nature of various research approaches and methodologies and apply them to examine a research problem.
- Present a well‐argued and logically structured research questions.
- Identify problems, develop, and justify research questions, and discuss ethical aspects of research proposals.
- Write well‐articulated and logically structured academic texts.

Subject: Econometric Analysis (COMM8102)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: ​​This course provides a strong econometric foundation for empirical research in business, with an emphasis on the connection between methodology and applications, and computing in R. The first part is a thorough discussion of linear regression including maximum likelihood estimation, regression diagnostics, asymptotics, and regularization, which have applications to a wide range of econometric models. The second part extends these ideas to generalized linear models. The remaining parts cover false discovery rates and time-series analysis.​
Learning Outcomes:
- Employ statistical software to fit econometric models to real economic data and critically interpret the results.
- Employ mathematical tools to characterise and analyse econometric models.
- Apply modern modelling methods—such as the bootstrap, regularization, and false discovery rate—to fit and interpret econometric models using real economic data.
- Synthesise ideas through succinct and clear work that communicates ideas in a logical, professional manner.
- Critically evaluate empirical research using the econometric tools and approaches introduced in the course.

Subject: Business Research Project Seminar (COMM8103)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course is part of the onboarding for students enrolled in the Business School’s doctoral programs to assist students in developing their research and communication skills. The course introduces students to the research culture of the School, the Business School and the University. The course will cover information and policies on research integrity and ethics, how to develop research questions and conduct literature reviews, how to select and design methodologies to conduct research, how to present research to both academic and professional audience, and how to provide respectively constructive feedback on other people’s research. The course is only for Business School students enrolled in the MPDBS program.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate other people’s research papers and presentations to provide constructive feedback in a respectful manner.
- Demonstrate written and oral communication skills by writing a research proposal and presenting your research, both individually and as a team, in a logical and professional manner.
- Identify the publication process and practice how to present yourself when networking at conferences and other professional settings.
- Develop and participate in a positive research culture, applying and demonstrating research integrity and ethics.
- Prepare for your research and confirmation by developing research questions and identifying the right audience for your research.
