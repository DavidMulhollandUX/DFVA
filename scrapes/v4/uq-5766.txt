This program is intended for practitioners who aspire to excellence and leadership in the profession of clinical neuropsychology and clinical psychology. The program entails a research thesis, specialist placements and advanced professionally oriented coursework, with opportunities for specialist concentration in the area of clinical neuropsychology. Students will gain a foundational knowledge of scientific bases and practice of clinical neuropsychology and clinical psychology as well as undertaking training in clinical practice which employs evidence-based best practice models in neurological and mental health settings.

Subject: Child and Adolescent Emotional Health (PSYC7043)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course takes a developmental and systemic framework in understanding how to work with children, adolescents and their families in the context of youth emotional or mental health presentations. Evidence-based assessment and treatment approaches to the most common emotional health problems experienced by children and adolescents will be presented. A combined academic and practical approach will be adopted with students having the opportunity to engage in experiential learning (by observing and practising) around the skills taught.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate an understanding of the importance of taking an approach to all aspects of child and adolescent emotional or mental health that is developmentally, systemically and culturally informed, including working with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children, young people and families.
- Articulate, manage and reflect upon common process issues that may arise when commencing work with a child or adolescent and their caregiver.
- Demonstrate a good understanding of the diagnostic criteria associated with each category of mental health problems commonly experienced by children and adolescents.
- Develop an appropriate developmentally and systemically informed formulation for common emotional health problems experienced by children and adolescents.
- Demonstrate a good understanding of the recommended assessment tools associated with each category of mental health problems commonly experienced by children and adolescents.
- Demonstrate understanding and use of evidence-based treatment strategies for treatment of specific emotional health problems in children and adolescents.
- Clearly and effectively explain key transdiagnostic concepts (e.g., what thoughts are and why they are important) and strategies to children and adolescents of different ages.
Assessment Tasks:
- Standard Triple P accreditation Hurdle: 30%% (Hurdle: True)
- Transdiagnostic skills competency Hurdle: 40%% (Hurdle: True)
- Managing a parent-related process issue Hurdle: 30%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week 2L 2T

Subject: Evidence based Parenting and Family Intervention (PSYC7053)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides advanced instruction in the application of evidence-based parenting and family intervention procedures for the prevention and treatment of behavioural and emotional problems in children and adolescents. It covers the theoretical, scientific and practical foundations of behavioural family intervention and its application to a range of child behaviour problems from infancy to adolescence. To provide a solid foundation in the field of behavioural family intervention students will receive training in the delivery of Level 4 of the Triple P-Positive Parenting Program. Lectures will be run as a series of skills training sessions, where participants will undergo practical training in the assessment, diagnosis, and family-based treatment of various child and family problems. The course is offered as an intensive during the University Orientation week for Semester 2.
Learning Outcomes:
- Discuss the theoretical basis of behavioural family intervention and the guided participation model of information transfer.
- Conduct an intake assessment with a parent to assess a child or family problem.
- Develop a functional assessment of a child or family problem.
- Discuss assessment findings with parents using the guided participation model of information transfer.
- Introduce parenting strategies and self-regulation skills.
- Demonstrate the use of psychoeducation and active skills coaching techniques such as behavioural rehearsal strategies to teach a variety of skills.
- Provide constructive feedback to parents following direct observation of a parent-child or couple interaction; manage process issues.
- Demonstrate skills of content delivery to families with children with diverse presenting problems, including challenging behaviour and anxiety.
Assessment Tasks:
- Core competencies Identity Verified In-person: 60%% (Hurdle: False)
- Standard Triple P Quiz and Fear-Less Triple P Quiz Online: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Workshop 5 Hours/ Week 5W

Subject: Adult Psychopathology (PSYC7113)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides an exploration of psychological disorders, with a strong focus on diagnostic systems, assessment methods, and understanding of treatment approaches. Through both theoretical and applied learning, students will develop a thorough and critical understanding of widely used classificatory systems, and gain hands-on experience in clinical interviewing, mental status examinations, and diagnostic assessments when working with adults. Special emphasis is placed on the biopsychosocial approach to diagnosis, case formulation and treatment planning, considering developmental, cultural, and ethical factors. Throughout the course, students will engage in critical analysis of the research evidence-base, fostering a reflexive and evidence-informed approach to their professional development.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply and critically evaluate diagnostic classification systems and their role in diagnosing psychological disorders in adults.
- Demonstrate competency in clinical assessment techniques.
- Apply biopsychosocial approaches to diagnostic case formulation and evidence-based interventions.
- Understand the psychosocial and pharmacological management for common psychological disorders.
- Understand how to apply ethical principles, cultural considerations when working with diverse populations, and reflective/reflexive practice to formulation and communication of clinical findings in multidisciplinary team settings (relevant to accurate documentation, case report writing, and verbal presentation of case summaries).
- Understand the impact of inequitable access to healthcare from a human rights perspective and its implications for working with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples.
- Critically engage with the research evidence base to inform clinical decision-making and competence in all (including digital) environments to foster ongoing professional development in psychological practice.
Assessment Tasks:
- Role play: Client Descriptions, Formulations and Reflection Hurdle: Pass/Fail Recycle% (Hurdle: True)
- Written Case Study Report: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Indigenous Health Modules and Reflection: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 45%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Psychological Skills & Evidence-Based Therapies (PSYC7114)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This foundational course introduces students to evidence-based therapeutic approaches and the core psychological skills needed to apply them. It covers fundamental concepts of intake and risk assessment, case planning and treatment, and covers Cognitive Behaviour Therapy (CBT), Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), trauma-informed care, and Motivational Interviewing. Students will develop key therapeutic skills for case formulation, risk assessment, and intervention planning while integrating research evidence into psychological practice. Emphasis in working across diverse populations, including Indigenous peoples, and considering the social determinants of mental health. Through theoretical learning, case studies, observation, modelling, role-playing, and practical applications, students will gain competencies and a strong foundation in evidence-based psychological practices.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the foundations of evidence-based therapy and demonstrate knowledge of core principles in psychological interventions, that include face to face and digital applications
- Develop therapeutic skills and apply foundational therapeutic strategies in practice
- Perform psychological risk assessments: assess and manage suicide risk and other psychological risks effectively.
- Develop structured case formulations and treatment plans using evidence-based principles
- Engage in self-reflection for professional practice and self-care
- Apply culturally responsive practices in their work with clients from culturally and linguistically diverse and Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander backgrounds.
Assessment Tasks:
- Intake interview and self-reflection Hurdle: Pass/Recycle/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- Risk Assessment Competency Hurdle In-person: 20%% (Hurdle: True)
- Skill Demonstration and Clinical Reasoning Competency Hurdle: 50% Each component is weighted 25%% (Hurdle: True)
- Self-Practice/Self-reflection Tasks Hurdle: 30%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Workshop 3 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week Workshop 8 Hours/ Semester

Subject: Advanced Cognitive Behaviour Therapies (PSYC7115)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This advanced course builds upon foundational psychological skills and focuses on specialised clinical skills in Cognitive Behaviour Therapy (CBT) and related evidence-based interventions. Students will dive deeper into cognitive skills training, as well as how to manage process issues, rupture and repair, and how to work with culturally and linguistically diverse populations. The course will also explore schema-focused therapy and compassion focused therapy. Emphasis is placed on developing competency in advanced therapy skills.
Learning Outcomes:
- Deepen understanding and mastery of CBT skills
- Develop individualised case formulations
- Apply deliberate practice strategies to improve therapeutic skill development
- Engage in reflective and reflexive practice, as well as self-care
- Manage process issues in therapy (e.g., working to repair therapeutic ruptures and working with shame)
- Able to apply CBT with culturally and linguistically diverse populations
- Able to continue to apply the scientist-practitioner model to your therapy practice.
Assessment Tasks:
- Mutli-Choice Question Quiz In-person: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Deliberate Practice Videos with Reflection Hurdle: 30%% (Hurdle: True)

- Apply the theoretical principles of the codes of practice to daily situations arising in psychology practice
- Understand the key factors to be considered in research design
- Identify potential ethical issues in a research context
- Formulate specific research questions
- Recognise and reflect on interprofessional practice skills including role clarification, team functioning, interprofessional communication, conflict management, collaborative leadership, and client-centred care.

Subject: Addiction Science and Practice (PSYC7261)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The aim of this course is to develop the knowledge and skills that a scientist practitioner needs to work effectively in the addiction treatment sector. You will be introduced to Australian drug and alcohol policy context and evidence based practices to address various phases of addiction: intoxication, withdrawal, acute treatment, and residential rehabilitation. - Critically evaluate AOD resources and apply them to specific audiences (e.g., clients of an AOD service, staff members, family members)

Subject: Advanced Clinical Neuropsychology and Neurorehabilitation (PSYC8181)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Building upon PSYC8161 Neuroanatomy for Neuropsychologists and PSYC8171 Diagnosis and Management of Neuropsychological Disorders, this course pursues a theoretical perspective for the manifestation of neuropsychological disorders such as attention, memory, language and movement. The teaching material will focus on the findings of recent paradigms which combine neuroscience techniques (imaging, behavioural and physiological recordings) with cognitive psychology and neuropsychology. The aim of this course is to provide a sound understanding of the cognitive mechanisms underlying neuropsychological disorders.
Learning Outcomes:
- Gain an understanding of brain plasticity as applied to neuropsychology
- Gain an understanding of emotional and psychological symptoms associated with certain pathologies in neuropsychology
- Apply theoretical principles to the diagnosis and management of neuropsychological disorders
- Have a solid understanding of theories of recovery and models of neuro-rehabilitation
- Apply neuro-rehabilitation to treat a range of neuropsychological disorders and evaluate outcomes

- Case Presentation and Reflection In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
- Rehabilitation Case Study (3,000 words): 50%% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Applied Gerontology (PSYC8291)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course offers an opportunity to study applied theory and practice relating to ageing in terms of positive ageing, assisting those with age-related illnesses, and the successful negotiation of life transitions. It will be particularly useful for mental health workers hoping to improve their knowledge and skills in working with older persons.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand current major theories pertaining to older adult development, positive ageing and aetiologies of age-related disease processes such as late-life depression and Alzheimer’s disease, including both Western and Indigenous perspectives.
- Understand and apply specialised assessment, case conceptualisation, diagnostic and treatment strategies to an older population, including both Western and Indigenous perspectives.
- Formulate management plans for older adults in specialist situations (e.g. long-term care settings, varying cultural contexts such as Indigenous or Culturally and Linguistically Diverse populations).
- Have working knowledge of age-related issues such as competency and guardianship, end of life and hospice care, psychometric and research paradigms, and public policy, including for diverse cultural groups.
