## Description
The Graduate Certificate is the first tier of two postgraduate programs in Construction Project Management. It is undertaken on a part-time basis. Students learn about project management principles and techniques needed to effectively manage large construction projects.

The Graduate Certificate program is suited to practicing personnel in relevant fields including building, construction management, construction economics, civil engineering, mining engineering, project management, quantity surveying, real estate who wish to enhance their career opportunities. This program also provides an opportunity to those who have relevant professional experience but limited formal qualifications to study at the graduate level.

Please note that the Graduate Certificate is not available to International students as it can only be undertaken on a part-time basis.

## Progression & Academic Rules
On completion of the program, students with grades of credit average or above may apply to upgrade to the 72 UOC Master of Construction Project Management program (8121) with full credits for the completed courses.

## Admission Requirements
**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
One of the following:

- A qualification equivalent to an Australian Bachelor's OR Australian Honours degree in a related field

- A qualification equivalent to an Australian Bachelor's degree in any field, plus 2 years industry experience with some managerial responsibilities

- An accredited diploma in a related field, plus 2 years industry experience with some managerial responsibilities

- An accredited diploma in any field, plus 3 years industry experience with some managerial responsibilities

- A recognised and relevant professional qualification such as membership of the Australian Institute of Building (AIB), Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS), Australian Institute of Quantity Surveyors (AIQS), Master Builders Association (MBA) or Chartered Institute of Building (CIOB), plus 3 years of industry experience with some managerial responsibilities

Related fields: Built environment disciplines, engineering, business or law.

Accepted roles at management level: Architecture (Senior Level), Bidding Manager, Construction Manager, Contracts Administrator, Cost Manager/Cost Consultant, OH&S Manager, Project Engineer, Project Manager, Property Manager, Quantity Surveyor, Site Manager, Project Planner/Scheduler (Senior Level), Project Risk Manager, Project Management Office (PMO) Manager

Relevant Professional Qualification: Evidence of membership is required.

**Limitations on Recognition of Prior Learning**:
No advanced standing nor exemption will be given for the Graduate Certificate program.

Subject: Management of Construction Organisations (CONS0002)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Management of Construction Organisations covers a framework for studying the principles of management in organisations and their application in practice, particularly in the context of construction organisations. Management is the process of planning, organising, leading, and controlling the work of organisational members and the use of available resources to achieve specific goals effectively and efficiently. In this course, you will explore how organisations are structured appropriately to the needs of the market, how leadership provides organisational vision and overall direction and how managers play a role in utilising an organisation’s resources (people, processes, systems, and tools) for success. In addition, you will learn about contemporary issues relating to the management of the construction industry, such as sustainability, construction defects, professional ethics, and Construction 4.0.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the principles of management in organisations such as planning, organising, leading and controlling.
- Examine the relationships between management theories and practice in the context of construction organisations.
- Propose organisational solutions to improve performance in construction organisations.
- Determine the efficacy of existing management procedures, systems, and culture in construction organisations.

Subject: Construction Informatics (CONS0005)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Construction Informatics introduces and applies Information Communication Technologies (ICT), Building Information Modelling (BIM), AI powered cloud software and Geographic Information Systems (GIS) in the construction management discipline. You will develop knowledge and skills essential for successful adoption of ICT, BIM, AI powered cloud software and GIS in construction planning, construction cost management, design coordination and modelling as well as infrastructure project planning and management.
Learning Outcomes:
- Integrate Building Information Modelling (BIM) into a Geographic Information System (GIS) platform.
- Apply Building Information Modelling (BIM) based quantity take off and clash detection for a construction project.
- Justify the advantages of using Building Information Modelling (BIM), AI powered cloud software and Geographic Information System (GIS) in managing construction projects.
- Create a Building Information Modelling (BIM) representation of a facility that includes simulation, AI based sustainability analysis, carbon analysis and cost estimation.

Subject: Construction Planning and Control (CONS0009)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Construction Planning and Control covers the time aspects of project management in the context of available resources and budget. Building upon basic project planning tools, including Bar Chart and Critical Path Method, this course teaches you a range of high-level construction planning and control techniques, as well as project control and risk estimation. The course requires you to create construction schedule plans for a complex construction project and produce a set of comprehensive planning documents using a similar approach used in industry practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Select and apply appropriate scheduling techniques for construction projects.
- Develop construction planning documents covering a schedule, scope of works, contingency and risk plan.
- Discuss probability scheduling for practical applications.
- Calculate schedule networks through a manual approach and with appropriate software.
- Develop time and resource scheduling plans based on resource and budget constraints.

Subject: Construction Contract Administration (CONS0010)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The course introduces you to procurement methods and the practice of construction contract management. You will learn about: (a) different options for project delivery and the options for contract price; (b) the common matters covered in the general conditions of a major construction contract, including (but not limited to) practical completion, extension of time, liquidated damages, latent conditions, delay costs, bonus and variation clauses, insurances; (c) the relevance of legal concepts, including unjust enrichment, frustration, waiver, estoppel, frustration and repudiation to contract administration; (d) alternative dispute resolution methods; (e) the purpose and application of the Security of Payments legislation for construction; and (f) the relevance of the Australian Consumer law.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply dispute resolution mechanisms in contract administration
- Understand how to formulate contracts at different stages in a project
- Evaluate payments in accordance with variations submitted
- Apply contract administration and legal principles to the practice of project management

Subject: Construction Management Theory and Practice (CONS0013)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this course, you will create a Construction Project Management Plan (CPMP) for a large-scale construction project. In the process you will refine your practical skills in planning, executing, monitoring, and controlling complex projects. You will learn how to incorporate various sub-plans, including scope, schedule, cost, risk, quality, health, safety, security, environmental, sustainability, procurement, stakeholder, communication, and change management plans into the larger CPMP.
Learning Outcomes:

- Demonstrate leadership and interpersonal skills through collaboration in managing complex construction projects.

Subject: Construction Risk Management and Business Analytics (CONS0016)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Construction Risk Management and Business Analytics explores concepts, key elements and applications of risk management and data-driven risk assessment. The course investigates standards, best industry practices and business analytics and their application to risk management in real-world situations in engineering and construction projects. Project risk management is an iterative process and assists organisations in setting strategy, achieving objectives and informing decision-making during the project lifecycle. The course focuses on commercial risks in delivering complex construction projects. You will learn about key risk categories such as work health and safety, environment, quality and other common risks which occur throughout the project lifecycle. You will also learn how to use industry risk assessment tools and business analytics to achieve smarter engineering and construction risk reduction decisions.
Learning Outcomes:
- Justify a research-based project Risk Management Plan, including a critical analysis and a data-driven solution plan.
- Analyse and evaluate complex risks and contingencies applicable to construction projects, including environmental risks.
- Apply tools and theoretical frameworks to assess risks and contingencies for construction projects.
- Explain business analytics in driving smarter engineering and construction decisions.
- Identify complex risks and uncertainties in relation to stakeholders’ objectives within the process of risk management.
