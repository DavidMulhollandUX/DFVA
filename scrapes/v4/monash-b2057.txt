## Bachelor of Digital Business
##### B2057
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview

### AQF level
Level 7 - Bachelor Degree
### Course duration (full time)


### Structure
The Bachelor of Digital Business is structured in four parts: Part A. Core studies, Part B. Capstone studies, Part B. Elective Studies and Part D. Indonesian studies

Part A. Core studies

These units will develop your capacity as a critical, creative and ethical professional. With an emphasis on decision-making and the management of digitalisation within organisations, you will be proficient in utilising your extensive knowledge of digital business to formulate solutions for business challenges.

Part B. Capstone studies

The capstone unit will consolidate your knowledge and skills acquired through the study of your discipline and provide experience in multi-disciplinary processes and decision-making.

Part C. Elective Studies

This will enable you to further develop your knowledge of the Indonesian context, or business more broadly, or study a business major. Alternatively, you can select units from across the Monash Indonesia campus in which you are eligible to enrol.

Part D. Indonesian studies

This will enable you to gain an understating of key political, social and cultural values that define Indonesia. 

Course progression map

The course progression map provides guidance on unit enrolment for each semester of study.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
communicate clearly, effectively and professionally in a digital business context with the ability to adapt to diverse contexts and audiences.
**2.**
integrate global contexts in business analysis and decision-making.
**3.**
exhibit effective collaboration in teams to address digital business challenges
**4.**
embed ethical considerations into decision-making processes and propose solutions that result in sustainable outcomes for stakeholders and the environment.
**5.**
demonstrate relevant knowledge of a range of digital business activities and their interrelationships as well as technical skills.
**6.**
identify, gather and critically evaluate sources of knowledge and business information to support evidence-based decisions
**7.**
demonstrate an inclusive mindset that embeds equity, diversity and inclusion in digital business practices by incorporating differing perspectives, cultures, identities, skills, and abilities.
**8.**
develop the ability to critically analyse problems in a digital business context and provide ethical, discipline-based solutions by applying business knowledge and technical skills.

Subject: Accounting fundamentals (ACI1001)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit introduces basic accounting concepts to non-accountants. The information requirements of two main groups of information users are examined - external users such as current and potential investors and internal users such as managers. This unit provides an introduction to the structure, meaning, analysis and interpretation of financial statements, in addition to exploring financial issues confronting managers, such as cost and performance measurement and budgeting.

- develop the ability to work effectively in a team and demonstrate the development of judgement, and skills in problem solving, critical thinking and analysis.

- apply financial and management accounting principles in the interpretation of financial statements
- measure and interpret financial and non-financial information for managers to use in planning, decision making and control

Subject: Evidence for action (BEI1067)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Evidence for action focuses on the skills and mindsets needed to investigate complex problems using a range of qualitative and quantitative methods. You’ll learn how to conduct interviews, run surveys, engage in fieldwork, and explore datasets, while also learning how to interpret, evaluate, and communicate what you find. This unit emphasises not just collecting information, but thinking critically about it: identifying patterns, surfacing assumptions, recognising bias, and assessing reliability and ethics. You’ll also be introduced to the practicalities of research responsibility, including informed consent, ethical review processes, and data care. Whether you're working with lived experiences, statistical models, or spatial data, you’ll be encouraged to ask: What do I know? How do I know it? And what does it mean for what we do next? Evidence for action helps you build analytical confidence and methodological flexibility, grounding leadership and innovation in diverse forms of knowledge that are both credible and ethically sound.
Learning Outcomes:
- collect, analyse, and interpret both qualitative and quantitative data using appropriate techniques
- assess the quality, reliability, and ethical dimensions of different forms of evidence, including research responsibility and data care practices
- communicate evidence-based findings clearly and responsibly to support innovation and leadership in real-world contexts.
- demonstrate understanding of diverse research methods for investigating complex challenges
- apply critical thinking to draw meaningful insights from varied evidence sources
Assessment Tasks:
- 1 - Written: 50% (Hurdle: None)
- 2 - Artefact: 30% (Hurdle: None)

Subject: Engaging complexity (BEI1501)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Engaging complexity is an immersive introduction to the world of complex challenges - social, environmental, institutional, and technological - where clear-cut solutions are rare, and understanding takes time. In this unit, you’ll learn to observe, listen, describe, and reflect before jumping to conclusions. You’ll work with tools like stakeholder mapping, systems diagrams, and controversy analysis to explore how problems are shaped by context, power, and perspective. Rather than rushing to solve, Engaging complexity invites you to slow down and sit with complexity: What’s going on here? Who is involved? What’s being said, and what’s not? You’ll begin to understand how complex environments work, and why that understanding must come before any attempt to navigate or intervene. Understanding complexity isn’t a distraction from action, it’s what makes meaningful leadership and innovation possible. This unit develops your ability to approach challenges with curiosity, care, and the skills to ask better questions - grounded in ethical awareness, humility, and the patience to trace complexity carefully, thread by thread, knot by knot.
Learning Outcomes:
- identify and reflect on how context, power, and perspective shape the way problems are defined and understood
- use mapping and observational tools (e.g. stakeholder mapping, systems diagrams, controversy analysis) to describe complex social and institutional environments
- practice careful, ethical observation and inquiry as a foundation for future innovation and leadership work
- reflect critically on your own assumptions and positionality in relation to the challenges you encounter.
- demonstrate understanding of complex systems and the value of situated, relational approaches to problem exploration
Assessment Tasks:
- 1 - Written: 50% (Hurdle: None)
- 2 - Artefact: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- 3 - Presentation: 20% (Hurdle: None)

Subject: Design thinking for global challenges (BEI2500)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Design thinking applies the skills and strategies developed by designers to a wide range of problems and situations. These skills and strategies include a range of conceptual and communication approaches, ways to explore and reconsider the context in which we work, as well as what should or could be achieved. Design thinking brings together some approaches that are known in the sciences and humanities, such as analysis and synthesis, but applies them in ways that are particular to a design approach. Design thinking can enable a more flexible approach to problem solving, and a more creative engagement with the complex issues of our contemporary world. This unit will introduce you to the key skills and practices associated with design thinking, and offer opportunities to explore the application of these basic creativity techniques through a series of projects.
Learning Outcomes:
- identify and analyse ill-defined, real-world project challenges and relevant design thinking approaches
- develop, test and evaluate original design proposals in response to a specific global challenge.
- demonstrate understanding of the methods and mindsets of design thinking and their application
- apply design thinking methods and mindsets to a project-based problem, to develop new perspectives on key issues and to identify potential opportunities

Subject: Microeconomics (ECI1100)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit provides you with an introduction to microeconomics. You will be given the tools to understand basic economic ideas and to apply their learning to analyse economic applications in everyday life. It begins with an introduction to supply and demand and the basic forces that determine an equilibrium in a market economy. Next, it introduces a framework for learning about consumer behaviour and analysing consumer decisions. We then turn our attention to firms and their decisions about optimal production, and the impact of different market structures on firms’ behaviour. The final section of the unit provides an introduction to some of the more advanced topics that can be analysed using microeconomic theory, including consumer choice, game theory and behavioural economics.
Learning Outcomes:

- recognise regulatory and political environments and how they impact on business.
- apply economic principles to explain and analyse economic institutions, economic decisions and performance

Subject:  Macroeconomics (ECI1200)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit uses aggregate demand and supply analysis as a tool for identifying and explaining the business cycle, output growth employment, inflation and the current account deficit. The investigation will illustrate the impact of the macroeconomy on decisions by firms and other economic agents in the economy and the effect of fiscal and monetary policies on business outcomes. Key strategic variables affecting decision making in the macro business environment are interest rates, inflation and the exchange rate.
Learning Outcomes:
- appreciate the influence of macroeconomic conditions on decision making in government, business and international trade
- analyse and critically evaluate policies designed to affect individual behaviour and aggregate outcomes.
- understand and evaluate the importance of factors that determine aggregate output, unemployment, interest rates, inflation and the exchange rate
