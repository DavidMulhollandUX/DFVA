## Master of Inclusive Education
##### D6014
##### 48 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
This course is designed for qualified teachers who wish to develop specialist knowledge and skills in inclusive practices in classrooms and schools. All units of study within this course will develop your understanding of the human rights basis for inclusive education as well as the legislative obligations that govern the education of students with disability. 

You will learn to apply contemporary frameworks such as the Multi-Tiered System of Supports, integrating Response to Intervention and Positive Behaviour Interventions and Supports, and to use the principles of Universal Design. You will acquire the knowledge to implement these frameworks in practice to ensure that all students without exception are provided with an accessible, equitable and high-quality education, as well as targeted support on the basis of need, so that they can make good academic progress, engage positively, and thrive at school.

The course will equip you with sound knowledge of evidence-based practices in instruction and assessment for academic and behavioural learning as well as student wellbeing. You will develop your skills in collaborating with students and their families, as well as leading professional collaboration with other teachers, teaching assistants, allied health professionals and other organisations in your community. You will develop expertise in designing, implementing and evaluating personalised education plans for students with disability and complex support requirements across learning, behaviour, wellbeing and communication. In undertaking this course, you will learn how to locate, review, and synthesise robust research across these elements of inclusive education to inform your professional practice and to support you as a lifelong learner.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
identify and implement interventions that facilitate and support inclusive education
**2.**
demonstrate an understanding of the philosophical views and legislative structures, both nationally and internationally, which drive inclusive education
**3.**
accommodate students with additional learning needs within regular classrooms
**4.**
apply inclusive practices, including supportive and differentiated strategies, to enable student progress
**5.**
establish learning environments which value diversity, recognise difference and encourage student engagement
**6.**
demonstrate critical thinking through engagement with current research in the field.

### AHEGS
The Master of Inclusive Education is an award of a postgraduate degree course taught and assessed in English. Subject: Understanding inclusion in learning communities (EDF5632)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: This unit considers key issues relevant to inclusive education and its relationship with learning across different educational contexts. A central emphasis of the unit is on the ways in which education can be transformed to include all learners regardless of their age, sexuality, gender, class, ethnicity, disability and/or social disadvantage. The unit addresses three major areas related to inclusive education. First, the unit demonstrates how various social, political, and economic forces underpin and influence inclusive education practices at global, national and local levels. Second, the unit explores how barriers to participation are created overtly and covertly at community, school and class levels for selected members of the society (particularly for those with a disability). Third, the unit offers various ways in which barriers to participation and learning could be addressed and inclusive practices developed in different educational contexts.
Learning Outcomes:
- evaluate practices that strengthen inclusion across different educational contexts
- analyse responsive inclusive practice for all learners including those with disabilities within a particular educational context.
- identify the key aspects influencing inclusive practice
- communicate evidence that explains the basis of exclusion from educational contexts
- interpret and critically evaluate the reasons that result in some members (particularly those with a disability) of the society are excluded from participation in a range of educational and social activities
Assessment Tasks:
- Inclusive practice analysis (4000 words or equivalent): 50% (Hurdle: None)
- Critical reflection project (4000 words or equivalent): 50% (Hurdle: None)

Subject: Practising inclusion (EDF5656)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: This unit addresses the inclusion of learners with diversities (e.g. sexual orientation, ethnicity, behavioural manifestations or disability) and how they are often excluded from participation in learning and social activities, from pre-school to tertiary education and in workplaces. A key focus of this unit is on the education of learners with disabilities. This unit addresses the reasons that such learners are excluded and identifies what can be done to enhance their participation in different contexts. This includes how we undertake authentic assessment so that everyone is included across different learning and social contexts. It also focuses on evidence-based strategies which could be applied to ensure that everyone is fully included in educational, social and physical activities.
Learning Outcomes:
- identify reasons some members of the society are excluded from teaching and learning
- acquire skills to work with key stakeholders (e.g. parents and paraprofessionals)
- use evidence-based strategies to enhance participation of excluded learners (including learners with behavioural issues and autism spectrum disorders)
- design and implement effective educational plans to deepen inclusive teaching and learning practices.
- assess learners and their learning environment
Assessment Tasks:
- Inclusion plan or literature review (4000 words or equivalent): 50% (Hurdle: None)
- Assessment protocol (4000 words or equivalent): 50% (Hurdle: None)

Subject: Positive behaviour support (EDF5667)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: This unit focuses on the key principles of using system-wide positive behaviour support (PBS) practices. PBS is a framework of practices that help improve the social and learning behaviours of students and decrease disruptions that interfere with instruction in educational settings and communities. The unit examines the wide-ranging research supporting PBS for learners of all age groups including preschool, primary, and secondary school students, and for students with severe emotional behaviours and those with developmental disabilities. You will critically review strategies for designing school-wide/system-wide PBS programs based on evidence and will be supported to develop your own PBS strategies that can be used to prevent and/or respond to challenging behaviours in your educational context. Consideration will be given to the work of leading and sustaining PBS strategies and practices across schools and systems.
Learning Outcomes:
- demonstrate knowledge about a range of PBS strategies and reinforce these through practice
- critically review strategies designed to develop whole school-wide/system-wide PBS plans
- communicate the rationale for using positive strategies to prevent and respond to challenging behaviours
- develop knowledge of broader strategies to lead and sustain PBS practices in schools and/or systems.
- design a PBS plan that responds to your educational contexts and issues
- identify an evidence-based approach that gathers data to underpin the development and evaluation of PBS practices
- understand the key principles of using a system-wide PBS framework
Assessment Tasks:
- Critical review of research on PBS practices (3200 words or equivalent): 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Designing a professional development program for a school (4800 words or equivalent): 60% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: The minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 288 hours per teaching period comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study.
