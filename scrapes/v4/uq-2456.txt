Study a Bachelor of Biotechnology to develop the skills and knowledge needed to translate innovative scientific ideas into commercially viable solutions.
You will combine theory and practice to examine biotechnology's varied applications, from antibody engineering to fight disease, genetic plant engineering to increase agricultural yields, vaccine design, cell and tissue culture technologies, and more.
Deepen your understanding of the key issues within biotechnology such as intellectual property, regulatory compliance, quality assurance and quality control. Learn to apply Quality Management Systems in Biotechnology so that products and technologies are safe and reproducible.
You will learn how to assess the market potential and financial viability of new products or technical services such as vaccines, diagnostics, biopharmaceutical drugs, transgenic plants or innovations in stem cell therapy.

Subject: Food for a Healthy Planet (AGRC1040)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The course addresses a pertinent challenge of humankind: how to feed 12 billion people while maintaining the integrity and function of our planet. It challenges participants with contrasting viewpoints for a nuanced understanding of the multidimensional aspects of food production and consumption. Course participants explore the food debate as consumers and scholars, with focus on the science behind innovation of food and food systems, locally and globally. Course participants map the future of food and agriculture with view of the UN's Sustainable Development Goals. This course was last offered in external mode in 2024.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the impacts of agriculture in the context of human and environmental health.
- Illustrate the processes involved in food consumption and food production.
- Collaborate effectively with peers and experts.
- Collate, critically evaluate and communicate scientific knowledge.
Assessment Tasks:

- Oral Group Presentation Team or group-based: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Examination Hurdle In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: True)

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=BIOL1000 =====
Subject: Introductory Biology (BIOL1000)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: In this course, students will examine how living things, from the building blocks of cells and the molecules that underpin them, to diverse organisms and ecosystems, persist and thrive by continuously responding to change. Through an integrated journey spanning biochemistry, cell structure, genetics, energy transformations, and evolutionary history, the course reveals how biological systems sense, adapt, and evolve. The narrative weaves together fundamental cellular mechanisms and the origins of biodiversity. By recognizing evolutionary innovations and physiological strategies, students will understand life¿s remarkable flexibility in the face of shifting environments. This multidisciplinary approach highlights both the unity and diversity of life, underscoring the importance of adaptation as a central theme in biology. Students may complete BIOL1000 and BIOL1020 concurrently, or BIOL1000 prior to completing BIOL1020, which is recommended.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the structure and function of cells, including organelles and macromolecules, as foundations of life.
- Describe energy flow in biological systems, including photosynthesis and cellular respiration.
- Summarise principles of genetics, inheritance, and reproduction in living organisms.
- Outline the diversity of life and evolutionary principles underpinning biological classification.
- Describe core ecological concepts, including organism-environment interactions, energy transfer, and ecosystem dynamics.
- Critically analyse and interpret biological data using digital and visual tools, including the ethical use of Artificial Intelligence (AI), to evaluate scientific evidence.
- Recognise how biological knowledge, including Indigenous knowledges and perspectives of Country, informs debates on conservation, environment, and human impacts, fostering inclusive and culturally aware understanding.
Assessment Tasks:
- Weekly Quizzes In-person Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Inquiry and Synthesis in the AI Era - Part 1: Written In-person: (Part 1: worth 18% of 30%)% (Hurdle: False)
- Inquiry and Synthesis in the AI Era – Part 2: In-Class assessment In-person: (Part 2: worth 12% of 30%)% (Hurdle: False)
- Final Exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Workshop 2 Hours/ Week

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=BIOL1020 =====
Subject: Genes, Cells & Evolution (BIOL1020)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Students taking this course will learn about the fundamentals of molecular cell biology and genetics by examining what cells are made of and how the genetic information to build cells and perform cellular processes are encoded, executed, and inherited. The course explores the connections between physical processes at the molecular level and the whole organism phenotype, and identifies how cellular, genetic, and evolutionary processes affect everyday life.
Learning Outcomes:
- Generic Skills - Identify and describe how cellular, genetic, and evolutionary processes impact everyday human life, including (but not limited to) effects on human health, agriculture, and biodiversity.
- Generic Skills - Be able to formulate testable hypotheses and evaluate those hypotheses against empirical results.
- Generic Skills - Demonstrate practical skills in different aspects in biology.
- Molecular & cellular biology - Compare and contrast the diversity in molecular components and cellular structures across the three domains of life and describe examples of fundamental similarities arising from the common origin of life.
- Molecular & cellular biology - Explain how cells are able to coordinate the basic molecular building blocks of life in order to divide, replicate, and survive.
- Genes to traits - Understand what genes are and how they link to traits, the physical nature of the gene and molecular processes underlying the “Central Dogma” of molecular biology.
- Genes to traits - Compare and contrast the gene regulatory mechanisms between bacteria and eukaryotes.
- Genes to traits - Describe sexual reproduction through meiosis and how it contributes to genetic variation.
- Genes to traits - Conceptual knowledge of viruses and synthetic biology
- Inheritance and Evolution - Explain and compare processes contributing to genetic variability, including but not limited to: mutation, recombination, transformation, gene flow, horizontal gene transfer, gene and genome duplication, genetic drift, and natural selection.
- Inheritance and Evolution - Understand the relationship between recombination and linkage.
- Inheritance and Evolution - How to detect if evolution is occurring by calculating allele frequencies.
Assessment Tasks:
- Practical preparation and participation In-person: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Mini Test 1 Identity Verified In-person: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Mini Test 2 Identity Verified In-person: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of semester exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 60%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week Practical 3 Hours/ Week

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=BIOL2015 =====
Subject: Ecology Field Studies (BIOL2015)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Ecology and sampling methods primarily taught over a five-day field trip in the June/July recess to K'gari (Fraser Island). Soil and vegetation assessment in a range of habitats (from coastal sand dunes to bush to rainforests), ecology of various animals (from crabs to frogs to birds and mammals). There are additional costs for the field trip. Preference will be given to students who have taken or are enrolled in BIOL2010, students enrolled in the Ecology and Conservation Biology Major and students requiring this course to fulfill their degree. Other students will be considered if places are available. There is a quota of 54 students attached to the course. The field trip is offered in the June/July break - not the mid-semester break.
