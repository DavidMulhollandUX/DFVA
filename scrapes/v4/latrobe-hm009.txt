## Master of Dietetics - Melbourne (Bundoora)
##### HM009

You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
If you're passionate about healthy eating and well-being, and want to promote better understanding of the benefits of nutrition in diverse communities and settings, La Trobe's Master of Dietetics has been established to reflect the emerging needs of this changing profession.

La Trobe has a long history of nutrition education, and our new contemporary Master of Dietetics both builds on that history and embraces newfound developments and changing ideologies. Our person-centred, socially conscious approach to nutrition science favours the teaching of flexible concepts over dogmatic facts, and aids you in establishing a strong professional approach and individual identity.

Applying your research skills in diverse practice settings, utilise La Trobe's long-term industry partnerships to gain on-the-ground experience in clinical and community spaces. Your experience will prepare you for work in priority sectors, with focus on diversity, community inclusion and counselling.

Our approach to dietetics values the importance of life-long learning, and teaches you how to incorporate novel technologies, cutting-edge science and emerging evidence into your developing practice. Graduate ready to succeed in your career, and ready to help people.

With La Trobe's Master of Dietetics, you’ll learn:

  * Care, counselling, medical nutrition  * Learn essential skills in nutrition assessment, diagnosis and counselling. Understanding medical nutrition therapy and patient support.

  * Individual case management  * During 50 days of placement, you’ll apply your clinical knowledge, counselling skills and the nutrition care process to support the health of individuals and groups.

  * Food service  * Through placement and practical classes, you’ll explore the food systems utilised in clinical settings, and support vulnerable and disadvantaged populations.

  * Entrepreneurship  * Get the skills to succeed as a professional dietitian. Learn how to set up your own business and explore professional ethics, leadership and management.

  * Future-focused topics  * Explore future-ready issues (sustainability, indigenous health) and specialist areas of dietetic practice. Use emerging, priority areas (mental health, disability, aged care) as a conceptual framework to explore interdisciplinary practice. Work with carers and translators, learn community impact and advocacy.

  * Professional identity and diversity  * Discover the scope and diversity of dietetic roles, from professional practice and standards through to the impact of colonisation and hierarchical systems on healthcare.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Critically analyse research evidence to enable knowledge translation for ethically informed and evidence based food, nutrition and dietetic practice and synthesise innovative and creative professional solutions in a dynamic environment
**2.**
Critically reflect on practice to identify learning needs and take a responsible and proactive approach to ongoing professional development
**3.**
Communicate, educate and advocate effectively with professional and non-professional stakeholders in a variety of settings, using a number of modes and media, and including demonstration of advanced digital literacy
**4.**
Critically analyse and demonstrate mastery of food, nutrition, biological, psychological and social sciences in order to plan, implement and evaluate food, nutrition and dietetic practice in diverse and changing contexts
**5.**
Exhibit autonomy, collaboration and leadership in decision-making in the ethical application of professional knowledge and skills in a range of food, nutrition and health related contexts
**6.**
Demonstrate mastery in the application of specialist knowledge in accordance with the required dietetic competencies
**7.**
Integrate and apply diverse understandings and strategies to support health and wellbeing among individuals, groups and communities

### Work based learning (placement) requirements
Compulsory placements (Work Based Learning)
During this course, you will participate in one or more supervised Work Based Learning (WBL) placements designed to allow you to practice newly acquired skills, behaviours and knowledge while integrating theory with practice. The placements will immerse you in professional work culture, enabling you to develop and further enhance your skills. WBL provides you with the opportunity to gain valuable industry experience at one or more of our local, interstate or international industry partners or agencies. WBL placements will generally be arranged by the Partnership Operations Team in the Office of the Provost at La Trobe University. Students may incur additional costs and it may be necessary to travel or relocate to complete a specific placement. Please refer to the individual subject entries for more information.
### Work integrated learning opportunities
Industry presentations, Industry informed curriculum, Industry-informed projects, Professional simulations
### Career outcomes
After graduation, you’ll be ready to work as a registered dietitian. You could find employment in a growing range of clinical and community settings, including:

  * Hospitals
  * Help hospital patients recover from injury or illness.

  * Private practice
  * Work in private practice to provide care for a wide range of individuals and help them reach their health goals.

  * Research
  * Work in universities and other research organisations to further our understanding of nutrition. Work in the food industry to develop food products.

  * Sports associations
  * Provide individual care and support to improve performance and health outcomes.

  * Community nutrition
  * Provide nutritional education for general good health and promote nutrition through community-level education.

Subject: DIETETIC PRACTICE AND COUNSELLING SKILLS (DTN5001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will examine the foundations of dietetic practice, including scope of practice, diversity of dietetic roles, professional behaviours, the national competency standards for dietitians and the importance of reflective practice and lifelong learning. You will develop communication and counselling skills for effective dietetic practice to engage and empower people as equal partners in their healthcare. This subject includes professional learning, practical experiences or portfolio activities which contribute to the completion of the Career Ready AdvantageTM award.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically analyse and apply counselling behaviour change theory in food and nutrition contexts.
- Evaluate the foundations of dietetic practice, including scope of practice, professional behaviours, the national competency standards for dietitians and perform critical reflective practice to construct an emerging professional Dietitian Identity.
- Develop and implement evidence-based communication strategies tailored to individuals' diverse health literacy levels and preferences, fostering active engagement and empowerment in healthcare decision-making processes.
- Critically reflect on the impact of emotions on practice and devise strategies to take responsibility for managing personal health and wellbeing.
- Critically evaluate processes for the collection, assessment and interpretation of athropometric, clinic and dietary data of individuals.
- Recognise strength-based approaches for culturally safe communication to foster trust and building of respectful relationship with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples.

Subject: MEDICAL NUTRITION THERAPY (DTN5002)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: The focus of this subject is on teaching you the key medical nutrition therapy and practice skills required to practice as a dietitian, including collecting data from clients and building clinical knowledge to formulate and modify intervention plans. In this subject, you will be introduced to the concept of medical nutrition therapy. You will learn about diet-disease relationships and how to locate, evaluate and apply evidence-based guidelines for the dietary management of a range of conditions. You will be introduced to the nutrition care process (nutrition assessment, nutrition diagnoses, nutrition intervention, nutrition monitoring and evaluation). This subject is taught at the city campus as a number of lectures will be delivered by clinical dietetics experts who work in large tertiary hospitals across metropolitan Melbourne.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically reflect on diet-disease relationships and medical nutrition therapy guidelines and demonstrate understanding in professional practice
- Collect and interpret medical, cultural, social, psychological, economic, personal, environmental, and dietary information using established nutrition assessment methods.
- Synthesize relevant nutrition assessment data to determine the nutrition status of individuals and make appropriate nutrition diagnoses.
- Develop and effectively communicate nutrition care plans to accepted standards for relevant audiences.
- Integrate nutrition assessment data and use clinical reasoning to prioritise key issues, formulate goals and objectives, and prepare individualised, realistic nutrition intervention plans.
- Implement appropriate nutrition education and counselling intervention strategies based on outcomes of nutrition assessment.

- Critically evaluate and explore a variety of tools and technologies to support the nutrition care process.
- Critically reflect on complex diet – disease relationships and demonstrate an understanding of this in professional practice.
- Perform nutrition assessments, demonstrating problem-solving, clinical reasoning and tailored approaches for individuals.
- Systematically implement, evaluate and adapt nutrition care plans, programs and services in accordance with legislation, policy or standards.
- Critically evaluate the roles, responsibilities, and priorities of interprofessional team members and employ collaborative practice to create nutrition plans that meet the shared vision of healthcare teams

Subject: DIETETICS FOR A DIVERSE SOCIETY (DTN5004)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will explore the diversity of the society we live in and how diversity impacts on health and dietetic practice. You will learn about principles of public health nutrition including the socio-ecological approach to health prevention. This subject will equip you to advocate for ethical and evidence-based solutions to address the nutrition-related health of individuals, groups and communities. You will critically analyse and apply the nutrition care process in public health settings. You will learn about Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander history and colonisation. In addition, you will engage with individuals with lived experiences of marginalisation to learn about systems of power, privilege, and oppression and undertake self-reflection to understand your place in these systems. This subject includes professional learning, practical experiences or portfolio activities which contribute to the completion of the Career Ready AdvantageTM award.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop teamwork strategies to effectively perform duties related to curriculum groupwork as part of developing professional practice
- Critically analyse and reflect upon social, historical and political significance of a range of cultural food practices and foodways exploring implications for communities and dietitians.
- Critically reflect on colonisation, systemic racism, and the social, cultural, behavioural, and economic factors which impact Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples’ health outcomes and demonstrate an understanding of how this might influence dietetic practice and outcomes.
- Evaluate the factors that influence public health nutrition priorities and critically appraise current public health nutrition priorities and programs in Australia.
- Critically appraise socio-ecological approaches in health promotion and public health nutrition that aim to influence population nutritional status.
- Acknowledge and critically reflect on the ways in which your own culture, values, beliefs, attitudes, biases, assumptions, privilege and power may influence your dietetic practice and critically appraise social structures which perpetuate systems of power and oppression, and the impacts of these systems on diverse peoples and communities.

Subject: DIETETICS FOR MENTAL HEALTH, DISABILITY AND AGED CARE (DTN5005)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will use three emerging areas of dietetic practice – Mental Health, Disability and Aged Care – as a conceptual framework to explore advanced practice dietetic skills, including those related to: working within interdisciplinary teams; person-centred care, working with carers and translators; prioritisation and advocacy; the impact of medications on nutrition care planning. You will explore the bidirectional relationship between clinical, social, economic, psychological and personal factors in ageing populations and individuals with a disability or experiencing mental health concerns. You will also explore the bidirectional relationship between nutrition and mental health. Using relevant examples, you will develop an understanding of modifiable and non-modifiable factors that influence the human gut microbiome, and the role of the gut microbiome in mediating the efficacy of dietary interventions. You will practice modifying foods for vulnerable populations in kitchen practicals.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically review and reflect upon opportunities, roles and skills required for dietitians to improve health outcomes in emerging dietetic workforce sectors such as aged care, mental health and disability
- Create nutrition plans and/or programs that demonstrate an understanding of the practical, cognitive, pharmacological and dietary issues impacting older people and people with mental health concerns and disabilities
- Critically evaluate and apply collaborative practice and advanced communication and counselling skills for priority populations, and their health care teams, families, carers and interpreters.
- Develop strategies to effectively and safely respond to disclosure of mental health concerns.
- Develop and apply key dietetic practice skills, including interprofessional practice, advocacy and prioritisation, to improve the food and nutrition of priority populations

- Critically review and reflect on the impact of whole systems including health and education as responsible for improving Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander health and collaborate with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander individuals and/or communities to advocate for social justice and health equity for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples.
