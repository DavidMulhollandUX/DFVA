## Description
The Bachelor of Economics / Bachelor of Advanced Science (Honours) program enables students to complete an in-depth course of study in advanced science from within UNSW Science which complements the economics program offered by the School of Economics in the UNSW Business School.

### 3566 Economics / Advanced Science (Honours) Special arrangements for Mathematics and Economics courses
Students should do the MATH courses which are required in the Advanced Science (Honours) specialisations they choose – eg. Mathematics and Statistics specialisations specify MATH1131/MATH1231 or MATH1141/MATH1241 rather than MATH1031/MATH1041. With a few of the Advanced Science (Honours) specialisations only one MATH course is required, in which case students will be required to complete one of either ECON1202 or ECON1203.
For ECON1202, the substitute courses are: MATH1031 or MATH1131 or MATH1141.
For ECON1203, the substitute courses are: MATH 1041 or MATH1231 or MATH1241.
The ECON courses are not substitutes for these MATH courses. No credit will be given if students also complete the ECON courses.
ECON1203 may be substituted by PSYC2001 for those students who also do a major in Psychology.
The courses ECON1202 and ECON1203 can only count towards the BEc component of the degree.

Subject: Issues in Financial Reporting and Analysis (ACCT3563)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Aspiring to become a CFO, audit partner, professional investor, or accredited accountant? You'll need a deep understanding of the complex topics and significant issues influencing corporate reporting outcomes. This course covers critical reporting choices financial leaders make and the ethical, theoretical, market, and other influences that bear on these choices. You will also learn how to account for complex matters such as financial instruments and leases, along with how accounting information affects share prices. Mastering the skills from this course will enable you analyse and positively influence accounting decisions that shape a firm's bottom line.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and evaluate current controversial trends in financial accounting.
- Describe alternative theories that explain accounting practices.
- Evaluate measurement issues, for example, fair value versus historical cost, related to the covered topics.
- Work collaboratively to complete a group assignment and class activities.
- Make judgments about measurement and recognition issues in relevant areas of accounting.
- Communicate clearly to a professional standard appropriate to an audience of senior accounting executives.

Subject: Superannuation and Retirement Benefits (ACTL3191)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course provides a comprehensive analysis of the economics and finance of retirement income provision. It is designed to provide students with a solid understanding of the theoretical and public-policy issues associated with the design and implementation of policies for retirement income provision in Australia and internationally. Topics covered include: demographic trends and the economic impact of population ageing; rationale for public provision and/or regulation of private retirement income provision; assessment of alternative models for retirement income provision; retirement income provision in Australia; the taxation and regulation of retirement savings and benefits; the structure, performance and efficiency of the superannuation industry; and international comparisons in policy design and implementation.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain key economic and financial principles underlying the efficient accumulation and decumulation of retirement savings.
- Describe and use the complex terminology surrounding the field of superannuation and retirement benefits.
- Evaluate the design and implementation of polices for retirement income provision in Australia and internationally.
- Analyse the superannuation industry and assess the regulations that govern it.

Subject: Retirement Saving and Spending Over the Lifecycle (ACTL3192)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Helping individuals make better financial decisions and improve their financial well-being over the lifecycle represents a grand challenge for government, business, and not-for-profit organisations. This synthesis course provides opportunities for students to work in diverse teams to solve real-world problems related to this grand challenge through applying, integrating and synthesising their knowledge and skills from multi-disciplinary perspectives. This course will be a fully problem-based learning experience. Throughout the course, students will apply their multi-disciplinary knowledge, critical thinking, problem-solving and teamwork skills to solve real industry problems. Students will have opportunities to engage with and receive feedback from industry partners. Building on students' pre-existing knowledge in their specific disciplinary domain, this course will cover the following topics to further equip students with relevant theoretical framework, industry knowledge and research insights to solve the industry problems: lifecycle theories of consumption and saving; human capital and the retirement decision; lifecycle investment strategy; risks facing individual retirees and providers of retirement income products (employment risk, investment risk, longevity risk, inflation risk, health risk); retirement income products; behavioural explanations for non-rational saving and investment behaviours including myopia, procrastination, mental accounting, framing, loss aversion etc. NOTE: This course is equivalent to ECON3117 Retirement Saving and Spending over the Lifecycle, which is no longer offered. Students intending to enrol in ECON3117 should instead enrol in this course.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically reflect on your professional skills and behaviours, including identified gaps, and how you will adapt to a changing business environment.
- Access, manage and use the appropriate digital resources and tools to deliver your recommendations or conclusions.
- Examine the influences of the global business environment on key ethical or sustainability considerations in relation to the contemporary business problem.
- Identify, apply and adapt business skills and knowledge acquired from across your program to critically analyse a contemporary business challenge.

To support skill development and peer connection across research groups, students will complete a self-paced online module and participate in a 1-hour interactive workshop each week. These activities provide guidance on how to complete the project milestones and assessments (e.g., literature review, video presentation), as well as introduce key skills and activities in academic research (e.g., ethics, data analysis and communicating results). By completing this course, students will gain important research and transferable graduate skills that are highly valued across a range of graduate pathways and will prepare them for further research opportunities in their program.
Learning Outcomes:
- Communicate approaches, outcomes and implications of a scientific investigation.
- Respectfully discuss relevant scientific topics through a variety of formats.
- Refine an individual research question and approach, considering the available research tools and relevant scientific literature.
- Apply discipline-specific research and data analysis skills and capabilities to investigate problems in science.
- Evaluate personal and collaborative effectiveness and make suggestions to enhance research progress.

Subject: Graduation Portfolio (SCIF3010)
Credit Points: 0
Overview: You've spent years developing scientific knowledge and skills—but can you confidently explain your unique value to potential employers? Do you know how your science degree translates to career opportunities beyond the lab? Can you articulate what makes you stand out in a competitive job market? These questions and more will be addressed in SCIF3010 Graduation Portfolio, your final step in the Bachelor of Science or Bachelor of Advanced Science programs. SCIF3010 is a compulsory capstone course that must be completed in the final term of your enrolment . Through three focused fully online modules delivered via Moodle and the SciConnect platform, you'll explore target industries and career pathways, assess your program learning outcomes and transferable skills, and develop a compelling digital portfolio that showcases your capabilities. The course requires approximately 15 hours of engagement and uses a Satisfactory/Unsatisfactory grading basis. By completion, you'll have transformed your academic achievements into a powerful professional narrative, equipped with the tools and confidence needed to successfully transition from university student to science professional. An overall Satisfactory grade is required to progress to graduation from your Bachelor of Science or Bachelor of Advanced Science program.
Learning Outcomes:
- Research the landscape of a target industry and the professional skills used in a chosen field.
- Identify, evidence, and articulate Program Learning Outcomes, UNSW Graduate Attributes, and transferable skills through digital portfolio development.
- Use written communication skills to convey unique value as a graduate through professional branding and effective presentation of career readiness to potential employers.
