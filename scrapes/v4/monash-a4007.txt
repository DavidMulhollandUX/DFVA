## Graduate Certificate of Communications and Media Studies
##### A4007
##### 24 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
There has never been a more important or exciting time to study the mediated world.

This course builds your understanding of the changing contexts of media and communication practices. You will gain insight into the key issues affecting the communications and media industries and you will examine these developments from cultural, political and theoretical perspectives.

This course develops an understanding of the contemporary communications and media environment. It is suitable to those pursuing careers in a wide range of communications and media roles, including in the areas of communications, media relations, social media, digital engagement, public policy and public relations.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
relate communications and media theory to politics, public administration, business and the everyday;
**2.**
communicate research findings in a range of formats to professional and non-professional audiences.
**3.**
utilise research skills to undertake a short, guided research task;
**4.**
explain diverse aspects of communications and media, including policy, content and technology change over time;
**5.**
analyse and critically evaluate a range of texts and cultural products pertaining to communications and media;

### Progression to further studies
Successful completion of this course may provide a pathway to the A6003 Master of Communications and Media Studies.

### AHEGS
The Graduate Certificate of Communications and Media Studies is a postgraduate award, taught in English. This course normally takes six months of full-time study or one year of part-time study or equivalent. Graduates have knowledge of the key ideas of communications and media studies (or international relations) and skills to apply these ideas in professional contexts. The Graduate Certificate of Communications and Media Studies is a Level 8 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Doing media and communications research (APG5050)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The unit introduces you to principles of problem-solving in communication and media research. It considers a range of research methods - including textual analysis, content analysis, interviews, ethnographic fieldwork, audience research and digital methods. You will learn the techniques of different research methods, as well as how to critically assess your appropriateness for different problems in the field. The unit develops your skills in doing and designing academic research in media and communications.
Learning Outcomes:
- apply professional ethics standards for media and communications research.
- design effective research questions and select appropriate research methods;
- analyse the role of research methods in addressing key challenges in media and communications;
- critically examine the major research methods used in communication and media studies;
Assessment Tasks:
- Research proposal: 50% (Hurdle: None)
- Annotated Bibliography: 40% (Hurdle: None)
- In-class exercise: 10% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Scheduled activities may include a combination of teacher directed learning, peer directed learning and online engagement.

Subject: Principles of global media and communications (APG5365)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit will develop foundation knowledge and skills in the field of global media and communications at graduate level. It engages with a range of contemporary professional media and communications practice and introduces principles and theories appropriate to understanding and analysing them. Particular focus is given to understanding the critical roles played by communications fields and industries across diverse sectors including politics, government, business, culture and everyday society. These roles are further explored in the context of the complexities posed by a world of digitalisation and globalisation.
Learning Outcomes:
- read and critically analyse scholarly publications in media and communications;
- critically appraise the relationship between theory and practice in the field of media and communications;
- Discuss and critically evaluate the role of media and communications in contemporary global societies and its relationship with a range of different sectors from local to global levels.
- identify and analyse core concepts and theories underpinning contemporary media and communications approaches;
Assessment Tasks:
- Demonstration: 10% (Hurdle: None)
- Critical Review: 40% (Hurdle: None)
- In - Class Participation Activity: 10% (Hurdle: None)
- Essay: 50% (Hurdle: None)

Subject: Media and social change (APG5397)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit explores the capacity of media and communications to advance social change. It provides you with an appreciation of how media discourse and representation have shaped our understanding of important social issues and conflicts. It also explores media and communications have been used creatively and strategically to address significant social problems. Drawing on a range of global case studies of social change media, the unit provides you with an opportunity to critically assess the extent to which media organisations, institutions and practices can shape public debates, illuminate important issues and serve social benefits.
Learning Outcomes:
- communicate research findings and insights in a clear, concise, accurate and engaging way using written and media elements.
- conduct independent research into media representation of social issues using relevant media and communications methods;
- assess the place of media and communications in shaping our understanding of, and response to, important social issues and debates;
- critically analyse and evaluate the capacity of media and communications to address significant social problems and conflicts;
Assessment Tasks:
- Media Task: 50% (Hurdle: None)
- Work In Progress Report: 40% (Hurdle: None)
- In-class participation activity: 10% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Scheduled activities may include a combination of teacher directed learning, peer directed learning and online engagement.

Subject: Digital cultures and platforms (APG5457)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit examines the effect of digital platforms on the media and communications industries. It will explore the transnational circulation of content, cultures, technology and profits in the contemporary media industries. The unit examines the emergence of big platform corporations that have created new centres of media power and influence. You will evaluate new and established media platforms and develop an in-depth understanding of the content producer and user cultures that have emerged alongside them.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the political, economic and regulatory contexts for contemporary media platforms and their impacts on wider media and communications industries;
- Research and analyse the business models of media and communications platforms and the content producers who use them;
- Analyse and discuss digital media user and production cultures;
- Communicate research findings and insights in a clear, concise, accurate and engaging way using written and media elements.
