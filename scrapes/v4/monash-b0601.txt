## Diploma of Business
##### B0601
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
The Diploma of Business is your first step towards gaining your Monash Bachelor of Business and Commerce degree.

You’ll learn how to work independently, think critically and make analytical decisions, while being introduced to the business concepts and theory you’ll need for University.

In the second year, you'll study the same units as your first-year University students and develop your specialist knowledge of business and commerce.

### AQF level
Level 5 - Diploma
### Post-nominals
DipBus
### Course duration (full time)


### Structure
This course is structured in two parts Part A. Foundation knowledge and Part B. Discipline knowledge.

Part A. Foundation studies

These units will provide you with the foundational business concepts and theory and practical skills you’ll need for University.

Part B. Discipline studies

These units are first-year University units that will provide you with specialist knowledge in business and commerce to enable progression to your destination bachelor degree.

Course progression map

The course progression map provides guidance on unit enrolment for each teaching period.

### Requirements
The course comprises 98 credit points structured in two parts: Part A. Foundation knowledge (50 credit points) and Part B. Discipline knowledge (48 credit points).

Units are 6 credit points unless otherwise stated.

Part A. Foundation studies (50 credit points)

You must complete the following nine units (50 credit points):

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
utilise technical skills to demonstrate understanding and problem solving in relation to business issues involving diverse stakeholders
**2.**
identify and communicate advice in a variety of business disciplines to address business problems in accord with management requirements
**3.**
critically apply theoretical and technical skills to solve problems in relation to a range of business disciplines
**4.**
manage work priorities and coordinate the work of others in accord with parameters set by management.
**5.**
demonstrate knowledge of technical and theoretical issues in a variety of business disciplines including management, marketing, statistics, accounting, business law, finance and economics

### Progression to further studies
This diploma leads to the destination courses listed below. To be eligible to apply for an internal course transfer to gain admission into the second year of a destination course, you must achieve the average mark required for the destination courses:

  * Bachelor of Business and Commerce (B2026) - 50%

### AHEGS
The Diploma of Business is an undergraduate award. The course is taught in English. Graduates have gained a range of technical and problem solving skills relevant to the business sector. Students demonstrate their ability to successfully undertake study at the university level. The course takes one and a half year of full-time study or part-time equivalent to complete. The Diploma of Business is a Level 5 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Business law (BTW1042)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit is designed for non-lawyers and its emphasis is on general legal principles that underpin business activities. It teaches you how to conclude enforceable contracts, mitigate unnecessary legal risks, avoid negligence when giving professional advice, and set up an appropriate business entity. The unit also covers other essential topics like the basic principles of the Malaysian legal system, consumer protection, and agency law. While the topics are discussed with reference to Malaysian laws - given the jurisdictional nature of the law, comparative aspects with other common law jurisdictions are also made to highlight notable legal developments. The unit’s emphasis is on critical thinking and problem-solving skills, without which you will not be able to solve the authentic, problem-based assessments inspired by real-life scenarios.
Learning Outcomes:
- identify the salient features of the Malaysian legal system, with a focus on its law making institutions, the interpretation of statutes and the various dispute resolution techniques
- apply the principles of contract law, tort law, and agency law to hypothetical legal problems
- examine the legal differences between partnerships and corporations
- demonstrate basic legal research using primary and secondary sources
- analyse the comparative aspects of Common Law within the context of business law topics
