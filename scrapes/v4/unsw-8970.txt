## Description
The Master of Counselling at UNSW is a dynamic program designed to prepare you for the mental health and wellbeing challenges of today’s complex world. Combining flexible online learning, a mandatory four-day in-person intensive at the UNSW Sydney campus, and a 200-hour supervised counselling placement arranged where possible within a reasonable travel distance of your home or work, the program ensures you develop practical skills and professional readiness.

Grounded in humanistic and person-centred approaches, informed by critical theories and practices, the program fosters counsellors who meet clients with authenticity and empathy, upholding the inherent dignity of each person within their unique social and structural contexts. A central focus of the program is on your personal as well as your professional development, building your capacity for effective therapeutic relationships that are the foundation for adjustment, growth, and meaningful change. You will deepen your understanding of contemporary counselling frameworks, explore specialist areas through electives, and learn from expert practitioners in a supportive, evidence-informed environment.

## Program Learning Outcomes (CILOs)
On successful completion of this program, students will be able to:

1. Apply an integrated understanding of knowledges informing counselling and the therapeutic relationship across diverse and complex client contexts
2. Utilise advanced and integrated supervision and counselling skills through reflective and culturally responsive practice
3. Effectively apply ethical and professional standards and advanced practices in counselling
5. Evaluate and integrate counselling frameworks and practices
4. Creatively apply inclusive counselling knowledges to develop an integrative counselling practice in digital and in-person contexts
6. Critically evaluate counselling theories and evidence to inform counselling practice and research

## Career Opportunities & Employment Areas
Counsellor, Clinical Supervisor, Practice Lead, Mental Health Coordinator, Youth Worker, Support Worker

## Professional Recognition & Accreditation
As a graduate, you’ll be eligible for professional registration as a counsellor with the Australian Counselling Association, ready to embark on a meaningful and rewarding career as an accredited counsellor.

Students must complete 72 Units of Credit (UOC) structured across the following academic components:

## Progression & Academic Rules
If you wish to exit this program early you may nominate to transfer to and graduate from the Graduate Certificate or Graduate Diploma in Counselling provided the requirements for the relevant program have been met.

## Admission Requirements
**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
A Bachelor degree or higher-level qualification in a cognate discipline* with a credit average (equivalent to a UNSW WAM of 65).

OR

A Bachelor degree or higher-level qualification in any discipline with a credit average (equivalent to a UNSW WAM of 65), plus 2 years relevant work experience**.

*Cognate Disciplines may include:

- Psychology, Social Work, Human Services, Education, Youth Work, Community Services, Health Sciences, Nursing, Mental Health , Behavioural Science, Social Science, Career Development, Law

**Relevant Work Experience may include:

- Applicants should demonstrate relevant experience reflecting professional readiness, that may include supportive work, effective communication, facilitation or mentoring. Part-time roles and internships undertaken during undergraduate studies will not be counted

Applicants who do not meet the above entry requirements may be eligible for entry into the Graduate Certificate or Graduate Diploma of Counselling. If the student successfully completes ZZCO5001, ZZCO5002, ZZCO5003, and ZZCO5004, they will then be eligible to articulate into the Masters.

**Limitations on Recognition of Prior Learning**:
Students may be eligible for up to 24 UOC of Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL). Credit will not be granted for the following courses: ZZCO5005 Applied Counselling Approaches, ZZCO5008 Advanced Counselling Practice, and ZZCO5014 Integrated Counselling Practice.

Subject: Foundational Counselling Skills (ZZCO5001)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course introduces you to the foundations of the therapeutic relationship and use of self in counselling. The course orients you to a humanistic, person-centred approach to counselling. Utilising the online environment, you will build your capacity to use counselling skills in digital contexts. You will apply First Nations knowledges (such as deep listening) while fostering reflective practice and critical self-awareness, through experiential and reflective learning. You will engage with experiential learning in small groups and roleplays to develop foundational counselling skills and practices, including the use of digital and online counselling.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate understanding of relevant therapeutic approaches
- Articulate the components of the therapeutic relationship
- Demonstrate core counselling skills
- Critically appraise your counselling skills

Subject: Ethics in Counselling Practice (ZZCO5002)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course introduces you to the ethical foundations and professional standards, informed by the Australian Counselling Association (ACA) that guide your development as a counsellor and your counselling practice. You will explore ethical theories and decision-making frameworks while emphasising reflexivity as central to professional identity. You will consider key issues of ethical practice, including boundaries, authenticity, and cultural responsiveness in the therapeutic relationship. You will be introduced to supervision as a site of ethical and reflective practice and critically consider emerging issues such as digital ethics (including use of AI) in counselling.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the use of supervision in ethical and reflective counselling practice
- Evaluate the role of professional standards in counselling
- Demonstrate an understanding of ethics, boundaries, authenticity, and cultural responsiveness in the therapeutic relationship
- Critically reflect on relevant ethical issues and theoretical approaches in counselling practice

Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course engages you in critical exploration of counselling practice within diverse personal, social, geographical and cultural contexts. - Evaluate the role of digital technologies in counselling, including accessibility and inclusivity
- Critically reflect on how social and cultural diversities shape counselling practices
- Articulate how First Nations knowledges and relevant frameworks inform inclusive and socially just counselling practices

Subject: Counselling for Grief and Loss (ZZCO5007)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This elective course will equip you with specialised knowledges and skills to work with end-of-life, death, dying, loss, and bereavement across the lifespan. You will interrogate contemporary theories and models informing grief and loss and critically reflect on cultural and spiritual dimensions of grief. Learning will incorporate systemic and political dimensions of loss including contemporary challenges such as climate grief. Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate an understanding of dying, and bereavement across the lifespan including cultural and spiritual dimensions
- Utilise advanced skills and practices in grief and loss counselling
- Interrogate contemporary theories and models informing grief and loss
- Articulate critical issues pertaining to grief, death and dying

Subject: Advanced Counselling Practice (ZZCO5008)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course builds on and extends your counselling knowledge, skills and practices to ground and prepare you for your first counselling placement(s). Exploring the counselling encounter with respect to diversities and contexts, you will develop advanced counselling practices that consider and apply professional standards, ethics and risk. The experiential learning in this course will extend your counselling proficiency to in-person environments. An in-person residential workshop will provide an in-depth opportunity to build your applied counselling practices and skills with your peers and guided by counselling practitioner teachers. This unique forum provides an interactive and collaborative opportunity to use deliberate practice to implement essential advanced counselling skills, including risk assessment. The workshop will also utilise innovative reflective techniques, to extend your capacity to form effective supervisory relationships, develop your reflective capacity, and attend to self-care.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate effective counselling skills in simulated in-person encounters utilising in person engagement.
- Critically apply counselling knowledge and practices to the counselling encounter as a whole
- Demonstrate an understanding of risk and risk assessment
- Use reflection and feedback to enhance your development as a counsellor and your counselling practices

- Critically reflect on your professional development as an accredited counsellor

Subject: Counselling Practice for Trauma, Crisis, and Violence (ZZCO5012)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This elective develops advanced counselling practices for working with people impacted by trauma, crisis, and violence across diverse contexts, including for example family and gendered violence, disasters, displacement, and intergenerational trauma. Interactive learning will orient to understanding the self of the counsellor, self care and applied skills and practices. The course builds and extends trauma-informed practice, integrating cultural responsiveness and intersectionality to recognise how social location, power, and systems influence help-seeking, and recovery. You will critically examine and apply risk assessment, counselling approaches and phased, evidence-informed frameworks including for example EMDR, and TF-CBT and the use of digital and online counselling technologies.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically analyse evidence-informed approaches to trauma, crisis, and violence, and their application across diverse contexts.
- Integrate advanced skills and applied practice in working with trauma, crisis, and violence
- Articulate an understanding of the contexts impacting trauma, crisis, and violence.
