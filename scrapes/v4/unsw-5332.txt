## Description
The Graduate Diploma in Advanced Psychology is designed to enable students who have completed a foundational sequence in Psychology to consolidate and extend their existing knowledge of the theories, perspectives, and practices in psychology, in addition to undertaking a significant research project. A key purpose of the program is also to provide an academic foundation for students, particularly those with the intention of pursuing a career in the professional practice of psychology, to continue on to further post-graduate study (e.g. Masters).

Students will conduct a year-long, 24 unit of credit (UOC) independent research project under the guidance of an academic supervisor. This is to culminate in the production of a written thesis based on the project, which is subject to independent assessment. In addition, students will undertake 24 UOC of advanced-level coursework, comprising 3 core courses and 1 elective.

The research project is conducted over 6 Hexamesters, and is divided into 6 units aligning with the major components of a research thesis: Introduction (over 2 hexamesters), Methods, Results (over 2 hexamesters), and Discussion/Thesis production. Potential options of focus area for the research project may include: psychopathology, mental health and well-being, social psychology, learning, cognition, personality, or developmental psychology. Content of the core coursework comprises statistical methods, the history of psychology, professional ethics, and evidence-based practice. The elective coursework component comprises advanced coverage of contemporary research and theory in a select range of topic areas that align with the expertise and research specialisations of academic staff.

The program is conducted 100% online, and may be undertaken in one year full-time. Completion of the GDipAdvPsych will qualify students to apply for entry into APAC-accredited Level 3-4 programs (e.g. Masters or PhD).

The Graduate Diploma is designed to provide the appropriate training in level 2 pre-professional competencies as defined by the accreditation standards published by the Australian Psychology Accreditation Council (APAC) on behalf of the Psychology Board of Australia (PsyBA). In doing this, it is specifically targeted at students seeking a pathway into professional practice (by subsequent training in a Masters-level program). Students wishing to progress instead to PhD programs are recommended to apply for the full-time, face-to-face Psychology Honours programs offered by the School of Psychology at UNSW.

## Program Learning Outcomes (CILOs)
On successful completion of this program, students will be able to:

1. Contribute to psychological discourse and practice in a manner that is culturally appropriate, sensitive to the diversity of individuals, and upholds the values and ethics of psychology.
7. Implement advanced research skills in the investigation of empirical questions relevant to the discipline of psychology, including review and consolidation of existing evidence, generating novel ideas and formulating solutions to complex problems with intellectual independence.
2. Produce original work in a variety of formats that synthesises a broad range of psychological concepts, theoretical perspectives, and empirical findings.
5. Describe basic strategies for psychological assessment and critically evaluate the theoretical and empirical bases underpinning construction, implementation and interpretation of some of the more widely used psychological test instruments.
8. Apply critical and creative thinking skills to communicate a clear and coherent exposition of advanced theoretical knowledge and ideas within a specialised domain of psychology to a broad audience.
3. Adapt high-level knowledge of psychological principles and practice for application across diverse, real-world contexts.
6. Compare how psychological intervention strategies can be applied across a range of contexts, and construct a critical analysis of these strategies with reference to relevant frameworks for legal and ethical practice.
4. Communicate in a clear, empathetic, and responsive manner in the context of psychological practice and research via the application of foundational interviewing skills such as active listening, effective questioning, clarifying and reflecting, summarising and paraphrasing, and developing rapport.

Students must complete 48 Units of Credit (UOC) structured across the following academic components:

Students must take 24 UOC of the following courses.

Students must take 18 UOC of the following courses.

- **PSYC5202**: History and Professional Ethics in Psychology  [6 UOC]
- **PSYC5203**: Application of Psychological Practice  [6 UOC]

## Admission Requirements
**Limitations on Recognition of Prior Learning**:
Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL) cannot be granted for this program.

**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
The number of available places in the program varies from year to year and depends upon the availability of school resources.

Students must have completed a 3-year undergraduate Psychology degree, or an equivalent bridging sequence, that is accredited by the Australian Psychology Accreditation Council (APAC) on behalf of the Psychology Board of Australia (PsyBA) as fulfilling the requirements of the accredited Level 1 Foundational competencies in Psychology, within the last 10 years. The minimum requirement for eligibility to apply for entry to the program is to achieve a grade of at least 65.

Anyone with international psychology qualifications must have them assessed by the Australian Psychological Society (APS) prior to submitting an application.

Subject: History and Professional Ethics in Psychology  (PSYC5202)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This is a core course that forms part of the Graduate Diploma in Psychology (Advanced) program. The course covers the history and systems of psychology and the ethical issues for psychologists. The history and systems section of the course provides an historical overview to some of the enduring conceptual issues in psychology. It aims is to clarify contemporary treatments of these issues by considering their past. The ethics section of the course deals with the principles of ethical conduct by psychologists and places emphasis on ethical issues in professional practice. It provides students with an understanding and familiarity with the regulatory and legislative framework pertaining to the practice of professional psychology. The course will cover ethical issues in psychological research, including animal research. Students will gain advanced knowledge in psychology via a combination of online instructional videos and activities and small-group seminars. This course is taught entirely online.
Learning Outcomes:
- Articulate advanced disciplinary knowledge, clearly and effectively through written information.
- Discuss the APS code of Ethics, legislation, and legal obligations relevant to the practice of professional psychology.
- Manage ethical issues by using advanced knowledge of ethical frameworks.
- Evaluate the key theoretical concepts of the history and evolution of psychology as a discipline.
- Identify and solve ethical issues presented in the context of case studies from the discipline of psychological practice.

Subject: Behavioural Neuroscience (PSYC5204)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course provides an advanced examination of the processes of learning and memory and the neurobiological mechanism that underpin them. These include learning about relations between events (Pavlovian conditioning), learning about relations between one’s behaviour and events (Instrumental conditioning), and how these forms of learning control behaviours. Emphasis is placed on contemporary theories and approaches including the role of interactions between environmental events, synapses and genes. Topics include: appetitive and aversive motivation in learning, behaviour and psychopathology; Pavlovian conditioning; instrumental conditioning; the neural mechanisms that underpin memory. Students will gain advanced knowledge in Behavioural Neuroscience, via a combination of online instructional videos and activities and small-group seminars. This course is taught entirely online.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply advanced skills in critical thinking in order to analyse the current peer-reviewed research across a range of topics in the field of behavioural neuroscience.
- Relate advanced knowledge in the field of behavioural neuroscience to a wider community context, with a focus on the translation of current research to psychological interventions.
- Present current research and theory in the field of behavioural neuroscience in a clear and effective manner, in both oral and written formats.
- Evaluate key concepts in the field of behavioural neuroscience with an advanced understanding of the theoretical perspectives and empirical findings that underpin these.
- Demonstrate empathetic communication skills that are sensitive to the diversity of others.
