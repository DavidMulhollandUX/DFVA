## Description
The Graduate Diploma in Mine Geotechnical Engineering is offered as a specialist postgraduate coursework qualification for individuals who currently, or plan in the future to, work within the mining industry with particular responsibilities in the field of ground / strata control. The program has been designed to cater for people with different backgrounds, including either engineering or scientific tertiary qualifications and/or relevant experience.

The program is particularly designed for people working at mine sites, or those who have access to a mine site for practical assignments. The course is offered in a flexible delivery format, with a large component available in a distance format, plus a limited number of face-to-face workshops/sessions. Assessments will include a number of practical, site-based assignments, including group and interactive work and presentations.

The Graduate Diploma in Mine Geotechnical Engineering will be awarded after successful completion of 48 Units of Credit of coursework. A majority of the courses will be offered as modules over a short block period to permit employees of mining companies and associated service providers to attend on a part-time basis.

After completion of the Graduate Diploma in Mine Geotechnical Engineering students with a 65% WAM or higher can articulate to the Master of Mine Geotechnical Engineering.

## Admission Requirements
**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
Entry into the Graduate Diploma in Mine Geotechnical Engineering requires access to mine site information, which is essential to complete the requirements of these programs and one of the following:

- completion of a 4-year Bachelor of Engineering degree (or equivalent) in Mining Engineering or Civil Engineering with a minimum of one (1) year industry experience in Mining, or

- completion of a 3-year Engineering Science degree in a cognate discipline and a minimum of two (2) years industry experience, or

- at least three (3) years of relevant responsible industry experience for those with limited tertiary qualifications.

**Limitations on Recognition of Prior Learning**:
Exemptions: Recognised prior learning (RPL) may be available towards the program award for selected courses within the program. A maximum of 12 UOC of RPL will be considered on application to the Program Authority, and according to the UNSW RPL Procedure. Criteria for approval will include: equivalent course duration at a recognised institution, equivalent course content and assessment.

5059 Graduate Diploma in Mine Geotechnical Engineering Graduates: Graduates of program 5059 will be eligible to receive recognition of prior learning (RPL) for all courses (48 UOC) completed towards 8059 Master of Mine Geotechnical Engineering, subject to satisfying the UNSW Credit Transfer Rules and Faculty Enrolment Rules.

Subject: Mining Methods, Mine Planning and Applied Geomechanics (MINE5040)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this course, a range of mining methods used in underground mining and the core geotechnical parameters and criteria that affect the choice or application of the methods are presented. The course will cover a broad range of content of applied geomechanics in both hard rock and coal mines, including but not limited to: mine entry systems; pillar mechanics and design procedures; geomechanics of caving; airblasts and windblasts mechanisms and controls; dynamic rock failures; mine subsidence mechanics and design; geotechnical equipment considerations; mine planning considerations; geotechnical design methodologies (methods, excavations, pillars etc.). A range of case studies will supplement this course content. Activities include course presentations and student assignments.
Learning Outcomes:
- Recognise mine subsidence mechanics and design for subsidence control.
- Apply the principles of coal and hard rock pillar mechanics and design procedures.
- Review mine planning considerations and design methodologies.
- Develop knowledge of caving-based mining and caving mechanics and associated risks.
- Examine mining methods used in underground mining and the core geotechnical parameters in different mining methods.

Subject: Ground Control Principles and Practice in Underground Mining (MINE5050)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course explores the principles of rock reinforcement and ground support in underground excavations, focusing on the interaction between mining methods, excavation layouts, and reinforcement systems. You will learn about active and passive support mechanisms, support requirements for different excavation types, ground reaction curves, and load- and displacement-controlled support responses. The course also covers various ground support and reinforcement hardware, support system design, installation procedures, quality assurance, and time-dependent effects on support performance, including remedial options. A key focus is on risk-based ground support systems and their integration into ground control management, ensuring students understand how to optimise safety and stability in underground environments. Understanding ground support is critical for ensuring the safety, efficiency, and longevity of underground mining operations. This course equips you with the knowledge and practical insights needed to design, implement, and manage effective support systems in diverse geotechnical conditions, making it essential for those pursuing careers in mining, tunnelling, or geotechnical engineering.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply the principles of ground support installation and quality assurance.
- Evaluate and design ground support systems by selecting appropriate reinforcement hardware and related technologies for various geotechnical conditions.
- Analyse the interaction between mining methods, excavation layouts, and reinforcement systems to optimise ground support strategies.
- Explain the principles of rock reinforcement.
- Identify support requirements for different excavation types and mining methods.

Subject: Mining Geomechanics (MINE8140)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course is designed to introduce engineers and geologists to the major geomechanics components associated with mining operations, primarily underground. The course will cover the rock mass responses to excavations in both coal and metalliferous operations. It is, therefore, ideally suited to engineers or geologists who have an understanding and experience in the mining industry but are seeking to develop more specialist skills in the geomechanics field. The course content will include but not limited to the following components: Fundamentals of rock mechanics Overview of soil mechanics Rock mass classification Coupled rock behaviour Numerical methods in mine geomechanics Rock testing Managing risks in geotechnical engineering Geotechnical design principles and design acceptance The course is structured to provide an initial overview of basic principles applied to rock mechanics and geotechnical engineering, plus the major geomechanical properties and behavioural characteristics of rock material. This then leads to the application of these principles to the practical issues of site investigation, excavation design and ground reinforcement. An important component will be an emphasis on the interdependencies between geotechnical parameters and mine design/operational decisions and requirements. The link between geological and engineering disciplines is an important component in successfully managing these dependencies. The course will be presented through a series of lectures, facilitated discussion sessions and case studies, but will also include practical tutorial sessions and laboratory exercises.
Learning Outcomes:
- Show a good understanding of key numerical methods used in mining rock mechanics.
- Describe the basic mechanical properties of rock and how these are applied to analyse problems in mining geomechanics.
- Demonstrate a sound working knowledge of fundamental mechanisms and geotechnical principles within the context of practical mining applications.
- Recognise the role and importance of these principles in a comprehensive range of mining applications, both from a technical perspective, and from the risk and operational management perspective.

Subject: Geotechnical Data Collection and Analysis (MINE8680)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course provides an in-depth exploration of geotechnical data collection and analysis techniques essential for mining engineering. Students will learn about various rock mass classification systems, sources of ground stress, and the application of statistical methods. The course covers the fundamentals of seismic monitoring technologies, data processing, and interpretation, with a focus on real-world case studies. Key topics include: Classifying rock masses using methods such as Q, RMR, GSI, CMRR, and GSR. Understanding and managing sources of stress in the ground. Applying statistical methods to describe, model, visualise, and analyse geotechnical data. Implementing seismic monitoring technologies and interpreting seismic data for geotechnical purposes. Selecting and installing underground geotechnical monitoring systems. Measuring and interpreting data from excavations. Conducting mine hazard mapping and addressing design issues. The course emphasises the critical role of data interpretation and statistical analysis in geomechanics for effective design, study, and operational management. Practical examples and case studies will illustrate both effective and ineffective data use, highlighting the necessity of understanding data before applying it in design and numerical modelling. Assessment will consist of assignments designed to demonstrate practical competence and a deep understanding of the material. In-class tutorials will reinforce learning and provide hands-on experience, preparing students to develop and communicate effective geotechnical models and management plans.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and evaluate seismic monitoring technologies, data, applications, processing, and interpretation, while identifying potential challenges and implications, using real-world case studies.
- Assess and interpret geological and geotechnical data collected through various monitoring methods.
- Develop and communicate effective geotechnical models and management plans that incorporate data from various sources.
- Understand and explain the significance of geotechnical data in geo-engineering, including rock mass characterisation methods, data collection techniques, and ground stress, and apply these methods in practical scenarios.
- Apply and analyse statistical methods to describe, model, visualise, and analyse geotechnical data.

Through practical examples and case studies, students will develop the skills necessary to effectively interpret and apply geological and geophysical data in mining operations.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain fundamental geological principles and their relevance to mining operations.
- Utilise geological and geophysical methods to estimate rock properties and assess mineral deposits.

Subject: Principles of Mining Excavations (MINE8860)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course explores the mechanics, technologies, and practical applications of rock fragmentation and excavation—critical aspects of mining and tunnelling. You will develop a deep understanding of rock breakage and fragmentation theories, rock mass properties, discontinuities, and stress states that influence excavation performance. The course covers the fundamentals of blasting mechanics, including types of explosives, initiation systems, and blast design for both underground and surface mining. It also examines blast hazard management, vibration control, and the latest advancements in blasting technologies, including alternatives to conventional blasting. Drilling methods and equipment are also explored, with a focus on production drilling techniques, drill bits, accessories, and performance considerations. In addition, you will study the principles of coal and rock cutting mechanics, cutting tool performance, and rock cuttability assessment. The course delves into advanced excavation technologies, including full-face and partial mining machines, tunnel boring machines, and modern drilling systems. This course integrates theory with real-world applications, equipping you with the knowledge and skills to tackle the challenges of rock excavation using conventional and emerging mining and tunnelling technologies.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the contribution and influence of in-situ stress and stress redistribution on excavation stability and potential failure mode.
- Apply fundamental principles to design safe, efficient blasting and machine mining for optimal blasts, cutterhead efficiency, and economic productivity.
- Identify relevant requirements for the security, storage and handling of explosives.
- Select appropriate methods of rock excavations for given in-situ rock conditions.

Subject: Mine Slope Monitoring Technologies (MINE8940)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course covers the instrumentation and monitoring devices available to the mining industry to monitor and manage pit slope movement. Students will be able to detail the different types of slope movement typically observed in excavated, constructed and dumped slopes, as well as the various warning signs of instability. The course will discuss monitoring strategies (e.g. targeted, background monitoring), and the link to the range of monitoring instrumentation and tools available to geotechnical engineers to manage and track slope movement. Practical cases are used to demonstrate why monitoring is required in pit slope risk management and the advantages and limitations of each tool (e.g., accuracy, precision, data corrections). Practical application and deployment of available monitoring instrumentation and devices will also be demonstrated through case studies and in-class demonstration. This includes estimating time to failure from radar monitoring data and using monitoring data to reconcile numerical models. Risk management strategies are also discussed using Trigger Action Response Plans, Critical Control Registers, and Geotechnical Hazard Alerts. The intensive four-day course will include guest lectures from industry.
Learning Outcomes:
- Design a most efficient monitoring technique for any particular surface mining application.
- Determine the role and importance of these techniques in a comprehensive range of surface mining applications, both from a technical perspective, and from the risk and operational management perspective.
- Describe the principles of mine slope monitoring and identify the conventional and advanced mine slope monitoring technologies that are currently in use.
- Summarise and compare the appropriateness and effectiveness of different monitoring techniques.

Subject: Sustainable Tailings Management (MINE8950)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course provides an overview of the critical considerations in the siting, construction, stability, operation, maintenance and rehabilitation of tailings storage facilities (TSFs) for sustainable tailings management. It is divided into four modules: (1) background, characterisation and behaviour of tailings, (2) TSF siting, design and construction, (3) TSF water management, operations and monitoring, and (4) TSF closure, rehabilitation and sustainable practices.
Learning Outcomes:
- Outline and evaluate TSF operation, governance, and practices including water management.
- Evaluate tailings characterisation and behaviour, historic TSF failures, regulations and guidelines and sustainability aspects, and compare the good and bad practices.
- Investigate emerging and leading practices of tailings closure, rehabilitation, and sustainable practices and develop strategies and an implementation plan for a selected mining operation.
- Employ Australian and International frameworks for siting, design and construction of TSFs and evaluate how they influence TSF safety and sustainability.
