## Master of Radiation Therapy
##### M6004
##### 96 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
This course provides you with the advanced knowledge and skills required to obtain a professional entry level qualification to work in the field of radiation therapy, if you are a graduate with a background in science or health care.

It provides a professional qualification at a postgraduate level of study. 

Employment opportunities for graduates exist in both public and private health sectors, in metropolitan and regional Australia. This is the only on-line graduate-entry radiation therapy program offered in Australia and it may be available for interstate students to study in their home state. 

An integral component of the course is 45 weeks of work integrated learning completed over the two years where you will be required to attend Victorian and Interstate, regional and metropolitan clinical placements.

Currently NSW clinical placement centres have a requirement that students undertaking clinical are from their state. This is a requirement as they are offering the clinical placements to be able to fill their future workforce. Without fulfilling these requirements we would not be able to offer these places to applicants. We will adjust the offering of applicants by rank but taking into account the location of applicants and clinical placement requirements.

### Structure
The course is structured in three parts: Part A. Foundation studies, Part B. Discipline studies, and Part C. Professional practice.

Part A. Foundation studies

These studies will provide you with an orientation to the field of radiation therapy at advanced undergraduate level. They examine the foundational sciences underpinning medical radiation science, such as physics, radiobiology and dosimetry. These units also introduce you to anatomy, patho-physiology, oncology and radiation therapy planning, treatment and patient care. An introduction to the practice of radiation therapy and aspects of your knowledge of the skills required for professional practice within the Australian health care system are also provided. 

Part B. Discipline studies

These studies will provide you with knowledge and skills relevant to thoughtful, innovative and evidence-based practice. You will acquire knowledge of medico-legal and ethical issues related to radiation therapy and oncology, and the impact of cancer on patients' psychological well-being. The units also continue to build on your knowledge of working as a professional in the Australian health care system. Your knowledge of the applications of advanced skills will be further developed with respect to radiation therapy planning, treatment, imaging and patient care in different contexts. 

Part C. Professional practice

The professional experience placements will enable you to connect the theoretical components of your course with the practical aspects of health care under the valuable guidance of practitioners.

On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Integrate knowledge of digital health and artificial intelligence into professional practice
**2.**
Evaluate global health issues, sustainability and climate change in the context of cancer and radiation therapy service delivery
**3.**
Synthesise the theories underpinning collaborative and ethical practice, team work, communication and interdisciplinary patient care integrating these into practice.
**4.**
Integrate self-awareness, self-directedness and knowledge of self-care and the care of others into practice
**5.**
Appraise within the legal and regulatory context the requirement for accurate and timely documentation of patient information
**6.**
Demonstrate cultural capability with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples including respect, communication, reflection, advocacy, safety and quality
**7.**
Engage in reflective, empathetic, ethical and compassionate approaches to practice, acknowledging diversity and individual differences, equity and equality, creating a culturally safe environment for the delivery of radiation therapy services
**8.**
Determine how the principles of learning and feedback can be applied in the practice of radiation therapy
**9.**
Analyse knowledge which underpins contemporary radiation therapy practice, and evaluate the importance of radiation safety in the delivery of patient centred radiation therapy
**10.**
Integrate the principles of lifelong learning and continuing professional development in the context of patient safety and professional regulation
**11.**
Access, analyse, synthesise and apply research evidence to practice explaining how contemporary evidence allows for knowledge creation and change in practice.
**12.**
Synthesise the knowledge, skills, technical competency and level of professionalism expected of an entry-level radiation therapist
**13.**
Communicate ethically, unambiguously and in culturally sensitive ways in various formats, using information management systems and digital technology

### AHEGS
The Master of Radiation Therapy is a postgraduate degree. The course is taught in English and takes two years of full time study. The biological and physical sciences are closely integrated through practice and graduates have the skills and knowledge to apply psychosocial, medico-legal and ethical principles to the assessment and management of patients. Graduates develop reflective and analytical skills to enable them to engage in evidence-based practice and deliver a high standard of patient-centred care. The Master of Radiation Therapy is a Level 9 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Radiation therapy science 1 (RTS4101)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The focus of this unit is an in-depth understanding of the key facets of radiation physics. You will be able to define radiation and radioactive decay. You will be able to describe in detail the interactions of radiation with matter, and explain how radiation is detected and measured. You will gain an appreciation for the importance of radiation safety, and will be encouraged to critically reflect on the implications of radiation protection in your clinical experiences. You will also be given an introduction to medical radiations instrumentation and techniques, with emphasis on the fusion of the conceptual theory with the practical applications. Whilst studying these topics, you will be encouraged to critically reflect, analyse and synthesise relevant information from the literature and your previous experiences of studying physics.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the importance of radiation safety and analyse the techniques and engineering controls used in radiation protection;
- Describe the principles and practice of Radiation Treatment Planning.
- Define and calculate radioactivity, and describe in detail radioactive decay processes;
- Recognise and describe the basic design features, operating principles and the quality assurance checks for medical radiation equipment;
- Discuss the principles of, and equipment used for, radiation detection and measurement;
- Explain the scientific concepts involving the production and properties of ionising and non-ionising radiation, and the physical processes involved in the interactions of ionising and non-ionising radiation with matter;

Subject: Radiation therapy science 3 (RTS4103)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit focuses on the action of ionising radiation on living things at the cellular level and the resulting effects on organs, tissues and the whole body. You will gain an in-depth knowledge on the biological effects of ionising and non-ionising radiation, and the mechanisms of repair to biological damage. You will be able to describe the systemic and total body responses to early/late effects of radiation. You will undertake a critical review on how the effects of radiation on biological tissue can be utilised in the practice of radiation therapy. You will acquire an appreciation of the potential hazards present in different radiation therapy procedures, and focus on the principles of dose reduction, while maximising the information produced from a particular dose of ionising or non-ionising radiation. Whilst studying these topics, you will be encouraged to critically reflect, analyse and synthesise relevant information from the literature.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse the rationale used to determine the most appropriate procedure to balance tumour control and normal tissue exposure with regard to biological effects;
- Discuss the radiobiology principles underpinning modifications to procedural techniques and treatment schedules to maximise the benefit from a particular dose of ionising radiation;
- Calculate the radiation dose and appraise the current scientific theories relating to the risk associated with radiation dose.
- Evaluate the mechanisms of repair to biological damage, and the systemic and total body responses to early and late effects of radiation;
- Predict the biological effects of ionising and non-ionising radiation, their severity and risks, at population, whole body, organ, cellular and molecular levels;
