There is a tremendous demand for people with experience in Biotech, both in the academic and industrial settings. Such experience can be used at the "coalface" level where the person actually carries out research, at the management level where a knowledge of techniques and approaches is required, at the regulatory level and in publicity/advertising.
The Research Extensive program is designed to enable scientists who wish to increase their technical and research skills in core areas of biotechnology and wish to obtain a more extended laboratory immersion than is available in the Master of Biotechnology. The extended program will also provide a strong preparation for entry into a PhD program.
This program is recognised by the US National Professional Science Master's Association as an affiliated Professional Science Master's program.

Subject: Advanced Protein & Nucleic Acids (BIOC7040)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The primary aim of this course is to examine how biological processes are studied at a molecular level. The emphasis is on the experimental approaches that are used to study proteins and nucleic acids: their identification, structural characterisation, and functional analysis (including bioinformatics).
Learning Outcomes:
- Correctly explain the theory and practice of modern protein analysis techniques to scientist peers using appropriate visual and oral modes of presentation.
- Search protein and literature databases to obtain information about proteins and the experiments used to discover and analyse them.
- Read, interpret, and re-present primary literature to peers.
- Employ critical analysis of the methods, results, and discussion in primary literature (specifically in studies that address protein structure and function).
- Use nucleic acid and protein bioinformatics tools to analyse problems in protein biology, biochemistry, and structure; and describe the use of these tools to an audience.
- Be able to propose and justify a workable experimental design for the analysis of a protein structure and/or function.
- Provide constructive criticism to a peer on their written and oral presentations and use feedback to make reflective value judgements about the quality of your own written and oral work.
Assessment Tasks:
- Article Review 1: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Article Review 2: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Research Proposal: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Final Exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Workshop 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Biologics (BIOT7018)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Biologics include a wide range of products that include vaccines, blood and blood components, somatic cells, gene therapy, tissues and tissue-engineered products, and recombinant therapeutic proteins. The course will cover a variety of topics associated with the discovery, development, characterisation and production of biologics as human therapeutics, focussing predominantly on recombinant therapeutic proteins. The course will provide an in-depth coverage of the technologies and bioprocesses associated with biologics production. The course will also have a major focus on the global biologics industry and its continued evolution.
Learning Outcomes:
- Competently appraise the global biologics industry and future opportunities.
- Explain the enabling technologies underpinning the research and development of biologics.
- Discuss in detail the classes of biologics, therapeutic modalities, production and purification processes, process economics and analytical testing of biologic preparations.
- Critically assess and appraise relevant literature in biologics and draw parallels between biologicals and conventional pharmaceuticals.
Assessment Tasks:
- TBL Workshop Session Blackboard Quiz Online: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Biologics Presentation Online: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Workshop 2 Hours/ Week 3C

Subject: Quality Management Systems in Biotechnology (BIOT7031)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course will provide students with a comprehensive introduction to the shared principles of all quality management systems used within the biotechnology sector and beyond. It will then focus on critical quality standards encountered along the pathway to commercialisation for biotechnology / pharmaceutical products.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate understanding of the shared principles of all quality management systems.
- Develop comprehensive knowledge of critical quality standards which may be encountered when taking a biotechnology product to market.
- Facilitate the conduct of a GLP study and confidently operate within a GLP environment.
- Facilitate the conduct of a GCP study and confidently operate within a GCP environment.
- Critique the requirements of a facility operating under GMP.
- Recognise the importance of teamwork in a professional environment and develop the skills and attributes necessary to successfully operate as part of a team.
- Appraise the way in which GMOs are controlled in Australia.

Subject: Biotechnology Intellectual Property Case Studies (BIOT7040)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Study of cases in intellectual property (IP) in biotechnology. Criteria for patentability. Breadth of claims, reduction to practice, fair basing. Process and product patents. Terms of patent in biotechnology. Differences in patent rules between countries. Plant breeders rights. Prohibitions/exclusions from patentability in biotechnology. Compulsory licensing. Illustrative Case studies in biotechnology IP: The GTG patents, the PCR patents, Taxol patents, Neem patents, Turmeric patents, rice patents, Affymetrix patents, "Southern" patents, EST patents, sequence patents, stem cell patents. Strategies for managing biotechnology IP Analysis of the parts of a patent and a critical examination of a patent in the context of prior art. Preparation of a case study in IP conflict, dispute or debate in biotechnology. Due to a limited number of places available in this course, there will be a selection process for enrolment with places being allocated based on the results from the prerequisite course and merit using the student's overall GPA as the criterion.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe several strategies for protection and commercialization of biotechnology IP
- List criteria that are used to make decisions about biotechnology IP protection and management strategies and apply those criteria to make decisions
- Deconstruct and analyse complex disputes in the field of biotechnology IP
- List the important parts of a biotechnology patent
- Demonstrate an awareness of the different interpretations of prior art in different countries in relation to biotechnology IP
- Examine and evaluate trade secret litigation in biotechnology
Assessment Tasks:
- Examination of a trade secret litigation case: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Deconstruction and analysis of a dispute: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Myriad decisions in the U.S. and Australia: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Biotechnology IP protection strategy Hurdle Identity Verified: 50%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week Contact 4 Hours/ Week 1T4C

Subject: Biomanufacturing Technology (BIOT7061)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides a current overview of cutting-edge innovation in biomanufacturing biotechnology. We will cover, in detail, specific enabling technologies driving change in biomanufacturing industries. This includes, but not limited to: Antibody design and production Cell line engineering Vaccine production Upstream and downstream bioprocessing optimisation Analytics and quality control Regulatory issues and process economy Students will learn about the current global trends in biomanufacturing and how cutting-edge biomanufacturing tools are changing industries to meet demand.
Learning Outcomes:
- Use their knowledge and expertise to evaluate biomanufacturing technologies and trouble shoot expression technologies.
- Critically evaluate the environmental, economic, regulatory, and stakeholder considerations involved in new biomanufacturing processes, and synthesise these perspectives to inform evidence-based decision-making.
- Critically assess the downstream processing requirements for large-scale biological manufacture, justifying the purpose, sequence, and trade-offs of each stage in the context of process efficiency and product quality.
- Perform and analyse data generated using key analytical techniques critical in the evaluation of biomanufacturing products and procedures.
- Interpret and apply current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP) requirements in biomanufacturing, and critically evaluate complex scenarios and case studies for regulatory compliance and risk.

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=MICR7001 =====
Subject: Food Microbiology & Biotechnology (MICR7001)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Examines the positive (fermentation) and negative (spoilage) roles microbes play in foods and methods of preservation. Rapid detection methods and molecular (DNA) methods relevant to food microbiology including genomics (WGS), real-time PCR and culture-independent identification are investigated. It also explores spores, biofilms and problems and applications of bacteriophages, GM microbes and bioreactors. Practical components include organic acid preservation of yeast in apple juice, extension of shelf-life using carbon dioxide, decimal reduction of bacteria using heat, the isolation and identification of food bacteria using 16s rDNA PCR, DNA sequencing and bioinformatics.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate the roles of microorganisms in food spoilage, preservation and fermentation, and their impacts on food quality, safety, and shelf life.
- Appraise rapid detection, molecular and genomic methods including real-time PCR, DNA sequencing, and whole genome sequencing for identifying and tracking food-related microorganisms.
- Synthesise knowledge of microbial stress responses, spores, and biofilms to assess their significance for food processing and control strategies.
- Justify the use of bacteriophages, biocontrol agents and genetically modified microorganisms in food and biotechnological applications.
- Interpret experimental data from food microbiology investigations to draw evidence-based conclusions.
- Appraise emerging applications in food microbiology and biotechnology, including precision fermentation and alternative protein production

- Group poster presentation Team or group-based In-person: 25%% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Principles of Entrepreneurship (TIMS7301)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Entrepreneurship is commonly regarded as the pursuit of opportunity beyond the resources apparently to hand. With this maxim in mind, we explore the theory and practice of starting then growing new ventures, where opportunities exist but the pathways to unleash promising outcomes are uncertain. Knowledge and skill development is built by identifying and developing a new business idea within the class, through adaptive execution to generate information by building and testing business hypotheses so to navigate uncertainties. These skills are then used to analyse conditions across various entrepreneurial firms. Topics include the process of entrepreneurial logic, designing business models for value capture and delivery, framing competitive strategy, determining assumptions-based finance needs, understanding different innovation types and managing growth to build stronger ventures.
Learning Outcomes:
- Articulate the foundational knowledge in the area of entrepreneurship.
- Develop, evaluate and communicate actionable business opportunities as part of a startup team.
- Create viable and innovative Business Models.
- Critically analyse knowledge creation in the startup process.
- Practice real-time entrepreneurial leadership to craft ideas and test impacts.

- Team Presentations | Pitch & Video Team or group-based In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Analysis | Across 4 Case Topics Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Case Study | Emerging opportunities Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
