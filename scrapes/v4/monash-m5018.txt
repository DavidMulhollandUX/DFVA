## Graduate Diploma of Occupational and Environmental Health
##### M5018
##### 48 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
This course, offered by the Department of Epidemiology and Preventive Medicine, gives health professionals the attitude, skills and knowledge necessary to responsibly provide preventive health services to both reduce the health impacts of disease and injury and assist to systematically address hazards arising from workplaces and within communities. The course caters for the special needs of medical practitioners, nurses, allied health personnel, scientists or OHS managers wishing to develop adaptable and responsible skills as OHS and environmental health practitioners. The course is offered by off-campus learning however compulsory block attendance is required each semester.

### Structure
The course comprises 48 credit points of Occupational and Environmental Health studies.

In these studies, you will gain an introduction to occupational health and safety, in identifying risks and hazards in the workplace and understanding the epidemiology of occupational and environmental diseases and injuries. You will learn to prevent and manage such risks and hazards and to implement occupational safety systems in the workplace.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
demonstrate an understanding of the factors underlying risk perception and be able to effectively and sensitively communicate - in both written reports and oral presentations - these risks to exposed people in the workplace and the community
**2.**
access, critically appraise and integrate hazard information from published studies and other relevant sources in the occupational and environmental health literature to demonstrate autonomy, expert judgement and responsibility in an occupational and environmental health professional and/or management role
**3.**
identify and make recommendations about preventative programs and environmental advice to occupational health services
**4.**
recognise and evaluate the level of exposure and assess the degree of risk to health from workplace and environmental hazards and provide advice on appropriate control measures in the workplace and the community
**5.**
demonstrate an understanding of the legal and ethical framework which governs occupational health and safety practice and the obligations of industry in regard to the environment

### Progression to further studies
Successful completion of this course may provide a pathway to the Master of Occupational and Environmental Health (M6026).

### AHEGS
The Graduate Diploma of Occupational and Environmental Health is a graduate award. The course is taught in English. The course takes 1 year of full-time study or part-time equivalent to complete. Graduates have demonstrated knowledge and skills to provide preventive health services to reduce health impacts of disease and injury arising from workplaces and communities. The Graduate Diploma of Occupational and Environmental Health is a level 8 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification.  The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Introductory epidemiology (MPH5040)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit is taken concurrently with MPH5041, as together they are prerequisites for clinical epidemiology elective units. Upon completion of this unit you will be able to measure frequencies of disease i.e. incidence, prevalence, "population at risk"; and measure associations in epidemiological studies; different study designs i.e. observational studies, experimental study designs; bias associated with study designs, misclassification, confounding and effect modification. You will critically appraise different epidemiological studies, apply diagnostic and screening tests, and examine outbreak investigation.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain and contextualise the purposes of descriptive and analytical epidemiology;
- Interpret and communicate the results of epidemiological studies; and
- Evaluate the adequacy of exposure assessment in epidemiological studies;
- Solve complex problems relating to the use of epidemiological concepts and study designs.
- Critically analyse epidemiological papers from the medical research and OHS literature;
- Analyse the strengths and weaknesses of different epidemiological study designs;
- Explain and contextualise the concepts of study questions, population, sampling, exposure assessment, bias and confounding;
Assessment Tasks:
- MCQ (30 Minutes): 10% (Hurdle: None)
- Development activities: 35% (Hurdle: None)
- Critical appraisal (2,100 words): 35% (Hurdle: None)
- Infographic (1 page of graphics): 20% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 12 hours per week including two-hour weekly workshops for 10 weeks.

Subject: Health and safety risk management leadership and governance (MPH5222)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this unit you will undertake a comprehensive exploration of core Health and Safety risk management concepts, risk assessment techniques, and decision-making processes. You will gain a deep understanding of the principles, framework, and process of ISO 31000 risk management standards, including assurance activities. The unit emphasises the comparison and contrast of ISO 31000 risk assessment processes with existing governance and compliance approaches. Additionally, you will learn to communicate the critical importance of engaging stakeholders in risk assessment and the implementation of effective controls. Upon successful completion of this unit, you will be proficient in demonstrating, explaining, comparing, and communicating key aspects of risk management, preparing them for advanced roles in Health and Safety risk management.
Learning Outcomes:
- Communicate the importance of engaging stakeholders in Health and Safety risk assessment activities including implementing effective controls.
- Plan, critically evaluate and apply various Health and Safety ISO 31000 risk assessment processes in alignment with existing governance and compliance approaches.
- Describe core Health and Safety risk management concepts, risk assessment techniques and decision-making processes.
- Critically evaluate and apply the principles, framework, and process of ISO 31000 risk management standards including assurance activities.
Assessment Tasks:
- Workshop-based critical reflection activity (1,800 words): 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Case analysis report (2,400 words): 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Integrated case-based learning and assessment (ICLA) (up to 1,800 words): 30% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 10 hours of off-campus study per week, including: 6 hours self-directed-online learning & 4 hours teacher-directed online learning per week; and 3-day block of face-to-face learning, on-campus.

Subject: Introduction to occupational health and safety (MPH5241)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The effects and human cost of occupational disease and injury, occupational health and safety law, worker's compensation, negligence, occupational rehabilitation, historical achievements and challenges, international and national organisations.
Learning Outcomes:
- Contextualise the legal framework applying to the workplace;
- Describe the theoretical underpinnings of various injury models and their use within Occupational Health and Safety Management Systems;
- Critically appraise contemporary Work Health and Safety (WHS) data sources;
- Analyse the human and economic cost and causes of workplace injury and death in Australia
- Analyse the WHS impacts of contemporary and changing work patterns and new and emerging challenges.
- Communicate the fundamental principles of WHS practice
Assessment Tasks:
- Case study analysis and reflection - end of week 6 (1,200 words): 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Return to work plan (1,200 words): 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Infographic (One page including graphics): 25% (Hurdle: None)
- Workplace Report (2,100 words): 35% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: On-campus: 6 contact hours, plus 6 hours self-directed study, per week. Off-campus: 12 hours self-directed study per week, plus 3 block days.

Subject: Psychosocial work environment (MPH5242)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Psychosocial effects of work on individuals are explained from a preventive viewpoint. You will examine topics including mental health and its effect on employment, alcohol and drug use, disability discrimination, equal employment opportunity legislation, workplace health promotion and shift work.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply the risk management process to assess and control work-related stress;
- Reflect critically on theoretical models of job design and organisational psychology;
- Identify common psychosocial hazards and risks in the work environment and impacts on health;
- Apply legislation relevant to the psychosocial work environment;
- Develop insightful and persuasive evidence-based recommendations to prevent, eliminate or reduce work-related stress and improve workplace culture and practice.
- Communicate fluently using appropriate terminology to describe psychosocial hazards and risks and their prevention and control;

- Risk management report (1,200 words): 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Final report (2,100 words): 35% (Hurdle: None)

Subject: Workplace hazards: measurement and management (MPH5243)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this unit, you will learn about hazards encountered in workplaces, including chemical and biological hazards. You will develop and apply skills to evaluate and measure hazards and the risks they present to workers. You will also learn how to assess and manage such risks in workplaces, enabling workers to do their jobs without undue risk to their health and wellbeing.
Learning Outcomes:
- Communicate fluently with diverse audiences about workplace hazards, risks and risk management, using appropriate terminology.
- Critically evaluate and apply evidence about the health effects of workplace exposures to inform evidence-based interventions
- Assess workplace hazards and understand the principles of measuring exposures, assessing the risks they present and risk management options
- Plan, critically evaluate and recommend practical strategies to mitigate risks presented by workplace hazards
Assessment Tasks:
- Case analysis report (2,400 words): 40% (Hurdle: Threshold)
- Integrated Case-based Learning and Assessment (ICLA) (up to 1800 words): 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Workshop-based tests (1,800 words): 30% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 10 hours of off-campus study per week, including: 6 hours self-directed-online learning & 4 hours teacher-directed online learning per week; and 3-day block of face-to-face learning, on-campus.

Subject: Humans, work and physical hazards (MPH5244)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit introduces physical hazards including noise, vibration, heat, cold, radiation and pressure effects and conditions such as work-related musculoskeletal disorders that are common in workplaces, the effects on human health, and prevention and control measures to improve work health and safety. You will examine and apply a range of approaches to identify and control physical hazards and risks and develop evidence-based recommendations for improving practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Communicate fluently using appropriate terminology to describe work-related physical hazards and risks and their prevention and control
- Apply legislation relevant to physical hazards in the work environment
- Apply a range of approaches to identify, assess and control physical hazards and risks including a systems approach, risk management process, the hierarchy of controls, and design of work
- Identify common work-related musculoskeletal disorders and preventive strategies
- Interpret the assessment results and develop and effectively communicate appropriate recommendations for risk controls.
- Identify and analyse the properties and health effects of common work-related physical hazards
Assessment Tasks:
- Critical reflections (1,800 words): 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Report presentation (10 minutes): 10% (Hurdle: None)
- Infographic (200 words plus graphics): 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Written report (2,400 words): 40% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 12 hours per week comprising 6 hours self-directed study/learning per week, plus 4 hours teacher directed learning per week, plus 3 on-campus block days.

Subject: Safety management systems (MPH5276)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Concepts & practice of occupational health and safety management systems within work environments, including the conduct of an audit are examined. Includes accident/incident causation theories and models, investigative techniques, reporting & statistics, safety systems, fire prevention & control. Incident causation and accident types, including slips, trips and falls are also examined.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop a Safety Management System and a Safety Management Plan for the workplace;
- Demonstrate how to communicate with organisations about planning and implementing Safety Management Systems.
- Apply the essential elements required of a Safety Management System which complies with relevant legislation, and;
- Evaluate the theoretical underpinnings and models of best practice for safety management in the workplace;

- Assignment 1 (2,500 words): 25% (Hurdle: None)
