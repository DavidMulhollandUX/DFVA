Help individuals and communities to maximise productivity, wellbeing and quality of life with La Trobe's Master of Occupational Therapy Practice.
Open to students from any undergraduate discipline, you'll graduate with a competitive edge through a combination of coursework and 1000 hours of hands-on, industry-responsive placements. Designed to meet the competencies for safe and effective practice, you'll develop advanced knowledge and skills in assessment and intervention, the therapeutic use of occupation and the adaptation of environments.
Get hands-on with a six-week, full-time professional practice block in your first year, then two eight-week, full-time professional practice blocks and a ten-week project-based learning placement in your second year.
Subject to completion of local requirements, graduates may be eligible to apply to practice in other countries including the UK, North America and Sweden.
By completing La Trobe's Master of Occupational Therapy Practice, you will:
  * benefit from an integrated learning approach that reflects modern occupational therapy practice expectations
  * focus on four key themes: First Nations' knowledge and cultural safety, evidence-based practice, occupation, and professionalism.
  * explore the theoretical foundations of occupational therapy, recognising the relationship between occupational participation, health and well-being
  * develop advanced skills in searching, appraising and integrating research evidence into practice
  * understand how to describe, critique and apply the principles of engagement, assessment, intervention and case management
  * learn how to integrate specialised knowledge, professional reasoning and professional behaviour within practice.

Work based learning (placement) requirements 
Compulsory placements (Work Based Learning)   
During this course, you will participate in one or more supervised Work Based Learning (WBL) placements designed to allow you to practice newly acquired skills, behaviours and knowledge while integrating theory with practice. The placements will immerse you in professional work culture, enabling you to develop and further enhance your skills. WBL provides you with the opportunity to gain valuable industry experience at one or more of our local, interstate or international industry partners or agencies. WBL placements will generally be arranged by the Office of the Provost Placements and WBL Team at La Trobe University. The number of hours undertaken can vary considerably, depending on the requirements of the course. Students may incur additional costs and it may be necessary to travel or relocate to complete a specific placement. Please refer to the individual subject entries for more information.

Professional recognition 
The Master of Occupational Therapy Practice is accredited by the Occupational Therapy Council of Australia Ltd (OTC). The Master of Occupational Therapy Practice is approved by the Occupational Therapy Board of Australia (OTBA) and the World Federation of Occupational Therapists (WOFT). Graduates of the Master of Occupational Therapy Practice will be eligible to apply for registration with the Occupational Therapy Board of Australia as an Occupational Therapist. Professional registration may be subject to additional or ongoing requirements beyond completion of the degree. Please contact the relevant professional body for details.

Core participation requirements 
Expand all
#### Behavioural and relational skills
_keyboard_arrow_down_
_**Core requirement**_  
  
Students must be able to manage their own responses and behaviour to work effectively in a range of learning environments with diverse people and tasks. This includes the capacity to be receptive and respond appropriately to constructive feedback. Students should also be able to work with others in a team or group environment and make and maintain effective relationships with a wide range of individuals in order to complete tasks or for professional practice.  
  
See also [Student behaviours policy](https://policies.latrobe.edu.au/document/view.php?id=60) for more information.  
  
_**Course-specific requirement**_
Occupational therapy students will be exposed to health care and human service contexts in which they will encounter diversity and adversity and will be required to draw upon behavioural and relational skills to manage these situations in a professional manner. Occupational therapy students require the capacity to cope with their own emotions and behaviour effectively when working with peers and individuals in practice settings. Effective verbal and non-verbal communication is fundamental to occupational therapy practice and needs to be respectful, culturally appropriate, clear, attentive, empathetic, honest and non-judgemental. The ability to observe and understand non-verbal cues assists with building rapport with people and gaining their trust and respect in academic and professional relationships; Displaying consistent and appropriate facial expressions, eye contact, being mindful of space, time boundaries and body movements and gestures promotes trust in academic and professional relationships; Being sensitive to individual differences displays respect and empathy to others and develops trusting relationships; The ability to observe and understand verbal and non -verbal cues is essential for safe and effective observation, recording and reporting of client symptoms and reactions to facilitate occupational therapy assessment and treatment.
#### Strength and mobility
_keyboard_arrow_down_
_**Core requirement**_  
  
Gross motor skills: Students should possess the strength, range of motion, coordination and mobility sufficient to manage their learning environment and ultimately, professional performance needs.  
  
Fine motor skills: Students should possess the manual dexterity and fine motor skills sufficient to manage their learning environment and ultimately, professional performance needs.  
  
_**Course-specific requirement**_
Occupational therapy students require sufficient gross and fine motor skills to perform, coordinate and prioritise care. Tasks that involve gross motor skills include lifting, carrying, pushing, pulling, standing, twisting and bending. Students must be able to demonstrate and perform these tasks consistently and safely to reduce the risk of harm to self and others.
Examples of gross motor skills that are important for occupational therapy include: Maintaining own and client's balance while providing client support, when transferring and mobilising individuals in a safe manner; Evaluating clients in a safe and effective manner, who have varying physical capacity, who are positioned on plinths and hospital beds, on mats on the floor, on chairs and when standing.
Fine motor skills are required for a range of tasks including: Performing assessment techniques, e.g. evaluation of hand sensation and joint movement; Performing treatment techniques, e.g. fabricating splints, demonstrating use of adaptive equipment, demonstrating fine motor tasks during activities of daily living.
Occupational therapy practice requires physical performance at a consistent and sustained level to meet individual needs over time to ensure safe and effective occupational therapy care. Examples include participating in tutorials and lectures throughout the day; Providing consistent occupational therapy management over a negotiated time frame while on placement.
#### Sensory (visual, auditory, tactile, olfaction)
_keyboard_arrow_down_
_**Core requirement**_  
  
Students should possess the ability to interact with visual, auditory or tactile inputs sufficiently to manage their learning environment and to meet professional performance needs.  
  
_**Course-specific requirement**_
Occupational therapy students require sufficient visual acuity to demonstrate the required range of skills, tasks and assessments to maintain consistent, accurate and safe care to self and others. Visual observations, examinations and assessment are fundamental to safe and effective occupational therapy practice. Examples include observing and detecting subtle changes in posture, movement and the ability to perform functional activities during assessment and treatment. Safely operating electronic equipment e.g. splinting equipment – heat guns and splinting pans.
Sufficient auditory ability is necessary to monitor, assess and manage individual health needs consistently and accurately. Auditory assessments and observations are fundamental to safe and effective occupational therapy practice. Examples from occupational therapy practice relevant during placements include: Detecting care requests by calls for help or activation of a call bell; Engaging appropriately to a client's responses during an assessment.
Sufficient tactile ability is necessary to monitor, assess and detect clients' physical characteristics and act on any abnormalities detected to provide appropriate occupational therapy management. Tactile assessments and observations are fundamental to safe and effective occupational therapy practice. Examples include: Detecting changes in circulation, e.g. temperature. Palpating joints, muscles, and soft tissues, and detecting anatomical or movement abnormalities.
#### Cognition
_keyboard_arrow_down_
_**Core requirement**_  
  
Students must be able to locate, acquire, retain and apply knowledge as part of their learning and for assessment. They must have the ability to sustain their attention over a designated period, maintain their focus in a variety of learning environments and remember information long enough to complete tasks in a reasonable and safe timeframe.  
  
_**Course-specific requirement**_
Safe and effective delivery of occupational therapy practice is based on comprehensive knowledge that must be sourced, understood and applied appropriately, this includes conceptualising and using appropriate knowledge in response to academic assessment items. It also includes appropriately applying knowledge of theory, research evidence, policies and procedures in the class and practice settings.
Literacy skills are required to: convey spoken and written messages accurately and effectively in a practice setting; paraphrase, summarise and reference in accordance with appropriate academic conventions in written assignments. Competent application of numeracy skills is essential in occupational therapy to facilitate the safe and effective delivery of occupational therapy care. Examples include recording accurate measurements in client notes; Prescribing adaptive equipment that correctly meets client measurement requirements e.g. wheelchair prescription.
Additionally, sufficient cognitive endurance is an essential requirement needed to perform multiple tasks in an assigned period to provide safe and effective occupational therapy care. Examples include participating in tutorials and lectures throughout the day; Providing consistent occupational therapy management over a negotiated time frame while on placement.

Subject: ENABLING CHANGE: MUSCULOSKELETAL AND NEUROLOGICAL  (OCT5001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject will enable you to describe, apply and critique principles of assessment and intervention for people with musculoskeletal and neurological conditions that impact on their capacity to participate in occupations. Clinical reasoning skills will be established which will be further developed through subsequent subjects. Intervention principles will focus on the use of meaningful occupation and incorporate environmental supports (advocacy, organisational change, environmental adaptation).
Learning Outcomes:
- Design, communicate and justify occupational therapy interventions that are culturally safe with respect to individual, cultural and linguistic diversity for people living with musculoskeletal and neurological conditions using professional reasoning
- Critically appraise research using appraisal tools and apply research findings to clinical practice, using principles of evidence- based practice.
- Interpret contextual factors relating to adults' physical, institutional, social, and cultural environments, including culturally safe communication when interacting with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander individuals and family members and transform this information into intervention designs.
- Select and justify the use of occupational therapy conceptual and practice models to guide assessment and intervention for people experiencing occupational activity limitations and participation restriction due to musculoskeletal and neurological conditions.
- Apply occupational therapy practice skills including, critical reflection, collaboration and teamwork.

Subject: OCCUPATIONAL PARTICIPATION: BODY STRUCTURE AND FUNCTION  (OCT5002)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will learn and apply human biosciences knowledge and functional anatomy principles in occupational therapy practice. You will incorporate body, structure and function considerations into your clinical reasoning related to lower limb, upper limb, trunk, spine, and brain impairment impacting occupational participation related to typical occupational scenarios.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate the ability to work cohesively in a team environment.
- Apply, critique, and justify assessment and intervention decisions using evidence-based principles relating to impairment of body function, activity limitations and occupational participation for adults and/or children with complex conditions.
- Apply knowledge of musculoskeletal and neurological systems within the upper limb, lower limb, trunk (vertebral column), and brain in occupational therapy assessment and treatment scenarios
- Explain the role of homeostasis and apply foundational understanding of cell to organ system anatomy and physiology to issues impacting occupational participation.
- Demonstrate high level communication of assessment and intervention plans applying culturally safe strategies/practice as required for a range of audiences.

Subject: EVIDENCE BASED PRACTICE IN OCCUPATIONAL THERAPY (OCT5007)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will develop an understanding of evidence based practice, advanced skills in searching, appraising and integrating research evidence into practice. You will develop and demonstrate effective communication and collaboration skills alongside investigative and critical thinking skills. This subject includes professional learning, practical experiences or portfolio activities which contribute to the completion of the Silver Career Ready AdvantageTM award.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically analyse and evaluate research independently
- Justify and communicate research propositions within a professional team environment
- Critically reflect on established research and formulate clinical research questions
- Communicate and interpret the clinical significance of current research to specialist and non-specialist audiences

Subject: OCCUPATIONAL THERAPY: ENABLING CHANGE C (CHILDREN) (OCT5ECC)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will be introduced to cases involving children and youth experiencing occupational performance challenges. You will learn to describe, critique, evaluate and apply principles of assessment and intervention for children and their families. Emphasis will be placed on supporting children's capacities to engage in activities, occupations and community life. Intervention approaches will incorporate strategies aimed at individual growth, adaptation and environmental modifications, including advocacy, organisational change and environmental adaptation. Practical workshops will develop your professional and clinical skills, aligned with the OT Australia Paediatric Occupational Therapy Capability Framework.
Learning Outcomes:
- Design, implement, and critically evaluate advanced intervention strategies that address limitations in occupational performance and support meaningful participation in children's daily lives.
- Demonstrate advanced oral and written communication skills to clearly and coherently convey occupational therapy reasoning and intervention plans to diverse audiences, including clients, families, and interdisciplinary teams
- Apply and critically reflect on occupational therapy practice skills including professional reasoning, collaboration, and effective teamwork in complex practice settings.
- Synthesise and evaluate complex assessment findings to design and justify evidence-informed, family-centred management plans for children with occupational performance issues.
- Critically apply advanced and specialised occupational therapy knowledge to assess and enhance the occupational participation of children experiencing complex occupational performance challenges.

Subject: MACRO STRATEGIES FOR ADVANCED PROFESSIONAL PRACTICE (OCT5MSP)
Credit Points: 30
Overview: In this subject, you will shift your view beyond the occupational challenges facing individuals, to consider the ways in which occupational therapists enable change at the level of the service/organisation, community or population. This is referred to as macro level practice. You will focus on gaining knowledge and skills required for your impending role as health practitioners in a rapidly changing health and social policy context. The subject is comprised of three key themes: health promotion and prevention, project management and preparation for contemporary practice. You will apply, critique and reflect upon major subject themes through appraising other projects and planning and conducting your own team-based project. This subject will develop skills in innovation and entrepreneurship, including the ability to tackle problems creatively, generating new ideas, taking calculated risks and creating change to achieve ambitions - now and in the future. This subject includes professional learning, practical experiences or portfolio activities which contribute to the completion of the Silver Career Ready AdvantageTM award.
Learning Outcomes:
- Work collaboratively with a range of stakeholders to manage the project process and present outcomes.
- Critically reflect on, and apply advanced professional capacities to successfully lead and influence the project management process.
- Integrate and apply understanding of macro approaches to practical/practice situations
- Critically reflect on capabilities, challenges and ongoing learning arising in the project management context and communicate these clearly
- Evaluate projects that target national health priorities regarding occupation, prevention and project management factors, as part of a team.
- Implement, evaluate and communicate project processes and expected outcomes using principles of project management to a range of stakeholders, individually and as part of a team.
- Propose, design and justify ethical health projects underpinned by project scoping principles, in consultation with external agencies

Subject: FOUNDATIONS OF OCCUPATIONAL THERAPY (OCT5OTF)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject will introduce you to the historical, philosophical and theoretical foundations underpinning the profession of occupational therapy to transition you to your postgraduate studies in the discipline. You will explore the relationship between occupational participation, health and well-being and integrate key concepts from the Model of Human Occupation (Taylor et el., 2024) and occupational science to develop an understanding of humans as occupational beings. Foundational skills in occupational analysis, person-centered intentional communication and facilitating occupational groups will be developed and applied, alongside application of evidence-based practice principles. This subject includes professional learning, practical experiences or portfolio activities which contribute to the completion of the Silver Career Ready AdvantageTM award.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse the relationship between occupational participation, health and well-being as experienced by yourself and others, including Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples.
- Analyse qualitative data and formulate an occupational profile using the theory of Kielhofner’s Model of Human Occupation
- Design, facilitate and evaluate occupationally focused group experiences using principles and processes of occupational analysis and group work.
- Illustrate and critique occupational practice skills including reflective practice, intentional communication, goal setting, environmental interventions and documentation.

Subject: PROFESSIONAL PRACTICE B (OCT5PPB)
Credit Points: 30
Overview: In this placement you will integrate occupational therapy specialized knowledge, professional reasoning and professional behaviour within practice. The focus of this placement is on being actively involved in all stages of the occupational therapy process within a particular setting and, where relevant, begin to manage a small caseload. The placement may occur across a range of health, education and social care settings. Your placement will be guided by learning objectives and supervised and assessed by a registered occupational therapist. Professional accreditation requires a minimum of 1000 hours of clinical exposure. Your attendance at professional placements will contribute to meeting this requirement for professional recognition as an occupational therapist. This subject includes professional learning, practical experiences or portfolio activities which contribute to the completion of the Silver Career Ready AdvantageTM award.
Learning Outcomes:
- Independently devise personal learning objectives and assume responsibility for own learning to support the development of skills, knowledge and behaviour for entry-level occupational therapy practice.
- Actively and effectively use culturally safe and responsive communication with key stakeholders within a professional context including clear articulation of professional reasoning and recommendations.
- Implement a range of evaluation and reflection strategies to make changes to service provision in response to new information.
- Analyse and interpret service user occupational performance information and implement effective service provision informed by relevant and contemporary theory, practice knowledge and best evidence.
- Competently and autonomously apply professional practice skills in an ethical, safe, lawful and accountable manner to support service user health and wellbeing through occupation.

Subject: PROFESSIONAL PRACTICE C (OCT5PPC)
Credit Points: 30
Overview: In this placement you will consolidate, apply and further develop your core occupational therapy practice skills in readiness to practice as an entry-level occupational therapist. The focus of this placement is the transition toward independent practice and, where relevant, management of a small caseload. Your placement may occur across a range of health, education and social care settings. Your placement will be guided by learning objectives and supervised and assessed by a registered occupational therapist. Professional accreditation requires a minimum of 1000 hours of clinical exposure. Your attendance at professional placements will contribute to meeting this requirement for professional recognition as an occupational therapist. This subject includes professional learning, practical experiences or portfolio activities which contribute to the completion of the Silver Career Ready AdvantageTM award.
Learning Outcomes:
- Select and implement appropriate methods of information gathering and interpret service user occupational performance information to formulate advanced intervention planning.
- Apply advanced culturally safe and responsive communication skills with a wide range of key stakeholders to deliver effective service provision to improve client outcomes.
- Design, deliver and document service provision with responsibility and judgement that is informed by relevant and contemporary theory, practice knowledge and using evidence-based principles.
- Independently and responsibly manage a caseload and address occupational performance and participation of service users.
- Critically evaluate own learning needs to independently devise personal learning objectives to consolidate skills, knowledge and behaviour, including continued cultural capabilities for entry-level occupational therapy practice.
- Independently evaluate and adapt service provision by implementing a range of evaluation and reflection strategies to improve client outcomes.
