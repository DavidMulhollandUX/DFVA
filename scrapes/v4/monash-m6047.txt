## Master of Clinical Psychology
##### M6047
##### 102 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
This course is designed to prepare you for work as a Clinical Psychologist in Malaysia. You will undertake coursework that is designed to develop your knowledge and skills in psychology and mental health to prepare you for working with clients where you will undertake assessment, diagnosis, formulation, treatment and prevention of psychological problems and mental illness across the lifespan.  

You will gain generalised and specialised clinical skills to manage complex mental health problems, that are culturally responsive, in order to help people thrive. You will undertake four placements supervised by experienced clinical psychologists. You will also conduct a clinical research project that has scope to positively impact the community under the guidance of experienced academics.

### Structure
The course is structured into two parts: Part A. Applied academic studies in clinical psychology, and Part B. Clinical placement in psychology.

Part A. Applied academic studies in clinical psychology

These studies will equip you to competently and ethically apply advanced psychological knowledge and skills to mental health challenges in the community.  The research project will incorporate a critical review of the relevant literature, and may include a service evaluation, experimental or qualitative study, or systematic review or meta-analysis.

Part B. Clinical placement in psychology

These opportunities will enable you to apply theory to practice under the supervision of experienced clinical supervisors. You will complete a minimum of 1200 hours of professional experience, with 600 hours of direct client contact, across three different hospital or community settings, which may include the University’s clinic.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Effectively communicate complex ideas and concepts about mental health problems and solutions to a variety of audiences.
**2.**
Engage in reflective practice through coursework, supervision and clinical work that enables flexible and adaptive clinical practice.
**3.**
Design, carry out, disseminate, and utilise empirical research relevant to clinical psychology to solve mental health challenges.
**4.**
Apply legal and ethical standards that govern professional psychology and ability to appropriately manage ethical issues in practice.
**5.**
Competently, and with cultural sensitivity, use psychological assessment tools and processes with a wide range of clinical presentations, to inform case formulation, diagnosis and intervention.
**6.**
Effectively work with diverse clients with respect and humility.
**7.**
Apply psychological theories and psychopathology, using both developmental and biopsychosocial frameworks, to guide practice with individuals, groups, and/or systems.
**8.**
Deliver and monitor evidence-based interventions to a wide range of clinical presentations that is responsive to the client’s goals, socio-cultural context, and psychotherapy processes.

### AHEGS
The Master of Clinical Psychology is a postgraduate award. The course is taught in English, and normally takes 2 years of full-time study or 4 years of part-time study. Graduates have advanced knowledge in the assessment, diagnosis, formulation and treatment of psychological problems and complex mental health conditions. Graduates have the cultural, theoretical and critical abilities obtained through case-based learning and clinical education, and the knowledge, skills and technical competence appropriate for an entry-level practitioner in clinical psychology.  The Master of Clinical Psychology is a Level 9 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Psychopathology 1 (PSY5401)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this unit, you will be equipped with a solid foundation in comprehending and assessing psychopathology in the adult population. You will learn to navigate the diagnostic criteria for a diverse range of adult psychopathological disorders. This knowledge will enable you to competently and ethically assess and differentially diagnose these conditions. Additionally, you will critically explore the aetiological factors contributing to adult psychopathology, integrating biological, psychological, and environmental perspectives for a holistic understanding. Ethical decision-making is at the core of our curriculum, ensuring you can navigate complex moral dilemmas inherent in clinical practice with integrity and professionalism. You'll also gain proficiency in utilising evidence-based assessment tools, honing your skills to effectively evaluate psychopathological symptoms and assess functional impairments in adult clients. Furthermore, you will learn to formulate evidence-based treatment plans tailored to the unique needs of adult clients, drawing upon current research and therapeutic modalities. Lastly, our emphasis on effective communication and interdisciplinary collaboration will prepare you to work seamlessly with other professionals in the field, providing holistic care to adults facing psychopathological challenges. This unit promises a rich learning experience, enabling you to become a well-rounded and compassionate clinician in the realm of adult psychopathology.
Learning Outcomes:
- Formulate evidence-based assessment plan to effectively evaluate the psychopathological symptoms and functional impairments experienced by adults.
- Develop effective communication and collaboration skills to facilitate integrated care for adult clients with psychopathological concerns.
- Apply diagnostic criteria for a range of adult psychopathological disorders to accurate assessment and differential diagnosis.
- Formulate evidence-based treatment plans tailored to the unique needs of adult clients, integrating current research findings and therapeutic modalities for various psychopathological conditions.
- Investigate and critically evaluate the multifaceted aetiological factors contributing to adult psychopathology, integrating biological, psychological, and environmental perspectives.
- Apply ethical principles and guidelines in clinical practice and demonstrate the ability to make morally sound decisions when dealing with complex ethical dilemmas related to adult psychopathological cases.
Assessment Tasks:
- Skills demonstration of clinical interview and MSE: N/A% (Hurdle: Competency)
- Group case presentation (45 minutes presentation + 15 minutes Q & A): 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Practice workbook (2,500 words - 50%) and oral exam (10 mins - 10%): 60% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload equals 144 hours per semester comprising: Contact hours for students: 3-hr in-person session per week Additional requirements for students: Independent study including individual/ group assignment and self-learning to make up the required minimum hours during the semester.

Subject: Intervention 1 (PSY5406)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this unit you will develop essential skills for effective therapeutic practice by bridging your theoretical knowledge with practical application. You will see that this involves emphasizing evidence-based interventions, cultural competence, and ethical considerations. Throughout the unit, you will explore psychological theories, therapeutic factors, methods to establish and maintain rapport, engage in discussions on transference and countertransference dynamics, and understand the basic framework of cognitive and behavioral therapies, with a focus on both children and adults. The content is designed to tailor interventions to diverse client needs, fostering a client-centered approach. Your learning experience will be dynamic, featuring a blend of lectures, workshops, and interactive sessions that encourage active engagement with both instructors and peers. The Moodle platform will be utilised, offering organised learning materials presented in a user-friendly format. The unit aims not only to provide a comprehensive theoretical understanding of interventions but also to enable you to demonstrate practical skills essential for competent and ethical clinical psychology practice. This unit is more than just a course; it is your pathway to becoming a proficient and compassionate clinical psychologist.
Learning Outcomes:
- Formulate treatment plans and implementing interventions with a diverse client population.
- Develop skills in reflection, evaluating therapeutic styles, recognize strengths and areas of improvement.
- Demonstrate the key components of the therapeutic alliance to establish and maintain a relationship with clients.
- Demonstrate competence in cognitive behavioral therapy principles and application of techniques by inculcating the cultural sensitivity according to the needs of the clients.
- Apply the principles and guidelines of ethics by considering the cultural sensitivity and meet the diverse needs of the client.
- Apply evidence-based practices while implementing psychological intervention.
Assessment Tasks:
- Case study analysis - Presentation (30 mins + 10 min Q&A): 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Skills demonstration: Role play exercises (15 min video) and reflection (800 words): 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Skills demonstration: Role play (30 min video) and reflective paper (1,500 words): 40% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 12 lectures and workshops for 12 weeks (total contact hours for 12 weeks - 36 hours) On campus teaching: Lectures & workshops = 3 hours on-campus session per week Student-directed learning (3 hours) Independent study Group Assignments Individual Assignments

Subject: Clinical placement 1 (PSY5421)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This placement unit consolidates your development in clinical practice in your role as a clinical psychologist in training. You will provide direct service to individuals and/or groups in placement settings. Placements are arranged via a network of providers with due regard given to the professional interests and competencies required of you in your final placement unit. You will be supervised in accordance with regulatory guidelines. During Case Analysis, you will participate in the discussion with your peers as well as by presenting at least one de-identified case. Throughout the Unit you will demonstrate your enhanced ability to integrate diverse and evidence-informed approaches to complex clinical, professional practice and evaluation issues.
Learning Outcomes:
- Knowledge of the discipline: Differentiate and discuss the multifaceted aspects of being a provisional psychologist within a range of clinical placement settings.
- Communication and interpersonal relationships: Develop effective professional relationships and communication (oral and written) with a wide range of colleagues, stakeholders, and clients with complex mental health presentations and disorders.
- Reflective practice: Use reflexive processes to enable adaptive practice, learning and behaviour change in clinical practice.
- Legal, ethical and professional matters: Manage ethical issues and adhere to professional practice standards (for example including confidentiality, risk and effective documentation) in increasingly complex circumstances.
- Research and evaluation: Apply the scientist-practitioner approach in the development, application and evaluation of evidence-informed treatment plans/interventions for people with complex mental health presentations and disorders.
- Psychological assessment and measurement: Use thorough assessment methodology and hypothesis testing approach to develop holistic clinical formulations for a wide range of complex mental health presentations (within a Diagnostic Framework).
- Intervention strategies: Apply a breadth of evidence-informed, culturally responsive intervention strategies to diverse individuals and/or groups with complex mental health presentations.
Assessment Tasks:
- OSCE Intervention Skills (50 minutes): 25% (Hurdle: Competency)
- Placement Portfolio: 50% (Hurdle: Competency)
- Case Analysis Presentation (20 minute oral presentation): 25% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: You are expected to complete approximately 300 hours in this placement towards your total of 1200 hours of placement (Work Integrated Learning) over the course of your degree. Contact hours for on-campus students: 270 hours of clinical placement experience including a minimum of approximately 120 direct client activity hours and 150 indirect hours including professional development activities, report writing, and administrative work Permission may be sought to transfer some days and hours between the three placements Minimum of 30 hours of supervision Group supervision sessions no more than 50% of total supervision There will be a one hour weekly applied session (Case Analysis) in which you are also expected to participate. This is included in the 300 hours of clinical placement experience. You are required to attend all scheduled applied sessions unless documentary evidence (such as a medical certificate or statutory declaration) is provided. Minimum attendance to pass the unit is 80%.
