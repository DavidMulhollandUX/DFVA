Subject: Aero Design and Manufacturing (AERO4100)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course examines aerospace structural design, with an emphasis on metallic structures. The course covers: 1) certification (i.e. what is required to demonstrate that a structural component is safe and compliant with applicable regulations); 2) stress analysis techniques used to design and certify the thin walled structures which comprise most of the aircraft structure; 3) characteristics and manufacturing processes for high strength aerospace metallic materials; 4) structural optimisation in a practical context (i.e. how to design a component, in terms of materials and sizing, to have minimum weight, the required strength, and be suitable for manufacture).
Learning Outcomes:
- Appropriately idealise the geometry of aerospace structural components subject to external applied loading, and calculate section properties and internal loading using standard analytical techniques for thin-walled structures.
- Predict the limit and ultimate strength of standard aerospace structural members, including columns, stiffeners, unstiffened and stiffened panels, and fastened joints.
- Use aerospace structural analysis techniques to perform preliminary sizing of an aerospace structural assembly and certify the new design in an engineering report.
- Identify and explain the characteristics, performance requirements, loading conditions, and mechanical property requirements of metallic materials used in aerospace vehicles.
- Identify and quantify the relationships between material composition, processing, microstructure, and properties, and explain the manufacturing processes used to produce aerospace structural components.
- Integrate knowledge of aerospace materials, manufacturing processes, and structural requirements to conduct material selection, and work in a team to design manufacturing solutions for advanced aerospace applications.
Assessment Tasks:
- In-class problems: 10% (max 1% per week; total mark calculated by adding the 10 best weekly marks)% (Hurdle: False)
- Assignment 1: Aircraft Structural Design: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Assignment 2: Manufacturing Process Design: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Final Exam Hurdle Identity Verified: 50% Structures(25%)/ Materials (25%)% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Applied Class 2 Hours/ Week Practical 8 Hours/ Week There will be two 4 hour pracs during the course

Subject: Aerospace Composites (AERO4300)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The course provides an introduction to composite materials, manufacturing and design with a primary focus on applications in the aerospace industry. Students will gain insight into the commonly used constituent materials, intermediates, and resulting composites and understand the many choices available in this hierarchy. The course will then introduce the students to the most common composite manufacturing processes for both thermoplastic and thermosetting matrix composites. Emphasis is put on enabling students to select the most appropriate manufacturing process for a given application and material combination. The design component of the course covers the key concepts of structural design and evaluation of composite materials. This includes introducing students to micromechanics, classical-laminate theory, and failure theory of composite materials using both analytical as well as numerical methods. In the final two weeks of the semester, the course ventures into advanced topics, including non-destructive testing, quality assurance, and ceramic matrix composite materials. The learning experience is enhanced through a hands-on manufacturing practical in which students work in teams, under the guidance of teaching staff, to manufacture aerospace-grade composite components.
Learning Outcomes:
- Select appropriate composite material systems to meet the requirements of specific applications.
- Determine suitable manufacturing methods for composite materials based on application, cost, and performance considerations.
- Apply composite mechanics and design principles to engineer composite structures.
- Specify appropriate destructive and non-destructive testing methods for quality assurance during manufacturing and through-life support.
Assessment Tasks:
- Materials and Manufacturing Poster: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Assignment Composite Mechanics and Design: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Workshop and NDT Studio participation assessment In-person: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Exam Hurdle Identity Verified: 60%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Studio 3 Hours/ Week Practical 4 Hours/ Ad Hoc

Subject: Polymer Engineering (CHEE3301)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Polymer synthesis & characterisation. Dependence of properties on molecular structure & microstructure. Polymer rheology. Unit processing operations, formulation & uses of polymers, mechanical properties, degradation & failure methods.
Learning Outcomes:
- Polymer chemical structure: Describe the basic molecular structure of common polymers and relate this to the synthesis route for the polymer.
- Polymer physical structure: Describe the physical morphology of polymer materials and relate this to relevant features of the chemical structure of the polymer.
- Polymer characterisation: Interpret data from polymer characterisation tests and relate this to identifying polymers and to solving problems encountered in polymer use.
- Polymer processing operations: Describe the basic unit operations used to manufacture polymer articles. You should be aware of the appropriate methods for making common articles and the basic characteristics of the polymers required for such operations. Practical work will be completed in a group.
- Polymer material properties: Describe the basic mechanical behaviour of polymers and relate this to the chemical and physical structure of the material and the molecular and bulk deformation processes. Practical work will be completed in a group.
- Polymer degradation: Understand the basic mechanisms and the effects of polymer degradation and become familiar with the common measures taken to either encourage or to limit these effects.
- Polymer selection: Using knowledge of polymer properties and polymer processing, select and justify suitable polymers for engineering uses.
- Polymers and society: Be able to articulate an engineering based response to contemporary issues relating to polymer use.

Subject: Professional Engineering and the Business Environment: Global Practice (ENGG7901)
Credit Points: 2

Subject: Environmental Performance of Materials (MATE7001)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Corrosion fundamentals, design against corrosion, corrosion protection principles and practice, corrosion in common environments, corrosion resistant alloys, corrosion mechanisms, environment assisted fracture and fatigue.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and describe the major forms of corrosion, their underlying causes and mechanisms.
- Apply the key thermodynamic principles to predict conditions under which metals corrode, using the Nernst equation, Electromotive Force (EMF) and Galvanic Series, and Pourbaix diagrams.
- Analyse and apply kinetic concepts by using mixed potential theory and polarisation curves to predict corrosion current and corrosion potential.
- Demonstrate a familiarity with the key testing methods for measuring corrosion and the proficiency to estimate corrosion rates from relevant test data.
- Evaluate the nature and limitations of passivity in metals and its influence on corrosion resistance.
- Explain the interaction between corrosion and stress in causing failure of materials.
- Demonstrate familiarity with industry-relevant corrosion mitigation principles and techniques for practical engineering applications.
- Design corrosion protection systems for assets exposed to corrosive environments by applying key concepts of corrosion science.
- Research and analyse a corrosion‑related topic and deliver an oral presentation that effectively communicates your findings, individually or in a group.
- Identify and assess real-world corrosion issues and apply corrosion knowledge to develop a comprehensive written technical report.
Assessment Tasks:
- Quiz Online: 7.5%% (Hurdle: False)
- Presentation on a Key Aspect In-person: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Corrosion assignment: 25%% (Hurdle: False)

Workload: Lecture 3 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week

Subject: Advanced Manufacturing (MATE7013)
Credit Points: 2

Subject: Advanced Materials Characterisation (MATE7014)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Materials Characterisation provides unique tools for understanding the materials and their demonstrated properties. Materials Characterisation techniques, such as x-ray diffraction, scanning electron microscopy, and transmission electron microscopy, allow detailed structural, chemical, and morphological characteristics of materials to be determined, which has become essential tools for materials research and their productions. By correlating the determined structural and chemical characteristics of a material with its fabrication/processing, the formation mechanism of the material can be clarified. This is vital for developing new material systems, and for identifying problems in the production lines. On the other hand, the correlation of the determined structural and chemical characteristics of a material with its demonstrated properties allows the material's structure-property link to be built, which is critically important for understanding the origin of the properties. For this reason, demand for learning various materials characterization techniques have increased sharply in the recent decades.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate an understanding of characterization - Select the most appropriate characterization techniques (diffraction, microscopy and spectroscopy) to perform high-level materials characterization tasks
- Demonstrate an understanding of characterization - Demonstrate expertise in operating characterization instruments for securing the best possible data

- Perform diffraction characterizations skillfully - Describe crystal structures professionally by effective structure indexing
- Perform diffraction characterizations skillfully - Estimate the microstructure features of materials based on X-ray diffraction data
- Perform diffraction characterizations skillfully - Employ the electron diffraction technique to identify crystal structures

- Perform diffraction characterizations skillfully - Design diffraction experiments to effectively determine the structural characterisation of materials

- Execute microscopic characterizations proficiently - Determine the structural and chemical characteristics of materials using scanning transmission electron microscopy
- Execute microscopic characterizations proficiently - Design microscopic experiments to effectively clarify the microstructures of materials
- Implement spectroscopic characterizations expertly - Employ the X-ray energy dispersive spectroscopy to determine the chemical characteristics of materials
- Implement spectroscopic characterizations expertly - Establish the bonding and chemical characteristics of materials using electron energy loss spectroscopy
