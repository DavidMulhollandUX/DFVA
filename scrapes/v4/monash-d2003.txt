### Overview
Learning designers create engaging and impactful educational and professional development experiences for learners of all ages in multiple contexts. They can also be known as instructional designers, educational designers, learning architects, ed-tech innovators, and online training and professional development providers. Learning designers are creative problem solvers with a deep understanding of human learning who collaborate with content experts, educators and media specialists, and who use new technologies to design effective courses. They work across a range of contexts including universities, technical and further education institutions, private enterprise, government agencies, educational technology companies, and not-for-profit organisations. Sometimes they use their creative ideas to become entrepreneurs in the ed-tech and other industries.

Within the Bachelor of Learning Design and Technology, you will engage in hands-on and practical problem-solving tasks. You will work in design teams to creatively produce engaging learning activities and instructional media content, and you will also learn how to apply industry standards and innovative technologies. You will be challenged to extend your critical and creative thinking skills, and will expand and consolidate your knowledge of human centred design, design thinking, innovation, and human learning and motivation.

Through industry engagement you will have the opportunity to apply your learning to a real-life context as you work alongside learning design teams to create real solutions for both educators and their learners, thus equipping yourself with the practical skills, creativity and deep knowledge required, to join one of the fastest growing international professions allowing you to forge an impactful global career.

### Structure
The course develops through four parts: Part A. Core studies, Part B. Specialist studies, Part C. Professional practice and Part D. Elective studies.

Part A. Core studies

In this part you will gain expertise in the principles and practices of a skilled learning design and technology professional.

These core units develop your knowledge and skills in designing educational experiences including foundational knowledge of learning design concepts, methodologies, systems, technologies and media. Through these units you will develop a toolkit of instructional practices and skills relating to the creation of educational media and learning designs.

Part B. Specialist studies

These core units will extend technical understanding and skills of visual design, media creation and internet technologies. These units provide a foundation upon which subsequent units in Part A can build, extending your skills in content creation for impactful learning.

Part C. Professional practice

The faculty's Professional Experience Team will source industry-based placements for you. If you are based outside of Melbourne, you will be guided to local placement opportunities. Through the industry engagement units, you will have the opportunity to develop your skills in readiness for working in educational design teams and in the professional workplace. Recognising the diverse nature of workplace settings and conditions, industry engagement may be on-site or virtual, and where appropriate may also include deemed equivalent experiences such as client-based projects, study tours and internships.

Part D. Elective studies 

The opportunity to select from a range of elective units provides you with an opportunity to pursue topics of particular interest in complementary disciplines, deepening your knowledge and skills which you can apply to educational design and related practices. A diverse range of preapproved units is offered, however alternate units may be taken if deemed suitable and approved by the faculty.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
integrate ethical, legal and policy considerations in designing for learning and learning technology applications
**2.**
critically evaluate learning design theories, learning technologies, research, contemporary issues and industry practices
**3.**
effectively communicate ideas and concepts through a variety of media.
**4.**
work independently and collaboratively in diverse teams
**5.**
identify and employ appropriate technologies to solve complex design problems
**6.**
create effective and innovative learning designs for diverse contexts and learner needs
**7.**
design for social justice, equity, inclusion, intersectionality and decolonising education
**8.**
seek and act upon feedback to improve practices and designs

### AHEGS
The Bachelor of Learning Design and Technology is an undergraduate award taught and assessed in English. Graduates have the ability and skills to create engaging and impactful educational and professional development experiences for learners of all ages in multiple contexts. Graduates are able to apply learning and motivation design theories, appropriate innovative instructional technologies and create various forms of assessments as well as digital content. The course usually takes three years of full-time study, or part-time equivalent, to complete. The Bachelor of Learning Design and Technology is a Level 7 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Credit Points: 6
Overview: Design thinking applies the skills and strategies developed by designers to a wide range of problems and situations.
