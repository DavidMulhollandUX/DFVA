## Description
The two-year Master of Data Science and Decisions has been developed to train the current and future generation of data scientists and data analysts to meet the strong demand in the workforce. Graduates will have broad and advanced knowledge and skills in data science across three areas: mathematics and statistics, computer science, and economics.  They will also gain deeper knowledge in one of these three areas by pursuing studies in one of four specialisations. Students will develop research and communication skills in data science through project work.

Students have two pathways for completing some of the required statistics coursework.  In Option 1, students take two courses (MATH5846 and MATH5856) that cover the foundations of probability and statistics necessary for data science. This pathway is intended for students who have limited or no previous exposure to probability and mathematical statistical theory from previous study. Option 2 is intended for students who already have taken prior statistics coursework. In this pathway, students take MATH5905 and an elective of their choosing. Students choosing Option 2 should have the following assumed knowledge: probability, random variables, the central limit theorem, Markov chains, hypothesis tests, confidence intervals, maximum likelihood estimation, and least squares methods.

## Progression & Academic Rules
Students usually complete their Research Project during their last one or two Terms. Students are not eligible to start a project until they have completed at least 36 UoC  (typically, 6 courses).

- Students who have passed 24 UoC can elect to exit the program with a Graduate Certificate (7959).

- Students who have passed 48 UoC can elect to exit the program with a Graduate Diploma (5959).

- Students who have completed the Graduate Certificate (7959) and have a WAM of 65 may apply to transfer into this Masters program and will receive up to 24 UOC of Advanced Standing.

- Students who have completed the full 48 UoC of the Graduate Diploma (5959), and have a WAM of 65, may apply to transfer into this Masters program and will receive up to 48 UoC of Advanced Standing.

- Students who have completed 24 UoC of the Graduate Diploma (5959), and have a WAM of 65, may apply to transfer into this Masters program and will receive up to 24 UoC of Advanced Standing.

## Admission Requirements
**Limitations on Recognition of Prior Learning**:
In limited cases, students with previous postgraduate or honours-level coursework, including Honours graduates in Mathematics or Statistics from UNSW (or equivalent), may receive advanced standing.  Advanced standing is limited to a maximum of 36 UOC.  Students who receive the maximum 36 UOC may complete the degree with 60 UOC, or 1.3 years of full-time study.

Advanced standing will only be granted for courses at the honours or postgraduate level, not courses at the undergraduate level. Students must apply for advanced standing and the School of Mathematics and Statistics will determine if the student’s completed coursework is deemed equivalent to UNSW counterparts.  Advanced standing is not guaranteed.

**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
The entry requirement for the Master of Data Science and Decisions is both of the following:

1. A recognised bachelor degree (or equivalent qualification) with a substantial quantitative component in a cognate discipline.

2. An overall average of 70 or higher as determined by the UNSW Postgraduate Coursework Entry Calculator.

Notes

Assumed knowledge: A mathematical background of a minimum of one course in several variable calculus and one course in linear algebra at the university undergraduate level.

International students: Prospective international students need to meet the University's English language requirements.

Credit Points: 6
Overview: Machine learning is the algorithmic approach to learning from data. The course also covers aspects of data mining, the application of machine learning to obtain insight from data. - Implement machine learning algorithms, apply them to realistic datasets and collect results to enable evaluation of their performance.
- Construct a well-defined learning problem for a given task, selecting representations for the data input and output, the model, and the learning algorithm.
- Explain key concepts from the foundations of learning theory, describe their applicability, and express knowledge of the general limits of machine learning.
- Compare different algorithms according to the properties of their inputs and outputs, and the computational methods used.
- Develop and describe algorithms to solve a well-defined learning problem.

Subject: Experimental Design and Causal Inference (MATH5852)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course in statistics that covers the design and analysis of experiments and observational studies to answer causal questions. Topics include potential outcomes, causal estimands, and the importance of the design phase in both experiments and observational studies. Subject: Bootstrap and Other Resampling Methods (MATH5868)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The course presents the basic techniques of resampling methods useful in statistics. These have grown very popular over the last two decades as they offer reliable alternatives to traditional asymptotic approaches, mostly based on Central Limit Theorems. The flagship instance of resampling-based approaches is the so-called `bootstrap' , by now considered as a real breakthrough in statistics. A very versatile technique, the bootstrap allows easy determination of the sampling distribution of any statistic of interest (estimator or test statistic) in a variety of situations. In fact, the bootstrap bypasses heavy analytical developments by substituting appropriate simulations. In many cases, the quality of the bootstrap approximation is better than its asymptotic counterparts, while in other situations, there is no asymptotic approximation available and the bootstrap turns out to be the only possibility for statistical inference. In this course, the bootstrap and other related resampling methods are first introduced in a general framework and their theoretical properties are investigated. Then, they are illustrated through many standard problems of statistical inference and common questions in related fields.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse the results and draw appropriate conclusions, when the resampling techniques are used to solve problems with real data.
- Create and develop short pieces of code in an appropriate software to generate output in regard to the previous learning outcome.
- Understand the underlying mechanisms for justifying the resampling approach.
- Select and apply correctly the main resampling methods for statistical inference.
