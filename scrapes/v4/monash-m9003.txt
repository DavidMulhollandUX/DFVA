## Professional Certificate of Public Health
##### M9003
##### 12 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
The Professional Certificate of Public Health builds knowledge of key principles in public health. This course will explore key principles of public health, including determinants of health, the role of policy and advocacy. It will develop knowledge and skills in health promotion and prevention, applied across public health challenges.

### Post-nominals
ProfCertPH

### Structure
The course comprises 12 credit points of study (2 units) where you will be introduced to public health at a graduate level.

### Requirements
Units are 6 credit points unless otherwise stated.

Core studies

You must complete the following: 

  * MPH5288 Public health fundamentals

  * MPH5002 Foundations of health promotion and program planning

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
describe key socio-demographic trends and major public health determinants and inequalities
**2.**
describe the principles and tools of public health practice
**3.**
apply the key principles, theories and frameworks, used in health promotion and prevention
**4.**
define the history of preventive health and health promotion
**5.**
apply public health related knowledge and skills to address a range of public health problems
**6.**
interpret a range of behavioural, social and cultural, environmental and political determinants of health

### Progression to further studies
Successful completion of this course may provide a pathway to the Master of Public Health (M6024) subject to satisfying the appropriate entry requirements. Credit may be granted towards other Faculty of Medicine, Nursing and Health Science postgraduate courses upon successful completion.

Subject: Foundations of health promotion and program planning (MPH5002)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit introduces you to key principles of, and planning for, health promotion. You will examine health promotion frameworks and theories that are used to promote health at a population level to compliment and contrast individual treatment. You will examine the values and principles that guide contemporary health promotion and its capacity to influence the determinants of health. You will also develop skills in needs assessment, priority setting, using evidence and theory to make intervention choices and program evaluation. The roles played by partnerships, capacity building and participation will be explored, and the steps toward improving program sustainability are examined.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and apply the key principles, theories and frameworks, used in health promotion and prevention.
- Define and explain the history of preventive health and health promotion
- Describe and apply the key steps of program planning including needs analysis, participation and the development of goals, objectives and strategies
- Identify and adapt a range of approaches for promoting health that include upstream, mid-stream and downstream actions.
- Critically reflect on the role of Health Promotion in addressing contemporary and emerging challenges in health.
Assessment Tasks:
- Health promotion program plan (2,100 words): 35% (Hurdle: None)
- Essay (900 words): 15% (Hurdle: None)
- Report (1,800 words): 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Health promotion and resource rationale (1,200 words): 20% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 6 hours of teacher-directed learning activities per week, and 6 hours of self-directed study per week. There is an expectation that students will attend 3 on-campus Block Days incorporating 23 contact hours.

Subject: Public health fundamentals (MPH5288)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit will examine foundational aspects of public health, and explore contemporary challenges facing public health both nationally and Internationally. You will be introduced to strategies and approaches including evidence-based practice, health promotion and health policy, as well as key principles guiding public health practice. You will apply this knowledge to critically appraise initiatives to address complex health issues from a public health perspective.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and articulate contemporary and emerging challenges in public health.
- Define the principles of evidence-based practice and assess their implementation across public health initiatives
- Identify and appraise methods and application of disease prevention and control at a population level
- Describe and integrate the basic principles and tools of public health practice
- Evaluate a range of behavioural, social and cultural, environmental and political determinants of health
- Evaluate and adapt a range of approaches and strategies involved in public health including health promotion and health policy, and;
Assessment Tasks:
- Written report (1,800 words): 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Briefing paper (2,400 words): 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Policy position statement (1,800 words): 30% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: The total workload requirement for this unit is 12 hours of work per week. This incorporates 23 contact hours over 3 block days + Off-Campus learning (OCL) (including private study and written reflections on public health issues and challenges and applications through an online forum).
