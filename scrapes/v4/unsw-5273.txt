## Description
The Graduate Diploma in Financial Planning is designed to develop or upgrade your investment, financial planning, taxation knowledge and skills for a career in personal financial advisory.
Suitable if you are seeking an exciting career working with clients to build long lasting and trusted relationships, providing sound financial guidance and education.

Taught by leading Finance and Taxation academics at UNSW Business School, this program provides you with a distinguished level of training to advance in the career of personal financial and investment advisory. Suitable for working professionals, you can choose from a combination of online and face-to-face courses to suit your work/life balance.

The program offers you a challenging learning environment to achieve an integrated learning in financial planning, investment and taxation. It also provides you with the learning required for professional accreditation. Graduates develop knowledge and skills in financial markets, financial products, investment (theory, evidence, and quantitative techniques), risk management, financial planning, taxation law and strategies, compliance, ethical and professional conduct.

## Program Structure & Degree Requirements
Students must complete 48 Units of Credit (UOC) structured across the following academic components:

### Prescribed Electives (12 UOC)
Students must take at least 12 UOC of the following courses.

- **TABL5551**: Taxation Law [6 UOC]
- **TABL5511**: Legal Foundations of Business [6 UOC]
- **FINS5548**: Financial Technology [6 UOC]

### Core Courses (36 UOC)
Students must take 36 UOC of the following courses.

- **FINS5539**: Estate, Retirement Planning and Asset Protection [6 UOC]
- **FINS5531**: Personal Risk, Insurance, and Superannuation for Financial Advisers [6 UOC]
- **FINS5512**: Financial Markets and Institutions [6 UOC]
- **FINS5513**: Investments and Portfolio Selection [6 UOC]
- **FINS5510**: Personal Financial Planning [6 UOC]
- **FINS5537**: Financial Planning Advice and Ethics [6 UOC]

Students in the Graduate Diploma in Financial Planning may exit early with a Graduate Certificate in Financial Planning if they have met the requirements of that program. Please contact The Nucleus: Student Hub for further information.

Subject: Personal Financial Planning (FINS5510)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course equips you with the essential knowledge and practical skills to effectively build, manage, invest, and protect your personal finances throughout your life. Tailored for university graduates, it delves into critical financial topics, including assessing your current financial situation, setting long-term goals aligned with your values and ethics, and creating a roadmap to achieve financial independence. You will explore key areas such as career planning, home ownership and debt management, tax planning, risk identification and management, and selecting the right investments to reach your financial goals. This course provides a comprehensive foundation to help you take control of your financial future with confidence and the foundational knowledge to determine whether you are interested in pursuing a career in the wealth management industry.
Learning Outcomes:
- Create personalised financial planning and investment strategies to achieve long-term financial goals, ensuring alignment with personal values, ethical considerations and evolving financial landscapes.
- Explain the role of professional financial planners and the structured financial planning process they use to provide advice to their clients.
- Analyse and critically assess financial and investment concepts.
- Demonstrate key personal financial planning and investment strategies, including effective household budgeting, property acquisition, debt management, retirement savings, tax planning, risk management and building a diversified investment portfolio.

Subject: Investments and Portfolio Selection (FINS5513)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course develops a basic conceptual framework to understand modern investments. Students will develop a more complete understanding of the risk-return tradeoff and the effects of diversification. Using this knowledge, students will learn how to price assets, build investment portfolios, and evaluate alternative investment strategies in securities markets, including equities and fixed income. By completing a real-world portfolio optimisation task, students will learn how to apply the theory in practical settings.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the limitations of the models in certain situations and the resulting implications for designing investment strategies.
- Apply the standard models of asset pricing to measure, price and manage risk; value securities; and solve real problems in investments.
- Analyse the operation of financial markets and common financial products.
- Create an optimal investment portfolio by applying portfolio theory.

Subject: Personal Risk, Insurance, and Superannuation for Financial Advisers (FINS5531)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course covers insurance, personal risk management, and superannuation from a personal perspective for financial planning for individuals, families, and businesses. The course focuses on providing financial advice concerning the relevant areas through performing needs analysis and analysing the clients’ goals and objectives to provide appropriate advice and personal strategies to assist the clients in meeting their goals and objectives. The course covers the provision of compliant scaled financial product advice relating to the relevant area in the form of a Statement of Advice (SOA).
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the Australian superannuation and social security systems, rules, and their taxation implications for providing personal advice to clients.
- Demonstrate an understanding of the advisers’ responsibilities in providing insurance advice to clients, including the communication of client contractual rights, responsibilities, and the claims process for a range of insurance policies.
- Develop strategies and communicate risk, insurance, and superannuation recommendations for personal advice, including constructing a statement of advice to the best interest duty standard.
- Determine retirement planning needs in specific client situations and evaluate the appropriateness of various superannuation structures and products.
- Demonstrate and apply an understanding of the critical elements of an insurance contract in the provision of personal insurance advice.
- Demonstrate and apply risk management strategies to determine a client’s insurance coverage needs to the best interest duty standard.

Subject: Financial Planning Advice and Ethics (FINS5537)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This is a capstone course in the financial planning curriculum to provide students with a practical understanding of the provision of financial product advice and managing clients’ relationships. It consolidates and completes student knowledge and skills required for pursuing employment in the financial planning industry. The course a study of important aspects of ethics, professional standards and Chapter 7 of the Corporation Act to the standards set by the Financial Adviser Standards and Ethics Authority. The course also covers the different elements of financial planning advice including understanding the client's situation and needs, conducting analysis and research, formulating appropriate financial planning strategies, choosing suitable products, preparing a fully compliant financial plan, implementing the financial plan and providing ongoing review and services to the client. The course also covers the knowledge and skills required for providing personal life insurance and general insurance advice. It is a required course for professional recognition in financial planning.
Learning Outcomes:
- Preparation and presentation of a comprehensive statement of advice
- Demonstrate a working knowledge of the operations of the financial planning industry.
- Demonstrate practical understanding of client relationships and practice management
- Describe the main pieces of legislation governing the financial planning industry.
- Explain the financial planning process and how to create a fully compliant financial plan.
- Be able to discuss and write about the links between financial planning techniques and compliance and ethical issues.
- Application of ethics and professional standards to the service of providing professional financial advice to clients
- Critique practitioner’s perspectives on how to offer financial services.

Subject: Estate, Retirement Planning and Asset Protection (FINS5539)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course provides students with key estate planning knowledge and skills to provide clients a holistic financial and estate planning service. The course deal with the practical application of how an adviser deals with other professionals including lawyers, accountants, tax advisers, etc to help manage the clients’ estate planning needs, including issues related to succession planning and asset protection. - Ability to identify possible estate and retirement planning strategy options.

- Ability to address key issues related to wills and appointment of power of attorneys.
- Ability to define and determine key asset protection and succession planning issues related to certain clients.
- Demonstrate a working and practical knowledge of estate and retirement planning relevant to the financial planning industry.
- Demonstrate practical understanding of client relationships and practice management.
- Dealing with complexity related to marriage breakdown, administering and estate, and asset transfer including superannuation and in particular SMSFs.
- Critique practitioner’s perspectives on how to offer estate planning service to financial planning clients include dealing with other professionals (accountants, lawyers, etc) and managing the process.

Subject: Financial Technology (FINS5548)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course examines the transformative landscape of technological advancements and their fast-evolving impact on the banking and finance industry across personal, institutional, and regulatory dimensions (collectively referred to as "FinTech"). It delves into innovative thought processes and frameworks designed to navigate complexity, foster new business opportunities, and manage risks effectively. Students will analyze the evolution of FinTech, exploring how these technologies are reshaping financial services, enhancing customer experiences, and creating new market opportunities. Topics include the evaluation of regulatory frameworks, strategic solutions for AI and blockchain integration, and the design of innovative financial products driven by data analytics. Through critical analysis on real-world use cases and collaborative projects, students will develop advanced competencies in assessing business models and formulating strategic solutions for modern financial challenges. The course culminates in a final group project, where students will propose a new FinTech startup idea that leverages the latest technological innovations, demonstrating their comprehensive understanding of the course material.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyze the transformative roles of FinTech solutions across various banking and finance domains, and evaluate the effectiveness of tools and methodologies in delivering ethical, responsible, and business-savvy solutions.
- Examine the evolution of FinTech across global banking and finance ecosystems, the challenges and opportunities presented by technological innovations, and their impact on the financial industry.
- Build knowledge in designing, building, and scaling FinTech platforms amidst rapid technological evolution, while also identifying essential building blocks for successful AI-powered FinTech innovations.
- Collaborate with peers, applying a framework to develop and pitch an innovative FinTech solution that addresses specific market demands (or creating new ones), reflecting the transformation of banking and finance in the digital economy.
- Assess the historical and current regulatory frameworks in FinTech, evaluating their effectiveness in addressing challenges posed by technological advancements such as AI, and identifying the necessary guardrails.
- Analyze business models of real-world FinTech platforms, assessing their abilities to adapt and sustain themselves amidst the increasingly dynamic technological landscape shaped by AI advancements.

Subject: Taxation Law (TABL5551)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Tax affects all of business and investment. Do you want to have a good knowledge of how tax affects those involved with businesses and investments, and understand how the government raises revenue? This course will help you understand the tax regulatory framework and the tax rules operating in the context of a business and investments. You will acquire and develop analytical skills in applying tax rules to commercial transactions. Topics include: brief coverage of tax administration rules; legal obligations of tax agents; income tax formula and tax liability, and liability for other levies and charges; concept of income and assessable income including net capital gains; overview of exempt income; general deduction provision and allowable deductions generally; specific deduction regimes that either supplement the general deduction rule (e.g. capital allowance regimes) or deny a deduction otherwise available; tax accounting rules, and tax treatment of trading stock; taxation of taxable income made through partnerships, trusts and corporations; selected specific anti-avoidance rules and overview of the general anti-avoidance rule; overview of cross border transactions and international tax rules; brief coverage of goods and services tax (GST), fringe benefits tax (FBT) and how these taxes interact with income tax; and the tax treatment of superannuation.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply the relevant tax law rules to solve a typical tax-related professional problem.
- Identify the relevant tax law rules that may apply in a typical tax-related professional problem.
- Explore the tax law to identify rules that do not meet their objectives and use your critical legal reasoning skills to construct arguments for a “new” tax law rule.
- Summarise and explain clearly, in writing, how the tax law rules under Australia’s tax regime studied in this course have been applied by judges in court cases and by members of relevant tribunals.
- Construct written work which is logically and professionally presented and communicates ideas in a succinct and clear manner.
- Locate, summarise and explain the Australian tax law rules that have been studied in this course.
