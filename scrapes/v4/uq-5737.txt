Subject: Plant Breeding (AGRC3041)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides students with an opportunity to explore the complexities and challenges of modern plant breeding. The course will cover the foundations of cultivar development through the application of cross breeding and selection, coupled with a range of advanced breeding technologies that are used in the development of breeding schemes leading to the release of improved plant varieties and cultivars. This will be examined in the context of population improvement and the development of inbred, hybrid, synthetic and clonal cultivars. Case studies and simulations will be used to highlight recent advances in plant breeding research, examine the impacts of gene action, heritability and genotype x environment interactions on the outcomes from breeding and selection.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply key concepts of biology and genetics (reproduction system, Mendelian, population and quantitative genetics) in plant improvement.
- Analysis and interpret data from plant breeding using appropriate tools and techniques.
- Evaluate breeding objectives and selection methods for different crop types and reproduction systems.
- Communicate plant breeding concepts effectively through visual and oral formats for academic and professional audiences.
Assessment Tasks:
- Quiz Identity Verified In-person: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Poster - Data analysis Online: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Sequential Presentation and Reflection task Identity Verified In-person: 40% Presentation (30%) Reflection (10%)% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 3 Hours/ Week Practical 2 Hours/ Week Fieldwork 9 Hours/ Ad Hoc

Subject: Industry Placement (AGRC3060)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course enables students to acquire practical skills and experience in an authentic work environment. Through the completion of 120-160 hours of placement in an individual or team setting related to their area of study, students will gain an understanding of the real-world application of their learning. Students will also learn about the nature of careers in their chosen field, identify possible career pathways, and reflect on their professional skills and personal development. Information about enrolling in a placement course, including registering your interest and application deadlines, can be found here: science.uq.edu.au/placements . Students enrolled outside of a Science program will need to request access to register their interest in this course by contacting enquire@science.uq.edu.au. IMPORTANT: This course is offered in Teaching Period 5 (Winter) and Summer Semester only. It is not offered during Semester 1 or Semester 2. Teaching Period 5 falls under the parent semester of Semester 2 for enrolment purposes. However, students enrolling in this course in Teaching Period 5 must undertake their placement during the winter inter-semester break.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate an enhanced understanding of your preferred field of study and potential career pathways through reflective engagement in either an individual or group-based professional placement.
- Apply theoretical knowledge to real-world contexts by undertaking tasks and responsibilities—individually or collaboratively—that develop practical skills and professional experience in a relevant workplace setting.
- Critically evaluate your own capabilities and contributions, as well as those of your team (where applicable), in the process of applying knowledge and skills within a professional environment.
- Demonstrate the ability to engage with workplace practices, policies, and professional standards, both independently and as part of a team, in alignment with expectations of the field of study.
Assessment Tasks:

- Placement Showcase Presentation Hurdle Identity Verified Online: Pass/Fail - Must Pass% (Hurdle: True)

Subject: Plant Physiology (AGRC7048)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Plants are integral to agricultural production systems. But do you understand the processes that drive plant growth and development? Plant physiology is the study of plant structure and function. - Explain plant resource acquisition and use
- Explain control of plant development driven by internal and environmental signals
- Relate physiological processes across scales (cell-organ-plant)
- Concisely communicate the use of plant phenotyping tools and results of experiments in written and oral formats

Subject: Sustainable Soil Management (AGRC7053)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course examines how soils can be managed sustainably to provide the ecosystem services that we rely upon for our survival, whilst simultaneously ensuring that these soils are not degraded for future generations. In particular, we will examine conservation agriculture, sustainable irrigation practices, soil nutrients and organic matter dynamics, soil degradation and soil health issues. In addition, we will also examine how soils determine sustainable land use and give consideration to impacts of soil management on the broader environment.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the importance of soils for regulating plant growth, food production, and climate change
- Evaluate how soils interact with the broader environment, including their influence on a range of environmental processes
- Explain the role of agriculture production systems in the sustainable management of soils
- Use soil water relationships to examine plant water use and optimise irrigation systems
- Experimentally investigate approaches for solving a range of soil science problems, including for management of soil degradation
Assessment Tasks:
- Sequential - Practical assessments In-person: 50% (all 4 reports are of equal weighting 12.5%)% (Hurdle: False)
- Final exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: True)
