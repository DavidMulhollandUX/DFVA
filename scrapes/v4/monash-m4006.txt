## Graduate Certificate of Health Management
##### M4006
##### 24 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
This course, offered by the Department of Epidemiology and Preventive Medicine, targets professionals working within the health care sector who wish to expand their knowledge and skills in the management of health services. It provides core competencies in health management, including leadership, human resource and financial management of health services.

### AQF level
Level 8 - Graduate Certificate

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
develop skills in the analysis of management problems and problem solving techniques
**2.**
demonstrate skills in literature review, report writing, oral presentations, verbal and visual communications
**3.**
understand the difference between leadership and management
**4.**
apply a detailed understanding of the principles of financial and resource management to a health care context
**5.**
critically appraise the interface between health care management theory and practice
**6.**
demonstrate the interpersonal, communication and team leadership skills appropriate for senior health care managers
**7.**
demonstrate knowledge and develop skills in two areas of interest.

### Progression to further studies
Successful completion of this course with a minimum weighted average mark of at least 60%, may provide a pathway to the Graduate Diploma of Health Management (M5007) or Master of Health Management (M6008).

### AHEGS
The Graduate Certificate of Health Management is a postgraduate award. The course is taught in English. Graduates have demonstrated core competencies in health management including leadership, human resources and financial management of health services. The course takes one year of part-time study to complete. The Graduate Certificate of Health Management is a Level 8 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Law for health systems (MPH5265)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Review of legal management principles related to health care by examining common law principles and statutes. Examination of the Australian legal system, including the Coroner's court, with an emphasis on Victorian and Commonwealth cases and statutes. Focus on key areas of medical and health law such as negligence consent, privacy of health information, clinical research, abortion, euthanasia, mental health, infectious diseases, health complaints, and law for health facilities such as hospitals.
Learning Outcomes:
- Interpret and transmit knowledge and complex health law principles to health care managers and non-specialist audiences.
- Interpret and synthesise the applicability of legal reviews and reports to health care management practice.
- Analyse critically, reflect on and synthesise the sources of law relevant to the management health care organisations.
- Research and apply legal principles to handling complex clinical situations.
Assessment Tasks:
- Critical Reflection (6 x 400 words to 5 x 480 words: (2,400 words): 40% (Hurdle: Threshold)
- Memo (2,100 words): 35% (Hurdle: None)
- Infographic (300 words + graphics): 25% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 6 hours of teacher-directed learning activities per week, and 6 hours of self-directed study per week, plus 2 block days via zoom.

Subject: Clinical leadership and management (MPH5266)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This Unit will review key leadership, management, and organisation theory and its application to healthcare settings. It will investigate the role of the manager, leadership skills, staffing issues, clinician engagement, performance management, career management, managing change, structuring organisations for patient care, developing strategy, consumer engagement, change management, and project management.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the application of complex management theories in the management of health care teams and organisations.
- Interpret the applicability of health care research findings to health care management practice.
- Communicate health care management solutions to specialist and non-specialist audiences.
- Demonstrate theoretical and practical approaches to leadership within health services and the importance of clinical engagement.
- Evaluate the perspectives of health care consumers and other stakeholders in health service planning.
- Formulate practical solutions and apply management experience to solve complex health management problems.
Assessment Tasks:
- Critical reflections: 40% (Hurdle: Threshold)
- Report (2,400 words): 40% (Hurdle: Threshold)
- Essay (20%) (1,200 words): 20% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 9 hours per week including 2 Block Days of 7.5 contact hours each

Subject: Principles of health care quality improvement (MPH5267)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this unit you will learn how historical, political & social factors impact on quality measurement in health care; the relationship of industrial and health care quality monitoring; epidemiological and statistical quality measurement principles; the strengths and limitations of current monitoring techniques; different sources of health care quality data; principles of clinical indicator programs; adverse event monitoring, satisfaction surveys and benchmarking; and the relationship between evidence based medicine, clinical practice guidelines and quality improvement. You will appreciate the complexities in designing, implementing and evaluating quality improvement programs in the clinical setting. The content aligns well with other management units in the program such as MPH5266 (Clinical Leadership and Management) and MPH5020 (Introduction to Biostatistics and Epidemiology). It is a core unit of the Graduate Diploma and Master of Health Services Management.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the key driving forces that underpin the need for healthcare quality improvement.
- Contribute to debates on safety and quality problems and potential solutions.
- Formulate practical solutions to safety and quality problems leading to improvement in a healthcare context.
- Apply safety and quality literature, research findings to healthcare practice.
- Apply quality improvement tools and methods to healthcare systems, processes and outcomes.
Assessment Tasks:
- Written report (2,400 words): 40% (Hurdle: Threshold)
- Critical reflections (5 x 480 words each. Total 2,400 words): 40% (Hurdle: Threshold)
- Letter to the editor (Letter x 600 words and supporting rationale x 600 words): 20% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 15 contact hours over 2 block day On average 10 hours per week to participate in online tasks and complete assignments

Subject: Financial management in health care (MPH5268)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The provision of effective healthcare is an essential component of contemporary society, however with people living longer and community expectations of and demand for healthcare increasing at an unprecedented rate, the financial costs of healthcare are growing at an ever increasing rate. Together with rapid advances in clinical capabilities and technological sophistication, meeting these social and financial challenges has never been more challenging. In this unit you will examine the basic accounting, financial reporting, budgeting and financial business planning concepts applicable to the healthcare sector, and how they are used by healthcare professionals to aid strategic, operational and service delivery decision making.
Learning Outcomes:
- Interpret operational and capital budgeting processes, and critique their importance in submissions for major items of capital expenditure
- Apply accounting terminologies, concepts and processes that underpin the preparation of key financial statements
- Appraise the various internal management reports typically used in health care organisations and evaluate their use in the day to day management of organisational sub units
- Analyse the statutory financial statements prepared and used by public and private sector health care providers
- Critically analyse the importance of business planning and its role in organisational growth and development
- Review the financial reporting requirements of the regulatory framework that applies to the healthcare sector and assess how they impact the preparation and use of financial reporting systems
Assessment Tasks:
- Case study analysis and reflection (2,400 words): 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Report (1,400 words): 40% (Hurdle: Competency)
- Essay (1,200 words): 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Audio visual presentation (10 mins): 10% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 12 hours of self-directed study per week, plus 3 block days.

Subject: Foundations of health policy (MPH5269)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Delivery of health services is underpinned by a framework of health policies and other health system elements. Health professional leaders and managers, and those who aspire to these roles, need to know about these policies and about the process of policy making so that they can understand why a policy is what it is, and how to engage in the policy making process. This unit involves a structured review of policy making processes, key challenges and major issues confronting governments and health systems around the world, and how policy can help address these. Throughout the unit, current Australian policies and senior health policy experts will be drawn on to apply theory to practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify the key components of policy development.
- Effectively communicate complex information related to health policy and its implications.
- Identify relevant stakeholders and critically analyse and interpret their policy interests.
- Describe the application of policy theory and process to relevant examples.
- Analyse formal and informal sources of information guiding policy development.
- Critically evaluate and explain the political context of policy development.
Assessment Tasks:
- Written assignment - critical analysis report of policy (1,800 words): 35% (Hurdle: None)
- Written assignment - development of policy position statement (1,800 words): 35% (Hurdle: None)
- 6 x short answer questions (400 words each. Total- 2,400 words): 30% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Off-campus: 12 hours self-directed study per week, plus 2 block days.

Subject: Principles of health systems (MPH5272)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit will examine reform and development in health services from an international, national and local perspective. The focus of the unit will explore the implementation of health policy reform and the multitude of issues, drivers, demands, complexities and consequent impacts related to reform. Areas of concentration include international and national governing entities, the Australian health care system, developed and developing country health systems, roles of institutions (hospitals), an various applications of reform movements/models in care delivery.
Learning Outcomes:
- Create proposals to respond to issues and challenges faced by national health systems.
- Critically appraise research and reports associated with health system development and reform.
- Identify issues and challenges in the design of national health systems.
- Comprehend a systematic theoretical framework for describing the design of a national health system.
- Effectively communicate issues and challenges associated with reforming and developing health systems to specialist and non-specialist audiences.
Assessment Tasks:
- Written report (2,000 words): 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Written critical evaluations (6 x 500 words. Total 3,000 words): 40% (Hurdle: Threshold)
- Written assignment (2,000 words): 30% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 22.5 contact hours over 3 block days
