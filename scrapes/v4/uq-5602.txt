This qualification provides economics training in core economics principles and concepts and is beneficial to non-economics graduates. Students gain an understanding of basic economic concepts in microeconomics, macroeconomics, statistics and mathematical techniques opening the way for graduates to undertake further study if they desire. Obtaining a higher qualification in economics is a career investment. Analytical ability, critical thinking and logic are highly valued commodities, and graduates of this program have the ability to succeed in a dynamic and an increasingly competitive environment. In obvious and subtle ways, economics permeates our daily life and has far reaching impacts. Graduates equipped with an advanced understanding of these forces and issues are an asset to any organisation.

### Program Structure (Courses & Requirements)

  * [ ECON7000 Foundations in Microeconomics ](https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=ECON7000)
  * [ ECON7001 Foundations in Macroeconomics ](https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=ECON7001)
  * [ ECON7150 Mathematical Techniques for Economics ](https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=ECON7150)
  * [ ECON7300 Statistics for Business & Economics ](https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=ECON7300)

Subject: Foundations in Microeconomics (ECON7000)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides students with a basic knowledge of the core economic principles. These principles will be explained and developed using a variety of examples involving businesses, governments, and households. Topics include supply and demand analysis, imperfect competition, producer and consumer theory, marginal analysis, welfare analysis, benefits and costs.
Learning Outcomes:
- Examine advanced economic concepts and theories.
- Critically assess economic policies related to real-world contexts.
- Explain policy discussions and decision-making processes.
- Evaluate contemporary economic challenges and their outcomes.
- Communicate economic ideas through written reports to both expert and general audiences.
Assessment Tasks:
- CML Quizzes Online: 25% (Best 5 out of 6 CML count)% (Hurdle: False)
- Contextualised Learning of Economics via Authentic Reflection - Journal Entries (CLEAR-JE) Online: 20% (Best 2 out of 3 CLEAR-JE count)% (Hurdle: False)
- End-of-Semester Exam Identity Verified In-person: 55%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=ECON7001 =====
Subject: Foundations in Macroeconomics (ECON7001)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This is an introductory course in Macroeconomics. This course covers important macroeconomic issues such as growth, inflation, unemployment, interest rates, exchange rates, technological progress, and budget deficits. The course will provide a unified framework to address these issues and to study the impact of different policies, such as monetary and fiscal policies, on the aggregate behavior of individuals.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply macroeconomic terminology correctly.
- Appraise the roles and functions of macroeconomic institutions.
- Use diagrams, simple equations and words to explain key causal relationships in macroeconomics.
- Analyse contemporary macroeconomic policy issues and their impacts on business and on society.
- Identify, compile and interpret macroeconomic data.
Assessment Tasks:
- Tutorial Preparation: 10% (Best 8 of 9)% (Hurdle: False)
- Policy Analysis + Infographic Presentation (Part A) Team or group-based In-person: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Policy Brief + Infographic (Part B) Team or group-based: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- End-of-Semester Exam In-person: 35%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=ECON7150 =====
Subject: Mathematical Techniques for Economics (ECON7150)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course will focus on the application of differential & integral calculus as well as matrix algebra to economic models. Simple unconstrained optimisation will be studied. The course will also introduce some aspects of multi-variate analysis.
Learning Outcomes:
- Represent and manipulate simple economic models algebraically.
- Use differential calculus, including partial derivatives and differentials, across a range of economic problems.
- Apply integration techniques to economic concepts such as consumer surplus and producer surplus.
- Apply methods of unconstrained and constrained optimization to simple economic problems.
- Use matrix algebra to solve sets of simultaneous equations and simple input-output models.
Assessment Tasks:
- Assignment 1 Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Assignment 2 Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- End-of-Semester Exam Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1.5 Hours/ Week

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=ECON7300 =====
Subject: Statistics for Business & Economics (ECON7300)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Statistical inference, probability & sampling distributions, estimation, hypothesis tests, correlation & regression, experimental design, sample survey design, quality sampling, modern business decision theory.
Learning Outcomes:
- Use basic probability concepts and apply various probability distributions to solve business problems.
- Draw conclusions about populations based only on sample information using methods of statistical inference.
- Use and interpret simple and multiple regression analysis in economics and business.
- Apply regression analysis and other statistical methodologies to forecast time series data.
Assessment Tasks:
- Pre-lecture Quizzes Online: 20% (best 8 of 11)% (Hurdle: False)
- Project Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- End-of-semester Exam Identity Verified In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week
