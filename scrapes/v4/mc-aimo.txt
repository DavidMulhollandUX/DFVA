Overview
Award title	Master of Artificial Intelligence
Year & campus	2026 — University of Melbourne Online

This course is delivered and assessed wholly online.

This course is delivered across six online terms per year whereby students typically enrol in one 12.5 credit point subject per online teaching term. This equates to a full-time study load of 0.75 EFTSL (equivalent full-time student load) per year. Please note, the course duration listed is based on a study load of 1 EFTSL – duration will vary for students taking a lesser study load and/or with advanced standing.

The Master of Artificial Intelligence provides students with a comprehensive understanding of AI principles, methodologies, and applications, preparing them for an AI enabled future in a range of innovative cross-sector roles.

Throughout the course, students will develop critical thinking, problem-solving, and communication skills essential for success in the rapidly evolving field of AI. They will learn to analyse and evaluate complex AI problems, design and implement innovative AI solutions, and communicate sophisticated concepts and findings to diverse audiences.

Students are introduced to the course with a solid foundation in the fundamental concepts and skills necessary for understanding and working with AI technologies. They begin with four core subjects that introduce key areas and principles of AI, fostering analytical and problem-solving abilities, developing skills in designing efficient computational strategies and understanding classical algorithms essential for addressing complex AI challenges. This foundation bridges knowledge of AI concepts, methodologies, and their real-world applications with practical implementation. Next, they build upon these foundations by developing expertise in areas such as planning for autonomous systems design, deep learning implementation in the context of foundation models, multi-modal data of images or text, and AI ethics assessment.

In the later stage of the course, students select two elective subjects from a diverse set of advanced topics, including areas such as optimisation in dynamical and multi-agent systems, human-computer interaction, and trustworthy AI development. This flexibility allows students to tailor their learning experience to suit their individual interests and career goals.

To complete their studies, students have two pathways to choose from: Coursework Capstone or Research Capstone.  The Coursework Capstone spans two subjects, offering students the chance to apply their knowledge and develop their practice from AI development up to implementation in industry-relevant contexts, complemented by formal reflections on their learning journey. Alternatively, the Research Capstone prepares students for potential doctoral studies, developing advanced research skills in AI.

The Master of Artificial Intelligence ensures graduates are well-equipped to tackle complex AI challenges, contribute to the field's advancement, and lead AI initiatives in both industry and research settings.

This course is not available to international students who are holders of an Australian student visa, for study onshore in Australia as this is not a registered CRICOS course. This course is available to all other international students to study wholly online.

Intended learning outcomes

On completion of this course, graduates should be able to:

apply fundamental mathematical, programming and problem-solving skills to interact with and utilise AI technology;
analyse and solve complex AI-related problems using appropriate tools, data sources and algorithms;
evaluate the performance and capabilities of AI models and algorithms using standard metrics and interpret results in real-world contexts;
communicate complex AI concepts and solutions effectively to both specialist and non-specialist audiences;
design, implement, and evaluate efficient AI systems and solutions for complex real-world problems, considering technical feasibility and ethical implications;
apply ethical frameworks to assess and address the societal implications of AI technologies in diverse settings;
synthesise knowledge from multiple AI domains to innovate and develop AI solutions that address complex, interdisciplinary challenges in professional practice or research contexts;
conduct research in AI to synthesise and apply expert, specialised knowledge and skills in areas of artificial intelligence to address problems in professional practice or research.
Generic skills
conduct advanced independent critical inquiry, analysis and reflection;
uphold a strong sense of intellectual integrity and the ethics of scholarship;
apply in-depth knowledge of their specialist area;
apply advanced skills in writing, research or project management, problem-solving and communication to complex challenges in professional contexts;
think critically and creatively, with an aptitude for continued self-directed learning;
critically examine, synthesise and evaluate knowledge across a broad range of disciplines;
demonstrate technical proficiency in AI tools and technologies, and apply this knowledge adaptively across various professional contexts;
initiate and implement constructive change in their communities, including professions and workplaces.
Graduate attributes

Graduates of the Master of Artificial Intelligence will have developed the following skills and attributes:

the ability to undertake advanced independent critical inquiry, analysis and reflection in complex cyber security contexts;
a strong sense of intellectual integrity and ethical responsibility, underpinned by in depth knowledge of cyber security principles, techniques, and best practices;
a versatile set of transferable skills, including effective communication, leadership, project management and collaboration, enabling success in diverse professional roles.

Algorithmic Thinking introduces the essential principles and practices to develop familiarity and competence in assessing and designing computer programs for computational efficiency. Although computers manipulate data very quickly, to solve large-scale problems, strategies must be designed so that the calculations combine effectively. This subject introduces students to the fundamentals of computational efficiency and to many of the classical algorithms and data structures that solve key computational questions. By the end of the subject, students will have developed a robust toolkit for algorithmic thinking, positioning them to approach complex computational problems with confidence and creativity.

Intended learning outcomes

On completion of this subject, students should be able to:

identify and apply the appropriate design techniques of standard algorithms to develop new computational solutions to problems;
formulate and implement a variety of techniques for solving sorting, searching and graph-related problems;
construct and evaluate efficient algorithms and data structures for a variety of fundamental problems;
conduct formal reasoning about problem complexity and algorithmic efficiency;
Generic skills

On completion of this subject, students should have developed the following generic skills:

an ability to apply fundamental knowledge in reasoning and problem solving;
capacity to reason and solve problems;
ability to undertake problem identification, formulation and solution;
capacity for creativity and innovation.

Machine Learning introduce students to the foundations of machine learning and practical skills in data analysis. Students will explore supervised and unsupervised learning algorithms, translating real-world challenges into machine learning tasks. The subject emphasises evaluating model performance using standard metrics and interpreting results. Students will learn to compare different models, considering their strengths and limitations for specific tasks. By the end of the subject, students will acquire skills in designing and implementing machine learning solutions using toolkits, equipping them to contribute to the rapidly evolving field of data science and artificial intelligence and its wide-ranging applications in industry.

Intended learning outcomes

On completion of this subject, students should be able to:

describe principles of key supervised and unsupervised machine learning models;
formulate machine learning tasks based on real world problems;
evaluate the performance and capacity of machine learning models using standard evaluation metrics and interpret the results applied to real data;
compare benefits and drawbacks of competing models for specific machine learning tasks;
design and build machine learning solutions for real problems using machine learning toolkits.
Generic skills

On completion of this subject, students should be able to:

apply analytical and problem-solving skills to unfamiliar challenges;
apply critical thinking and analytical skills to formulate real-world problems;
give and receive constructive feedback related to problem solving;
communicate complex concepts effectively in written, oral, and digital forms.

Please note: this subject is delivered wholly online and only open to students enrolled in the wholly online Master of Artificial Intelligence. Subjects in this course are delivered in an online accelerated learning model and therefore, students typically enrol in one 12.5 credit point subject per online teaching term.

This subject introduces the foundations of autonomous agents—intelligent systems that perceive their environment, reason about their goals, and make decisions to act effectively over time. We focus on how such agents can autonomously choose the best course of action in discrete environments, such as planning routes for travel, coordinating transport operations, or scheduling tasks in manufacturing.

Students will learn how to represent and solve these problems as sequential decision processes, where each decision impacts future states, actions, and outcomes. The course covers key algorithmic approaches including combinatorial search, automated planning, and reinforcement learning. Emphasis is placed on how these methods enable agents to plan and adapt in dynamic settings. The subject culminates in an open-ended project that challenges students to design intelligent agent behaviours in a realistic scenario—applying the principles and techniques learned throughout the course.

Intended learning outcomes

On completion of this subject, students should be able to:

apply advanced theoretical concepts of reasoning about actions to formulate and solve sequential decision problems in both single-agent and multi-agent autonomous systems;
critically analyse and design automated planning and reinforcement learning techniques to solve sequential decision problems;
implement and evaluate the strengths and limitations of various approaches to reasoning about action in autonomous systems;
select and justify appropriate planning and learning techniques for diverse problem domains in reasoning about action;
communicate complex technical solutions and design decisions related to automated planning and reinforcement learning clearly and effectively to both specialist and non-specialist audiences.
Generic skills
communicate complex findings and decision-making processes clearly and effectively to both specialist and non-specialist audiences;
plan and execute complex projects within given time constraints;
integrate knowledge to design solutions to address real-world challenges.

AI in Society explores the complex interplay between Artificial Intelligence (AI) and society, equipping students with the critical thinking skills necessary to navigate the ethical and social implications of AI technologies. Students will learn to identify potential risks and unintended consequences of AI, while also exploring the positive potential of AI technologies. By the end of the subject, students will be well-prepared to contribute and communicate thoughtfully in discussions on AI ethics and to approach AI development with a keen awareness of its broader societal implications.

Intended learning outcomes

On completion of this subject, students should be able to:

examine and describe a range of philosophical issues, theories, and positions relating to the role of AI in society;
critically evaluate arguments in the contemporary literature on AI from across some technical and empirical disciplines, and analyse their core concepts, assumptions and implications;
articulate and defend written arguments for and against theses about the ethics of AI using rationally persuasive argumentation;
apply philosophical ideas and arguments to devise solutions addressing real-world challenges posed by AI;
engage in well-reasoned oral discussion and debate about AI and its role in society, substantiating viewpoints with justified arguments.

analyse and interpret complex information;
undertake problem identification, formulation, and solution;
