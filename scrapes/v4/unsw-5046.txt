## Description
This program provides professional development in mine ventilation and mine environment monitoring for mining engineers and other mining personnel. The Diploma is structured to meet the needs of both the metalliferous and coal mining sectors. The accredited programs offered by UNSW for the appointment of Statutory Coal Mine Ventilation Officers in both NSW and Qld can be taken as options in the Diploma. The course contents have been developed from standard texts, industry guidelines and case studies. These are delivered from both a theoretical and operational perspective with the aim that course contents will be immediately relevant to industry. Assessments are geared to practical evaluation of mine ventilation systems.

## Admission Requirements
**Limitations on Recognition of Prior Learning**:
No RPL is currently permitted except for the Statutory Ventilation Officers (VO) professional development. These students are given RPL for 50% of the Graduate Diploma in Mine Ventilation.

Note that the Statutory Ventilation Officer’s course is currently run as Professional Development course.

**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
Relevant industry experience or access to an underground mine is essential for entry to the Graduate Diploma in Mine Ventilation. International students who qualify for entry and have access to an underground mine are accepted.

Entry is considered for each individual based on portfolio supporting the application, including details of relevant industry experience. An entry interview may be required.

In addition to access to an underground mine, and relevant experience, the following entry requirements apply to this program:

1. A Bachelor degree in Mining Engineering (or equivalent) plus one-year relevant working experience in Mining

or,

2. A Bachelor degree in a discipline cognate to Mining Engineering (or equivalent) plus two years relevant working experience in Mining

or,

3. A minimum of 4 years relevant working experience in mining PLUS one of the following - Deputy's certificate, Undermanager’s certificate, Mine Manager’s certificate or Mine Surveyor’s certificate

or,

3. Students who can demonstrate equivalent prior learning may be approved an alternative entry pathway by written permission of the Program Authority.

Subject: Ventilation and Mine Services (MINE9901)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course module covers laws and relationships required to describe the behavious of mine or ducted ventilation systems. These relate to fluid flow, friction losses, fans and network analysis. Use of ventilation surveys to provide design parameters or reconciliation with predictive models is also covered. This course is one of the required courses for the statutory appointment of Ventilation Officers in NSW and QLD and Graduate Diploma in Mine Ventilation (MINE5046).
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe various fan types, their application and performance in mine or ducted ventilation circuits.
- Appraise the various equipment employed for monitoring and surveying of mine ventilation systems and the range of parameters that may be encountered in water, air and gas reticulation systems.
- Explain scientific principles and calculation methods required to describe fluid flow in mine ventilation networks and associated gas drainage or water reticulation systems.
- Demonstrate methods for analysing mine ventilation networks using both simple series and parallel airway groups and iterative (computer simulation) methods.

Subject: Environmental Contaminants (MINE9902)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course module deals with the occurrence, effects and control of atmospheric contaminants in underground mine environments. These include toxic and or flammable gasses and dust originating from strata, mine equipment or the mining process. The causes, effects and control of mine fires are also considered. This course is one of the required courses for the statutory appointment of Ventilation Officers in NSW and QLD and the Graduate Diploma in Mine Ventilation (MINE5046).
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply scientific principles and calculation methods required to describe the occurrence and behaviour of dust and fire in mine ventilation networks together with methods of control.
- Describe the various instruments and systems available for detecting mine gases. In particular what systems are appropriate under various circumstances, their limitations and interpretation of data obtained.
- Explain scientific principles and calculation methods required to describe the occurrence and behaviour of gases in mine ventilation networks.
- Recognise scientific principles and calculation methods required to quantify radon sources and control requirements in underground mines.

Subject: Heat in Underground Mines (MINE9903)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course module deals with the issues of heat in underground mines. The module topics are psychrometry, heat transfer, sources of heat and heat stress management. The module provides the means to analyse a mine's ventilation circuit to determine the magnitude of heat management controls required, such as refrigeration. This course is one of the eight required courses for the Graduate Diploma in Mine Ventilation (MINE5046). Ventilation Officer students are not required to take this course.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply knowledge of the requirements for heat management plans and calculatation of heat stress indices.
- Explain scientific principles and calculation methods required to describe the properties of moist air and quantify heat and moisture sources in underground mines.
- Demonstrate working knowledge of laws and relationships to calculate heat load arising from identified heat sources and air temperature changes.
- Explain scientific principles and calculation methods required to quantify mine refrigeration requirements.

Subject: Coal Mine Hazards and Control (MINE9905)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course module describes hazards and controls specific to underground coal mines, such as seam gas emission, outbursts and spontaneous combustion. The module includes methods of quantifying or predicting management requirements based on the properties of the working section and adjacent seam gas reservoirs. This course is one of the required courses for the statutory appointment of Ventilation Officers in NSW and QLD and the Graduate Diploma in Mine Ventilation (MINE5046).
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the various instruments and systems available for detecting mine gases. In particular what systems are appropriate under various circumstances, their limitations and interpretation of data obtained.
- Apply scientific principles and calculation methods required to characterise coal mine seams in terms of gas emission regimes.
- Explain scientific principles and calculation methods required to describe the occurrence, detection, prevention and control of spontaneous combustion in underground coal mines

Subject: Spontaneous Combustion and Reactive Ground (MINE9920)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Spontaneous combustion is considered one of the core risks in underground coal mines. Similarly, reactive ground provides a similar risk to underground metalliferous mines. Ventilation control forms a significant part of the management plans to control these hazards. Significant incidents have occurred in Australia and overseas that had the potential to cause loss of life. This course will address the causes, incidents and controls required to manage this hazard. Additionally, case studies and spontaneous management plans will be reviewed. This course is one of the required courses for the statutory appointment of Ventilation Officers in NSW and QLD and the Graduate Diploma in Mine Ventilation (MINE5046).
Learning Outcomes:
- Determine the risks associated with spontaneous combustion of coal and reactive ground in metal mines.
- Describe and evaluate the detection and analytic processes used to determine the presence of an event happening in the mine.
- Develop ventilation strategies to manage and control the risk of spontaneous combustion and reactive ground.
- Describe and evaluate the detection and analytic processes used to determine the propensity to risk
- Evaluate existing management plans to control spontaneous combustion and reactive ground

Subject: Mine Ventilation Legislation (MINE9921)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Explosions, disasters accidents and incidents have been an unfortunate part of working life. These events invariably led to changes in legislation. By studying past disasters participants will be better equipped to prevent their re-occurrence. Current legislation will be studied in this context rather than just studying the statutes. Also, the historical reasons for the establishment of limits will allow the participants the opportunity to explain the significance of legislative compliance. This course is one of the eight required courses for the Graduate Diploma in Mine Ventilation (MINE5046). Ventilation Officer students are not required to take this course.
Learning Outcomes:
- State and interpret the relevant legislation pertinent to mine ventilation
- Describe the legislative responsibilities and duties of a ventilation practitioner
- Evaluate incidents that have occurred with in the mining industry both domestic and international.
- Develop strategies to assist mines in compliance with Acts, Regulations, codes and standards relevant to mine ventilation

Subject: Mine Ventilation Practices (MINE9922)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Mine Ventilation Practice details the techniques and processes required to put ventilation theory to effective use in an underground mine. Examples of the world's best practices will be discussed and reviewed. Techniques used by coal mines will be explored as to their applicability to hard rock mines and vice versa. Through directed learning participants will design a ventilation system for a mine. This course is one of the eight required courses for the Graduate Diploma in Mine Ventilation (MINE5046). Ventilation Officer students are not required to take this course.
Learning Outcomes:
- Define and describe the issues arising from the selection of various ventilation circuits
- Describe and assess the factors to be considered in mine ventilation design
- Evaluate the types and requirements for various ventilation control devices
- Evaluate the need for and methods of pressure control and balancing
