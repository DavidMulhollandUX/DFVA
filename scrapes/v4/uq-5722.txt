Subject: Research Thesis (ADPS7060)
Credit Points: 4
Overview: This course provides an opportunity to develop research skills and specialist knowledge through a guided independent research project. ADPS7060 provides students with the opportunity to undertake a focused research task in a specific area of research interest, conducted through a series of advisory meetings and self-directed study. The course expects students to achieve an advanced level of academic argument and the mastery of instruments for conducting and expressing that argument. The research project will result in a body of work that communicates the process, outcomes and value of the research. The mode of communication and scope shall be agreed upon between the student and the advisor. Students submit a Thesis describing the research and outcomes in a durable and communicable scholarly form. The Thesis may include written analysis, fieldwork reports, computational modelling, surveys, designs and other creative work, material experiments, or any other record and analysis of the program of research agreed with the Advisor. Students commencing in Semester 1 enrol in ADPS7060 for Sem 1 and Sem 2; Students commencing in semester 2 enrol in ADPS7061 for Sem 2 and the following Sem 1. Information on enrolling in ADPS7060 is available on the School of Architecture, Design and Planning website: https://adp.uq.edu.au/apply-coursework-research-thesis.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically analyse, evaluate and position an argument on a body of literature or creative works according to disciplinary conventions.
- Articulate a relevant research problem and contextualise your research question/s within its field.
- Design a coherent methodology including appropriate research method/s that answer the stated research question/s.
- Implement the research design through data collection or data-informed design practice.
- Generate and synthesise findings through data analysis or reflective design practice.
- Select and apply a range of communication strategies, including written, verbal and visual methods, according to disciplinary standards.
- Reflect on appropriate, informed and ethical use of a range of research tools and methods, including AI where utilised.
Assessment Tasks:
- Assessment 1 – Research Plan and Annotated Bibliography Identity Verified: 20% IVA% (Hurdle: False)
- Assessment 2 – Literature Review, Methodology & Ethical Reflections Hurdle Identity Verified: 20% IVA Hurdle% (Hurdle: True)
- Assessment 3 – Thesis Development and Final Document Hurdle Identity Verified: 60% 50% Submission + 10% Oral Presentation% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Contact 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Industry Placement (ADPS7300)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides the opportunity for students to learn in a workplace environment. Students are responsible for finding and arranging a professional work placement, subject to approval, that provides meaningful work experience in the profession. Students are required to complete between 100 hours and 130 hours of placement in the academic term for which the student is enrolled. The course will require attendance, participation, observation, critical reflection, and report writing on workplace activities and experiences. The emphasis of critical reflection and report writing will be on identifying and describing aspects of professional relevance incorporating: professional practice; collaboration and teamwork; workplace health and safety; professional ethics; and other aspects of the workplace experience. Information about enrolling in a placement course, including registering your interest and application deadlines, can be found here: https://www.eait.uq.edu.au/prepare-for-your-placement
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate an improved understanding of a technical, administrative, and personal skills and knowledge required to contribute effectively to the profession.
- Acquire practical skills and experience in professional workplace including working with others and working independently.
- Critique and contextualise the differing sectors, markets, disciplines and interests which contribute to and influence practice and its ability to meet sustainable development goals.
- Critique and contextualise the integrity and ethics issues in professional practice and the role of ethical standards in the profession.
- Use graphic, written and verbal presentation skills to communicate the placement experience.
- Reflect and evaluate your own approach to planning practice taking into account your personal values and ethics, completed course work, placement workshops and workplace experience.
Assessment Tasks:
- Assessment 1: Linkedin Profile and Networking Paper: 20% Individual% (Hurdle: False)
- Assessment 2: Placement Report, Poster and Presentation Hurdle Identity Verified Online: 50% Individual% (Hurdle: True)
- Assessment 3: Placement Reflection and Portfolio Identity Verified: 30% Individual% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Placement 100 Hours/ Semester Workshop 10 Hours/ Semester Students are required to complete between 100 hours and 130 hours of placement.

Subject: Research Lab: History and Heritage (ARCH7084)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course focuses on the uses and understanding of built environments constructed in the past, how we can learn from them and manage their on-going lives. The course introduces theoretical and methodological issues of historiography such as forms of analysis, argument and evidence; and research practices such archival research and oral history; the collecting and maintaining of archives of architecture; and Digital Humanities techniques such as 3-d scanning, data analysis, and visualisation. The course includes a critical understanding of the principles and statutory regulation of cultural heritage. Students will employ the learning in a researching, documenting and analysing a building, persons or institutions, or ideas
Learning Outcomes:
- Conduct a critical review of academic literature relevant to researching a building, building type, architect or other historical actor, or architectural concept.
- Apply an appropriate historiographic and/or heritage strategy in undertaking the research.
- Analyse primary research findings and their relation of the relevant secondary literature. Consider the presentation of research findings as an aspect of cultural heritage significance and/or evidence for an argument or description of architectural history.
- Undertake a largely self-directed research project and complete an essay or report to a professional standard.

- Essay/Report Plan In-person: 10%% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Conservation Planning & Management (ENVM7202)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: We are currently in the midst of a global biodiversity crisis, with many species and ecosystems on the brink of extinction, which in turn threatens humanity¿s life support system. Working to protect and recover biodiversity in the face of competing values, unclear priorities and limited resources is a daunting task. We will use practical examples of real conservation challenges to illustrate key concepts and how to break down complex problems into manageable segments. We will work through the tools and techniques for structuring conservation decisions, engaging with different interest holders and their values, and incorporating disparate information sources. We will also delve into the Open Standards for conservation, a widely-used framework for planning and learning from conservation action that is used around the world. Case study topics we cover include management of protected areas, management of fire, and systematic spatial planning of protected area networks. This subject provides complementary material to other environmental science and management courses. It is designed to be interactive and with a focus on critical and creative thinking.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the value of, and apply, structured decision-making and prioritisation approaches to conservation planning and management problems
- Explain key actors involved in conservation planning and management and their roles, and how different values can be incorporated into these processes
- Source and synthesise disparate forms of evidence to guide conservation planning and decision-making
- Explain the importance of and potential for Traditional Knowledge to inform and underpin conservation management
- Map out and analyse the context and scope of a conservation problem and create a clearly-articulated management plan aligned with the Open Standards for Conservation, including consideration of links to human wellbeing
- Apply critical and creative thinking skills to inform discussion and debate over current real-world challenges in conservation planning and management such as innovative financing, evaluating conservation effectiveness, and management of fire
Assessment Tasks:
- Assignment 1: Draft Conservation Project Plan: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Assignment 2: Full Conservation Project Plan: 45%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Examination Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Practical 1 Hour/ Week
