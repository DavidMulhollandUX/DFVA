## Description
The Graduate Diploma in Social Impact focuses on building the professional capacity of private, public, and social sector managers and entrepreneurs of the future, enhancing their capacity to lead organisations creating social and environmental value and to operate in a changed cross-sector social landscape where the dynamism of the market is also directed at social innovation.

This century has witnessed an accelerating economic relationship and a blurring of the boundaries between government, business and the third sector to deliver social (including environmental) value in communities. These factors are also powerful drivers of social innovation. Key changes have occurred within each of the sectors: the changing role of government from direct provider to enabler; the emergence of corporate responsibility within the business sector; the emergence of social enterprises and socially responsible
businesses within the third sector. The interdependencies of the global economy also heighten the value of cross-sector experience for particularly in the consequent implications for leadership and management generally.

The Graduate Diploma in Social Impact is designed for a new generation of leaders:

- Those who aspire to hold executive positions in organisations that are committed to maximising social benefit and meeting corporate responsibilities through their products and practices;
- Those who seek a leadership career in social enterprises and those already in the sector who wish to enhance their professional capacity to create social and environmental value through leadership of third sector/not-for-profit organisations;
- Those who wish to become senior public administrators working with community groups or, public service executives with a responsibility for developing the public policy framework utilizing and understanding of social impact and social; and
- Those individuals, foundations and organisations that seek to ensure that their social investment delivers desirable social impact.

Subject: Leading Change for Sustainability (COMM5205)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Leading Change for Sustainability is a course designed for future leaders who are passionate about creating positive social and environmental change. Your ideas and passion for sustainability and resilience are just the first step. You need skills in leadership, creativity, systems thinking, communication and models for cultural change so that you can influence key decision-makers, build networks and make your idea a reality. You need an awareness of change processes and an understanding of ethics and human rights to lead sustainable change, locally and globally. How do you support cultural change towards a sustainable future for all organisational types? How do you pursue social and environmental goals from within a multinational company or a start up? Sustainability has become a key driver in many organisations, from large foundations to social enterprises, not-for-profits working with government to provide services, and small to large businesses. This course explores how to create and implement sustainability strategies for different organisational types. We take a broad view of sustainability based on the Sustainable Development Goals, acknowledging the human, social, economic and environmental elements of positive cultural change.
Learning Outcomes:
- Become familiar with transformation strategies in the context of sustainability initiatives in business.
- Recognise and apply the features of sustainable leadership practice in organisational sustainability initiatives (including ethical and human rights concerns).
- Reflect upon skills in building teams and networks in order to align businesses with sustainability objectives.
- Communicate written and oral ideas in a clear and succinct manner.

Subject: Social Impact (COMM5701)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: COMM5701 Social Impact is the core course in the Graduate Certificate in Social Impact and the MBAX (Social Impact) programs. It is highly recommended that students take COMM5701 either as their first Social Impact course (or one of the courses in their first study session that includes Social Impact courses). The course is an introduction to Social Impact covering fundamental concepts and key developments in what has become a complex social purpose eco-system. The course introduces an understanding of changes across what was formerly seen as a distinct social sector to examine the cross-sectoral social purpose developments that includes traditional organisational structures of Charities and Not For-Profits, Business and Government, and new and emerging ‘hybrid’ structures and programs, including social enterprises. Key concepts include: systems thinking and mapping, measuring impact, funding and investing in impact, organisational challenges including scaling, strategy, hybridity, and leadership for social impact. The course explores how intractable social, economic, and environmental problems embedded in society are being addressed through social innovation in business entrepreneurship, enterprises and cross-sector collaboration.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply critical analysis and systems thinking to an understanding of the complex and changing social purpose eco-system
- Explain and describe the trends and drivers re-shaping the dynamics of the social economy and its impact on complex, social issues
- Develop critical thinking, research and communication skills for social impact work
- Identify new organisational forms, business models and cross-sector collaborations for social innovation and impact
- Analyse and respond to global trends and organisations that effect social change, social innovation and impact
- Demonstrate a strong understanding of key foundational concepts within the social impact field

Subject: Social Impact Investment (COMM5703)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course is managed by the Centre for Social Impact. The landscape of social finance and impact investment has undergone a transformation – driven by global economic realignment, the privatisation of community and government services, shifting patterns of wealth distribution and intergenerational wealth transfer, and the rising expectations of corporate citizenship and philanthropy. These forces have accelerated the growth of social impact investment: a field in which commercial enterprise, blended finance, and philanthropic capital are increasingly deployed together to address complex social and environmental challenges. COMM5703 Social Impact Investment develops critical understanding of the history, core concepts, and current issues shaping philanthropy, fundraising, and social impact investment – both in Australia and globally. Students will examine the full capital spectrum, from philanthropic giving to impact bonds and ESG-aligned investment vehicles, exploring how these instruments are structured, deployed, and evaluated across diverse economic regulatory, and cultural contexts. Ethical and philosophical perspectives, impact measurement, and the legal dimensions of social investment programs are also examined, equipping students to engage as informed and strategic practitioners in this field.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and apply key instruments of social impact investment
- Identify and critically evaluate the roles and interrelationships of diverse players in the social impact investment ecosystem
- Explain the trends, drivers and challenges shaping the dynamics of the social impact investment market in Australia and globally
- Discuss the drivers of the emergence of social impact investment and the role it plays within the capital spectrum

Subject: Design for Social Innovation (COMM5706)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Social innovation can be developed in almost any context. It is often a response to an identified problem, issue or opportunity. One of the most challenging aspects of developing and delivering social innovation is identifying the essence of what needs to be addressed, as the problems can be wicked in nature. Design thinking, methods and practices offer a broad set of tools that facilitate a multi-disciplinary to transdisciplinary approach to solving problems and innovating, taking a whole systems (rather than a silo) approach to offer different perspectives to traditional business tools and problem solving. Design for Social Innovation offers participants the opportunity to learn and apply design tools and methods to real projects. COMM5706 Design for Social Innovation is an elective subject for the Graduate Certificate in Social Impact and other post-experience programs. It provides an overview and introduction to the role and application of design methods in social innovation and social enterprise. This course will provide the knowledge and tools necessary to understand and apply design thinking principles at a project or organisational level.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate an ability to apply design thinking for problem solving in a social innovation context.
- Describe the complex nature of social issues/problems.
- Identify and apply tools and methods that can assist in the application of design thinking in governance, operational, development and delivery contexts, within the area of social innovation.
- Communicate about design thinking and social innovation verbally and in written format.
- Apply design methods to develop implementation strategies for social innovation.
- Engage in current scholarly research and writing within the fields of design thinking and social innovation.

Subject: Corporate Responsibility and Accountability (COMM5709)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Corporate Responsibility and Accountability is a core course for the Global Sustainability and Social Impact specialisation in the Master of Commerce, and an elective for those undertaking the Graduate Certificate in Social Impact, Graduate Diploma in Social Impact or MBA Programs. It examines how business manages its social, environmental and economic impact on society. The course first reviews the historical evolution and development of the concept of Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) and Corporate Citizenship (CC) and why it is now a key part of managing the business enterprise. Second, the course examines what acting responsibly means for corporations in terms of the market, community, environment, workplace and government. Issues covered include business and human rights, business-community partnerships, corporate philanthropy, the human resource management implications of CSR, business and the environment and businesses' relationship with government. Third, the course examines how business accounts for its behaviour and impact in society through sustainability reporting and the role of external assurance and outlines debates on the appropriate role of government policy in either regulating and/or enabling responsible corporate behaviour.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate and apply ethical and accountability issues and frameworks in business
- Describe the key drivers behind its adoption by business
- Analyse and understand a corporation's impact in the social, workplace, market, environment and political spheres;
- Apply Corporate Responsibility theories and frameworks to critically evaluate corporate responsibility performance;
- Describe and critically evaluate the concepts of Corporate Responsibility and Accountability, and future trends in the role of the corporation in society

Subject: Managing Just Transitions (COMM5710)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Managing Just Transitions introduces students to the emerging framework of Just Transitions , to examine how leaders can manage complex socio-ecological transitions in the face of systemic challenges such as climate change, economic inequality, and unsustainable development. It focuses on the governance of change across interconnected systems, where decisions in one domain can create significant implications for markets, organisations, communities, and the environment. A central question is: how can systemic transitions be governed effectively and equitably, while balancing competing interests, risks, and opportunities? This course adopts a systems-level perspective that investigates decisions of justice, fairness, and inclusion in navigating structural changes such as decarbonisation, digitalisation, artificial intelligence, and automation. Students will critically examine how businesses, investors, governments, and civil society can work together to reimagine economic futures that are both environmentally sustainable and socially inclusive . Through interdisciplinary theories and real-world case studies, students will explore Just Transitions across five key conceptual domains: System Thinking – A system value domain sits at the core of this course, recognising that just transition occur within interconnected systems shaped by power, institutions, stakeholder relationships and competing ideas of justice. Civic Wealth Creation – Examining how community-driven entrepreneurship, participation, and coalition-building generate economic, social, and communal value. Place-Based Development and Investment – Investigating financial models that acquire, absorb, and deploy capital equitably across different communities and geographies. Indigenous Knowledge and Systems – Exploring the Indigenous business sector and the role of cultural values in fostering sustainable, community-led development. Ethical Infrastructure and Intergenerational Equity – Engaging with long-term governance frameworks that consider who benefits from change, who bears the costs, and how future generations are impacted. Designed for future leaders, strategists, and change agents, this course equips students with the critical tools to design, assess, and lead transitions that align business innovation with societal and environmental resilience.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically evaluate the range of theoretical and conceptual frameworks that inform Just Transition strategies across economic, social, and environmental domains.
- Demonstrate advanced research, analytical, and communication skills relevant to professional and academic work in the field of Just Transitions.
- Explain the concept of Just Transitions, including its origins, evolution, and significance in contemporary business and policy contexts.
- Analyse emerging trends, drivers, and governance challenges shaping Just Transitions at local, national, and global levels.
- Identify and apply the core design elements, stakeholder considerations, and measurement approaches required to develop effective Just Transition initiatives.

Subject: Leadership for Social Impact (COMM5902)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Leadership for Social Impact is designed for a new generation of leaders Those who seek a leadership career in social enterprises, and those already in the sector who wish to enhance their professional capacity to create social and environmental value through leadership of third sector/not-for-profit organisations Those who aspire to hold executive positions in companies which are committed to maximizing social benefit and meeting corporate responsibilities through their products and practices Those who wish to become senior public administrators working with community groups, or public service executives with a responsibility for developing the public policy framework; and Those individuals and foundations that seek to ensure that their social investment is applied to greatest social impact. - Communicate ideas in a clear and succinct manner;
- Outline the approaches to people management that are best suited to third sector organisations;
