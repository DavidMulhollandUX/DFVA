## Master of Critical Care Paramedicine
##### M6015
##### 72 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
The Master of Critical Care Paramedicine offers registered paramedics the opportunity to expand their knowledge and skills to enable them to practice at an advanced level. This course is designed to provide you with the necessary education and training for a critical care paramedic role as well as prepare you for leadership, education and research positions. The course fosters the integration of reflection, professionalism, leadership, teamwork, interpersonal communication, and patient focused care at an advanced level into all areas of clinical practice.

The course is designed to provide you with the skills and training to successfully complete a critical care internship and provide the highest level of paramedic care for cardiac, respiratory, medical, trauma, paediatric and obstetric patients. You will develop autonomous clinical reasoning and decision-making abilities based on an advanced understanding and interpretation of clinical presentations. You will also learn and demonstrate advanced techniques and treatment regimes which will enable you to provide the highest level of out-of-hospital care for your specialist area of paramedic practice. Furthermore, you will have the opportunity to choose between a coursework or research pathway.

### Structure
The Master of Critical Care Paramedicine is structured into two parts: Part A. Clinical studies and Part B. Discipline elective studies.

Part A: Clinical studies

These studies will extend your clinical knowledge and practice in your chosen specialisation.

Part B: Discipline elective studies

These studies will further advance and broaden your understanding. You will have the opportunity to choose between one of the following pathways:

a) Coursework – in this pathway you will undertake a core capstone unit (PAR5104 Prehospital Quality Improvement and Clinical Leadership), and combine it with a program of units to suit your career interests. In the capstone unit, you are expected to integrate, extend and apply knowledge and skills gained in the course to real-world professional practice development, particularly in relation to project management and implementation and communication, that can contribute to more effective practice and outcomes.

b) Research – in this pathway you will gain the practical skills required to formulate a research question and design and assess a research project. If you wish to use this master's course as a pathway to a higher degree by research you should take this option. Consultation with the Course Director is required prior to enrolment in the research project units.

### Requirements
The course comprises 72 points structured into two parts:  Part A. Clinical studies (48 points) and Part B. Discipline elective studies (24 points).

Units are 6 points unless otherwise stated.

Part A. Clinical studies (48 points)

You must complete the following units:

  * PAR5401 Foundations of advanced paramedic practice (12 points)

  * PAR5420 Critical care management of cardiac emergencies

  * PAR5440 Critical care management of trauma

  * PAR5450 Advanced care paramedic management of paediatric and obstetric patients

  * PAR5480 Critical care management of medical emergencies

Part B. Discipline elective studies (24 points)

You must complete one of the following pathways:

Coursework pathway

  * PAR5104 Prehospital Quality Improvement and Clinical Leadership (12 points)

  * 12cp of elective units. Electives can be chosen from any Monash Postgraduate coursework units that are relevant to this course, and in consultation with the Course Director.

OR

Research pathway

  * PAR4001 Literature Review (12 points)

  * PAR4002 Honours research proposal (12 points)

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
critique contemporary educational methods of teaching and learning to foster lifelong learning in key stakeholders in healthcare
**2.**
formulate research that addresses emerging challenges in paramedic practice that contributes to the knowledge base of the profession.
**3.**
synthesise information to make complex and safe decisions that show expert clinical competence, autonomy, and professional judgment in dynamic patient care settings
**4.**
lead, influence, and contribute to the development and transformation of collaborative paramedic practice, at a local, national and global level
**5.**
engage proactively to influence and promote practice development, policy and/or continuous quality improvements
**6.**
facilitate learning and development in others by role modelling, mentoring, and delivering high-quality educational experiences in clinical and academic settings
**7.**
translate contemporary research to inform evidence-based practice and identify areas for quality improvement

### Progression to further studies
Successful completion of the research option may provide a pathway to a graduate research degree. Students are advised to check the graduate research degree entry requirements.

### AHEGS
The Master of Critical Care Paramedicine is a postgraduate award. The course is taught in English and normally takes one and a half years of full-time study or part-time equivalent to complete. Graduates have demonstrated advanced skills, knowledge and clinical practice by making high level, autonomous and evidence based clinical judgements in their specialist area of critical care paramedicine. They have gained sound knowledge of and can demonstrate current best practice principles in the delivery of critical and complex community emergency health. The Master of Critical Care Paramedicine is a Level 9 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

- Apply critical thinking, analytical, project management and problem-solving skills in the independent management of a research project.

Subject: Foundations of advanced paramedic practice (PAR5401)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: In this unit, you will be introduced to concepts and knowledge that underpins the provision of advanced paramedic care. This unit will enable you to develop and apply graduate academic skills to appraise and reference evidence relevant to your practice. The content will enable you to explore advanced level clinical skills and knowledge including, patient assessment, clinical decision-making, managing complex patients, and scene leadership. You will also examine key concepts underpinning and influencing clinical practice such as human factors and self-reflection. Finally, this unit will extend your knowledge of foundation pathophysiology, pharmacology and clinical toxicology principles. You will be challenged to integrate the fundamental concepts of pathophysiology, pharmacodynamics and pharmacokinetics as the basis for devising treatment regimens.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply reflective practices which enable lifelong learning.
- Examine advanced pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic principles with relevance to patient care.
- Analyse the influence of human factor ergonomics on clinical judgment and teamwork.
- Distinguish knowledge, skills and attributes required for ongoing professional practice.
- Synthesising advanced knowledge of pathophysiology, anatomy, and pharmacology into patient care.
- Appraise and apply research evidence to inform advanced paramedic decision-making and safe clinical practice.
Assessment Tasks:
- Case study: 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Research forum (Pass/Fail): 0% (Hurdle: Competency)
- Concept map: 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Professional portfolio (Pass/Fail): 0% (Hurdle: Competency)
- Clinical quiz: 20% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: You will be expected to engage in 12 hours of teacher directed online and face to face learning activities and 12 hours of self-directed learning per week.

Subject: Critical care management of trauma  (PAR5440)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this unit, which explores trauma care through the lens of contemporary systems and evidence-based triage, you will develop advanced knowledge and skills in the management of patients requiring critical care. You will examine how structured trauma triage processes and assessment tools support clinical decision-making under pressure and guide the delivery of care to the right patient, at the right time, in the right place. Emphasis is placed on integrating system-level understanding with high-level clinical reasoning. This enables you to make rapid, informed decisions in complex and dynamic environments, particularly when managing critically injured patients in prehospital, retrieval, or emergency settings. You will analyse specific injury patterns in depth and develop advanced clinical skills to manage them effectively. To achieve the outcomes of this unit, active engagement in relevant clinical practice, placements, or high-fidelity simulation is essential.
Learning Outcomes:
- Implement appropriate pharmacotherapeutic and treatment regimens for the critical care management of trauma.
- Analyse contemporary evidence to determine best practice approaches for management of trauma at a critical care level.
- Synthesise the need for critical care interventions when there is trauma-related pathophysiology and physiology derangement.
- Explore the role and impact of prehospital critical care clinicians in broader trauma systems.
- Apply advanced educational and mentoring strategies to strengthen interprofessional trauma care in the prehospital critical care context.
Assessment Tasks:
- Evidence based guideline review: 45% (Hurdle: None)
- Professional portfolio: 25% (Hurdle: Competency)
- Clinical practice video: 30% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: You will be expected to complete at least 6 hours of distance education directed learning and a minimum of 6 hours of private study and self-directed learning per week. If completed on-campus, You will be expected to complete the equivalent of 6 hours of lectures, seminars or online tutorials per week in addition to 6 hours of self-directed learning.

Subject: Critical care management of medical emergencies  (PAR5480)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this unit you will advances theoretical and clinical foundations for managing acute medical emergencies in the adult population within a critical care paramedicine context. You will explore the pathophysiology, assessment, and evidence-based interventions relevant to high-acuity presentations across a range of commonly encountered medical conditions. Through integration of contemporary research, clinical reasoning, and advanced decision-making frameworks, you will refine the knowledge and skills required to deliver safe, effective, and timely care in dynamic and often unpredictable prehospital environments. You will explore epidemiology, and evidence-based management of medical conditions enabling you to formulate treatment pathways and regimes. You will also analyse and evaluate the role that critical care paramedics play within the health system when caring for patients with medical emergencies. You will evaluate your past clinical experience, and begin to recognise the importance of experience in identifying the key differences in the advanced life support measures and interventions available to manage these conditions as a critical care paramedic.
Learning Outcomes:
- Integrate and appraise contemporary, high-quality evidence to guide advanced decision-making in the management of complex medical emergencies.
- Analyse and interpret the epidemiology and burden of acute and chronic medical conditions to inform strategic clinical responses.
- Justify the role of the critical care paramedic in the holistic and longitudinal management of medical conditions.
- Lead and deliver the professional management of high-acuity medical emergencies.
- Justify pharmacotherapeutic regimens and advanced interventions for the critical care management of medical conditions.
- Design and evaluate comprehensive treatment pathways for critically ill medical patients.
Assessment Tasks:
- Vodcast: 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Literature Analysis: 60% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: You will be expected to complete at least 6 hours of distance education directed learning and a minimum of 6 hours of private study and self-directed learning per week. If completed on-campus, you will be expected to complete the equivalent of 6 hours of lectures, seminars or online tutorials per week in addition to 6 hours of self-directed learning.
