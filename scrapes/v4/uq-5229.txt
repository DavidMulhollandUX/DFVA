Subject: Drug Design and Formulation (PHIP7121)
Credit Points: 4
Overview: Students studying this course will gain a foundation in concepts integral to pharmaceutical industry led drug discovery and dosage form design. Students will acquire knowledge about advanced drug delivery methods; high throughput drug discovery techniques; and the importance of biotechnology approaches in current and future therapies to treat a variety of diseases. Students will demonstrate an understanding of key dosage form design theories, teamwork skills and good laboratory practice through learning activities where students will apply theoretical knowledge and research skills to gain an understanding of processes required to formulate a pharmaceutical product. Students will also engage in a pharmaceutical company like presentation to develop their research and communications skills and to enhance their understanding of the intersect between cutting edge research developments and advances in patient care. Students will hear from and meet pharmaceutical industry professionals to gain even deeper insights into the topics and core skills which are features of this course.
Learning Outcomes:
- Present and discuss new approaches in drug discovery and therapeutic development
- Acquire and apply formulation knowledge to the research and development of a simple pharmaceutical product
- Integrate and apply a range of formulation concepts in the science of dose form design
- Independently source and analyse evidence that is important in the formulation and evaluation of pharmaceuticals
- Propose and justify a range of evaluation methods for a specific pharmaceutical product
- Apply good manufacturing and good laboratory practice including time management skills
- Work effectively in a pharmaceutical research team
- Demonstrate knowledge of advanced drug formulations
Assessment Tasks:
- Poster Presentation Identity Verified In-person Online: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Formulation Prework and Plan (Group assessment) Team or group-based: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Advanced Drug/Vaccine Delivery Quiz Online: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Good Laboratory Practice (group assessment) Online: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Integrating Theory (Individual assessment): 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Workshop 2 Hours/ Week Practical 6 Hours/ Week

Subject: Pre-clinical Drug Development (PHIP7122)
Credit Points: 4
Overview: Students taking this course will have the opportunity to engage in experimental and theoretical skill development in pharmaceutical analysis and quality assurance in pharmaceutics. They will also be exposed to the intricacies of pre-clinical research, how research uses animal models and ethical boundaries. The course will highlight the role of pharmacodynamics, pharmacokinetics and toxicology as well as emphasise the need for collection of safety data in the early stages of drug development. Students will learn about the type of and need for recording results and documentation. The students will meet a career professional and learn about the practical application of the knowledge gained within the course. The theoretical aspects of the course are supported by a large pharmaceutical analysis and pharmaceutical quality assurance individual research project that involves analysis of drug concentrations using industry standard techniques with an emphasis on analytical design, method development and interpretation of findings both in report form and oral defence.
Learning Outcomes:
- Contextualise the role Preclinical Drug Discovery has in the drug life cycle.
- Identify and explain the role of toxicology in light of preclinical and clinical drug evaluation.
- Apply the principles of pharmacodynamics and pharmacokinetics.
- Justify the role of animal research and the ensuing ethical considerations and regulatory processes.
- Collaborate and apply skills in research learning.
- Design and deliver a presentation on scientific research.
Assessment Tasks:
- Pharmacokinetics and Pharmacodynamics Online Quiz Online: 5%% (Hurdle: False)
- Poster Presentation of Preclinical Research Identity Verified In-person Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Toxicology Quiz Online: 5%% (Hurdle: False)
- Pharmaceutical Analysis Research Project Report: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Quality Assurance Research Project Team or group-based: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Seminar 2 Hours/ Week Workshop 6 Hours/ Week Practical 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: Clinical Drug Development and Biometrics (PHIP7141)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course covers medicines regulation, clinical trial phases, designs and protocol development, along with trial governance, monitoring and audit. You will gain a comprehensive knowledge of Good Clinical Practice (GCP), the clinical and regulatory aspects of drug development, and the significance of risk management. You will also recognise the importance of the regulations in ensuring appropriate biometric data management and transparency of the drug development process.
Learning Outcomes:
- Articulate the regulatory process as it applies to investigational, registered, and listed medicines in Australia
- Demonstrate understanding of and apply the principles of good clinical practice (GCP)
- Describe and differentiate clinical trial designs and phases of drug development
- Plan and evaluate the collection, management, and analysis of biometric data
- Identify potential risks and consider strategies to mitigate risk in clinical trials
- Describe the role for consumers in trial design and planning, emphasising the importance of diversity, and respecting the distinct needs and contribution of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples
- Critically appraise clinical trial reporting
- Work effectively both independently and as a member of a team to present information in a written and oral format
- Use reflection as a process for recognising performance gaps and developing your skills and strategies
Assessment Tasks:
- Series of quizzes Online: 25% (5% each)% (Hurdle: False)
- Diversity and cultural practice in clinical trials Online: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Development of a clinical trial protocol: 35%% (Hurdle: False)
- Critical appraisal Identity Verified Team or group-based: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Seminar 4 Hours/ Week

Subject: Post-registration Life Cycle Management (PHIP7142)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Students studying this course will gain a comprehensive understanding of post-marketing environment by interacting from industry experts within the allied disciplines, which oversee the continued growth of the product once regulators have approved the product. These allied disciplines include medical affairs, pharmacovigilance and risk management, health technology assessment (including economic evaluations), and marketing. By hearing from industry professionals in these fields, students will acquire a significant understanding of the roles in managing the product through various stages of its growth and how these professionals work with government to bring needed medicines to the consumer. Students will extend their learning by undertaking assessment that uses real life cases.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the main features of the Australian health care system
- Explain the functions of medical affairs and marketing in drug development
- Describe and differentiate the guidelines, documents, and safety events in pharmacovigilance
- Critically appraise the core components of health technology assessment and economic evaluations including costs, health outcomes and institutional context
Assessment Tasks:
- Quiz - Australian health care system Online: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Poster presentation - medical affairs, marketing and codes Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: True)
- Quiz - Pharmacovigilance Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Critical appraisal - Health Technology Assessment: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Device Development, Drug Manufacturing, Regulatory Affairs and Governance (PHIP7161)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: In this course students will learn about process of medical device development, manufacturing of drugs under strict GMP conditions, as well as regulatory and governance aspects of the two. Medical device development is an increasing area of clinical research and development in Australia, and globally. Students will learn in detail about methods of device manufacturing, and cutting-edge advanced techniques for developing and fabrication of medical devices, including 3D printing. They will learn about the guidelines that govern good practices in the R&D of medical devices. They will learn about the guidelines that govern good practice in manufacturing of devices and drugs. This will extend into description of upscaling processes for small batches required for pre-clinical proof-of-concept studies through to large batches required for clinical trials. They will learn about the various types of packaging for different medical devices and medicines, and this will be aligned to regulations set by the Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) of Australia. They will learn about the various labelling requirements on the global stage and innovative labelling and packaging practices. Exposure to the regulation of therapeutic goods in Australia, how it compares to those overseas and why we have regulators along with the principles of research governance is a feature of this course.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the development processes of medical devices
- Identify the regulatory processes for approval of medical devices
- Evaluate good manufacturing practice and how it relates to medical device manufacture
- Compare and contrast GMP between medical devices and drug manufacturing
- Describe the development and role of pharmaceutical packaging and labeling
- Explain the importance and application of quality control systems
- Work effectively in a team environment
Assessment Tasks:
- Digital poster & pitch of drug/medical device Identity Verified Team or group-based In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Pharmaceutical Quality System scenarios Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Quiz Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week Workshop 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Project Management and Commercialisation in the Pharmaceutical Industry (PHIP7162)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Students taking this course will gain insight into the ethics and business-related aspects of product development in the pharmaceutical industry (MTP Sector). Aspects important in the product development life cycle that will be addressed include: history, role and purpose of ethics including the cultural safety in, and social impact of, research in society; legal responsibilities in research and the need for intellectual property protection; various commercialisation pathways that can bring a product to market; the business of product development touching on principles of project and financial management. Students will meet career professionals in these roles in the pharmaceutical industry and learn about the real-life practical application of theory learned.
Learning Outcomes:
- Recognise the roles and diverse perspectives of human research ethics committees
- Describe the purpose of the human research ethics committee and the National Statement
- In a collaborative/group work setting, apply different intellectual property (IP) protection strategies, evaluate their impact on a commercial model and know how patent law applies to an invention
- Evaluate the various commercialisation pathways of a novel product/invention and demonstrate the pros and cons of each
- Describe and apply the principles of project management, identifying possible risks that may occur and reasons for project success
- Identify common stakeholder roles and responsibilities in project management
- Identify the different categories of expenses in product development
Assessment Tasks:
- Intellectual Property and Patent Law Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Commercialisation Pitch Identity Verified Team or group-based: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Human Research Ethics Application: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Project Management plan - Gantt chart: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week
