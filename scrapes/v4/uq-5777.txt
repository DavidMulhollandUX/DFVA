Subject: Ethical and Sustainable HR Strategies (MGTS7622)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course will equip students with an understanding of the nature of ethical and sustainable human resource management (HRM). It examines the role of HR in shaping responsible organisational performance, aligning people strategies with business goals and values, and embedding practices that enhance individual well-being, equity, and inclusion. The course analyses how HR systems can balance commercial imperatives with ethical responsibility, and how these systems contribute to long-term organisational value and positive societal impact. Students will also evaluate emerging HR trends and the challenges of embedding ethical and sustainable practices in the future of work.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically evaluate principles, frameworks, and models of ethical and sustainable HRM to align with organisational strategy, values, and stakeholder expectations.
- Make evidence-based decisions on the design and implementation of HR systems and practices to achieve responsible and sustainable outcomes for organisations and their people.
- Work collaboratively with peers to analyse HR issues and present strategic, ethical, and inclusive recommendations.
- Analyse and evaluate the impact of HR systems from multiple stakeholder perspectives, including the impact on Indigenous Australian Peoples and diverse cultural contexts.
- Ethically reflect on different approaches to workforce management, with a focus on equity, diversity, inclusion, and sustainability.
Assessment Tasks:
- Executive Board Presentation Team or group-based In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Presentation Peer Assessment: Peer evaluation may impact individual marks.% (Hurdle: False)
- HR Problem-Solution Report to Executive Board Online: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Reflection on HR Practice Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Workshop 39 Hours/ One Off Intensive mode

Subject: Introduction to Psychology: Understanding How People Think, Feel and Act (PSYC7401)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides an introduction to psychological science. In this course, you will learn to use psychological concepts, theories and data to understand human behaviour. Topics covered include how people learn, the fallibility of human memory, individual differences in personality and intelligence, motivation, emotion, mental health and well-being, and social and interpersonal processes. You will learn how to collect and interpret psychological data, and how to critically evaluate psychological claims.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand key concepts and principles in psychology
- Apply psychological concepts and principles to the analysis of human behaviour in real-world settings
- Evaluate and explain the validity of research claims in psychology
- Communicate psychological concepts, principles and insights to a business audience
- Critique methods of collecting and interpreting psychological data
Assessment Tasks:
- Cognitive Task Analysis Team or group-based: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Job Analysis: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Personnel Selection: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Workshop 1 Hour/ Week 1W

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=PSYC7402 =====
Subject: Performance Psychology: Unlocking Human Potential (PSYC7402)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course examines how we can use psychology to help people achieve their full potential. The course examines how to develop skills and expertise in high stakes environments, how to help experts make better decisions, how to motivate people, and how to manage the impacts of stress, workload and fatigue on performance.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply key concepts and principles from performance psychology to analyse and improve performance in real-world settings.
- Critically evaluate research, including research designs used in applied contexts of performance psychology.
- Communicate evidence-based arguments and recommendations for performance improvements to a business audience.
- Apply motivational interviewing techniques and coaching frameworks in the conduct of a coaching conversation.
- Diagnose performance issues, and design and evaluate interventions to address them.
- Develop analytical skills to collect, analyse and interpret psychological data, including simple between-group comparisons such as t-tests and ANOVA.
- Work collaboratively as part of a team to use evidence-based strategies to resolve complex real-world challenges in the field of performance psychology.
Assessment Tasks:
- Assessment 1: Performance diagnosis and intervention: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Assessment 2: Coaching Conversation: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Assessment 3: Group project: Applied performance intervention Team or group-based: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Assessment 4: Peer assessment: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Workshop 1 Hour/ Week

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=PSYC7403 =====
Subject: The Psychology of People at Work (PSYC7403)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course examines how we can use psychology to enhance the productivity, safety and well-being of people at work. It examines the psychological factors influencing the effectiveness of individuals, teams and organisations, and considers how we can use that knowledge to develop interventions to improve these outcomes.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify the psychological factors influencing the effectiveness of individuals, teams, and organisations in relation to productivity, safety, and well-being.
- Evaluate and diagnose real organisational problems.
- Select and apply evidence-based interventions to enhance productivity, safety, and well-being in diverse business environments.
- Communicate evidence-based arguments and recommendations for enhancing productivity, safely, and well-being to a business audience.
- Demonstrate teamwork behaviours to resolve complex, real-world challenges in the field of business psychology.
Assessment Tasks:
- Case study analysis: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Group pitch Team or group-based Online: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Interview Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Workshop 2 Hours/ Week
