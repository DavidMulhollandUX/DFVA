### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Communicate responsibly about basic preventive oral health approaches with a range of stakeholders
**2.**
Discuss ethical practice in preventive oral health care
**3.**
Describe basic health promotion strategies linking general health and oral health
**4.**
Describe common preventable oral health issues
**5.**
Identify current research relevant to preventive oral health care

Subject: WOMINJEKA LA TROBE: INDIGENOUS CULTURAL LITERACY FOR HIGHER EDUCATION (ABS0WOM)
Credit Points: 0
Overview: Wominjeka La Trobe is a short one-hour compulsory online subject that introduces all La Trobe students to Indigenous Australian history, culture and customs. A rich and relevant cultural heritage education is important for everyone. This topic highlights the link between Indigenous knowledge and values and broader cultural literacy. It asks you to think critically about your own attitudes, values and beliefs.
Learning Outcomes:
- Reflect critically on your own cultural attitudes, values and beliefs
- Demonstrate knowledge of and respect for Indigenous Australian perspectives and experiences, history, culture and customs, including an appreciation of the local people and country
- Recall important details regarding the history, culture, customs and people of Indigenous Australia
- Understand the need for and value of appropriate and effective intercultural communication

Subject: ORAL HEALTH PRACTICE (DEN1001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you'll delve into the essential skills and knowledge required for dental and oral health practitioners. You'll engage in hands-on learning in the pre-clinical laboratory to enhance your understanding of preventive, periodontal, and restorative procedures. Additionally, you'll gain insight into the scope of practice, safe working practices, and minimally invasive dentistry.
Learning Outcomes:
- Perform basic dental procedures in the management of periodontal and restorative cases in a simulated setting.
- Demonstrate personal and professional behaviour in a pre-clinical setting.
- Apply diagnostic investigation to the practice of minimal intervention dentistry.
- Apply the principles and practices of infection control in the preclinical environment with respect to the provision of clinical care.

Subject: PRECLINICAL ORAL HEALTH SIMULATED PRACTICE (DEN1002)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you'll advance your fundamental skills and knowledge. Through hands-on practice in the pre-clinical laboratory and simulated patient settings, you'll develop expertise in examining periodontal health. Additionally, you'll learn about the scope of practice, safe working practices, and minimally invasive dentistry. By the end of the subject, you will be expected to have achieved competency in foundational pre-clinical skills.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply different diagnostic techniques and procedures and when they would be incorporated into a clinical setting.
- Demonstrate restorative skills for restoration in the dentition.

- Demonstrate personal and professional behaviour in a pre-clinical setting.

Subject: DENTAL SCIENCE A (DEN1DSA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will be introduced to the anatomical organisation of the body and the basics of cell structure, function, nutrition and metabolism. The fundamentals of the nervous and endocrine systems will be explored in the context of mechanisms of physiological control and homeostasis. This information will provide the foundation for the study of the major organ systems of the body, and an understanding of the oral systemic connections. Communication is important in engaging patients and communities in relation to oral health, so you will also learn to communicate in groups with peers about the body systems.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain how a given body system contributes to homeostasis under normal conditions.
- Explain how cellular activity contributes to the function of organs and the body as a whole.
- Relate the anatomical organisation of the human body to whole body function.
- Work effectively individually and with peers to apply professional and medical terminology into communicating oral-systemic connections.

===== SOURCE: https://handbook.latrobe.edu.au/subjects/2026/DEN1DSB =====
Subject: DENTAL SCIENCE B (DEN1DSB)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will learn about the gross anatomy of structures predominantly in the region of the head and neck. The subject will include a histology component where you will learn details of normal histology of structures within the oral cavity The subject will also include embryology where you will learn about the development of teeth. Principles of anatomy will be introduced and applied to the anatomy of the head and neck relevant for Dentistry and Oral Health. The subject will also introduce you into other visceral cavities of the head and neck which will then provide you with relevant background for future clinical practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the histological features of different tooth components and discuss the processes involved in the formation and differentiation of dental tissues during embryogenesis
- Explain the structure and function of the head and neck including its role in housing visceral structures, special sense organs and the central nervous system using appropriate medical terminology, surface anatomy and observational skills
- Be able to identify anatomical structures and infer their relationship to function and communicate anatomical concepts using correct medical terminology in writing.
- Describe the anatomical basis of some common abnormalities of, or injuries to structures in the head and neck.

Subject: SCIENTIFIC AND CLINICAL KNOWLEDGE A (DEN1SKA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will study the anatomy of the head and face with a focus on anatomy of the teeth. You will study human tooth morphology, where morphology is the study of the form and structure, in this instance of teeth and their specific structural features. This subject will also introduce you to the core values of a health practitioner and awareness of the First Nations culture which set a foundation to the development of your professional identity.
Learning Outcomes:
- Articulate core concepts related to the foundational cultural awareness and cultural responsiveness for improving the health outcomes in the First Nations peoples
- Identify and discuss the anatomy of each tooth in the human dentitions and determine their functions
- Contextualise the characteristics of a professional oral health practitioner with reference to professional behaviour, ethical principles and communication
- Apply discipline-specific terminology and nomenclature to describe the functional anatomy of the orofacial region

Subject: SCIENTIFIC AND CLINICAL KNOWLEDGE B (DEN1SKB)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will explore microbiology (bacteriology, mycology and virology) and immunology, gaining foundational knowledge for future study in both general and oral health. You will examine dental caries and periodontal disease in terms of their aetiology and pathogenesis, classification and prevention.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the classification, aetiology and pathogenesis of periodontal disease.
- Analyse the aetiology, pathogenesis and modifying factors of dental caries.
- Apply concepts of inflammation and microbiology to general health and oral disease.
- Communicate concepts of immunology, microbiology, periodontal disease and dental caries using multimedia presentations.

Subject: RESEARCH AND EVIDENCE IN PRACTICE (HLT1RAE)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject is an introduction to the use of research-based evidence in professional health care practice. Working in interprofessional teams and using a range of case scenarios, you will develop research skills in areas relevant to your field of practice. Through online activities and workshops, you will learn about the role of evidence-based practice in health. Areas of study include systematic approaches to acquiring evidence, critical appraisal of the literature, interpretation of research design, descriptive and inferential statistics and assessment of research outcomes. You will learn how an evidence-based approach in health informs clinical practice. You will develop research skills to determine the most appropriate intervention techniques for application in a given clinical population, while understanding the complex interaction between social, economic and environmental influences that contribute to sustainability thinking in health research.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically appraise some components of the validity of health research evidence
- Explain the relative merits and uses of the different types of evidence used in practice
- Communicate research outcomes appropriate to peers
- Develop an effective search strategy to address practice based research questions
Workload:

Subject: RURAL COMMUNITY ENGAGEMENT (HLT1RCE)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: The subject provides you with the opportunity to develop your understanding of the diversity and complexity of rural communities. You will work in interdisciplinary teams on defining and developing a project that your team identified through visiting a rural community and community consultation and discussion on needs, priorities and real-world issues. You will have the opportunity to develop skills in communication, teamwork, and self-management and gain insight into the opportunities, complexities of and challenges to health and wellbeing in rural communities.
Learning Outcomes:
- Reflect on professional learning needs to devise a professional plan on self-management and communication.
- Collaborate and cooperate as a member of a project team to achieve shared goals.
- Apply verbal and written communication skills to engage with rural communities.
- Apply key community engagement principles to a rural health and wellness context.
- Identify the characteristics of rural communities and opportunities to maximise health and wellbeing.

Subject: ACADEMIC INTEGRITY MODULE (LTU0AIM)
Credit Points: 0
Overview: The academic integrity module introduces academic integrity standards to inform and support students in avoiding plagiarism and academic misconduct. Students complete four parts that encompass academic misconduct and academic integrity decisions, including cheating, plagiarism, and collusion. Students learn about Turnitin (the text-matching tool) used at La Trobe University, and how to get help and support their development of referencing skills.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the importance of the acknowledgment and the referencing of sources
- Locate, select, and utilise information about La Trobe's approach to academic integrity to support appropriate acknowledgement of references used.
- Recognise and understand academic integrity responsibilities.
