This course is delivered and assessed wholly online.

The Master of Human Resource Management equips students with the strategic mindset and skills needed for human resource leadership in today's global business environment. Aligned with the Australian Human Resources Institute (AHRI) capability framework, this program covers critical areas such as strategic people management, evidence-based practices, diversity and inclusion, and human resource analytics, emphasising practical application and ethical decision-making. Blending theory with real-world case studies, the course develops advanced problem-solving skills and cultural awareness essential for managing diverse workforces. The capstone project allows students to apply their learning to real-world scenarios, contributing to the field's knowledge, providing a pathway to further study. Graduates emerge as transformative human resource leaders, prepared to drive organisational success through strategic thinking, data-driven decision-making, and fostering inclusive workplace cultures. This program combines academic rigor with practical skills, positioning graduates to navigate modern human resource complexities and make significant contributions to their organisations and the broader business community. This program is accredited with the Australian Human Resources Institute (AHRI).

This course is not available to international students who are holders of an Australian student visa, for study onshore in Australia as this is not a registered CRICOS course. This course is available to all other international students to study wholly online.

Online Term 5

Please note: this subject is delivered wholly online and only open to students enrolled in the wholly online courses Master of Human Resource Management and Graduate Certificate in Human Resource Management. Subjects in this course are delivered in an online accelerated learning model and therefore, students typically enrol in one 12.5 credit point subject per online teaching term.

This subject examines the fundamentals of managing people in organisations. Students will explore key Human Resource policies and practices, including employee attraction, retention, development, health and wellbeing, and performance management. The subject includes the foundations of management, including psychological, individual and group behaviour, cognition, and social and political dynamics. Students will learn to evaluate Human Resource strategies from multiple stakeholder perspectives and apply psychological principles and insights from the social sciences to understand and address common managerial challenges. By integrating Human Resource practices with organisational behaviour concepts, this subject provides a comprehensive understanding of how to effectively manage people and enhance organisational performance.

Intended learning outcomes

On completion of this subject, students should be able to:

identify HR's operational, managerial, and strategic responsibilities;
analyse the link between human resource strategies and organisational performance;
apply research-based, practical knowledge on how organisations recruit, select, develop and reward employees;
apply key concepts and research to individual HR problems, presented in case studies;
analyse managerial problems using different human resource theories and frameworks;
investigate how different human resource management practices shape employee experience and behaviour.
Generic skills
Critically assess and develop improvement strategies;
Critically analyse case studies applying relevant theories;
Analyse, interpret, and evaluate initiatives;
Develop evidence-based recommendations to inform decision-making.

The performance of employees can affect the capacity of an organisation to achieve its objectives. In this subject we identify the tools and techniques used by organisations to motivate and evaluate employee performance. We then investigate how these performance assessments are used to allocate employee rewards and recognition. Our evaluation of performance and reward management systems is focused on the fairness and effectiveness of these systems for various stakeholders in a dynamic organisational environment and the implications for employee equity and wellbeing.

explain and critically evaluate systems for defining, assessing, and developing employee performance;
explain and critically evaluate systems for motivating employees through rewards and recognition systems;
apply theory and research to interpret human resource management data and information to make evidence-based decisions;
evaluate the implications of different performance and reward management systems from the perspective of employees, supervisors and human resource professionals;
critically evaluate performance and reward management systems in terms of fairness, equity and employee wellbeing within dynamic organisational environments.

Online Term 6

This subject explores how organisations can design and sustain environments that promote employee health, wellbeing, and engagement in an era of increasing complexity, technological disruption, and shifting workforce expectations. Drawing on interdisciplinary research and real-world case studies, students will examine the strategic value of wellbeing as a driver of organisational performance, resilience, and sustainable success. Key areas of focus include job design, psychological safety, burnout, trauma-informed human resource practices, and the evolving role of leadership in fostering engagement. Students will also consider the ethical and practical implications of wellbeing initiatives across diverse employee groups, including the impact of technology and the importance of inclusive design. Through applied assessment and interactive learning, students will develop the ability to craft evidence-based strategies that enhance both employee experience and business outcomes.

Intended learning outcomes

On completion of this subject, students should be able to:

analyse the relationship between employee wellbeing, engagement, and organisational effectiveness, including return on investment considerations;
evaluate and apply evidence-based HR strategies to promote health, resilience, and psychological safety in diverse workplace settings;
design inclusive wellbeing initiatives that account for trauma-informed practice, hybrid work environments, and ethical use of HR technologies;
critically reflect on the strategic role of HR in shaping sustainable and equitable employee experiences;
communicate the value and impact of engagement and wellbeing strategies to organisational stakeholders using data and insights.
Generic skills
Develop evidence-based recommendations to support decision-making;
Critically assess and develop implementation strategies;
Analyse, interpret, and evaluate initiatives;
Communicate complex concepts to diverse audiences.

Diversity, equity and inclusion (DEI) continues to be a critical topic for managing people and organisations and for society more broadly.  This subject offers a contemporary and critical perspective on how organisations can approach DEI to maximise the benefits for themselves, as well as employees and external stakeholders.  Students will be introduced to frameworks that can be used to analyse, plan and evaluate DEI strategies and interventions and apply these to real-world organisational examples. In addition, the subject offers opportunities to learn more about specific DEI groups and topics, and how organisations can attract and retain diverse talent and foster an inclusive working environment.  This will include discussion of gender, work and family, race and ethnicity, disability, age and other issues of current interest.

Intended learning outcomes

On completion of this subject, students should be able to:

explain and critically evaluate alternative rationales for diversity, equity and inclusion;
critically analyse current organisational approaches to diversity, equity and inclusion;
develop evidence-based recommendations to improve organisational practice in diversity, equity and inclusion;
demonstrate in-depth understanding of the challenges facing diversity, equity and inclusion groups and how organisations can assist in addressing these through their human resource practices;
construct a diversity, equity and inclusion strategy that reflects the operations, business objectives and environment of a given organisation.
Generic skills
Analyse, interpret, and evaluate initiatives;
Critically assess and develop improvement strategies;

This subject examines individual and collective human behaviour in and around issues of organisational change. The subject will cover a broad theoretical basis that assists in understanding how change at the employee, group and strategic levels affects individuals. Both the planned approach and emergent approach to change management will be considered.

Topics to be covered include: drivers of change, the role of internal and external change agents, tools for successful change management, and the implementation and consequences of specific change initiatives, and the use of Gen AI to identify change issues and leveraging Gen AI to engage stakeholders.

Intended learning outcomes

On completion of this subject, students should be able to:

identify and evaluate theories and models concerning organisational change;
synthesise and apply major theories and models to problems ;
use technology to identify issues and create evidence-based recommendations;
analyse the roles of change agents in facilitating organisational change;
evaluate the effectiveness of approaches to change management in various organisational contexts.

critically assess and develop improvement strategies;

Law and Human Resource Management (MGMT90315)

Graduate courseworkPoints: 12.5Not available in 2026

About this subject
Overview
Eligibility and requirements

Overview

Please note: this subject is delivered wholly online and only open to students enrolled in the wholly online Master of Human Resource Management. Subjects in this course are delivered in an online accelerated learning model and therefore, students typically enrol in one 12.5 credit point subject per online teaching term.

Paid work is central to the lives of most adults – it provides an income and is constitutive of identities. For society at large, the organisation of paid work relationships is crucial because of the need to produce goods and services and to protect those engaged in production. Regulation of these work relationships by law is, therefore, important.

This subject provides a thematic overview of the legal regulation of work relationships in Australia in an industrial, social and political context. It examines how work relationships are regulated through statutory regimes how they manifest in human resource management functions and practices. A major focus of the subject is the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth), which sets minimum employment conditions, regulates modern awards and enterprise agreement-making, provides redress in relation to adverse action and provides processes to deal with issues of sexual harassment. Anti-discrimination and equality law will also be examined.

This subject is designed to be of assistance to students without previous (or recent) legal study in this area.

Indicative list of principal topics:

Statutory standards under the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth) including unfair dismissal, minimum wage rates, hours of work, leave, adverse action and 'right to request' regimes
The regulation of employment rights and working conditions by modern awards and enterprise agreements under the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth)
The distinction between the status of employee and independent contractor
Anti-discrimination and workplace health and safety law
Compliance and enforcement mechanisms.
Intended learning outcomes

On completion of this subject, students should be able to:

critically analyse the legal principles of Australian employment law;
engage in informed debate on emerging and contemporary issues in the field of Australian employment law;
analyse and evaluate the complex range of situations in which legal issues and disputes may arise in work relationships and management practices;
clearly and accurately communicate and convey complex information regarding employment law to relevant specialist and non-specialist audiences.
Generic skills
Critically interpret and apply relevant frameworks to inform professional decision making
Identify, analyse and develop solutions to complex problems
Communicate complex ideas to diverse stakeholders.

This subject involves the analysis of organisational decisions and actions with a focus on implications for human resource policy and practice. A key focus is on aligning human resource strategies with broader organisational goals to ensure human resource practices actively support long-term business success. Many organisational decisions have implications for human resource and employees including the number of people to be employed, the type of skills required and the way employee performance is assessed and rewarded. In this subject students will draw on fundamental business concepts and human resource management studies to critically analyse business issues and develop recommendations that align with organisational strategy and promote organisational sustainability.

Intended learning outcomes

assess the impacts of organisational decisions on the workforce and HR practices;
identify and evaluate the potential HR implications of organisational decisions prior to their implementation;
discuss the strategic value and measurable contribution of HR to sustained organisational performance and success;
design proactive HR strategies and interventions in response to organisational decisions that enhance workforce capability and promote sustainability;
formulate evidence-based HR recommendations that align with organisational strategy and support long-term organisational sustainability.
