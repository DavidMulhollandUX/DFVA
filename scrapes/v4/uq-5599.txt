There is currently a tremendous growth in the international biotechnology industry and allied service sector. Research and development managers require scientists with core technical skills, knowledge of commercial imperatives and current regulatory environments.
This Masters program is designed for scientists who want to update their technical and research skills in core areas such as molecular biology, protein technology or bioinformatics, and who wish to acquire more extended laboratory research than is available through the three semester MBiotech. The program will provide a strong training for entry to a PhD.
This program is recognised by the US National Professional Science Master's Association as an affiliated Professional Science Master's program.

Subject: Introduction to Molecular Biology Laboratory (BIOC6001)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course will introduce you to key skills required to carry out modern molecular biological research. By the end of this course, you will have an understanding of how many commonly used molecular biological techniques work, as well as their advantages and limitations, together with the fundamentals of experimental design and the ethical considerations of research. To get to this point you will reinforce and apply material learnt in lectures and tutorials in regular laboratory practical sessions where the detection, quantification, characterisation, and purification of proteins and nucleic acids will be explored.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand and articulate the theoretical basis of common molecular biological techniques.
- Competently carry out common techniques in the laboratory.
- Describe and apply the principles of basic experimental design.
- Apply and describe practices that demonstrate scientific ethics.
- Collect, interpret and report molecular biological data.
- Present findings in common forms of scientific communication.

- Competently find information in the field of molecular genetics using electronic and other resources and be able to evaluate the veracity of this information.

Subject: Advanced Protein & Nucleic Acids (BIOC7040)

Subject: Biologics (BIOT7018)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Biologics include a wide range of products that include vaccines, blood and blood components, somatic cells, gene therapy, tissues and tissue-engineered products, and recombinant therapeutic proteins. The course will cover a variety of topics associated with the discovery, development, characterisation and production of biologics as human therapeutics, focussing predominantly on recombinant therapeutic proteins. The course will provide an in-depth coverage of the technologies and bioprocesses associated with biologics production. The course will also have a major focus on the global biologics industry and its continued evolution.
Learning Outcomes:
- Competently appraise the global biologics industry and future opportunities.
- Explain the enabling technologies underpinning the research and development of biologics.
- Discuss in detail the classes of biologics, therapeutic modalities, production and purification processes, process economics and analytical testing of biologic preparations.
- Critically assess and appraise relevant literature in biologics and draw parallels between biologicals and conventional pharmaceuticals.
Assessment Tasks:
- TBL Workshop Session Blackboard Quiz Online: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Biologics Presentation Online: 40%% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Biotechnology Intellectual Property Case Studies (BIOT7040)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Study of cases in intellectual property (IP) in biotechnology. Criteria for patentability. Breadth of claims, reduction to practice, fair basing. Process and product patents. Terms of patent in biotechnology. Differences in patent rules between countries. Plant breeders rights. Prohibitions/exclusions from patentability in biotechnology. Compulsory licensing. Illustrative Case studies in biotechnology IP: The GTG patents, the PCR patents, Taxol patents, Neem patents, Turmeric patents, rice patents, Affymetrix patents, "Southern" patents, EST patents, sequence patents, stem cell patents. Strategies for managing biotechnology IP Analysis of the parts of a patent and a critical examination of a patent in the context of prior art. Preparation of a case study in IP conflict, dispute or debate in biotechnology. Due to a limited number of places available in this course, there will be a selection process for enrolment with places being allocated based on the results from the prerequisite course and merit using the student's overall GPA as the criterion.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe several strategies for protection and commercialization of biotechnology IP
- List criteria that are used to make decisions about biotechnology IP protection and management strategies and apply those criteria to make decisions
- Deconstruct and analyse complex disputes in the field of biotechnology IP
- List the important parts of a biotechnology patent
- Demonstrate an awareness of the different interpretations of prior art in different countries in relation to biotechnology IP
- Examine and evaluate trade secret litigation in biotechnology
Assessment Tasks:
- Examination of a trade secret litigation case: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Deconstruction and analysis of a dispute: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Myriad decisions in the U.S. and Australia: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Biotechnology IP protection strategy Hurdle Identity Verified: 50%% (Hurdle: True)

Subject: Principles of Entrepreneurship (TIMS7301)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Entrepreneurship is commonly regarded as the pursuit of opportunity beyond the resources apparently to hand. With this maxim in mind, we explore the theory and practice of starting then growing new ventures, where opportunities exist but the pathways to unleash promising outcomes are uncertain. Knowledge and skill development is built by identifying and developing a new business idea within the class, through adaptive execution to generate information by building and testing business hypotheses so to navigate uncertainties. These skills are then used to analyse conditions across various entrepreneurial firms. Topics include the process of entrepreneurial logic, designing business models for value capture and delivery, framing competitive strategy, determining assumptions-based finance needs, understanding different innovation types and managing growth to build stronger ventures.
Learning Outcomes:
- Articulate the foundational knowledge in the area of entrepreneurship.
- Develop, evaluate and communicate actionable business opportunities as part of a startup team.
- Create viable and innovative Business Models.
- Critically analyse knowledge creation in the startup process.
- Practice real-time entrepreneurial leadership to craft ideas and test impacts.
