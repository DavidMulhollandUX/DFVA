## Description
The financial services industry has been experiencing a technological revolution, not only in Australia but globally. As a result, there has been an explosion in demand for individual that can combine both finance and technology skills. This is a tremendous opportunity for universities to develop programs that can respond to this demand. The fully-online Graduate Certificate in Financial Technology (FinTech) will draw on thought leadership and advanced practice to create a program that is contemporary, relevant, practical, and multi-disciplinary.

The program will provide professionals with the necessary skills to be able to respond to the technological revolution that the financial services industry has been experiencing. The program covers the major changes that have recently occurred in the financial services industry, the main types of business models adopted, the skill-set required to cope with the profound transformations that have occurred in this space, and the future career opportunities that can still open in this industry. It includes the discussion of case studies in FinTech, which will be backed by testimonials from industry practitioners in a Podcast setting. These practitioners will share the practical challenges that they have faced in their own projects, the trends that they observe in the industry, and the upcoming opportunities that they envision for a career in FinTech. These guest speakers will be from several areas of FinTech, such as crowdfunding, data mining, robo advice, payments, cross-country money transfers, regulation, etc. The program concludes with an applied team-based project that will be presented to a FinTech accelerator for discussion and validation. This final project will ask students to consult on a real-world problem in finance that requires a tech solution.

This program is suitable for a range of professionals, including those from traditional positions in the financial services industry who have finance knowledge but are looking to refresh their technical skill-set, who want to move for more senior positions, or who want to join or remain relevant in the constantly changing labor market. This program is also suitable to IT professionals who are looking to acquire knowledge in financial products, regulations, and specific technologies used in the FinTech sector. Last but not least, this program is suitable for recent graduates in business/commerce/finance who are looking for advanced education in both finance and technology to stay competitive in the labor market.

Overview: This course offers a comprehensive exploration of robo-advisors, highlighting their role in today's financial planning and investment management. Students will begin by building efficient portfolios using Modern Portfolio Theory (MPT) and gaining hands on experience with practical applications in Excel. - Explain the foundational concepts of traditional financial theory, behavioural finance, and household finance as they relate to robo-advice and decision support.
- Evaluate various behavioural biases exhibited by clients, linking these biases to demographics, financial literacy, and common investment mistakes to achieve better investment outcomes.
- Design or critically analyse financial decision support tools, utilising insights from behavioural finance to tailor recommendations to client circumstances.

Subject: Portfolio Management (ZZBU8701)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course aims to develop a basic conceptual framework within which the modern investment environment can be understood. You will firstly contextualise learning by being provided with an institutional and product overview of the investment markets. You will then develop a more complete understanding of the risk-return relation in order to review alternative investment strategies and asset pricing models, with a focus on use for portfolio construction and valuation purposes. Real-world circumstances in which these models and strategies may or may not apply will be explored. The material will be taught in a way that focuses on how the theory applies to real-world situations and will aim to be as up-to-date as possible by emphasising recent developments in investment management.
Learning Outcomes:
- Create an optimal investment portfolio by applying portfolio theory.
- Identify the limitations of the models in certain situations and the resulting implications for designing investment strategies.
- Utilise the models taught to interpret and analyse real problems in investments.
- Describe the operation of financial markets and common financial products
- Apply the standard models of asset pricing to measure, price and manage risk; value securities; and exploit mispriced securities.

Subject: International Finance (ZZBU8703)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course provides students with the knowledge required for the management of financial functions of multinational firms. In addition to building a rich theoretical grounding in international finance concepts, the course teaches you how to apply those concepts via real-world data analysis and scenario-based studies. The course begins by exploring international monetary policy and foreign exchange markets at the macro-economic level and moves on to a theoretical understanding of exchange rate determinants. We then shift the focus from the global- to the transaction-level and learn how to identify and develop hedging strategies to deal with risk related to exchange rate and interest rate fluctuations. With the core conceptual understanding of hedging instruments in place, we expand our knowledge to application and decision-making at the firm-level and students learn how multinational firms can invest and finance in international markets and manage related risks. At each stage of the student journey, learning material, as well as weekly activities and assessments, are designed to build a community of inquiry and to articulate theory through real-world examples, cases or data exercises. It is recommended to undertake this course later in the program, where possible.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify exchange rate risk and develop hedging strategies to deal with it.
- Compare, contrast, and assess varying exchange rate systems in various countries and understand the determinants of foreign exchange rates.
- Analyse and discuss the international macro-economic and political environments that are relevant to multinational firms.
- Evaluate foreign direct investment and tax and regulatory arbitrage opportunities.
- Apply the theories on exchange rate determinants and global portfolio models to real problems in global business using dynamic real-world data.
- Construct professional and logical written work in the context of international finance.
Assessment Tasks:
- Fully online - Intensive: N/A% (Hurdle: None)

Subject: Private Equity and Venture Capital (ZZBU8704)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course examines venture capital and private equity investments. In particular, it focuses on issues surrounding the funding of entrepreneurial firms that are financed by venture capital or private equity funds. The course departs from conventional investment approaches that examine risk and return of publicly listed securities, focusing on issues associated with financing and innovation intensive private businesses.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the key determinants that drive the choice of IPO (initial public offering) versus trade sale when exiting and in investment.
- Identify the risks associated with venture capital/private equity investments and explain how to mitigate them.
- Illustrate the key differences between the private and public financial markets.
- Explain and discuss how cultural and social aspects can affect decisions in a private equity/venture capital context.
- Evaluate private equity and venture capital funds performance and their contribution to a portfolio.
- Negotiate an appropriate investment structure, both from an investor and investee perspective.
- Describe the responsibilities of directors of private companies, in particular, in a private equity/venture capital context.
- Determine which valuation method is most appropriate depending on the context of the valuation exercise, the nature of the opportunity and the stage of its development.

Subject: Fixed Income (ZZBU8706)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course presents a foundational knowledge framework for professionals seeking understanding of the quantitative and qualitative characteristics of fixed-income assets (i.e. debt securities). Students firstly get an overview of various types of fixed-income assets before looking in detail at the financial calculations relevant to their valuation and risk analysis. A crucial element of the course is the development of students’ financial mathematics skills, which will be achieved with practical demonstrations and exercises linking course work concepts with real-world applications. The course is predominantly aligned with CFA Level 1, but also delves into more advanced topics such as yield curves, risk management of fixed income portfolios, structured financial products, and credit default swaps.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate understanding of additional debt security complexities such as accrued interest, floating rates, inflation, embedded options and structured financial products.
- Demonstrate understanding of credit risk, credit ratings, and credit default swaps.
- Demonstrate the relationships between a bond’s price, coupon rate, maturity, and market discount rate (yield-to-maturity) in spreadsheet calculations.
- Describe government and corporate debt markets and their stakeholders.
- Explain the concepts of duration and convexity and apply them to manage the risk of a bond portfolio.
- Demonstrate the relationship between yield curves, and spot and forward interest rates in spreadsheet calculations, and demonstrate understanding of the relationship between yield curves and economic circumstances.

Subject: Ethics and Professional Standards (ZZBU8708)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course describes why a high level of ethical standard is needed by those employed in the financial services industry. Initially, significant ethical theories are explored. Next, the six components of the Code of Ethics are addressed and each of the seven Standards of Professional Conduct examined in detail. The course also analyses the scope and key features of the Global Investment Performance Standards (GIPS), the reason for their creation, to whom they apply, and how to verify GIPS compliance. The course includes interviews with leaders in the industry who reflect on the role of ethics in their careers.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the ethical responsibilities required by the Code and Standards, including recommended guidance and procedures for compliance practices.
- Evaluate ethical dilemmas through application of the Code and Standards (including sub-parts).
- Explain the role Global Investment Performance Standards (GIPS) play in promoting an ethical framework for calculating and presenting investment performance history.
- Identify the role ethical theories, including the CFA Code of Ethics, have in the financial services industry.

Subject: Foundations of Data Science (ZZSC9001)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This is an intensive course that covers the fundamentals of Data Science as it is applied in Computer Science, Economics, Mathematics and Statistics. The course will introduce topics such as databases, data analytics, data mining, Bayesian statistics, statistical software, econometrics, machine learning, and business forecasting. The course also introduces the Data Science and Decisions program, indicating the relevance of the courses that follow in the program (including electives) and their place in data science and its applications. This course includes lectures, tutorials, and workshops and is delivered completely online. The course will take the flipped classroom approach. Students are assigned the “homework” of watching video lectures and reading materials relevant to the online lecture session. During each session, a recap and overview of the content will be given and students will practice what they’ve learned through examples and tutorial problems.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply probability rules in a given setting to calculate key quantities.
- Use R/RStudio to perform statistical computations and simulations.
- Use key theoretical tools to explore the properties of random variables.
- Apply various data visualisation tools, perform regression analysis and draw casual inference from data.
- Apply key methods of statistical inference in applied settings.
- Apply fundamental data science techniques and tools, including machine learning, Naïve Bayes classification, Decision trees, K-Nearest Neighbour, unsupervised learning and neural networks.

- Fully online - Intensive: N/A% (Hurdle: None)
