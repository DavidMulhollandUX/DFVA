## Master of Health Information Management - Melbourne (Bundoora)
##### HMHIM
##### 240 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
Combine the clinical and business sides of healthcare and use data to enhance the quality of patient care with La Trobe’s multidisciplinary Master of Health Information Management.

Excel in a specialised profession that ensures patient health information is accurate, complete, accessible and secure. Hone your skills in clinical classification, data analytics, health informatics, health systems and workforce management. Expand professional networks with placements in healthcare agencies and management training to help you step straight into senior healthcare roles.

By studying La Trobe's Master of Health Information Management, you will:

  * master IT skills to implement and manage health information systems in the context of cybersecurity, identifier and interoperability requirements
  * analyse and interpret health data for safety and quality reporting, health service benchmarking and population health
  * build skills in health classification and data application to address funding reimbursement, organisational planning and research practice
  * graduate ready to apply for membership of the Health Information Management Association of Australia (HIMAA), the Australasian Institute of Digital Health (AIDH) and the Clinical Coders' Society of Australia (CCSA).
*Please be aware that this course includes mandatory in-person placement requirements. Students are expected to complete these placements in Australia.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Employ professional skills and practice including high level communication, negotiation skills, strong commitment to continuous learning both individually and as a team member to prepare for management/leadership roles in dynamic health care environments.
**2.**
Generate, research, implement, and evaluate new understandings and concepts in the science of health information management.
**3.**
Apply high standards of professional integrity through an advanced and informed understanding of ethical, legal and practical applications and issues pertaining to health data and informatics across the health data ecosystem.
**4.**
Apply advanced conceptual, theoretical and practical knowledge in domains of health data analytics, health classification, health systems management and health informatics to solve complex problems in health information environments.

### Work based learning (placement) requirements
Compulsory placements (Work Based Learning)

Compulsory placements (Work Based Learning) During this course, you will participate in one or more supervised Work Based Learning (WBL) placements designed to allow you to practice newly acquired skills, behaviours and knowledge while integrating theory with practice. The placements will immerse you in professional work culture, enabling you to develop and further enhance your skills. WBL provides you with the opportunity to gain valuable industry experience at one or more of our local, interstate or international industry partners or agencies. WBL placements will generally be arranged by the Office of the Provost Placements and WBL Team at La Trobe University. The number of hours undertaken can vary considerably, depending on the requirements of the course. Students may incur additional costs and it may be necessary to travel or relocate to complete a specific placement. Please refer to the individual subject entries for more information

When you graduate, prepare for a rewarding career in a range of healthcare settings, including hospitals, health insurance and health information technology companies, and government and community agencies, such as the Department of Health and the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare (AIHW). Possible roles include:

  * automation operations lead
  * health information manager
  * clinical coder
  * clinical trials manager
  * EMR HIM analyst
  * health data analyst or manager
  * information integrity director
  * project manager
  * research manager
  * systems or business analyst.

### Additional costs
All students who have not completed a degree in medicine, biomedical science, science (with studies in physiology and anatomy), dentistry, nursing, allied health or health sciences (containing anatomy and physiology subjects) must complete a La Trobe or equivalent self-funded Bridging course in Physiology and Anatomy. The approximate cost is $550.
Applicants will be required to hold a valid Working with Children Check (WWCC). There is no cost for a volunteer WWCC. For more information and costs refer to the Working with Children Check Victoria website.
Applicants will be required to hold a valid Police Check which is renewed annually. Students will have access to a reduced fee when applying via a La Trobe University provider. For more information on what a police check entails, refer to the Victoria Police website.
Prior to commencing a placement, students are required to meet all of the requirements of the degree in relation to immunisation. Students must visit a medical practitioner who will complete and sign the Immunisation and Health Record form.

Depending on a students immunisation history, the overall costs for vaccination may be up to AUD$1030. Students are responsible for all costs incurred. Further information will be provided post enrolment.
Clinical placements may incur accommodation, meals and travel costs and these will vary depending on the placement venue (occasionally interstate) and duration. Payment of these costs are the students responsibility.
Students should allow for costs to purchase specialised health classification books. The approximate cost is AUD$550. These items may be purchased from any supplier and remain the students’ personal property.

Subject: REVIEW AND ANALYSIS OF HEALTH INFORMATION SYSTEMS DESIGN (HIM4001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will review and analyse broad concepts of health data and the system development lifecycle and the purpose, scope, design and functionalities of health information systems. You will appraise various stages of the systems development and health data lifecyles including issues that impact systems, data and information security. Through your assessments, you will critically analyse healthcare cybersecurity frameworks, create user stories and data flow diagrams, and theoretically evaluate systems design. Having completed this subject, you will be able to differentiate between the various roles and positioning of health professionals within the health information systems development and health data lifecycle.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify the weaknesses and strengths in health information system features using interface design and user-centred approaches.
- Analyse and innovate workflow processes using system modelling approaches.
- Review and respond to health information system issues affecting the end user, work process, health information and system infrastructure relating to the health data and system development lifecyles
- Critically analyse health information and clinical systems to create system model components using standard modelling tools.
- Collaborate and communicate on health information system issues to develop the professional attributes, skills and knowledge of the Business/System analyst and Health Professionals.

Subject: HEALTH DATA ANALYSIS AND FUNDING CLASSIFICATIONS (HIM4002)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will develop specialist working knowledge of the regulatory frameworks governing both public and private sectors, and explore the various funding classifications, alongside clinical costing, modelling and policy and funding guidelines. Models of Activity Based Funding (ABF), such as casemix-based systems will be explored with linkages made to the fundamental role of the Health Information Manager. You will develop an understanding of health data standards, health data collections, and reporting requirements across various sectors, including government, insurance, and other domains; and explore the interconnections between these data practices and their impact on health service measurement and financing. Case studies deepen your understanding of these concepts and your analyses of morbidity, mortality, clinical, productivity, and health service data. You will develop skills in analysing and reporting hospital and health service utilisation statistics and interpreting published health data.
Learning Outcomes:
- Examine frameworks for health data at jurisdictional and population levels with understanding of demography and population health monitoring.
- Compute and present hospital and health service utilisation statistics and analyse published health activity and service-related data.
- Work collaboratively to analyse and evaluate the uses of administrative health data collections by state, territory and national health authorities, health service planners, health service managers and researchers.
- Analyse and report health data underpinned by broad and coherent knowledge of models of health service and care funding, including Activity Based Funding and casemix-based approaches.
- Demonstrate well-developed skills in applying the principles and standards related to health data to the processes of collection, reporting and verification for administrative health data.

Subject: HEALTH CLASSIFICATION AND CLINICAL CODING 1 (HIM4003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will develop your competence and confidence in the use of the current edition of the Australian-Modified version of the World Health Organization's International Classification of Diseases, the Australian Classification of Health Interventions, and the Australian Coding Standards for accurate assignment of codes in a timely manner. This subject is graded as a Pass (P) or Fail (F). A mark of 75% or greater is required to achieve a Pass grade in line with industry standards and expectations.
Learning Outcomes:
- Create and apply internal quality control practices for health classification.
- Analyse clinical documentation and interpret and apply Australian Coding Standards, Conventions and ratified advice to accurately classify diseases and interventions.
- Apply Australian Coding Standards, Conventions and ratified advice at a level of expertise and within timeframes that align with industry expectations.

Subject: HOSPITAL PROFESSIONAL PRACTICE (HIM5001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will explore theory and practical applications relevant to health information managers working in, and managing, a Health Information Service. This includes organisational structure, quality activities, department budgeting, occupational health and safety and other topics relevant to the HIM context. You will undertake a supervised, face-to-face professional practice placement in the Health Information Service of an allocated, regional healthcare facility or equivalent. You will achieve practice competencies, and you will undertake key functions on placement to gain understanding of the four key domains of: management, informatics, data analysis and clinical coding roles of hospital health information managers. To meet professional competency requirements students must pass both the theory and placement components to pass this subject. Placement hours: 112.5
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically analyse organisational structures and communication processes, and evaluate their impact on health information service operations and the role of the Health Information Manager
- Compare and contrast principles and processes related to health-based privacy, confidentiality and information access and release, organisational health and safety, and departmental budgeting.
- Integrate theoretical knowledge and practical skills across management, informatics, data analysis, and clinical classification, including Diagnosis Related Groups (DRG) calculations
- Examine and assess the role of internal auditing and reporting of Health Information Managers
- Demonstrate high ethical standards, effective professional communication, and the ability to work in multidisciplinary teams required for managing a health information service

Subject: ANALYSIS OF HEALTH DATA B (HIM5AHB)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will analyse, interpret and use coded and non-coded health data beyond the treatment and disease/operation classification stages. - Work collaboratively to demonstrate the team dynamics involved in completion of activities such as a research proposal ethics application .
- Appraise the principles and processes of establishing, managing and analysing data in specialised clinical and epidemiological databases and registries, including the legislative, privacy and ethics-related requirements.
- Solve key data quality issues, and management protocols and functions, in specialised clinical and epidemiological settings.
