## Bachelor of Fine Art (Honours)
##### F3701
##### 48 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
This is a prestigious program in the discipline and practice of fine art for high achieving students who have completed a relevant undergraduate degree. You will undertake coursework, research and practical training with a focus on fine art studio, curation, or art history and theory. Carrying out an independent research project in your selected area of specialisation, you will work closely with a supervisor who will provide you with individual guidance and academic counselling. The course offers a pathway to higher level research in fine art. 

 Honours Research Areas

  * Fine art studio

  * Curating

  * Art history and theory

### Structure
This course consists of a combination of coursework and research. In the coursework component you will develop advanced theoretical and/or technical knowledge of fine art and appropriate research methodologies. In the research component you will plan and execute a research project under the individual guidance of an academic supervisor.

### Requirements
This course comprises 48 points structured in two parts: Part A. Coursework (18 points) and Part B. Research (30 points).

Units are 6 points unless otherwise indicated. 

Part A. Coursework studies (18 points)

You must complete:

  * AHT4503 Advanced fine art research: Theory

  * AHT4504 Advanced fine art research: Writing

  * FNA4011 Advanced fine art research: Methods

  * OHS1000 Introduction to art and design health and safety (0 points) - if not previously passed or exempted

Part B. Research studies (30 points) 

You must complete:

  * FNA4001 Fine art project studies (12 points)

  * FNA4002 Fine art research project (18 points)

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
identify, evaluate and communicate relevant research concepts in relation to aesthetics, history, theory and cultural issues at a level compatible with entry level higher research degrees
**2.**
identify and apply occupational health and safety measures appropriate to the area of study.
**3.**
interpret, represent and respond to diverse modes of human experience and expression, including the socio-economic and cultural contexts of fine arts practice and the ethical responsibilities of the profession
**4.**
contextualise, generate and apply advanced understanding of the discipline of fine arts to communicate and position your research practice critically within it
**5.**
critically analyse and refine both studio and/or theoretical research in order to undertake sustained independent practice at a professional level

### Progression to further studies
Successful completion of this course may provide a pathway to a Monash master's by coursework degree, and if admitted you may be eligible for credit. 

Successful completion of this course may also provide the preparation necessary to undertake a master's by research degree or a doctoral (PhD) degree.

### AHEGS
The Bachelor of Fine Art (Honours) is an undergraduate award. The course is taught in English. Students who have completed the course have demonstrated the capability to practice at a professional level in their research medium, an understanding of relevant issues and concepts and their application in the fine arts, an advanced understanding of the discipline of the fine arts and the ability to position their practice critically within it. This will include the capacity to undertake an independent research project and to communicate their work to a high standard. The course takes one year of full-time study and comprises a fourth year of study following the completion of the normal requirements of the Bachelor of Fine Art degree or equivalent degree. The Bachelor of Fine Art (Honours) is a level 8 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Advanced fine art research: Theory (AHT4503)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit examines key histories, theories, and frameworks of contemporary art, introducing students to a range of prominent contemporary artists, historians, curators, and theorists. The unit aims to equip students undertaking an honours year – in either art history, fine art or curatorial practice – with an enriched understanding of the art-historical context in which they are working. It also introduces students to advanced research methodologies for writing about contemporary art.
Learning Outcomes:
- Distinguish between different frameworks and methods for analysing artworks and exhibitions (i.e., queer, feminist, decolonial, Marxist, etc.;
- Undertake detailed, original, and imaginative analyses and interpretations of artworks and exhibitions;
- Undertake advanced and original research on artworks, exhibitions, and relevant art history and theory.
- Demonstrate awareness of current discourses in a range of disciplines that bear on the production, exhibition, and interpretation of contemporary art;
Assessment Tasks:
- Literature review: 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Research essay: 60% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 12 hours per week including 3 contact hours plus 9 hours of independent study.

===== SOURCE: https://handbook.monash.edu/2026/units/AHT4504 =====
Subject: Advanced fine art research: Writing (AHT4504)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Designed as a flexible unit, with an innovative small-group mode of delivery that enables the unit content to be student-led. It is specifically designed to enable you to contextualise your own creative practice in theoretical terms. With an emphasis on creative and critically aware approaches to visual production, the unit aims to open up and consolidate research avenues that may be particularly productive for your individual personal directions.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate a grasp of key concepts in contemporary art theory relevant to the area of practice which you are pursuing;
- Explain the senses in which your art practice involves research.
- Practice criticism in small forums, identifying critical questions of other people's practice and considering and conjecturing how aesthetic standards are the expression of cultural values;
- Relate the terms of your practice to the broader field of contemporary art;
- Sustain an exegetical defence of your work and its placement in current visual production;
Assessment Tasks:
- Mini-Exegesis or Research Essay: 60% (Hurdle: None)
- Annotated Bibliography, Abstract, and Research Question: 40% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 12 hours per week including 2 contact hours plus 10 hours of independent study.

Subject: Fine art project studies (FNA4001)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: Fine Art Research at Honours level is largely self-directed and non-prescriptive in terms of content. You will produce a proposal at the beginning of the semester but are free to develop the project in new directions as needed. You will meet regularly with your mentors while undertaking independent research and your work will be discussed and debated in group settings and weekly crit sessions.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate an understanding of site, positioning and environment within your research practice.
- Identify and critically evaluate discourse and techniques relevant to your chosen area of research;
- Extend and refine your critical and creative capabilities through the development of a major practice-based research project;
- Communicate and present your research through appropriate modes of practice within Fine Art Studio, Curating or Art History;
- Critically evaluate and apply an appropriate research methodology to support sustained independent practice as an artist, curator or art historian;
Assessment Tasks:
- Single project / folio: 100% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 24 hours per week including 6 contact hours and 18 hours of independent study, or equivalent.

Subject: Fine art research project (FNA4002)
Credit Points: 18
Overview: This is the second part of your Fine Art Studio, Curating or Art History research project. Following on from FNA4001, it provides an opportunity to extend and complete your research, working towards a resolved project presented at the annual graduate exhibition (Fine Art Studio and Curating) or submitted as a thesis (Art History). You will participate in regular consultations with your mentor, and receive peer and staff feedback via group critique and individual tutorials.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply a cogent language for your practice that is defined by appropriate material and conceptual frameworks within contemporary artistic production and discourse;
- Apply research methodology to conduct advanced studio based, curatorial, or scholarly research in your chosen field;
- Apply advanced interpretations and discourse to critically position outcomes within your chosen field;
- Extend the application and critical evaluation of methodologies, whether studio-based or scholarly, to foster sustained independent practice research practice;
- Demonstrate an advanced level of discipline specific approaches and understanding.
Assessment Tasks:
- Single project / folio: 100% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 36 hours per week including contact hours and individual supervision.

Subject: Advanced fine art research: Methods (FNA4011)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit is designed to complement Fine Art Studio, Curating, and Art History and Theory projects by introducing you to advanced research methodologies and developing your understanding and articulation of research practice. Through a discursive engagement with a range of methodologies and associated fields of enquiry, you will be able to develop a research methodology appropriate for your individual Honours year project.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop a research methodology relevant to your practice;
- Demonstrate an understanding of place, positioning and environment within your research practice.
- Demonstrate a critical understanding of research methodologies in contemporary practice;
- Demonstrate independent and reflective critical thinking, including the ability to identify and apply ethical principles and integrity within your research;
- Develop a discursive approach to your field of enquiry;
- Develop and apply advanced research in your field of enquiry;
Assessment Tasks:
- Presentation 2: 60% (Hurdle: None)
- Presentation 1: 40% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 12 hours per week including 3 contact hours plus 9 hours of independent study.

Subject: Introduction to art and design health and safety (OHS1000)
Credit Points: 0
Overview: This is an online module in Moodle. A quick and easy introduction to occupational health, safety and environmental training within the context of courses and units offered by the Faculty of Art, Design and Architecture.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify the main types of hazard which may be encountered in studios and workshops - chemical, physical, and biological;
- Demonstrate an understanding of the nature of health and safety in the workplace, community expectations, and the relevant legislation;
- Demonstrate an understanding of the importance of controlling risk and how this is done.
- Understand the terms hazard, risk, risk assessment, and risk management, and have an understanding of the procedures for assessing and managing risk;
- Demonstrate that you are conversant with procedures for dealing with emergencies;
Assessment Tasks:
- Short quiz: 100% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: There are no classes - the unit is a short online module in Moodle, which will take approximately 15 minutes to complete. You should complete it as soon as possible after commencing study.
