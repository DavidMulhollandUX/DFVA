### Structure
Double degree courses include all the features of the component degree courses, except that electives may be reduced or redirected. 

This course is structured in four parts: Part A. and Part B. focus on Master of Marketing and Digital Communications studies. Part C. and Part D. focus on Master of International Sustainable Tourism Management studies.

Part A. Core studies - Marketing and Digital Communications

These studies draw on best practices within the broad realm of communications and marketing practice and research to further your understanding of communications and marketing systems both locally and globally. These studies engage with theoretical and industry-based perspectives to provide an understanding of how organisations can connect and engage with their different stakeholders to achieve specific communication and marketing objectives. Studies focus on the opportunities and challenges posed by the emergence of digital media, globalisation and increasing levels of cross-cultural exchange. The impact of technological change on marketing and communication behaviour will also be critically analysed.

Part B. Applied studies - Marketing and Digital Communications

The emphasis of these studies is professional and project work, focused locally or globally, that can contribute to a portfolio of professional development. In this part of the program you can also undertake higher level research.

Part C. Core studies - International Sustainable Tourism Management

These studies will immerse you in international sustainable tourism management studies at graduate level. Through a combination of academic and practice-based work, you will gain a solid foundation in all production technologies - print, video, audio and online - in metropolitan, regional and international contexts.

Part D. Applied studies -International Sustainable Tourism Management

The focus of these studies is professional or scholarly work that can contribute to a portfolio of professional development.

Subject: International tourism marketing research project (APG5183)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: This is a research project based unit designed for students who are focused on developing a career in international tourism marketing and destination development. The centrepiece of this unit is a European industry study tour followed by attendance at ITB Berlin, the world's largest travel symposium and trade fair. Pre and post departure classes are delivered on campus with a focus on the internationalisation process of destinations and their overall competitiveness in the global tourism economy. The workshops are supported by a specifically designed industry seminar and site visit program in the Baltic States highlighting the development of the tourism economy and attendance at the ITB Berlin industry symposium. Learning in this global context combines with theoretical approaches to give context to student-led research projects.

- Collaborate constructively with fellow students in designing and undertaking a substantial research project in an international professional context;
- Critically assess the opportunities and challenges facing international tourism marketing and product development, particularly in the context of developing tourism economies;
- Develop confidence in approaching and working with individuals and organisations in a professional international setting.
- Produce written work and oral presentations to deadline, making effective use of the conventions of scholarly presentation, including citations and bibliography where required;
- Demonstrate effective skills in research design, methods, analysis and project management;

Subject: Contemporary tourism and development in emerging economies (APG5390)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: The challenges facing the sustainable development of the industry in emerging economies are identified and the roles played by key sectors of the tourism economy such as aviation and transport, accommodation, national tourism boards, NGO development organisations and the public and private sector are examined. The impact of market facing development, the growth of "new tourism" business models and independent travel on emerging tourism economies are reviewed as are the economic concepts of leakage and linkage. The unit is presented via a number of on campus seminars complemented by an industry focused field work tour to a "case study" tourism economy such as; Fiji (South Pacific}, Vietnam/Cambodia (S.E Asia} or the Baltic States (Eastern Europe) or Northern Australia. (The selected study tour destination will be nominated at the start of semester and may vary from year to year)
Learning Outcomes:
- Enhanced written and oral communication skills suitable for progress in the tourism profession;
- An understanding of key issues facing the future development of the industry in emerging economies;
- An understanding of the challenges facing tourism development in emerging tourism economies/regions.
- An understanding of key problems and issues in the current and future development of the global tourism industry;
- A developed capacity to undertake original research on an area of their particular interest;
