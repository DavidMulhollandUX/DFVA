Looking for an entry into La Trobe University while studying the VCE Vocational Major? The Prepare-Intermediate Program is designed for VCE Vocational Major students to complete alongside your secondary school studies.The program focuses on building your learner confidence and skills in the areas of academic literacy, critical thinking, science and mathematics. Completion of the program gives you La Trobe Entrance Score, which you can use to apply for La Trobe University courses.
Your subjects are delivered in a supportive, online learning environment. Prepare-Intermediate consists of six micro-subjects, taken individually across six La Trobe Terms. The subjects are delivered asynchronously, however you must meet assessment deadlines. Students completing Prepare-Advanced have access to online academic support through their subject facilitators as well as access to all on campus university services.
You will learn:
  * Academic Literacy: academic integrity, writing and presentation skills.
  * Critical and Creative Thinking: critical thinking, reasoning and inquiry skills.
  * Foundation Mathematics: algebra, statistics and mathematical problem-solving skills.
  * Foundation Science: scientific innovations, applied numeracy and scientific thinking skills.

Subject: ACADEMIC CULTURE (EDU0AC1)
Credit Points: 5
Overview: This subject introduces students to academic culture and different forms of academic communication. Students examine the conventions of academic culture and identify strategies to support learning success. They compare the purposes of different genres of academic communication in the context of discipline differences and are introduced to the concept of academic integrity. Students develop transferable skills in time-management, note-taking, academic writing, critical reading, case study analysis and online learning. Assessments are low stakes and include text summary and written comprehension as well as text analysis.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and apply study and learning strategies in an online academic environment
- Recognise and apply the specific characteristics of academic communication and culture
- Compare different academic genres and styles and identify their requirements required for specific purposes
- Identify principles of academic integrity and relate them to the attribution of work

Subject: ACADEMIC WRITING (EDU0AC2)
Credit Points: 5
Overview: This subject enables students to develop skills in researching, planning, drafting and editing an essay for academic purposes. Students identify the conventions and purpose of academic essay writing and the development and anchoring of an academic argument. All stages of the essay writing process are supported by peer review and lecturer feedback. Assessments include an annotated bibliography, an essay plan and a final essay draft.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify, source, critically analyse, organise and reference research materials to support an opinion
- Analyse an essay question, form an opinion and develop an academic argument in response
- Recognise and apply the principles of essay writing for academic purposes
- Identify principles of academic integrity and relate them to the attribution of work

Subject: ACADEMIC PRESENTATIONS (EDU0AC3)
Credit Points: 5
Overview: This subject enables students to develop skills in researching, planning, drafting and submitting individual digital and oral presentations for academic purposes. Students identify the conventions and purpose of presentations in different discipline contexts and the development and anchoring of an academic argument on a subject of their choice All stages of the development process are supported by reflection and constructive lecturer feedback. Assessments include an annotated bibliography, an abstract and a digital presentation in an approved mode.
Learning Outcomes:
- Use appropriate strategies (including identifying relevant support services to manage concerns related to giving presentations
- Appropriately use selected technology to develop and deliver a presentation for academic purposes.
- Source, organise and correctly attribute research materials to support an opinion
- Demonstrate skills in choosing, analysing and refining a research topic for presentation in an academic context.

Subject: THINKING CRITICALLY (EDU0CT2)
Credit Points: 5
Overview: Students are introduced to critical thinking as concept and as practice. Students are encouraged to engage with differing viewpoints, question received knowledge and to recognise the difference between lower and higher order thinking. Opportunities are provided for the development, transference and application of metacognitive thinking, critical analysis and creative problem solving. Using the concept of World View, students interrogate their own identity positions and analyse how these social, environmental and biological factors impact their perspectives. Through a range of learning tasks, students develop capacity for reasoning, analysis and reflection. Assessments include a peer reviewed reflective journal and a short research essay.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate skills in reflective and critical writing for academic purposes.
- Explain and apply critical thinking concepts, relating them to metacognition and to lower and higher order thinking skills.
- Identify that World View is individually mediated by a composite of biological, social and environmental factors.
- Give and receive constructive peer feedback in the context of developing a reflective journal

Subject: CRITICAL INQUIRY (EDU0CT4)
Credit Points: 5
Overview: Students develop and enhance their critical thinking skills and critical literacy through the analysis of a diverse range of media and popular cultural texts. In the context of contemporary issues and arguments, students learn to identify a range of persuasive techniques and representational strategies and to recognise that all media is socially constructed. Students are encouraged to engage with differing view points, challenge received knowledge and to question issues of voice, identity, access and power. Through a range of learning tasks, students develop capacity for textual analysis, collaborative group work, reasoning and reflection.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain why popular culture media representations should be subject to critical analysis
- Analyse the way that media representation directly and indirectly impacts structures of power and received knowledge
- Identify and give examples of specific representational strategies and persuasive techniques
- Create a persuasive text using digital media

Subject: THINKING MATHEMATICALLY (EDU0MA1)
Credit Points: 5
Overview: This subject introduces students to a spectrum of mathematical thinking and mathematics skills required for future learning in general university programs. Students will develop conceptual knowledge and procedural skills in a range of topics including arithmetical rules and operations, percentages and decimals, rates and ratios and measurement and data. Students will be enabled to solve a variety of mathematical problems using appropriate strategies, methods and techniques and to apply mathematical concepts and knowledge to different real world situations. Assessments tasks are based on cumulative development of skills and include online quizzes and two project tasks.
Learning Outcomes:
- Recognise and apply appropriate concepts and methods to solve mathematical problems
- Explain the reasoning, arguments and strategies involved in solving problems and recognise incorrect reasoning
- Identify and mathematically solve a problem or situation
- Use whole numbers, fractions, percentages, decimals and ratios to calculate and interpret answers across a range of situations and contexts

Subject: MATHEMATICAL COMMUNICATION (EDU0ST3)
Credit Points: 5
Overview: This subject introduces students to concepts and conventions relating to mathematical expression and communication. Students will develop conceptual knowledge and procedural skills in a range of topics, including the application and interpretation of significant figures and scientific notation. Students will be enabled to solve and express a variety of mathematical problems using appropriate language, strategies, methods and techniques and to apply mathematical concepts and knowledge to different real world situations. Assessment tasks include online quizzes and a project task.
Learning Outcomes:
- Interpret and use scientific notation to express and solve problems at a foundational level
- Articulate the language of mathematics when applied through measurement
- Recognise and apply index and logarithmic laws at an introductory level.
- Apply significant figures to data and measurement

Subject: WORLD CHANGING SCIENCE (EDU0ST4)
Credit Points: 5
Overview: Students are introduced to a range of world changing ideas in science, from initial concept to gradual adoption to mainstream understanding. Key scientific advances in agriculture, medicine, technology, energy and conservation are overviewed in the context of their global impact. The ethical considerations of scientific advancement are analysed and applied to real world situations as is the reporting and communicating of scientific information. Students will undertake an inquiry investigation and write a report on a selected field of scientific research and development. Students will also maintain a digital journal detailing the development of their scientific thinking.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply a scientific approach to inquiry and investigation
- Identify world changing ideas in science and the transferability of knowledge about small ideas to larger applications and understanding
- Demonstrate familiarity with ethical considerations and applications in science
- Explain the relationships between science, technology, human society and the environment
