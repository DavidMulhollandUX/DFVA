## Master of Nursing Practice
##### M6016
##### 96 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
The Master of Nursing Practice course is a pre-registration program that, on successful completion, will allow you to apply to the Nursing and Midwifery Board of Australia to register as a Registered Nurse.

The curriculum provides you with the clinical skills, scientific and technical knowledge, and professional understanding and expertise required to practise as a Registered Nurse in contemporary Australian healthcare contexts. Teaching and learning in the Master of Nursing Practice is based on the principles of student-centred adult learning, emphasising innovation and problem solving, discovery, analysis and integration of information, as well as further developing you as a life-long learner.

Please note the Master of Nursing Practice is an entry to practice degree that offers a professional nursing qualification for individuals who have gained a non-nursing undergraduate degree. The Master of Nursing Practice is not a postgraduate degree for individuals who are already nurses and who wish to undertake a study to advance their practice or who wish to pursue a research pathway.

### Structure
The course is structured in two parts: Part A. Professional development studies and Part B. Clinical skills and practice.

Part A. Professional development studies

Part A comprises five units in which health care and nursing is studied through a social model of health. These studies include fundamental concepts of nursing within the various healthcare settings in Australia and the evolution of contemporary nursing practice. This includes ethical and legal aspects of nursing-patient relationships, communication for therapeutic relationships, evidence-based nursing care, the concept of caring and helping, and delivery of healthcare that is culturally safe and respectful of all people. The final unit in Part A incorporates theories of health leadership and the impact of leadership on quality patient outcomes. 

Part B. Clinical skills and practice

Part B comprises five units which enable the development of clinical competence for nursing practice in clinical settings. These units include the foundation knowledge and skills needed for the care of individuals across the life span, including the physical assessment of clients' health status in order to determine and deliver competent nursing care. Acute and chronic illness are covered, including pharmacology, pain management, wound assessment and collaborative care. Care and management of clients presenting with mental health issues and deteriorating clinical conditions are explored, focussing on evidence-based interventions and management strategies.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
evaluate the clinical application of evidence-based research and the ethical principles in the management of complex nursing situations
**2.**
demonstrate safe evidence-based clinical practice based on the integration of theoretical principles and practical skills
**3.**
demonstrate leadership through application of best practice principles and evidence informed nursing practices
**4.**
critically evaluate contemporary issues within Australian health care contexts
**5.**
reflect critically on your actions and on the nature of nursing practice in relation to the management of complex health situations, and practice in accordance with NMBA Registered Nurse Standards for Practice and other professional standards
**6.**
observe, measure and present clinical data and discuss the validity of the data
**7.**
discuss the physical, psychological and social changes that affect the person with health problems
**8.**
identify and utilise culturally relevant and age-specific strategies that promote independence, self esteem and dignity
**9.**
examine and implement theoretical frameworks for ethical and legal nursing practice
**10.**
demonstrate clinical decision-making and clinical reasoning skills
**11.**
demonstrate the application of science and nursing knowledge when conducting and documenting comprehensive person-centred assessments
**12.**
discuss ethical principles in the management of complex nursing situations

### Progression to further studies
Successful completion of this course may provide a pathway to the Master of Advanced Nursing (M6006) for specialist nursing or research training.

### AHEGS
The Master of Nursing Practice is a postgraduate award. The course is taught in English and normally takes 2 years of full-time study. Graduates have gained a professional nursing qualification. The course is a pre-professional program. Upon successful completion, students are eligible to apply to the Nursing and Midwifery Board of Australia to register as a Registered Nurse. Graduates have gained clinical skills and knowledge needed to deliver competent nursing care, an understanding of the ethical and legal frameworks for health practice and knowledge of the social, economic, and environmental factors that are determinants of health and of access to health care. The Master of Nursing Practice is a Level 9 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification.  The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Nursing leadership and patient safety (GHS5850)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: The Australian Registered Nurse Standards for Practice mandate that nurses should: participate in and/or lead collaborative practice; provide comprehensive, safe, quality practice; and contribute to quality improvement. In this unit you will examine nursing leadership and the safety and quality of patient care in the context of clinical nursing practice. You will critically analyse leadership theories and explore their relevance to clinical nursing leadership. You will also examine adverse events, risk management and continuous quality improvement in health care and the essential requirements for optimising patient safety and care quality.
Learning Outcomes:
- Formulate strategies to address an identified risk in health care delivery using a quality improvement process.
- Examine the evidence base for strategies to address the national health care priority areas.
- Critically analyse and apply leadership theories to nursing practice.
- Demonstrate research literacy skills in searching and critically applying evidence in clinical leadership and safety.
- Critique approaches and techniques for improving patient safety and health care quality.
- Explore and analyse the impact of nursing leadership on patient safety and care quality.
- Critically evaluate your clinical leadership readiness and related skill sets.
- Discuss how clinical risk, near misses, errors and risk assessment and incident monitoring promote a culture of patient safety and care quality.
- Critically analyse issues in nursing practice that could benefit from the quality improvement process.
- Apply risk management principles to identify, assess and report potential risks in healthcare delivery.
Assessment Tasks:
- Group presentation (40 minutes): 50% (Hurdle: None)
- Concept map and essay (3,500 words): 50% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 24 hours per week of teacher facilitated and self-directed learning including tutorials and online activities. Please refer to Moodle for tutorial types and requirements.

Subject: Indigenous health and cultural safety in healthcare practice (NUR1014)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit is informed by the Australian Government's, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Curriculum Framework and is provided via an innovative partnership between Nursing and Midwifery and Indigenous academics from the Gukwonderuk Indigenous Health Unit. Nurses and midwives make up a large part of the healthcare workforce and through their practice have a significant opportunity to make a difference in healthcare provision for Indigenous Australians. In this unit, you will have the opportunity to strengthen practical skills required to improve Indigenous health equity and cultural safety in healthcare. You will reflect on understandings of your own worldview and consider how this may impact working with diverse Indigenous Australian peoples and health equity. You undertake online learning journeys, which feature stories from Indigenous Elders and healthcare workers, videos, podcasts and interactive activities. In addition, critical reflections on real life case studies and activities to develop lifelong learning are provided.
Learning Outcomes:
- Respect: Reflect on Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples’ ways of knowing, being and doing, in the context of history, culture and diversity.
- Communication: Examine culturally appropriate, safe and sensitive communication that facilitates trust and builds respectful relationships with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples.
- Reflection: Analyse how one’s own culture and dominant cultural paradigms influence perceptions of and interactions with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples.
- Safety and Quality: Reflect and apply evidence and strength-based best practice approaches in Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander health care.
- Advocacy: Reflect on and discuss advocacy for equitable outcomes and social justice in regard to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Health.
Assessment Tasks:
- Written assessment (2,700 words): 45% (Hurdle: None)
- Group assessment (2,700 words): 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Online activities (Total 900 words): 15% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 12 hours per week of teacher facilitated and self-directed learning including tutorials and online activities. Please refer to Moodle for tutorial types and requirements.

Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit introduces you to nursing across the lifespan in a variety of healthcare settings. Founded in person and family centred care and evidence-based practice principles, the milestones and transitional periods across people's lives are explored. - Explore the socio-political and psychosocial factors that impact on people across the lifespan living with illness, chronic conditions, disability, cognitive decline, and dementia.

Subject: Contemporary nursing in context 3 (NUR5033)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: This unit examines foundations of knowledge for nursing and education for clinical practice. Knowledge generation through research, and the application and dissemination of that knowledge, are central themes of this unit. The development of a research culture as the cornerstone of professional nursing is emphasized. Research processes from various traditions are examined and their specific relevance to nursing in diverse practice settings is explored. The significance of evidence-based nursing in the context of the contemporary health care environment is highlighted. As a key feature of this unit, knowledge transfer and translation is examined in primary, acute and rehabilitative settings. Strategies for peer teaching and health promotion through teaching are developed through exploration of theoretical concepts and practical application in the classroom setting.
Learning Outcomes:
- Discuss the ethical principles underpinning the research process
- Evaluate the effectiveness of a teaching session through reflection on practice
- Recommend strategies for the dissemination of knowledge to facilitate improved health outcomes
- Critically analyse the philosophical underpinnings, methodologies and principles of research relevant to nursing practice
- Apply evidence-based educational practices to plan, design and deliver a teaching session using EBP in health care
- Identify strategies for the translation of research evidence and its application to nursing practice.
- Explain the concept of evidence-based practice (EBP) and its application to nursing practice
- Analyse the concept of knowledge generation in professional nursing through the critique of research
- Critique theories of teaching and learning
