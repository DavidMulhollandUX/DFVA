## Career Opportunities & Employment Outcomes
Graduates of the Graduate Diploma in Information Technology will have completed an industrial-scale system from initial specification through design, implementation and documentation and will have skills software engineering, information systems, discrete mathematics, basic operating systems, networking, relational databases, advanced databases, ontology and the semantic web, web information systems and service-oriented architectures.
Depending on their area of expertise, many of our graduates will have specialist knowledge in human-computer interaction,algorithms and data structures, networking, information security, artificial intelligence, and artificial minds.
Graduate Diploma in Information Technology graduates are employed in areas as diverse as electronic commerce, information systems, technology management, computer science, health informatics, geographical information systems, and biotechnology.

Subject: Operating Systems Architecture (COMP3301)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Implementation and design techniques for operating systems. Core material includes advanced kernel-level and device driver programming techniques, how operating systems principles are realised in practice, principles and practice of operating system support for distributed and real-time computing, case studies and different approaches to operating system design and implementation, including different models of software ownership.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and define key components of operating systems such as the kernel, process management, memory management, and file systems.
- Explain the role and functionality of operating systems in managing hardware and software resources and how software interacts with hardware.
- Create and apply design decisions for low-level operating systems components at the kernel and device driver level.
- Analyse the performance and trade-offs of operating system components like schedulers or file systems.
- Evaluate security features and vulnerabilities of operating systems and propose improvements or alternatives.
- Design a simple operating system module or enhance an existing module, such as a scheduler or a file system, using appropriate programming tools and environments.
Assessment Tasks:
- Programming Assignment One Hurdle In-person: 15%% (Hurdle: True)
- Programming Assignment Two Hurdle In-person: 25%% (Hurdle: True)
- Programming Assignment Three Hurdle In-person: 25%% (Hurdle: True)
- Final Exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 35%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week IT Computing 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Digital Health Software Project (COMP3820)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course will help you learn the basic fundamentals of digital health technology through hands-on experience, and through exposure to the real-world use of data and informatics in the health industry. The course includes a MOOC component that provides a broad introduction to healthcare informatics. There will also be a number of guest lecturers from industry talking about the current digital health landscape. A major part of the course is composed of a group software development project building a FHIR app for use in a clinical (or clinical education) setting, with real-world clients. FHIR (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources) is the new standard for sharing health data that is revolutionising how clinicians and patients are interacting with digital technology and has been adopted by major global IT companies (e.g., Apple, Google, Microsoft, etc.) and digital health vendors (e.g., Cerner, EPIC, etc.). Be at the forefront of healthcare application development by learning about and developing new FHIR and SMART-on-FHIR applications.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and explain key aspects of the digital health landscape.
- Apply the Software Development Life Cycle and human-centred design principles to design, develop, and document a functional digital health application using FHIR and interoperability standards.
- Analyse and address user experience challenges in the design and development of a digital health application.
- Communicate effectively with stakeholders to gather requirements and manage expectations over the course of the project.
- Demonstrate an ability to collaborate effectively within a team to achieve shared project goals, while managing individual responsibilities.
- Evaluate and justify software development processes and design decisions within the context of building digital health applications.
Assessment Tasks:
- Team FHIR Project Team or group-based: 55% (Team)% (Hurdle: False)
- Individual Course Review Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: Pass/Fail (Individual)% (Hurdle: True)
- Individual Portfolio Hurdle: 30% (Individual)% (Hurdle: True)
- Team Final Demo Team or group-based In-person: 15% (Team)% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Studio 4 Hours/ Week

Subject: Natural Language Processing (COMP4703)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The Internet has transformed much of the world's knowledge into unstructured text, and the amount of data being made available every day continues to grow exponentially. Developing new techniques to turn this data into knowledge is crucial in the age of information. Processing natural language text is both challenging and rewarding. Learning how to work with web-scale data collections is a critical skill to develop in Computer Science, and understanding the computational methods currently available to achieve scalable data processing will position students to be innovators in AI technologies in their future careers. This course will explore state-of-the-art techniques in natural language understanding and language generation. Students will develop an understanding of the key algorithms used in natural language processing, and be exposed to a diverse range of applications including machine translation, text mining, sentiment analysis, and question answering. Python will be used extensively in this course, and so students are expected to have an intermediate level of knowledge with Python programming.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe and discuss key concepts in Natural Language Processing.
- Create computational models of natural language based on the concepts learned in the course.
- Evaluate the mathematical and/or algorithmic basis of common NLP techniques.
- Apply relevant techniques and/or interface with existing python APIs.
- Create end-to-end research experiments, including evaluation of text corpora as well as presentation and interpretation of results.
- Critically analyze and evaluate state-of-the-art text processing systems and communicate criticisms clearly.
Assessment Tasks:
- Weekly Quiz Online: 10% Best 5 of 7 (2% per quiz)% (Hurdle: False)
- Assignment 1: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Assignment 2: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
