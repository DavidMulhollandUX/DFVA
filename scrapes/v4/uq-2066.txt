## Career Opportunities & Employment Outcomes
Graduates are eligible to apply for registration with the Queensland College of Teachers (QCT) as a teacher, to work as a secondary school teacher in Queensland. QCT registration could be used to apply for permission to work as a teacher in other Australian states/territories or internationally. Students who specialise in music or Language Other Than English (LOTE) may also seek employment in primary schools.
An education degree opens up a vast range of career opportunities within educational settings such as head of department, principal, guidance officer, learning support teacher, student advisor and education advisor. The development of key competencies throughout the Bachelor of Education program provides knowledge and skills sought by employers in business and industry, especially in areas where an understanding of program development and training are required.
All students are required to successfully meet the Literacy and Numeracy Test for Initial Teacher Education (LANTITE) standards prior to graduation. This pre-service teacher education program is available only as a dual program. The Bachelor of Arts (BA) provides content knowledge for two teaching areas while the BEd(Sec) provides skills and knowledge for the teaching of this content. Teaching is a challenging and rewarding career, with secondary teachers in demand globally. This dual program prepares students for secondary school teaching, or industry training.
The BEd(Sec) offers school-based learning and draws on the latest research into effective teaching, ensuring graduates are well equipped to teach. The BA provides the content knowledge for two Year 7-12 Teaching Areas or a Year 7-12 Teaching Area and a Senior Specialist Teaching area. Year 7-12 Teaching Area majors are: Ancient History/History; Chinese or Chinese Translation and Interpreting; Drama; English; French or French Advanced; Geography; German; Japanese or Japanese Advanced; Mathematics; Music; and Spanish. Majors for Senior Specialist Teaching Areas are: Economics; Film and TV Studies; Legal Education Studies; Media and Digital Cultures; and Psychology.
Note:
The final year of the program (Professional Year) commences in Semester 1 only and must be completed full-time.

Subject: Learning and Development for Educators (EDUC1650)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course will introduce students to both classic and contemporary theories of human learning and development particularly as relevant to childhood and adolescence. The processes of development across multiple systems will be examined, and students will acquire an understanding of the major influences on child development. Students will explore the implications of developmental issues for children and teachers, and teaching and learning, and consider how to apply developmental information in their practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe and explain a range of contemporary learning theories and in relation to these theories identify optimal conditions for learning. (APST 1.2)
- Reflect on the impact of physical, social and intellectual development and characteristics of students and how these may affect learning in secondary school contexts. (APST 1.1)
- Apply knowledge of contemporary learning theories to learning in secondary education settings. (APST 1.1, 1.2, 1.5, 3.3, 3.4)
- Use appropriate communication skills to present clear, coherent and independent exposition of knowledge and ideas studied in this course.
Assessment Tasks:
- Task 1: Reflective journal, starts in Week 2 - 13: 40% 2000 words% (Hurdle: False)
- Task 2: Annotated Bibliography: 20% 2000 words in total% (Hurdle: False)
- Task 3: Design for learning: 40% 1800 words% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week

Subject: A Sociological Orientation to Education (EDUC1710)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This foundational course is designed to encourage critical examination of key sociological issues within the context of education. It will explore the structures of schooling that shape access to educational outcomes. It challenges preconceived notions of schooling and education, including a consideration of appropriate learning and teaching practices for students with diverse linguistic, cultural, religious and socioeconomic backgrounds, with particular attention given to Indigenous students. The course offers the opportunity to investigate, analyse and synthesise important foundational, social, civic, legislative and ethical knowledge for pre-service teachers entering the profession in the 21st century, which has seen unprecedented levels of diversity in schools, and invites them to generate and evaluate complex ideas and concepts for their professional practice and scholarship to engage all students in their classrooms.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate an understanding of key sociological concepts in education.
- Critically examine key sociological issues within the context of education.
- Develop a reasoned argument supported by appropriate evidence.
Assessment Tasks:
- The importance of teachers seeing sociologically: Recorded video response: 40% 15 minutes% (Hurdle: False)
- Sociological investigation and position paper: 60% 2000 words% (Hurdle: False)
- Series of in-person tutorial tasks Hurdle In-person: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week

Subject: Indigenous Knowledge & Education (EDUC2090)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Indigenous knowledge and education is a course based on the principles inherent within the Indigenous worldview that proposes Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander pathways to knowledge as a necessary enhancement of teaching practice and a vital component of a balanced school curriculum. The course specifically aims to give students capacity in relation to the Australian Professional Standards for Teachers 1.4 Strategies for teaching Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander students and 2.4: Understand and respect Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples and promote reconciliation between Indigenous and non-Indigenous Australian.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate a critical understanding of theoretical underpinnings and debates, curriculum, legislative and policy frameworks, and pedagogical practices which make up the landscape of Indigenous education in Australia today.
- Demonstrate understanding of the importance of critical and continual reflection on their professional, political and personal worldview and research-informed practices as an educator in relation to how Indigenous Australian students engage in education and learning, their peoples and cultures, and their histories and languages.
- Demonstrate understanding of and reflection on Indigenous Australian education as located within broader educational frameworks, system policies, processes and contexts.
- Demonstrate understandings of evidence-based teaching and learning strategies, alongside your worldview as a teacher, in relation to working with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander students.
- Demonstrate a growing capacity to work effectively and respectfully within the Indigenous Australian education landscape.
- Demonstrate the skills and knowledge to engage effectively and work in partnership with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples and communities.
- Work effectively and collaboratively in teams to make informed team decisions, demonstrate interpersonal, leadership and communication skills, and critically evaluate personal and peer contributions to team processes.
Assessment Tasks:
- Reflective learning journal: 40% 1200 words% (Hurdle: False)
- Indigenous Education Professional Portfolio: 60% 2000 words% (Hurdle: False)
- Micro-teaching episode Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week

Subject: Teachers as Educational Innovators and Agents of Change (EDUC2604)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course will explore strategies to support your development as a future educational innovator and agent of change in classrooms and schools. Within this broader context, the enactment of innovation and change is explored within (i) the expansion of curriculum learning opportunities for and engagement of students through the implementation of teaching strategies using ICT; (ii) evaluation of teaching programs; and (iii) the improvement of teaching practice through engagement in professional learning. Preservice teachers will also consider practical implementation of safe, responsible and ethical use of ICT in learning and teaching.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate understanding of contemporary educational innovation and change.
- Demonstrate understanding of strategies to support their work as innovators and change agents in classroom and school contexts.
- Articulate ways in which innovation and change can contribute to improved teaching practice and enhanced learning opportunities for students.
- Demonstrate knowledge of teaching strategies to engage students in their learning and expand curriculum learning opportunities through appropriate selection of resources, with particular emphasis on safe, responsible and ethical use of ICT.
- Demonstrate knowledge of strategies to effectively evaluate teaching programs to improve student learning.
- Demonstrate understanding of the relevant and appropriate sources of professional learning and the rationale for continued professional learning for teachers.
- Demonstrate clear, fluent, and coherent communication skills consistent with personal, professional and academic conventions.
- Work effectively and collaboratively in teams to make informed team decisions, demonstrate interpersonal, leadership and communication skills, and critically evaluate personal and peer contributions to team processes.
Assessment Tasks:
- Assignment Task 1: Professional Development Byte Team or group-based: 30% 10-12-minute group presentation% (Hurdle: False)
- Assignment Task 2: Critical Reflection: 30% 1500-word critical reflection% (Hurdle: False)
- Assignment Task 3: Case Study: 40% 2000-word case study analysis% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Literacy and Numeracy Across the Curriculum (EDUC2630)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Literacy and numeracy are essential to students¿ success in and beyond the classroom. Confident and critical use of language and mathematical knowledge, skills and dispositions, is necessary to their personal enablement, civic participation and to social equity. By providing meaningful opportunities for literacy and numeracy learning across the curriculum, teachers and school play a significant role in students¿ development as literate and numerate citizens. In this course, pre-service teachers will discuss contemporary theories, challenges and possibilities in literacy and numeracy teaching. They will explore and reflect on the nature of literacy and numeracy, including what it means to be literate and numerate in the 21st century, and learn how these general capabilities can be taught explicitly by all teachers in diverse classrooms, using appropriate subject matter expertise and effective pedagogical practices, including professional relationships with parents/carers. They will investigate and respond to literacy and numeracy demands in the curriculum and their teaching areas, with opportunities to critically evaluate relevant teaching strategies and their own task design. This course contains a 10 day professional experience component in a school. Pre-service teachers require a valid Blue Card for the duration of the placement. Note : This course runs to the standard Semester 1 calendar for coursework, with the 10 days block professional experience commencing during Semester 1 central examinations period.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the nature of literacy and numeracy in the 21st century, related issues and opportunities for effective pedagogical practice.
- Demonstrate knowledge, understanding and analysis of explicit literacy and numeracy teaching and learning strategies and their application in their teaching areas.
- Research, select and evaluate literacy and numeracy resources, including ICTs, to effectively engage students in literacy and numeracy learning in their teaching areas.
- Demonstrate knowledge and understanding of strategies for involving parents/carers in their child’s literacy and numeracy learning and identify professional strategies to effectively engage with parents/carers sensitively and confidentially.
- Demonstrate clear, fluent and coherent communication, which is appropriately referenced and consistent with personal, professional academic conventions.
- Demonstrate and apply ethical practices appropriate for the teaching profession and an understanding of the place of legislative, school and employing authority requirements in the teaching and learning process.
- Demonstrate an understanding of schools as workplaces and be familiar with the statutory, ethical and other requirements that govern the work of teachers in schools.
- Demonstrate all teaching and professional requirements of the professional experience placement.
Assessment Tasks:
- AT1 Core content modules Hurdle Online: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- AT2 Research essay — literacy teaching and learning: 50% — 2000 words + reference list% (Hurdle: False)
- AT3 Design and presentation of a numeracy lesson plan In-person: 50% — Completed template + 5-7 min presentation; up to 2000 words + reference list/appendices% (Hurdle: False)
- AT4 — Part 1: Policies and procedures document for professional experience Hurdle Online: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- AT4 — Part 2: Professional experience placement & final evaluation report Hurdle: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week Placement 60 Hours/ One Off Students complete 60 hours (10 days/2 week block) of placement.

Subject: Supporting Student Behaviour and Wellbeing (EDUC2770)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides pre-service teachers an opportunity to explore, practise, and reflect on a range of research-informed, effective pedagogical practices and strategies for supporting student behaviour and promoting teacher and student wellbeing. Current research will be used to critique policies, frameworks, and practices used by schools to create and sustain positive learning environments. Preservice teachers will be provided with an opportunity to examine how to establish and enhance classroom climate, strategies to support student connectedness and engagement, and evidence-based and culturally responsive approaches to respond to and de-escalate student behaviour. Pre-service teachers will also be given an opportunity to explore multi-tiered systems of support including universal practices that work at the whole-school and classroom level, as well as targeted and intensive approaches that are effective for students who require additional support. Further, there will be a focus on how to build strong, ethical, equitable and respectful relationships with students, parents, and colleagues.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically appraise the evidence underpinning practices used to support student engagement, behaviour, connectedness, and wellbeing and establish and sustain safe and supportive learning environments.
- Identify and apply evidence informed approaches specifically aimed at providing multi-tiered systems of support that include social, emotional, and behavioural supports for students.
- Design safe and supportive learning environments for school students and apply approaches that teach and reinforce desired classroom behaviours.
- Analyse the relationship between function of behaviour and environment, including cultural and contextual factors.
- Synthesise research to explore links between student behaviour and wellbeing, and engagement and learning.
- Investigate practices that can prevent student disengagement and develop plans for students who are exhibiting behaviours of concern.
- Critically reflect on implications for promoting positive student wellbeing and behaviour in professional practice and productive relationships with students, families, and communities.
Assessment Tasks:
- Weekly Quizzes Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: N/A% (Hurdle: True)
- Action Plan: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
