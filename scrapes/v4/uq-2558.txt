Subject: Contemporary Australia (AUST1000)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course explores key issues and debates in understanding Australia as a nation, a culture and a society. Topics examine the forces shaping contemporary Australia and their historical context. The course is designed for both local and international students. AUST1000 is an interdisciplinary course, just as Australian Studies is an interdisciplinary field. Students will be introduced to recent developments in the fields of media/cultural studies, literature, history, politics, Indigenous studies and gender studies. A range of cultural texts including film, television, print media and advertisements will be used to develop the key concepts of the course. Employability Statement This course develops a range of skills valued by employers. You will develop knowledge, attributes, skills, and experience in the following: An understanding of historical and current political policies that have and continue to impact First Nations people, migrants, asylum seekers, and refugees. A sensitivity and respect for different, diverse, and intersectional experiences/perspectives. Knowledge of current issues and debates that circulate in contemporary Australian media. Verbal communication abilities including the capacity to discuss complex issues in group settings. Critical thinking skills and competence in questioning, analysing, interpreting, evaluating, and synthesising information. The ability to interpret, negotiate, and make meaning of information presented in different media forms. Written communication skills including proficiency in applying academic theory and research-based evidence to case studies.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate knowledge of the central issues affecting contemporary understandings of Australia as a nation
- Be able to compare, contrast, and analyse a range of different perspectives on current debates about Australian society and culture
- Be able to make links between individual identities and national identities
- Show reasonable knowledge of the historical context of current issues and recognise ways in which history is re-interpreted to meet contemporary needs
- Demonstrate an understanding of concepts and methods important to the field of Australian Studies
Assessment Tasks:
- Workshop Assessment Activities In-person: 30% 10 x 3%% (Hurdle: False)
- Focused Analysis 1 Online: 15% 500 words (+/- 10%)% (Hurdle: False)
- Focused Analysis 2 Online: 15% 500 words (+/- 10%)% (Hurdle: False)
- Digital Exhibition Concept and Rationale Online: 40% 2000 words (+/- 10%)% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Seminar 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Understanding the News (COMU1050)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: You will examine news as a global cultural product and practice across print, broadcast and online platforms. The course utilizes methods such as framing, agenda setting, content analysis and discourse analysis to analyse the structure and content of news.
Learning Outcomes:
- understand news, news values, and the various considerations that go into news production across multiple platforms;
- appreciate the importance of objectivity as one of the canons of journalism, and the constraints on professional reporting/production of news;
- understand issues affecting diversity in the newsroom;
- reflect on and understand alternative forms of journalism;
- understand how media frame and represent various groups and social movements; and
- understand and apply fundamental theories of journalism and communication.
Assessment Tasks:
- Tutorial Participation Identity Verified In-person: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Problem-Based Assessment Task: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- End-of-Semester Examination Identity Verified In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Introduction to Strategic Communication (COMU1052)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course is an introduction to public relations practice. It is designed to give students a fundamental understanding of how to research a communication problem and develop an appropriate program while considering its legal and ethical contexts.
Learning Outcomes:
- DESCRIBE the diverse nature of contemporary practices of strategic communication, the relationship of public relations practice to other public communication practices like marketing, advertisement and journalism, the role of stakeholders and publics and the communication practitioner itself in and outside of an organization (corporate, NGO, political and educational institutions etc.).
- UNDERSTAND and INTERPRET the key elements of communication theories, strategies and tactics, and, thus, the character and operationalization of a best practice communication planning framework.
- PRODUCE strategic communication practices by constructing and presenting a communication plan that is based on research and contains a situation analysis, target audiences, goals and objectives, communication strategies and tactics, and evaluation methods. Work effectively in teams to make informed team decisions and demonstrate interpersonal, leadership and communication skills.
Assessment Tasks:
- Learning Reflection: 25% 1000 words (2 reflective journal entries, 500 words each) plus appendix with weekly video takeaways% (Hurdle: False)
- Case Study Analysis: 25% 1500 words% (Hurdle: False)
- Campaign Pitch, Documents, Peer Group Evaluation Team or group-based In-person: 50% 15 minute presentation supported by 2000 word documentation and peer review% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Data and Society (COMU1130)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course is designed to explore the centrality of the internet in contemporary communication and culture. You will learn about the various technologies of the internet impact on how we organise and communicate online. You will also actively engage in critical debates relating to anonymity, surveillance, censorship, online abuse, algorithmic culture, the digital divide, and digital disruption. Practically you will learn valuable skills in relation to basic web coding, online publication, content management, and introductory media analytics.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand key technical and social elements of online media platforms
- Develop critical media literacy to analyse the way platforms generate, process, and commodify user data
- Investigate business models of online platforms and their social, economic, and democratic implications
- Develop skills and attributes of collaborative teamwork and group-based decision making.

- Essay on Platformization (Assessment 2): 35% 1800 words% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Writing for Strategic Communication (COMU1152)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Students gain hands-on skills in writing, editing, and designing effective promotional publications and creating/producing strategic materials. In this rich immersion experience, students produce work within traditional and new media environments, for a real client.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse the rapidly changing context for the composition and dissemination of strategic communication material, and how this shapes organisational reputation.
- Become familiar with using different forms of strategic communication writing, the range of strategic communication writing tools used in practice, and the adaptation of tools for cross-platform use.
- Critically assess the effectiveness of strategic communication writing with a focus on the usefulness of AI prompts and reflection.
- Undertake effective reviewing, revising and editing of strategic communication writing with and without the use of artificial intelligence tools.
- Apply strategic writing principles including clarity, conciseness, consistency, coherence, creativity and use of plain language.
- Demonstrate an ability for connecting with target audiences using strong professional writing skills for various forms of strategic communication writing.
- Learn to design and produce a range of strategic communication collaterals to suit an organisation's needs.
- Understand the ethical and legal standards of practice in the composition of strategic communication material, including through the use of AI tools.
Assessment Tasks:
- Media Release project - part 1 In-person: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Media Release project - part 2: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week Tutorial 1.5 Hours/ Week
