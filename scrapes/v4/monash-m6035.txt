## Master of Wound Care
##### M6035
##### 72 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
The course is designed to enable you to advance your knowledge and skills in the provision of appropriate care of individuals with wounds to optimise healing. You will gain the specialist skills necessary to enable the formulation, implementation and evaluation of management plans that are evidence-based. You will also be able to hone your skills in leadership, professional communication, reflective practice and innovation in wound care to improve clinical practice and patient outcomes. You can choose between a coursework stream and a more research-focused stream where you will undertake a practice-based research project.

### AQF level
Level 9 - Master's Degree (Coursework)
### Post-nominals
MWoundCare

### Structure
The course is structured in three parts: Part A. Foundation studies, Part B. Discipline studies and Part C. Applied studies.

Part A. Foundation studies

In these studies, you will gain the theoretical foundation to effectively manage individuals with wounds, utilising contemporary evidence-based wound care practices to optimise healing.

Part B. Discipline studies

In these studies, you will be able to consolidate the theoretical foundation gained in Part A. You will gain further knowledge and skills to enable you to integrate evidence-based interventions and high-level problem-solving skills into the formulation and evaluation of management plans for those with more complex wounds. Furthermore, you will develop your ability to work as part of a multidisciplinary team, improving your communication skills at patient, peer and workplace level.

 Part C. Applied studies

In these studies, you will develop more specialised wound care related skills. You will choose a pathway to suit your interests and career aspirations. You have two options:

  * a coursework pathway where you will investigate clinical risk management in relation to wound prevention, as well as consolidating your advanced clinical practice skills in wound care. This pathway will also allow you to explore elective units to suit your own interest, workplace needs or career aspirations.
  * a coursework and research pathway. Through this option, you will follow a set study plan that will help you gain specialist professional knowledge and advanced skills in research by developing and undertaking a wound-related project.

Master's entry points

Depending on prior qualifications you may receive entry level credit (a form of block credit) which determines your point of entry to the course:

  * if you are admitted at Entry Level 1 you will complete 72 credit points, comprising Parts A, B, and C
  * if you are admitted at Entry Level 2 you will complete 48 credit points, comprising Parts B and C
  * if you are admitted at Entry Level 3 you will complete 24 credit points, comprising Part C

Note: If you are eligible for credit for prior studies, you may elect not to receive the credit.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
consolidate research skills in advanced wound care to implement and/or create new knowledge that enhances patient outcomes
**2.**
contribute to the ongoing development of wound care knowledge through education, research and scholarly debate
**3.**
utilise evidence-based knowledge/practice that informs the development of safe, effective and ethical wound care in a variety of settings
**4.**
exhibit professional leadership and ethical practices in the context of wound care
**5.**
communicate complex concepts effectively to various audiences, harnessing various technologies and digital platforms
**6.**
apply critical knowledge of anatomy and pathophysiology of tissue repair in the prevention and management of wounds
**7.**
utilise clinical reasoning skills to assess the cultural and holistic needs of the person with a wound including their carers and communities
**8.**
develop holistic prevention and management strategies for a person with acute or chronic wounds as part of a multi-disciplinary team

### Progression to further studies
Successful completion of this course may provide a pathway to a graduate research degree if you have completed the coursework and research pathway.

### Entry requirements
A Monash Graduate Certificate of Wound Care (M4027) with a minimum mark of 60% in all units.

A Monash Graduate Diploma of Wound Care (M5028) with minimum marks as follows:

  * 70% in MWC5205 Integrated wound care practice.
  * 65% in PGC5101 Evidence based practice.
  * 60% in all other units.

### AHEGS
The Master of Wound Care is a master’s degree. The course is taught in English and usually takes three to five years of part-time study to complete. The course requires the successful completion of twelve units through one of two streams available, coursework or coursework and research. The curriculum is focused on developing skills in evidence based practice and the provision of appropriate care of patients with complex wounds. Graduates gain the advanced skills necessary in the formulation and implementation of management plans for patients with complex wounds and demonstrate a high level of leadership, professional communication and innovation in wound care to improve clinical practice and patient outcomes. The Master of Wound Care is a Level 9 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification.  The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.
