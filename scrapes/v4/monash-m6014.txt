### Overview
The Master of Addictive Behaviours is offered in partnership with Turning Point, an internationally renowned clinical treatment and research centre. It is delivered by a team of national and international experts from Monash University, Turning Point, and other leading universities and addiction centres both nationally and internationally.

The course provides you with a comprehensive overview of addiction science, policy, and practice. It is a multidisciplinary, modular post-graduate course that is delivered online, but which retains a strongly interactive and dynamic focus. Covering key contemporary issues, such as problem drinking and gambling, and misuse of prescribed and illicit drugs.

This course is built on a strong conceptual and theoretical foundation and covers key topics in addiction science, epidemiology, public health and policy, prevention, assessment and treatment, sociocultural factors and research methods.  The course commences with an evaluation of current models and theories of addiction, including its aetiological and public health models. It explores the effective identification, management, and treatment of individuals with addictive behaviours.  Emphasis is placed on effectively critiquing research evidence on intervention approaches to apply the strategies in practice. The course develops expertise in professional or scholarly work that can contribute to a portfolio of professional development through a research stream or a coursework stream.

The research stream will enable you to develop skills in professional research in addictive behaviours This comprises a literature review and  designing and conducting an ethics approved research study for communication to a wider professional audience.  The coursework stream will enable you to develop an in-depth and broader understanding of specialised areas of the addiction field.  This comprises co-occurring disorders in mental health and addiction and current trends in gambling, gaming, and prevailing and emerging process addictions.

This course is designed for professionals currently employed as a Registered health professional (e.g., General Practitioner, Nurse, Psychologist) or in the Alcohol and Other Drug sector (AOD), but is also suitable for graduates in psychology, criminology, nursing, medicine, and social work, and for professionals engaged in criminal justice, mental health, community health and public policy. It enables you to enhance your career development, improve your academic skills, and gain a rewarding and satisfying educational experience.

### Structure
The course is structured into three parts: Part A. Foundation studies, Part B. Core studies, Part C. Specialist studies.

Part A. Foundation studies

These studies provide you with foundational knowledge and skills to recognise and respond to substance use disorders and other addictive behaviours. You will become familiar with theories and models of addiction and examine research evidence and how this has informed contemporary policy and public health responses, including harm minimisation initiatives. Practice-relevant education on the application of screening and assessment tools for the identification of people experiencing addiction will be complemented by an introduction to evidence-based addiction treatment and support options. The studies furthermore explore current self-care and self-management strategies to support wellbeing and resilience, when working with people affected by addiction.

Part B. Core studies

The focus of these studies is to advance your knowledge and skills, emphasising critical analysis to appraise evidence and application in varied contexts, and enhancing your ability to address complex, real-world issues. You will gain an understanding of trends in substances and behavioural addictions and develop your knowledge in current and emerging treatments and therapeutic interventions.

You will learn the major historical, political, economic, and social factors that have impacted on our understanding and treatment of addiction. You will also be able to critically evaluate the empirical and theoretical literature across a range of socio-cultural perspectives of addiction. You will learn about the history of policy development and the public health measures employed around the world, including prevention and harm minimisation. 

Part C. Specialist studies

The focus of the specialist studies is professional or scholarly work that can contribute to a portfolio of professional development. You have two options: a research pathway or a coursework pathway to suit your interests, skills, and career goals.

Research pathway

In the research pathway you will further your skills required to conduct professional focused research through the review of existing research literature. You will create a research question, select an appropriate methodology, conduct the research and finalise a research protocol for institutional ethics approval. Through an interactive and collaborative process with your supervisor, you will create a research report which will communicate your research findings to a wider professional audience.

Coursework pathway

In the coursework pathway you will receive in depth and broader understanding of more specialised areas of addiction. You will learn about best-practice methods for identifying, managing, and treating complex populations experiencing co-occurring disorders in mental health and addiction. You will also engage in specialist study focussing on behavioural (process) addictions such as gambling, gaming and other emerging addictive behaviours with a focus on evidence-based screening and assessment of process addictions, available therapeutic interventions and public health responses.

### AHEGS
The Master of Addictive Behaviours is a postgraduate award. The course is taught in English and normally takes three years part-time study to complete. Graduates have demonstrated a range of critical thinking and analytical skills, and cross-cultural competence and communication skills necessary to work in the broad domain of addictive behaviours.  These include having an advanced knowledge of biopsychosocial models of addiction as well as the historical, political, economic and social factors that influence addictive behaviours in Australia and internationally; an understanding of the theoretical basis for effective identification, management and treatment of individuals with addictive behaviours; and the skills to effectively critique the research evidence on medical, psychological and social intervention approaches to addictive behaviours and apply the strategies in practice. Depending on their chosen specialties they would have demonstrated skills and knowledge in co-occurring disorders in mental health and addiction and gambling, gaming and other process addictions. The Master of Addictive Behaviours is a Level 9 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Fundamentals of addiction science and addiction policy (ASC5001)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: This unit introduces you to the concept of addiction and addictive substances, addiction theories and models, drug policy and public health responses to alcohol and other drug use. Consideration is given to identifying biological, psychological, sociocultural, and environmental factors that may contribute to the development of addiction. You will be engaging with evidence from neuroscience, genetics, psychological and sociological theories to understand how addiction can develop or be maintained. You will be introduced to Australian drug laws and policies within an international context. Prevention and harm minimisation approaches are considered regarding their impact on moderating addiction-related complications and consequences for different populations. Students completing this unit will integrate key theoretical models of addiction with public health and policy approaches to minimising harm. This unit provides the necessary foundational knowledge in addiction theory to undertake further study in this area.
Learning Outcomes:
- Engage in reflective practice to enhance own professional skills and knowledge.
- Evaluate the impact of harm minimisation approaches, policies and public health interventions.
- Differentiate the biopsychosocial model of addiction as a holistic alternative from other theories and models of addiction.
- Appraise primary, secondary, and tertiary public health prevention strategies for addiction.
- Analyse key evidence in relation to addiction theories, frameworks, models in the context of public health responses.
- Discuss the evidence base for biological, psychological social and environmental factors of addiction.
- Describe characteristics of substances and substance use, addiction, and addictive behaviours in context of classification and diagnostic criteria.
- Employ strategies that promote inclusivity and reduce stigma for individuals using substances or experiencing addiction.
Assessment Tasks:
- Case study analysis and reflection (2,000 words; reflection 500 words = total 2,500 words): 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Report (draft 1,000 words - 10%, report 3,000 words - 30% = total 4,000 words): 40% (Hurdle: None)

Subject: Sociocultural perspectives of addiction (ASC5002)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: In this unit you will explore the role played by social determinants and culture in the genesis and maintenance of problems with addiction. You will explore how a range of factors may impact on the prevalence and experiences of addiction and how these factors can intersect in such a way as to account for the higher prevalence of problems with addiction experienced by some communities including youth, GLBTIQ+ and Indigenous Australians. You will examine how different socio-cultural contexts have shaped addiction patterns across different populations from a current and historical perspective. You will consider insights and perspectives from a broad spectrum of social science disciplines and learn how different cultures perceive addiction, the social constructs surrounding it, and the stigmatisation associated with it. This will foster empathy and sensitivity towards diverse perspectives. You will appraise the role of socio-cultural factors in changing patterns and phenomenology of addictive behaviours and look at trends and shifts in addiction prevalence, as well as cultural, social, and technological influences on addictive behaviours.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and describe substance use or addiction-related challenges and experiences among different populations in context of contemporary cultural practices, identities, meanings and values.
- Examine the applicability of theoretical models of addiction from a sociocultural perspective.
- Describe how socio-cultural and ethical considerations intersect with science and policy to shape cultural norms, values and socialisation processes.
- Analyse general and specific population trends and shifts in addiction prevalence and cultural, societal and technological influences on addictive behaviours.
- Appraise the influence of socio-cultural, physical and psychological factors on initiation, maintenance and recovery from addictive behaviours.
- Communicate and reflect on the impact of language and cultural aspects on discourses using a socio-cultural lens.
- Explain effects of historical and current addiction discourses and framing of addiction through popular media, public dialogue and scientific inquiry.
- Develop evidence-informed, culturally sensitive and inclusive approaches that address unique needs and backgrounds of diverse population.
Assessment Tasks:
- Culture change project proposal: 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Case study analysis and reflection (2,500 words): 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Curated learning portfolio (learning journal): 30% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Students enrolling in ASC5002 will be expected to contact the course coordinator regularly throughout the semester and participate in online discussions with fellow students and academic staff, some of these live via zoom. Students should expect to spend around 24 hours a week on scheduled activities, directed learning and independent study. Subject: Advanced policy, prevention and public health  (ASC5003)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit considers a population-based, public health approach to responding to addiction-related harms in the community. You will learn about the social and historical context of drug policy, review and compare local and international examples of public health and policy responses for legal and illegal substance use and other addictive behaviours (e.g., gambling and other process addictions). Drawing on contemporary literature, you will learn about the social and political history of drug policy and explore local and international examples of public health strategies. You will develop understanding of how epidemiology informs public health policy and evaluate how prevention measures have been applied in a range of settings to reduce addiction-related harm. The impact of individual, community, and institutional forces on policy processes will highlight the unique complexities related to alcohol and other drugs and addiction policy implementation. You will critique the evidence for different prevention and harm minimisation and analyse how research is translated into effective policy and practice. This unit will help to prepare you for further study, research, or employment related to addiction and substance use policy and public health practice in a range of health and community setting.

- Explain how epidemiological data, public health and prevention contributes to reducing addiction-related harms in the community.
- Evaluate primary, secondary and tertiary prevention measures and other harm minimisation strategies aimed at improving public health outcomes for people who engage in substance use and other addictive behaviours.
- Critically review the evidence for effective public health policies in reducing harms attributed to legal and illegal substance use and addictive behaviours (e.g., gambling and other process addictions).
- Critically compare public health responses between different drugs and behaviours and evaluate their effectiveness.
- Synthesise ideas to effectively communicate issues and concepts relating specifically to addiction epidemiology, public health, and policy.
- Appraise local and international public health strategies, interventions, and policy and investigate how individual, community and institutional forces can influence policy.

Overview: This unit will present the latest evidence about gambling disorder, gaming disorder and other emerging behavioural addictions / disorders. - Evaluate risk factors and harms associated with gambling and gaming disorders, and other putative behavioural addictions, using both primary and secondary sources.
- Critique similarities and differences between established and putative behavioural addictions and substance use disorder, using biological, psychological and social theories of addiction.
