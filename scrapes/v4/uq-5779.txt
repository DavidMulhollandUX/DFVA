Subject: Principles of Strategic Management (MGTS7303)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course investigates the concepts and techniques of strategic analysis and implementation at the business and corporate levels. Specific concepts examined include industry and environment analysis, business strategy analysis, organisation performance analysis, capabilities analysis, gap analysis, business and corporate strategic options, strategic decision making, implementation, corporate strategic analysis, mergers and acquisitions, and strategic alliances.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate an in-depth knowledge about the dynamic nature of business and the role of strategy in generating and sustaining competitive advantage.
- Investigate and discuss the most important internal and external influences on an organisations strategy.
- Evaluate business strategies, discussing how particular courses of action may (or may not) provide competitive advantage.
- Reflect critically on the issues involved in integrating the various functional activities of a firm into a coherent strategy.
- Show an awareness of the fundamental uncertainties that strategic managers face in charting a firms course over time, articulating consequences and proposing solutions to resolve these challenges.
- Participate collaboratively and productively as a member of a team.
Assessment Tasks:
- Case Study Analysis Exam Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Business Simulation of Strategic Choice Identity Verified Team or group-based In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Reflection: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Seminar 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: Ethical and Sustainable HR Strategies (MGTS7622)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course will equip students with an understanding of the nature of ethical and sustainable human resource management (HRM). It examines the role of HR in shaping responsible organisational performance, aligning people strategies with business goals and values, and embedding practices that enhance individual well-being, equity, and inclusion. The course analyses how HR systems can balance commercial imperatives with ethical responsibility, and how these systems contribute to long-term organisational value and positive societal impact. Students will also evaluate emerging HR trends and the challenges of embedding ethical and sustainable practices in the future of work.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically evaluate principles, frameworks, and models of ethical and sustainable HRM to align with organisational strategy, values, and stakeholder expectations.
- Make evidence-based decisions on the design and implementation of HR systems and practices to achieve responsible and sustainable outcomes for organisations and their people.
- Work collaboratively with peers to analyse HR issues and present strategic, ethical, and inclusive recommendations.
- Analyse and evaluate the impact of HR systems from multiple stakeholder perspectives, including the impact on Indigenous Australian Peoples and diverse cultural contexts.
- Ethically reflect on different approaches to workforce management, with a focus on equity, diversity, inclusion, and sustainability.
Assessment Tasks:
- Executive Board Presentation Team or group-based In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Presentation Peer Assessment: Peer evaluation may impact individual marks.% (Hurdle: False)
- HR Problem-Solution Report to Executive Board Online: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Reflection on HR Practice Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Workshop 39 Hours/ One Off Intensive mode

Subject: The Data-Driven Workforce (MGTS7623)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course offers a comprehensive exploration of leveraging technology, and specifically people analytics, to optimise organisational performance. Through a blend of theoretical study and practical application, students will delve into methodologies for collecting, analysing, and interpreting HR data to drive informed decision-making. Students will discuss key HR metrics, build skills in statistical analysis and develop an understanding of tools and technologies to extract intelligence from datasets. The course also unpacks the impact of AI on workers in organisations and the ethical implications of its increased use when selecting, evaluating and managing employees. Upon completion, students will be equipped to lead strategic HR initiatives grounded in evidence-based approaches to human resource management.

Subject: Psychological Research Methodology (PSYC7010)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The aim of this course is to expand student's understanding of theory and practice of research methodology, data analysis and report writing in psychology. Topics covered include design and measurement issues, methods for analysing psychological data, procedures involved in the reporting of psychological research, and issues concerned with the interpretation of such research.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically review and apply the theoretical background of, application of, and mathematical formulation for the following statistical procedures: 1. z-test 2. Repeated measures and independent groups t-tests 3. Power 4. ANOVA 5. Correlation 6. Multiple regression 7. Non-parametric versions of each of the above
- Interpret results from statistical analyses involving multiple independent variables or predictors, and identify the implications of the results for hypotheses and research questions.
- Report and discuss the results of statistical analyses that involve multiple independent variables or predictors, following standard conventions in Psychology.
Assessment Tasks:
- LITERATURE REVIEW: 35%% (Hurdle: False)
- PRESENTATION: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- BUSINESS REPORT: 35%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: 3W

Subject: Introduction to Psychology: Understanding How People Think, Feel and Act (PSYC7401)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides an introduction to psychological science. In this course, you will learn to use psychological concepts, theories and data to understand human behaviour. Topics covered include how people learn, the fallibility of human memory, individual differences in personality and intelligence, motivation, emotion, mental health and well-being, and social and interpersonal processes. You will learn how to collect and interpret psychological data, and how to critically evaluate psychological claims.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand key concepts and principles in psychology
- Apply psychological concepts and principles to the analysis of human behaviour in real-world settings
- Evaluate and explain the validity of research claims in psychology
- Communicate psychological concepts, principles and insights to a business audience
- Critique methods of collecting and interpreting psychological data

Subject: Performance Psychology: Unlocking Human Potential (PSYC7402)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course examines how we can use psychology to help people achieve their full potential. The course examines how to develop skills and expertise in high stakes environments, how to help experts make better decisions, how to motivate people, and how to manage the impacts of stress, workload and fatigue on performance.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply key concepts and principles from performance psychology to analyse and improve performance in real-world settings.
- Critically evaluate research, including research designs used in applied contexts of performance psychology.
- Communicate evidence-based arguments and recommendations for performance improvements to a business audience.
- Apply motivational interviewing techniques and coaching frameworks in the conduct of a coaching conversation.
- Diagnose performance issues, and design and evaluate interventions to address them.
- Develop analytical skills to collect, analyse and interpret psychological data, including simple between-group comparisons such as t-tests and ANOVA.
- Work collaboratively as part of a team to use evidence-based strategies to resolve complex real-world challenges in the field of performance psychology.

Subject: Business Skills for Consultants (PSYC7405)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The aim of this course is to develop the knowledge and skills that a consultant needs to work effectively in an organisational context. Issues addressed include consulting models; consulting in different business contexts; organisational systems & functions; diagnosis, intervention & evaluation; budgeting; project management; negotiation & conflict management; strategic thinking and judgment; report writing; and presentation skills. The course is structured using an action-learning approach. Students work in teams on a series of case studies throughout the semester under the supervision and guidance of an experienced consultant. This involves assessing the needs of the client, defining the scope of the project, developing a project plan (including milestones and deliverables), developing a budget, preparing the proposal or tender, and making a pitch to the client.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand diagnostic and analysis tools that a business consultant needs to address organisational problems including appropriate scoping, process mapping and stakeholder management
- Conceptualise, design and conduct interventions and evaluations showing practical application of business consultancy skills and knowledge of psychological science through experience in practical case studies.
- Understand and demonstrate skills in business communications, contracting and evaluation to support the end to end consultant process and show professional skills
- Understand some of the key contemporary topics in business psychology
Assessment Tasks:
- Scoping report: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Group Presentation: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Case Study report: 55%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 3 Hours/ Week 3L

Subject: Principles of Entrepreneurship (TIMS7301)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Entrepreneurship is commonly regarded as the pursuit of opportunity beyond the resources apparently to hand. With this maxim in mind, we explore the theory and practice of starting then growing new ventures, where opportunities exist but the pathways to unleash promising outcomes are uncertain. Knowledge and skill development is built by identifying and developing a new business idea within the class, through adaptive execution to generate information by building and testing business hypotheses so to navigate uncertainties. These skills are then used to analyse conditions across various entrepreneurial firms. Topics include the process of entrepreneurial logic, designing business models for value capture and delivery, framing competitive strategy, determining assumptions-based finance needs, understanding different innovation types and managing growth to build stronger ventures.
Learning Outcomes:
- Articulate the foundational knowledge in the area of entrepreneurship.
- Develop, evaluate and communicate actionable business opportunities as part of a startup team.
- Create viable and innovative Business Models.
- Critically analyse knowledge creation in the startup process.
- Practice real-time entrepreneurial leadership to craft ideas and test impacts.
Assessment Tasks:
- Team Presentations | Pitch & Video Team or group-based In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Analysis | Across 4 Case Topics Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Case Study | Emerging opportunities Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Seminar 3 Hours/ Week
