Subject: Digital Storytelling (COMU7025)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Stories are powerful, and they are everywhere. And, through digital media, we have more opportunities to make, share, and consume stories than ever before. In this course, theory and practice are blended to encourage students to consider the many ways in which stories may be told and received using digital media. Students develop the skills to critically analyse digital stories through narrative and media analysis. They also develop the skills to create their own digital stories for education, entertainment, and profit.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate knowledge of relevant theories and conventions related to narrative structures.
- Critically evaluate digital narratives in multimedia forms.
- Produce short digital stories that effectively and affectively communicate intended messages.
Assessment Tasks:
- Essay: Effect, Affect, Interpretation: 30% 1500 words% (Hurdle: False)
- Digital Story Presentation and Conversation Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: True)
- Final Project: Digital Story: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=COMU7111 =====
Subject: Risk Communication (COMU7111)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The course provides students with knowledge of the principles and practice of risk communication focusing on the areas of environment and health. The course will provide an understanding of risk communication including approaches, challenges, and ethics. It will also provide guidance on how to plan risk communication and how to put the risk communication plan into action. There will be consideration of different communication contexts (e.g., face-to-face, media, and social media) and different risk contexts (e.g., crises). Students will gain practical skills that will be valuable to a wide range of professions that have to deal with risk.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand theories, challenges and principles of risk communication
- Identify the steps required to plan risk communication
- Demonstrate good written risk communication and the principles that underpin it
- Understand key issues related to different risk communication formats
- Evaluate risk communication practice
Assessment Tasks:
- Risk communication case study analysis: 35%% (Hurdle: False)
- Written risk communication: 30% 1500 words% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Exam Identity Verified In-person: 35%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Seminar 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: Communication Research Design (COMU7292)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course equips you with the knowledge and skills you will need to undertake academic research in the broad area of communication. It explores the paradigmatic assumptions and theoretical perspectives behind the many different ways of conducting research in communication, and by extension, the broad area of humanities and social sciences where communication plays a central role. We provide practical examples and guidance as to how communication research should be planned and implemented.
Learning Outcomes:
- Build a solid knowledge base of the theoretical assumptions underlying different approaches to research.
- Critically review literature relevant to your project and use it to form a conceptual framework for your project.
- Apply knowledge and skills to design a valid research project and choose appropriate methods to address your project aims and research questions.
- Evaluate cultural and ethical issues related to research processes and design strategies to properly address them.
- Use clear and concise language suitable to the conventions of academic writing in presenting your research project.
Assessment Tasks:
- Problem Statement & Research Plan: 25% 1000 words% (Hurdle: False)
- In-Class Tests (5 tests) Identity Verified In-person: 30% 6 marks x 5 tests% (Hurdle: False)
- Research Proposal: 45% 2000 words% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week 1L, 2T

Subject: Performative Communication: Presentation and Public Speaking (DRAM7400)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course will develop your public presentation and performance skills. Taught in a workshop environment, it will provide essential training in physical and vocal communication, argument structure, and pitching to an audience.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate foundational skills in vocal performance including postural alignment, breath support, resonance, and clear articulation.
- Plan, structure and deliver a presentation for a specific audience with professional integrity.
- Utilise rhetorical and expressive performative devices to enhance the impact of a presentation.
- Critically analyse, apply and reflect on the methods and theories of performative communication.
Assessment Tasks:
- Tutorial Participation In-person: 40% Written Activities (20%), Practical Activities (20%)% (Hurdle: False)
- Pitch Presentation Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Reflective Essay: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Academic English & Thesis Writing (WRIT6001)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course is for (post)graduate students who are undertaking written research assessments, projects and theses. It is open to domestic and international students. The course seeks to develop a basic understanding of academic study skills in English at the (post)graduate level. Students will explore a range of academic writing skills, such as the development of critical thinking skills, knowledge of genre and register, academic tone and language, editing and proofreading, finding and evaluating sources of information, and planning and structuring academic writing. Students will review articles relevant to their disciplinary area (e.g. Arts, Sciences) in order to understand the writing conventions of their own discipline and that of others. The main forms of assessment are completion of online activities prior to tutorials, and the completion of a final written assessment (essay, research report or case study) that requires a reflection on what has been learning during the course and how this knowledge has been applied to the final written assessment. Online course content is complemented by the weekly tutorial sessions through small group activities, class discussions, and short presentations with a small peer feedback component.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the nature of overarching issues in academic writing which often cause problems for students who use English as a second language.
- Demonstrate the ability to convey ideas and information clearly and fluently in written form through written assessment, including through the appropriate use of digital tools and technologies that support academic writing.
- Demonstrate the ability to collect, evaluate, analyse and organise information from scholarly and digital sources for use in academic writing tasks.
- Demonstrate critical thinking skills by identifying, analysing, and revising issues in academic writing, including evaluating AI-generated or AI-assisted text for accuracy, coherence, and appropriateness.
- Demonstrate knowledge of ethics and ethical standards in academic writing, especially as they pertain to conducting research.
- Demonstrate the acquisition of scholarly research and writing skills which enable students to engage critically with academic literature and contribute meaningfully to academic discussions.
- Demonstrate the ability to interact and collaborate effectively with others in academic contexts, including peer review and collaborative writing activities, to achieve shared learning and writing goals.
Assessment Tasks:
- In-class Performance: Finding and Evaluating Sources Identity Verified In-person: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Comparative Article Review Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Mini Research Paper Hurdle Identity Verified In-person Online: 30%% (Hurdle: True)
- Extend Completion and Reflection Video Hurdle Identity Verified Online: 20%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week 1 hour online content per week

Subject: Workplace Writing and Editing (WRIT7035)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Workplace Writing and Editing will give students training in skills necessary for advanced writing tasks in the workplace. It will feature modules on documenting procedures, applications and grant writing, plain English for government and the law, academic editing and style guides. Each module will be aimed at producing assessable work samples that will then be added to students' e-portfolios.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the theories, practices and contexts of workplace writing.
- Effectively plan a range of workplace documents.
- Effectively write a range of workplace documents.
- Effectively edit workplace documents.
- Understand principles and practices of team communication, and apply those ideas to communicate effectively in teams
Assessment Tasks:
- Communication skills memo: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Technical edit Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Remote collaboration project Team or group-based: 45%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Seminar 2.5 Hours/ Week
