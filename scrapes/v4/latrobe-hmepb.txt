## Master of Exercise Physiology - Bendigo
##### HMEPB
##### 180 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
Gain the expertise to become an allied health professional with the skills to create tailored exercise, lifestyle and behaviour change programs with La Trobe's accelerated Master of Exercise Physiology.

Get the clinical skills to prescribe exercise to improve the health and function of people with or at risk of developing cardiovascular, metabolic, musculoskeletal, neurological, immunological and mental health conditions. Learn how to analyse conditions, perform assessments, critically interpret outcomes, and design, deliver and monitor safe and effective clinical interventions.

By studying La Trobe's Master of Exercise Physiology, you will:

  * discover essential skills in person-centred and evidence-based clinical practice by evaluating published literature to design therapeutic treatment plans
  * fit study around your life by combining online coursework with two intensive and immersive on-campus learning weeks at Bendigo or Melbourne Campus
  * complete three extensive clinical placements with La Trobe's health partners to apply your skills in real-world settings
  * prepare for a rewarding career as a key player in this high-demand profession, crucial to addressing the rise in chronic disease and supporting our ageing population.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Reflect on published evidence and individual practices to refine and ensure health care practices are safe and respectful for people with diverse backgrounds.
**2.**
Evaluate and adapt communication strategies to enhance teamwork, interprofessional practice, and clinical outcomes.
**3.**
Evaluate, synthesise and create evidence surrounding the delivery of clinical exercise services to lead new concepts for professional practice.
**4.**
Apply relevant clinical frameworks to support the adoption and compliance with clinical exercise, guide treatment, and communicate these effectively to individuals and members of the health care team.
**5.**
Analyse individual risk, capacity and function and apply appropriate risk mitigation strategies when designing and implementing clinical exercise programs for people with health conditions and injury.
**6.**
Critically analyse health outcomes and data to design and deliver a broad range of health care services as appropriate, complying with the foundational scope of practice of an accredited exercise physiologist.

### Course features
### Work based learning (placement) requirements
Compulsory placements (Work Based Learning)
During this course, you will participate in one or more supervised Work Based Learning (WBL) placements designed to allow you to practice newly acquired skills, behaviours and knowledge while integrating theory with practice. The placements will immerse you in professional work culture, enabling you to develop and further enhance your skills. WBL provides you with the opportunity to gain valuable industry experience at one or more of our local, interstate or international industry partners or agencies. WBL placements will generally be arranged by the Office of the Provost Placements and WBL Team at La Trobe University. The number of hours undertaken can vary considerably, depending on the requirements of the course. Students may incur additional costs and it may be necessary to travel or relocate to complete a specific placement. Please refer to the individual subject entries for more information.
### Work integrated learning opportunities
Interprofessional learning, Career development learning
### Career outcomes
The demand for accredited exercise physiologists is high, and expected to increase in future years to address the rise in chronic disease and supporting our ageing population. Prepare for a rewarding career in settings such as private practice, public health organisations, hospitals, community centres, aged care and disability, workplace health and rehabilitation providers, policy and leadership. Possible roles for exercise physiologists include:

  * chronic disease prevention, treatment and management
  * injury rehabilitation
  * case management
  * health promotion
  * education and research.

### Additional costs
First Aid and/or CPR certification is required to meet the professional requirements of the degree. Further information will be provided post enrolment.
Clinical placements may incur accommodation, meals and travel costs and these will vary depending on the placement venue (occasionally interstate) and duration. Payment of these costs are the students responsibility.
Applicants will be required to hold a valid Working with Children Check (WWCC). There is no cost for a volunteer WWCC. For more information and costs refer to the Working with Children Check Victoria website.
Prior to commencing a placement, students are required to meet all of the requirements of the degree in relation to immunisation. Students must visit a medical practitioner who will complete and sign the Immunisation and Health Record form.

Depending on a students immunisation history, the overall costs for vaccination may be up to AUD$1030. Students are responsible for all costs incurred. Further information will be provided post enrolment.
Applicants will be required to hold a valid Police Check which is renewed annually. Students will have access to a reduced fee when applying via a La Trobe University provider. For more information on what a police check entails, refer to the Victoria Police website.
Prior to commencing a placement, students may be required to complete a National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS) worker screening check. For information please refer to the Services Victoria website. Further information will be provided post enrolment.

Subject: CLINICAL PRACTICE FOR EXERCISE PHYSIOLOGY (EXS4001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will advance your knowledge of movement and functional capacity. You will integrate your skills in analysing movement and functional capacity and posture to inform therapeutic exercise aiming to restore and optimise musculoskeletal and neurological function.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply and critically evaluate assessments to analyse body and movement mechanics, posture and functional capacity appropriate for use by Accredited Exercise Physiologists.
- Critically evaluate literature investigating the components of exercise prescriptions to inform therapeutic exercise treatment plans.
- Expertly interpret and communicate the outcomes of functional capacity evaluations and explain the potential contributions to treatment from various healthcare practitioners.

Subject: ASSESSMENT AND EXERCISE PRESCRIPTION FOR CARDIORESPIRATORY AND METABOLIC DYSFUNCTION (EXS4ACD)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will utilise online platforms along with face-to-face theoretical and practical sessions to analyse the pathophysiology of cardiovascular, pulmonary and metabolic conditions. Pathophysiological knowledge will be applied to perform appropriate clinical assessments and examinations of individuals, critically interpret outcomes, and design, prescribe, deliver, and monitor safe and effective clinical exercise interventions. Exercise interventions will be prescribed with knowledge and appreciation of expected clinical responses, supervised to ensure safety and monitored for adverse responses.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate, critique, and interpret assessments of cardiorespiratory and metabolic dysfunction including physical capacity with consideration of typically prescribed pharmaceutical medications.
- Critically analyse expected and adverse cardiorespiratory and metabolic outcomes from, and responses to, different types of exercise.
- Analyse and synthesise cardiovascular, pulmonary and metabolic pathophysiology as they relate to the Accredited Exercise Physiologist scope of practice.
- Reflect on clinical measures and consider outcomes to design, prescribe, deliver, and monitor safe and effective clinical exercise interventions for individuals with cardiorespiratory and metabolic dysfunction.
- Critique and interpret cardiorespiratory and metabolic testing results.

Subject: MENTAL HEALTH AND BEHAVIOUR CHANGE IN EXERCISE THERAPY (EXS5001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject will provide students with opportunities to explore and understand mental health pathologies and recognise factors that contribute to mental health conditions. Students will consider and implement the guidelines around diagnosing and managing mental health conditions from the context of an exercise physiologist and implement exercise-based therapies. The use of pharmaceutical medications and interactions with exercise will be considered, and the common physiological pathways that exercise and pharmaceuticals activate will be explored. The implementation of behaviour change frameworks will be critiqued and practiced to enhance compliance with prescribed therapies.
Learning Outcomes:
- Appraise presenting health symptoms for determining pathophysiology of mental health and guiding the health assessment
- Evaluate guidelines relating to diagnosing and managing mental health conditions in the context of providing exercise therapy
- Collaborate with health care providers and consumers to deliver patient-centred evidence-informed care for mental health conditions
- Critique behaviour change frameworks to inform clinical practice that facilitates behaviour change and therapy compliance in people with mental health conditions

Subject: EVIDENCE-BASED CLINICAL PRACTICE (EXS5ECP)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will explore the evidence base for clinical practice to treat and manage people with a range of pathologies across a series of complex case scenarios. Using available clinical frameworks and individual scopes of practice, you will evaluate published literature to design therapeutic treatment plans that include all appropriate medical and allied health disciplines. You are required to interpret clinical outcomes from complex case scenarios (including those with chronic disease and mental illness) and plan treatment that includes behaviour change techniques.
Learning Outcomes:
- Create evidence-based, person-centred health and wellness interventions, using relevant clinical frameworks, which consider psychological outlook and current behaviours.
- Critically evaluate barriers to active participation in clinical treatment along with the role of different health professionals in managing people with a range of clinical conditions.
- Differentiate clinical pathologies, interpret clinical data and design appropriate therapeutic interventions and clinically reasoned treatment plans.

Subject: PROFESSIONAL PRACTICE FOR EXERCISE PHYSIOLOGY (EXS5PEP)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will explore concepts relating to professional practice as an Accredited Exercise Physiologist (AEP). These concepts will revolve around effective communication with patients, accessing clinical information, conducting appropriate assessments and effective documentation and clinical reporting. You will also analyse different clinical frameworks and compensatory schemes to implement reasoned case management with high professional and ethical standards.
Learning Outcomes:
- Obtain and evaluate clinical data and provide appropriate clinical reasoning for proposed clinical exercise interventions for individuals with pathology consistent with those listed in the Accredited Exercise Physiologists Scope of Practice.
- Critically interpret and stratify risk factors, indicators and contra-indicators, and manage emergency situations
- Evaluate various clinical frameworks and compensation schemes that are used to guide clinical practice.
- Critique strategies (verbal and non-verbal) for communicating with patients, medical and allied health professionals.

Subject: PROFESSIONAL PRACTICUM A (EXS5PPA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will complete professional practicum within an approved health organisation. These hours will contribute to the requirements for registration as an Accredited Exercise Physiologist with Exercise and Sports Science Australia. Your professional skills, including communication skills, assessment and clinical exercise prescription skills will be developed under direct supervision from appropriately qualified and experienced clinicians working in a range of healthcare settings. You will develop your ability to be a reflective practitioner through learning activities completed while undertaking your practicum. Students will complete a pre-clinical program prior to commencing practicum.
Learning Outcomes:
- Implement a range of strategies for communicating with patients, medical and allied health professionals.
- Investigate and apply skills in exercise assessment to inform management strategies in clinical populations.
- Apply clinical exercise physiology principles that demonstrate adaptability and responsibility.
- Apply advanced theoretical knowledge of, and skills in, exercise-based interventions to implement effective healthcare delivery in clinical populations.
- Critically reflect upon theory, contemporary research and supervision feedback to improve professional practice.

Subject: RESEARCH STUDY (EXS5RES)
Credit Points: 30
Overview: In this subject, you will develop professional practice as an allied health professional by using research to inform clinical reasoning. This subject aims to develop your research skills and apply discipline-specific knowledge and techniques to address a clinically relevant question. Both individually and as part of a small group, you will work on a research project that will include: planning, project management, data collection/cleaning, data analysis, critical interpretation/evaluation and dissemination of findings. These are essential skills to support evidence-based clinical practice. You will be expected to work independently in self-directed learning.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically examine, analyse and interpret data using appropriate quantitative and/or qualitative methods to inform clinical reasoning and implications for practice.
- Disseminate and defend findings, actions and recommendations using oral and written formats.
- Synthesise and critique relevant literature to inform clinical practice or areas for further research.
- Conduct independent and collaborative scholarly work to review and evaluate the efficacy and effectiveness of clinical interventions to health and healthcare.
