Subject: Accounting for Decision Making (ACCT1101)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Introduction to the accounting environment, analysis and interpretation of financial statements and other business information for the purpose of decision making.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and explain accounting terms and concepts that are relevant to business decision making.
- Record and interpret business transactions and financial statements.
- Apply relevant and appropriate accounting knowledge and techniques in business problem-solving.
- Make and justify effective business operating, financing and investing decisions informed by relevant accounting information.
- Demonstrate management and cost accounting techniques, including the use of budgets, to support business decisions.
Assessment Tasks:
- Inspera Case Study Part 1 Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Inspera Case Study Part 2 Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- End-of-Semester Examination Identity Verified In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=BISM1201 =====
Subject: Transforming Business with Information Systems (BISM1201)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: As global competition intensifies, organisations that leverage the power of information systems thrive. This course explores the use and management of information systems within organisations, introducing key information systems components such as computer hardware and software, processes, information systems development, implementation and operation in organisations. It provides both analytical and practical experience with spreadsheets, data management, and other business applications to address real-world business problems.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the terminology and concepts that are the fundamental building blocks of current and emerging business information systems.
- Analyse and evaluate the role of business information systems in organisations.
- Utilise problem solving strategies to analyse and solve authentic business information system challenges.
Assessment Tasks:
- Information Systems Analysis Report: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Excel Workbook: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Final Exam Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=ECON1011 =====
Subject: Economics for Business (ECON1011)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course aims to set students on the path to becoming effective decision makers. It equips students with the core economic principles that are necessary to understand how decision makers make choices and how choices can be made that make the best possible use of limited available resources. Whether you are operating in the role of a consumer apportioning your income, a company manager striving to maximise profits, or a government policy-maker seeking to tackle environmental degradation, these principles are fundamental to effective decision making.
Learning Outcomes:
- Interpret, explain and utilise basic economic terminology.
- Identify and illustrate appropriate economic theory related to real-life contexts.
- Evaluate contemporary economics issues with the use of appropriate economics models.
- Communicate economic thinking in non-technical, plain language.
Assessment Tasks:
- Author, Moderate and Answer Questions via RiPPLE Online: 15% (3 x 5%)% (Hurdle: False)
- Critique a Celebrity Online: 35% (10% + 25%)% (Hurdle: False)
- Final Exam Identity Verified In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=FINM1416 =====
Subject: Introduction to Financial Management (FINM1416)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides an introduction to the fundamental principles of finance that are at the core of financial management decisions. After completing this course, students will be familiar with the financial markets available in Australia and be able to: analyse and interpret financial statement data; value financial instruments using time value of money; evaluate financing and investment (capital budgeting) alternatives; and understand the relationship between risk, return and diversification.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain different theories on the role of business and finance, and analyse financial decisions in the context of global challenges.
- Apply the concept of time-value of money to a wide range of valuation problems and financial instruments (shares, bonds), and describe how financial markets work.
- Make project investment decisions based on an understanding of capital budgeting techniques and critically evaluate the resulting analysis.
- Evaluate the risk, return, and cost of capital of an investment based on an understanding of the relationship between risk, return and the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM).
- Interact effectively with a team to discuss ideas and work towards a common outcome.
Assessment Tasks:
- Assignments Team or group-based: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- In-semester Exam Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Final Exam Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Workshop 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=LAWS1100 =====
Subject: Business Law (LAWS1100)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides an applied introduction to the Australian legal system and the laws that regulate business activities. It is designed for students in accounting, business and related fields who require a practical understanding of how the law operates in a commercial environment. Students will develop the ability to recognise common legal issues that arise in business settings, including the legal system, contract law, consumer protection, business structures and selected regulatory obligations, and understand how legal rules can be used to guide decision-making. Emphasis is placed on developing an understanding of the legal framework within which business is conducted, including key legal principles and institutions and the ability to apply that knowledge to realistic business scenarios and communicate clear, practical responses to legal problems.
Learning Outcomes:
- Interpret relevant legal materials (including legislation and case law) within an Australian business law context.
- Identify legal issues and potential risks arising in common business and commercial situations.
- Apply core legal principles (including contract, negligence and consumer law) to analyse and resolve business-related legal problems.
- Communicate legal issues using clear, structured legal methodology in plain English to a range of audiences.
- Recognise professional and ethical responsibilities in business contexts, including appropriate responses to legal risk and when to seek legal advice.
Assessment Tasks:
- Tutorial Preparation Online: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Online In-Semester Examination Online: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Problem Scenario Exercise Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- End-of-semester Examination Identity Verified In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=MGTS1301 =====
Subject: Introduction to Management (MGTS1301)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course introduces the fundamental principles of managing business organisations. You will learn about the functions of management, strategic frameworks, and responsible management in the context of diverse cultural and knowledge traditions. Through interactive activities, you will apply theory to practice, develop leadership and teamwork capabilities, and reflect on your future role as a respectful and culturally capable leader.
Learning Outcomes:
- Recall key management concepts, models, and theories.
- Work in teams to analyse real-world management problems by applying relevant theory.
- Explain how culture and context shape management practice in a global environment.
- Identify Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander business practices from a strength-based perspective.
- Propose recommendations to improve managerial decisions by considering relevant ethical standards, relationships, and stakeholders.
Assessment Tasks:
- Diary - Everest Simulation: 5%% (Hurdle: False)
- Structured Analysis of Simulation Experience: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Team-based tutorial assessment Team or group-based In-person: 20% Best 3 of 5% (Hurdle: False)
- Final Exam Identity Verified In-person: 45%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 1.5 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1.5 Hours/ Week

Subject: Organisational Behaviour (MGTS1601)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Introduction to organisational behaviour, developing understanding of employee personalities and attitudes, motivation and leadership, power, group dynamics and culture on employee attitudes and behaviour.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate understanding of organisational behaviour as a field of study, including key theories, models, and concepts.
- Apply relevant organisational behaviour theories to solve contemporary work challenges and problems.
- Design and carry out an experientially-based group workshop exploring key organisational behaviour topics and problems relevant to managers today.
- Analyse personal strengths and weaknesses within the context of a team.
- Critically analyse the strengths and weaknesses of a team and implications for team outcomes and propose solutions.
Assessment Tasks:
- Individual and Team Profile Report: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Team Workshop Team or group-based In-person: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Team Evaluation Report: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Final Examination Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Seminar 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1.5 Hours/ Week

Subject: Foundations of Marketing (MKTG1501)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Examines theories of markets and marketing, consumer behaviour, applications of behavioural science and basic marketing principles.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the scope of marketing and its role as a core organisational capability.
- Define the central role of the customer.
- Identify the key internal and external influences on an organisations overall capability.
- Explain how marketing activities create value.
- Critique and analyse marketing strategies.
