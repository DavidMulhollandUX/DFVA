## Career Opportunities & Employment Outcomes
Upon completion of the Masters of Nursing (Graduate Entry) program students are eligible for entry on the Australian Health Practitioner Register as a Registered Nurse. Graduates may find employment as a nurse in a variety of public or private healthcare settings including: medical and surgical nursing intensive care emergency coronary care perioperative care drug and alcohol nursing mental health community clinical practice (rural and metropolitan) aged care aid organisations.

Subject: Working with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples, Families and Communities (INDH7107)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Gain knowledge of the contemporary, historical, social, political, and environmental contexts relevant to working with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples, families, and communities to inform your practice as an emerging nurse or social work practitioner. Through participation in the course, you will begin to develop an insight into your own culture, values, and beliefs personally and professionally and how this may impact your practice. The purpose of this course is to develop knowledge and skills for working with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples, families, and communities in a culturally safe and responsive way. This is a core course in the Master of Nursing Studies and the Master of Social Work (Qualifying) programs. These programs are accredited respectively by the Australian Nursing and Midwifery Accreditation Council's Registered Nurse Accreditation Standards 2019 and the Australian Association of Social Workers (AASW) Social Work Education and Accreditation Standards (2020). These education and accreditation standards stipulate that accredited nursing and social work programs are required to include content that addresses the following core curriculum areas: Constructions of the professions' purpose, place, and practice Power, oppression, and exploitation The history and contribution of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples Culture, identity, and discrimination Psychosocial health and wellbeing across the lifecycle Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples' history, culture and health This content is developed, updated and delivered by, and in collaboration with, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander nurses, midwives, social workers, community leaders and Elders, consumers, carers, students and other relevant stakeholders. This course is developed based on the Nursing and Midwifery Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Health Curriculum Framework and the Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Health Curriculum Framework.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically appraise policies that have been put in place regarding decision making for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples, families, and communities.
- Critically analyse why Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples believe in the right to self-determination.
- Critically analyse and evaluate the impact of historical and contemporary policies and contexts on the health and wellbeing of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples, families, and communities.
- Develop an understanding of culturally safe, responsive and respectful practice and interpret and effectively communicate its importance in working with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples, families, and communities.
- Develop and critically evaluate strategies for effective communication and collaboration with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities to address health and social issues.
- Critically reflect on your personal and professional cultures, values and beliefs and how these may impact your practice.
Assessment Tasks:
- Quality Appraisal Tool: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Critical Reflection Essay: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Exam Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: 2L2W

Subject: Acute Care Nursing (NURS7123)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The aim of the course is to continue to develop students' foundational knowledge and skills related to professional nursing practice. The course will examine concepts related to delivering safe patient and person focused care to adults experiencing acute health care needs. The National Health Priority Areas will guide the course structure. The course builds on evidence-based practice approaches to delivering care to people with acute health care needs. There is an expectation that students continue to reflect on how their practice meets the NMBA Registered Nurse Standards for Practice. The course content will be delivered over a 13-week period, utilising flipped classroom teaching approaches in theoretical IBL sessions, online learning activities and self-directed learning.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically evaluate the role of the registered nurse and in providing safe person-centred, ethical and legal care to adults experiencing acute health care needs.
- Critically analyse and evaluate best practice models of care for supporting the management of individuals experiencing acute health care episodes.
- Analyse how cultural diversity impacts on individuals in acute care environments
- Through the development of theoretical knowledge; assess, plan, implement and evaluate a plan of care for persons experiencing acute health issues in a range of healthcare environments, using best available evidence.
Assessment Tasks:
- In-semester examination In-person Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Clinical reasoning case study: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of semester oral viva examination In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Practical 2 Hours/ Week 2L2P

Subject: Clinical Nursing Practice 1 (NURS7124)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course is the first of four clinical courses in the MN(GE) program which has the overall aim of developing students' competency in relation to the Australian National Competency Standards for the Registered Nurse. - Demonstrate safe and effective communication skills to develop initial care plans for individuals across the lifespan, ensuring culturally safe and person-centred care through application of therapeutic communication principles.
- Apply knowledge to safely and accurately administer medications.

Subject: Older Adults' Health (NURS7125)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: In this course students will explore principles related to promoting, maintaining and restoring the health of older adults using a biographical approach to care planning. The course will develop students' understanding of theoretical knowledge underpinning caring for older adults across a range of healthcare and community settings. Adopting a healthy ageing model, students will explore the physical, psychological and sociological theories of ageing. Students will develop knowledge around healthy ageing, common health issues for older adults and preventive strategies. They will examine the role of the registered nurse as a member of an interprofessional team, in supporting older adults and their significant others. Learning extends to legal and ethical principles associated with caring for older adults. Learning activities are aimed at developing and integrating students' theoretical understanding of older adults' health with the practical knowledge inherent in working with older adults and their significant others and to support students' undertaking their first clinical placement.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse physical and psychosocial theories related to ageing and examine issues related to ageism.
- Examine common health conditions and issues related to older adulthood and identify strategies to implement person-centred care across a range of healthcare settings.
- Explore and critique nursing roles and inter-professional roles and relationships within health and community care settings used by older adults.
- Demonstrate cultural responsiveness when working with older adults and older adults from Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities and culturally and linguistically diverse (CALD) backgrounds.
- Demonstrate inclusiveness and supportive care when working with older adults from lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender and intersex communities.
Assessment Tasks:
- Online Invigilated MCQ Quiz (in-class) Identity Verified In-person Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Essay: Person Centred Care Within the Context of Dementia: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Presentation: Video and PowerPoint: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Fortnight Problem Based Learning 2 Hours/ Fortnight 2L2PBL per fortnight

Subject: Managing Chronic Health Issues (NURS7126)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The aim of this course is to develop students¿ foundational knowledge and skills related to professional nursing practice and the care of individuals with chronic health issues. The course is grounded in the principles of person-centred care and considers the unique values, needs and preferences of the individual and their support systems, including families, and the registered nurse's role in providing holistic, person-centred care. Students will develop theoretical knowledge and practical skills in health promotion, prevention, and early intervention in the context of chronic health conditions. They will learn to apply the clinical reasoning cycle to assess, plan, implement and evaluate care. Throughout the course, students will explore the key importance of interprofessional collaboration and approaches that promote self-management to improve health outcomes and quality of life for individuals with chronic health conditions and their families.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and evaluate common chronic health conditions and their management in a variety of care settings.
- Apply and evaluate a population health approach to understanding chronic conditions to facilitate health promotion and prevention principles when assessing individuals.
- Critically analyse and apply evidence-based approaches to supporting individuals with a chronic health issue and their significant others.
- Critically analyse and evaluate differences between models of care in acute and non-acute healthcare environments to explore and evaluate the role of person-centred care in the context of chronic illness.
- Critically analyse the role of the registered nurse and the interprofessional team in supporting individuals with chronic conditions.
Assessment Tasks:
- Online Quiz (In-Class) Identity Verified In-person Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Oral Viva and Group Presentation Identity Verified Team or group-based In-person Online: 40% (Part A 20%, Part B 20%)% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Paediatric and Primary Health Care (NURS7133)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The aim of this course is for students to consolidate and extend their theoretical and clinical knowledge to the provision of paediatric and primary health nursing care. This course introduces students to the role of the registered nurse in providing comprehensive, evidence informed, holistic patient- and family-centred care to infants, children, adolescents, and adults across diverse paediatric and primary health care settings. It takes a broad socioecological approach to understanding the complex interplay between social and biological determinants of health, and examines the use of health promotion and disease prevention strategies to support health and healthy development across the lifespan. Students examine key principles for providing safe, effective, age-appropriate care in the paediatric and primary health care settings, including legal, ethical and cultural considerations when planning, delivering and evaluating care for children, individuals, and communities.

Subject: Therapeutics and Safety in Medicine Management (PHRM7203)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course focuses on developing understanding of basic therapeutic pharmacological principles, quality use of medications and the nurse role in safe medication practices. There is an emphasis on mechanisms of action, physiological effects, clinical indication, pharmacokinetics and adverse effects of drugs. A key component is the role of the nurse in safely administering medications.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain principles that determine the absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion of drugs.
- Critically discuss how medications administered for their clinical use act on their target(s) to produce beneficial pharmacological actions and adverse drug reactions.
- Demonstrate how the mechanisms and actions of clinical use of medications affect the cardiovascular, gastrointestinal, renal, respiratory and nervous systems when administered to patients.
- Demonstrate the application of knowledge of mechanisms of actions and clinical use of antibiotics, antifungal, antiviral, anti-inflammatory and analgesic medications guide therapeutic use when administered to patients.
- Demonstrate the legal, ethical and professional requirements associated with the quality use of medicine as part of safe nursing practice.
- Apply the principles that determine the quality use of medicines to patient therapy in healthcare and professional nursing practice including complimentary and alternative medicines.
