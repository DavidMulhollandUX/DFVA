## Master of Strategic Communications Management
##### A6030
##### 96 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
The Master of Strategic Communications Management provides you with the professional skills, specialist knowledge and key capabilities relevant to career pathways in a wide range of strategic communication related fields. This industry focused degree develops core competencies in critical areas of advocacy and persuasion, public relations and crisis communications, audience research and data analytics which can be applied to the government, corporate, not-for-profit and community sectors. Drawing on best practices locally and globally, graduates will be equipped to build effective and ethical communication strategies across local and international cultures and be responsive to a digitally integrated world. Taught by a combination of leading academics and practitioners in the field, you can also choose to develop deep knowledge in key elective fields including policy communication, health communication, communication for sustainability and environment, communication for social change and development and management, leadership and entrepreneurship.

### Structure
The course is structured in four parts: Part A. Core studies, Part B. Applied studies, Part C. Professional practice studies and Part D. Elective studies.

Part A. Core studies

These studies draw on best practices within the broad realm of strategic communications studies practice and research to further your understanding of strategic communications management both locally and globally. Studies focus on the challenges posed and how communication management can play a key role in an increasingly global and digital environment.

Part B. Applied studies

As tomorrow’s leaders, you will develop a suite of transferable professional skills to respond effectively to pressing strategic communications issues. You will learn the skills to engage necessary stakeholders to allow them to effectively formulate, influence and evaluate communication practices in a variety of professional contexts. The focus of these studies is professional or scholarly work that can contribute to a portfolio of professional development. You will have the skills to work together to analyse and, identify best practices and understand and evaluate their transferability, and develop innovative and impactful solutions with real world applications.

Part C. Professional practice studies

Designed in association with industry experts, select from this suite of professional enhancement offerings to build essential competencies for your career pathway.

Part D. Elective studies

Elective units enable you to further your knowledge of your chosen focus, or strategic communication management more broadly. 

Master's entry points

Depending on prior qualifications you may receive entry level credit (a form of block credit) which determines your point of entry to the course:

  * If you are admitted at entry level 1 you complete 96 credit points, comprising Part A, Part B, Part C and Part D.
  * If you are admitted at entry level 2 you complete 72 credit points, comprising Part A (24 credit points), Part B, Part C (6 credit points), and Part D (30 credit points).
  * If you are admitted at entry level 3 you complete 48 credit points, comprising Part A (24 credit points), Part B, and Part D (12 credit points).

Note: If you are eligible for credit for prior studies you may elect not to receive the credit and complete one the higher credit-point options.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
apply a variety of research methodologies to real world problems of communication management, and develop effective solutions within an ethical framework;
**2.**
enter professional practice equipped with industry relevant specialist skills that demonstrate intercultural and global competence in a world of constant technological and social change.
**3.**
apply advanced reading, communication and analytical skills, both written and verbal;
**4.**
demonstrate and analyse the possibilities, challenges and impact of strategic communication;
**5.**
critically interpret and evaluate secondary literature in the field of study, and the key texts and cultural products pertaining to strategic communication;

Successful completion of this course may provide a pathway to a graduate research degree if you complete the research pathway.

### AHEGS
The Master of Strategic Communications Management is a postgraduate degree. The course is taught in English and normally takes two years of full-time study or four years of part-time study or equivalent to complete. Graduates develop expert critical knowledge and skills to deal with strategic communications analysis issues and resolutions across the spectrum of public and private sector activities. The Master of Strategic Communications Management is a Level 9 Australian Qualification Framework (AQF) qualification. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Professional internship (APG5044)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: You will conduct an internship with a domestic or international host partner organisation. Partner organisations may be from a diverse range of industries and sectors, including government departments, private industry and not-for-profit organisations. While undertaking your internship, you will undertake tasks and/or contribute to a workplace project as defined by the host partner organisation. Internship tasks, such as research, may be carried out on an affiliated arrangement where physical location at the host organisation is not required. The host partner organisation will appoint a field supervisor for the project and the Unit Coordinator and/or Chief Examiner at Monash University will assess your assignments for the unit.
Learning Outcomes:
- Communicate in a clear and coherent way that is effective for the purpose and the intended audience
- Reflect on workplace experiences and craft a meaningful and insightful response to these reflections
- Apply broad discipline knowledge to find solutions to complex problems
- Show technical skill in designing, conducting and reporting on an independent research project
- Collaborate with others on a project in a workplace setting
- Exercise critical thinking and professional judgement in developing new understandings
Assessment Tasks:
- Exercise: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Presentation: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Report: 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Applied Practice Project - Analysis: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Career development task: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Applied Practice Project - Reflection: 40% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 288 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Scheduled activities may include a combination of teacher directed learning, peer directed learning and online engagement.

Subject: Gender and development (APG5045)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The unit will introduce you to the main debates, concepts and practices in the field of gender and international development. You will explore broader debates and conceptual frameworks related to the study of gender, gender equity, and gender justice, including how gender operates with other relations of power, such as race, ethnicity, class, and disabilities, that inform current development practice. Using available datasets and indicators, we will examine historical and contemporary trends in gender inequality and analyse why and how gender inequality and injustice are pervasive, albeit variegated, worldwide. We will identify approaches to reduce gender inequality and promote social inclusion in development practice. You will gain an overview of gender analysis methods, tools and frameworks that can be applied across a range of sectors. You will be given the opportunity to apply these skills to real case studies drawn from contemporary non-government organisations, government and social movements grappling with complex geopolitical and environmental realities. The unit will conclude by examining the drivers and actors involved in transformative change. You will have the opportunity to develop reflexive skills that are essential for navigating the complex terrain of working in international development.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse how individual and collective agency is exercised to advocate for and organise for change in various ways;
- identify, assess, and apply gender analysis methods, tools and frameworks commonly used in development practice;
- Develop and apply reflexive thinking that can be applied to a wide variety of professional environments.
- Develop an understanding of strategies to work with policymakers when pursuing policy or legislative change pertaining to gender equality;
- identify and explain theories and concepts on gender and gender relations as they pertain to development practice;
Assessment Tasks:
- Learning portfolio: 60% (Hurdle: None)
- Research project: 40% (Hurdle: None)
