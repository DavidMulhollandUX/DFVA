## Description
University of New South Wales’ Master of Physiotherapy and Exercise Physiology is a unique program that will fast track your career to become a Physiotherapist and an Accredited Exercise Physiologist (AEP)* and is the only program of its kind in Australia. This innovative extended masters will prepare you with the necessary knowledge, skills and attributes to successfully pursue a career that combines two complementary professions. The program has a strong emphasis on interprofessional education, evidence-based and reflective practice as well as a substantial professional practice and clinical placement component, right from the first year. You will learn from experienced and accredited physiotherapists and exercise physiologists in a wide range of clinical settings, including musculoskeletal, neurological, cardiorespiratory, intensive care, community and workplace rehabilitation and aged care.

Physiotherapists and exercise physiologists are in high demand in Australia and overseas and find employment in a wide range of clinical and community settings, working with all age groups, including in public and private hospitals, medical centres, private practices, aged care facilities, out-patient rehabilitation centres, mental health settings, schools and educational facilities, research institutes, independent living/respite centres, as well as elite/local level sporting clubs.

This program is only available as part of the integrated Bachelor of Exercise/ Master of Physiotherapy and Exercise Physiology double-degree (3896) and is not offered as a separate standalone program for direct entry.

*Pending accreditation these programs meet the criteria to become registered as a physiotherapist and exercise physiologist set by the Australian Health Practitioner Registration Agency (AHPRA) and Exercise and Sports Science Australia (ESSA).

- Justify assessments, exercise prescription and monitoring approaches while considering contraindications and considerations associated with exercise at different stages throughout the lifespan while maintaining culturally responsive and person-centred approaches
- Conduct and analyse assessments to appropriately prescribe culturally responsive, person-centred, evidence-based, tailored and progressive exercise programs for the stages across the lifespan

Subject: Neurological Clinical Practice (EXPT3175)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course is specifically designed for students enrolled in the Physiotherapy and Exercise Physiology program. It focuses on the pathology, pathophysiology, clinical signs and symptoms, and assessment of neurological function, as well as impairments, activity limitations, and participation restrictions arising from disease and trauma to the nervous system. An evidence-based and person-centred approach to neurological physiotherapy is emphasised. The course covers acute neurological disorders (such as stroke, acquired brain injury, and spinal cord injury), cerebral palsy, and neurodegenerative diseases (such as Parkinson’s disease and multiple sclerosis). You will develop competency in the assessment of motor impairments and secondary adaptations, and gain skills in planning and delivering safe and effective neurorehabilitation. The course employs a hybrid learning approach and includes simulated case scenarios, live/ pre-recorded lectures, online learning modules, and practical hands-on workshops focused on applying theoretical knowledge to clinical reasoning and practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate effective and context-appropriate interpersonal and communication skills with diverse populations, including people of different ages, genders, cultural backgrounds, faiths and neurodiversity, with particular emphasis on Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities.
- Demonstrate accurate and concise clinical documentation and a clear understanding of professional scopes of practice and referral processes in neurological rehabilitation.
- Integrate and apply knowledge of neuroanatomy, pathophysiology, commonly prescribed medications, and other determinants of health and function in people with neurological conditions to develop person-centred physiotherapy and exercise physiology interventions.
- Apply knowledge and clinical reasoning skills to conduct safe, effective, and person-centred assessments and outcome measures, identifying impairments, activity limitations, and participation restrictions.
- Apply clinical reasoning to deliver, monitor, and evaluate evidence-based physiotherapy and exercise physiology interventions, as well as self-management strategies (including physical activity), for people with neurological conditions.

Subject: Cardiorespiratory Clinical Practice (EXPT3178)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course is specifically designed for students enrolled in the Master of Physiotherapy and Exercise Physiology program and introduces common conditions that affect the cardiorespiratory and cardiovascular systems. You will integrate knowledge of cardiorespiratory and cardiovascular anatomy, physiology, pathophysiology and other determinants of health to inform safe and effective exercise testing, assessment and delivery of exercise-based interventions. You will develop competency in evaluating physiological responses and adaptations to acute and chronic exercise. You will also develop skills in appropriate planning, delivery and monitoring of safe and effective exercise-based interventions for people with cardiorespiratory and cardiovascular conditions. The International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health (ICF) model will be used to develop clinical reasoning to plan a comprehensive evidence-based and client-centred physiotherapy assessment, treatment and evaluation plan for cardiorespiratory impairments across the lifespan. Indications, mechanisms of action and adverse effects of medication commonly used in cardiorespiratory and cardiovascular conditions will also be critically reviewed.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply clinical reasoning to deliver, monitor and evaluate evidence-based physiotherapy, exercised-based interventions and self-management strategies for people with cardiorespiratory conditions.
- Integrate and apply knowledge of cardiorespiratory anatomy, physiology, pathophysiology, commonly prescribed medications and other determinants of health and function in people with cardiorespiratory conditions to develop a client-centred physiotherapy and exercise-based intervention.
- Apply safe, effective, client-centred assessments and outcome measures to identify impairments, activity limitations and participation restrictions and to monitor and evaluate the effectiveness of physiotherapy and exercise-based interventions in people with cardiorespiratory conditions.
- Demonstrate accurate and concise clinical documentation and clear understanding of professional scopes of practice and referral processes.
- Demonstrate effective stakeholder management skills that include communication strategies that is socio-culturally responsive, including with First Nations peoples.

Subject:  Leading Change in the Health Professions (HLTH3001)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course prepares future health professionals to lead change through reflective practice, evidence-based decision-making, advocacy, and quality improvement. You will develop leadership, negotiation, and advocacy skills to address challenges in healthcare delivery. Emphasis is placed on designing and evaluating improvements using recognised quality improvement tools and methodologies such as 'Plan-Do-Study-Act (PDSA)' and 'Design Thinking'. You will also explore how health professionals' actions can contribute to achieving the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals, particularly those related to health equity, access to care, and sustainable health systems. Through interdisciplinary collaboration and real-world case studies, you will learn to advocate for clients, communities, and your profession while promoting ethical, safe, and equitable care.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the role of advocacy within health professions and how effective change leadership, including through advocacy and quality improvement strategies aligned with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals, can be initiated in diverse settings and populations.

Overview: In this course, you will learn skills essential to your career and career progression such as interview skills and CV preparation. Subject: Health Research Project (HLTH5002)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this course, you will contribute to an in-depth study of a relevant health science issue. You will apply the research skills you have acquired throughout your program to: identify a specific issue that requires investigation, formulate a research question, ethically and systematically collect relevant data, analyse, interpret and discuss your findings, draw conclusions, articulate the implications of your results, and disseminate the findings to the target audience. The research project may be a negotiated student-led proposal or be a proposal offered by an academic supervisor. Projects may be in a cross-disciplinary partnership with another School or Faculty at UNSW, or with an external stakeholder. You will be expected to carry out the research project independently, with the support of academic supervisor(s). With your academic supervisor(s), you will develop a ‘contract’ which outlines the roles and expectations for each, including an agreed meeting schedule, communication methods, milestones and timeline. This activity is a hurdle task and your contract with your supervisor must be submitted at the beginning of the first of the two research terms. You will not receive feedback on this task To fulfil the research project requirements of your degree, you must enrol in this course over two terms (2 x 6UOC) to complete a 12UOC research project. The assessment tasks for your research project are spread over two terms (Term 2 and Term 3 of Year 5). You will receive an ‘EC’ (enrolment continuing) on your academic transcript at the end of your first term of HLTH5002. Once all assessment tasks have been completed and graded at the end of your second term, a numeric grade will be applied to your second term of HLTH5002. Further details on the UNSW grade definitions can be found here: https://www.student.unsw.edu.au/grade
