## Master of Global Medicines Development
##### P6006
##### 72 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
The Master of Global Medicines Development is a joint degree offered by Monash University and The University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill. This program provides you with a comprehensive understanding of how the discovery, development, and distribution of new medicines unfold on a global scale.  In the units, taught collaboratively by academics across the two institutions, you will confront pressing issues such as funding medicines development in developing countries, navigating global drug regulations, and addressing disparities in access to appropriate medicines. By engaging with academics and your peers from around the world, you will develop leadership, data and financial management skills, and the  cross-cultural understanding needed to work effectively in diverse teams. Through interdisciplinary coursework, team-based projects and a final semester placement opportunity, you will develop the expertise required to tackle pressing global challenges in the development of new medicines.

### Structure
The course is structured in three parts:  Part A: Core studies; Part B: Specified elective studies; Part C: Professional Practice

Part A. Core studies

This part of the course covers core topics that provide the foundational knowledge in pharmaceutical sciences underpinning the discovery, development and regulation of medicines in a sustainable and socially responsible context. You will also develop leadership, financial and data management, and other related skills to help you to prepare for professional placement, and to effect meaningful change in the global medicines development landscape.

Part B. Specified elective studies

In this part of the course, you will be able to select an elective unit to extend your knowledge in regulatory science, post-marketing or manufacturing and controls.

Part C. Professional practice

In this part of the course, you will undertake a professional placement in the pharmaceutical or related industries, or in a research laboratory, giving you the opportunity to apply your knowledge and skills to solve contemporary problems in medicines development of global significance in a workplace setting. To accompany your industry experience and demonstrate mastery of course learning outcomes, you will complete a capstone project, related to the work you complete on your professional placement.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
synthesise and communicate the principles and application of pharmaceutical science in the discovery, development, formulation and distribution of medicines, in a global context;
**2.**
apply methods of data science to critically analyse, synthesise and communicate data related to global medicines development;
**3.**
demonstrate mastery in a specific professional development topic, such as entrepreneurism, global leadership or global pharma management.
**4.**
demonstrate advanced and integrated understanding of a specific subdiscipline of pharmaceutical science, such as drug discovery, product formulation, clinical development or regulatory affairs;
**5.**
demonstrate skills and integrated understanding of cutting-edge technologies required to solve global problems in medicines discovery and development;
**6.**
effectively communicate knowledge, skills and ideas and work collaboratively in global team settings;
**7.**
critically analyse, reflect on and synthesise principles of environmental and economic sustainability when solving problems;
**8.**
adapt to and adopt appropriate ethical, cultural and social principles in global medicines development;

### Progression to further studies
Successful completion of this course may provide a pathway to a graduate research degree.

### AHEGS
The Master of Global Medicines Development is a joint master’s degree offered by Monash University and The University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill. The course is taught in English and normally takes 1.5 years of full-time study or 2.5 years of part time study to complete. The curriculum is focussed on developing advanced knowledge and skills in key areas of medicines  development in a global context. These include the fundamentals of pharmaceutical science that underpins drug discovery, development and regulation; application of knowledge and skills through the collection, analysis and interpretation of data; leadership and financial and data management skills required to effect meaningful change in the global medicines development landscape. The guiding principles of sustainability, social responsibility and a productive pharmaceutical sciences sector underpin all learning in this course. The Master of Global Medicines Development is a Level 9 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Global medicines development: Fundamentals of regulatory affairs (GMD5001)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This foundational pharmaceutical science unit will provide an overview of regulatory affairs with a focus on global pharmaceutical product development. You will learn about medical product regulation in major international markets including US, Europe, United Kingdom, Japan, and Australia. You will also learn about the process for obtaining and maintaining global marketing approvals along with tools and approaches used by regulatory professionals. This graduate-level unit will be taught asynchronously but will include regular touch points with unit instructors and other students.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the differences between the types and subtypes of drugs, biologics, and medical devices
- Recognize the key international authorities, organizations, and standards involved in the regulation of medical products
- Explain how regulatory professionals support post-market compliance with FDA, EMA, MHRA, PMDA, and TGA regulatory requirements.
- Explain how regulatory professionals support product development teams to bring new medical products to international markets
- Summarize critical international regulations and regulatory documents impacting medical products
- Describe the origins of laws and regulations related to medical products in the US, Europe, UK, Japan, and Australia
Assessment Tasks:
- Quizzes: 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Final asssessment: 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Discussion Board: 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Longitudinal Project: 20% (Hurdle: None)

The unit requires on average 10-12 hours of learning activities per week, including teacher directed learning, independent study and reading time, participation in online discussions, research and preparation for assignments.

- Develop and present a comprehensive business case that incorporates cost analysis, market assessment, and strategic recommendations

Subject: Overview of drug discovery and development (MPS5101)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit introduces you to the principles of modern drug discovery and development and foundational laboratory skills required for ongoing studies in pharmaceutical science. You will be assigned with colleagues to a virtual pharmaceutical company, where you will undertake a semester-long project to discover and develop a new medicine. Working with the pipeline that underpins modern medicines development, you will undertake a range of individual and team-based enquiry activities, presenting your findings in a variety of written and oral modalities. In doing this, you will be able to articulate how the interplay between the pharmaceutical sciences – operating in a sustainable, socially-responsible and ethical framework – contributes to the discovery, development, formulation, manufacture and regulated clinical use of new drugs. In parallel, you will undertake a series of practical exercises to give you a solid platform to develop laboratory-based research skills. Upon completing this unit, you will have a sound foundation of knowledge and skills on which to base further advanced studies in pharmaceutical science.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate mastery of foundational laboratory techniques relevant to pharmaceutical science.
- Demonstrate the development of communication and critical thinking skills via effective reflective practice
- Use effective written and oral communication skills to present outcomes of enquiry-based research in aspects of drug discovery and development to diverse audiences
- Demonstrate effective teamwork practices in carrying out group enquiry-based research in pharmaceutical science
- Investigate and critically analyse how modern medicines are discovered in the context of the discovery and development pipeline
- Critically evaluate the impact of sustainable and socially responsible practices in drug discovery and development
Assessment Tasks:
- Web page from enquiry-based investigation 1: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Online quizzes: 10% (Hurdle: None)
- Oral presentation from enquiry-based investigation 1: 25% (Hurdle: None)
- Written extension task from enquiry-based investigation 2: 25% (Hurdle: None)
- Learning portfolio reflection: 10% (Hurdle: None)
- Laboratory competency assessment: N/A% (Hurdle: Competency)

Subject: Drug stories: Lessons from the past and now (MPS5201)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit immerses you into real-life challenges and triumphs associated with drug discovery, using case studies of medicines from the nineteenth century through to COVID-19 vaccines. Working in groups and individually, you will develop a framework for evaluating any drug development scenario, including all stages of the development and approval pipeline, and marketing. Considerations of ethical and sustainable practice will be applied to each case. You will investigate four drug stories via information provided, combined with your own inquiry, and evaluate each case using your new framework. You will present your evaluations, including comparisons between drug stories, via oral and written presentations and debates. At the completion of the unit, you will have a deep understanding of the key challenges of drug development and a refined conceptual framework that you can use to evaluate novel examples of drug development you encounter in future.
Learning Outcomes:
- Carry out a logical and nuanced analysis of sustainability and ethical considerations involved in specific examples of drug development
- Develop communication and critical thinking skills via effective reflective practice.
- Critically investigate systemic changes in the process of drug discovery that were driven by historical examples
- Use effective oral communication to present and defend an informed evaluation of examples of drug development
- Evaluate and report on how contemporary technologies can be used to expedite the discovery of novel pharmaceutical agents for existing diseases and those of emerging importance
- Critically evaluate examples of drug discovery and development using a logical framework
- Demonstrate effective teamwork practices within the context of a group report
Assessment Tasks:
- Team report 1: 15% (Hurdle: None)
- Team report 2: 15% (Hurdle: None)
- Workshop assessment: 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Viva: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Learning portfolio reflection: 20% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 24 hours of preparation for classes Twelve 3-hour workshops 72 hours of independent study 12 hours of group work

Subject: From ideas to medicines: Investigator perspectives (MPS5202)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit takes you into the minds of leading researchers in the pharmaceutical sciences to unravel the thinking that goes into transforming an idea into a clinically useful drug. From a series of recorded and live interviews and Q&A sessions with the investigators, you will be able to interrogate research leaders on the qualities they possess and the strategies they use to help them generate ideas, test hypotheses and guide research programs whose primary aim is the discovery and development of new medicines. Working in groups and individually, you will critically examine four investigator-driven drug discovery and development examples and, from your enquiry, formulate a comprehensive dossier on the interplay of science, scientists and prevailing circumstances in pharmaceutical discovery. You will present your findings, including ethical and sustainability issues that you encounter, in a variety of written and oral modalities. Upon completing this unit, you will have a mature understanding of the human nature of pharmaceutical research which will allow you to holistically evaluate contemporary and future pharmaceutical endeavours.
Learning Outcomes:
- Use effective oral communication to present an informed evaluation of the specific examples of investigator-driven pharmaceutical research
- Critically evaluate the role of the investigator in specific examples of drug discovery and development
- Predict how the presence of contemporary technologies – had they been available at the time – might have influenced the approaches and strategies chosen by investigators to develop specific pharmaceutical products in the past
- Present a logical and nuanced analysis of how scientific, ethical and commercial considerations inform decision-making in specific examples of drug development
- Develop communication and critical thinking skills via effective reflective practice.
- Demonstrate effective teamwork practices within the context of a group report
Assessment Tasks:
- Team report 2: 25% (Hurdle: None)
- Viva: 35% (Hurdle: None)
- Workshop submissions: 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Team report 1: 10% (Hurdle: None)
- Reflection on learning: 10% (Hurdle: None)
