## Bachelor of Biomedical Science (Honours)
##### M3702
##### 48 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
This is a prestigious program of coursework and research in biomedical science for high achieving students who have completed the Bachelor of Biomedical Science or a comparable degree course. 

You will undertake research methodology training and carry out a research project in your specialist field, working closely with a supervisor who will provide you with individual guidance and academic counselling. The course offers a pathway to higher level research in biomedical science. 

There is a wide breadth of biomedical research available in the Faculty of Medicine, Nursing and Health Sciences, providing a high degree of flexibility for you to pursue your honours year in a field of your choice. 

Studies can be undertaken within one of the schools of the faculty, including:

  * Biomedical Sciences

  * Central Clinical School

  * Clinical Sciences at Monash Health

  * Primary and Allied Health Care

  * Public Health and Preventative Medicine

Studies can also be undertaken with one of the affiliated institutes such as the:

  * Australian Regenerative Medicine Institute

  * Baker IDI

  * Hudson Institute of Medical Research

### Structure
This course consists of a combination of coursework and research. In the coursework component you will develop advanced theoretical and/or technical knowledge of your area of focus within biomedical science. In the research component you will develop research methodologies appropriate to your focus and plan and execute a research project under the individual guidance of an academic or industry-based supervisor.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Demonstrate cognitive skills to identify, retrieve, interpret, critically review and synthesise the relevant literature in the field
**2.**
Apply advanced knowledge and technical skills including theoretical, experimental and computational approaches as appropriate for the field of research to demonstrate autonomy, well-developed judgement, adaptability and responsibility as a learner
**3.**
Identify a gap in knowledge or evidence and develop critical, creative and innovative solutions to address the research problem
**4.**
Apply cognitive and technical skills to a range of complex problems including developing and executing a research project and collecting, organising, analysing and interpreting data with some independence
**5.**
Demonstrate ethical, professional and personal responsibility and behaviours consistent with good research practice and professional codes of practice
**6.**
Communicate ideas and results perceptively and effectively to professional audiences in oral and written formats

### Progression to further studies
Successful completion of this course may provide a pathway to a Monash master's by coursework, and if admitted you may be eligible for credit.

Successful completion of this course may also provide a pathway to a graduate research degree.

### AHEGS
The Bachelor of Biomedical Science (Honours) is an undergraduate award. The course is taught in English. Graduates of the course have demonstrated advanced theoretical knowledge and techniques in an area of Biomedical Science and relevant research skills including the capacity to undertake a research project and to communicate their findings to a high standard. The course takes one year of full-time study and comprises a fourth year of study following the completion of the normal requirements of the Bachelor of Biomedical Science degree or equivalent. The Bachelor of Biomedical Science (Honours) is a Level 8 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification.  The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Biomedical science research project (BMS4100)
Credit Points: 36
Overview: You will undertake a supervised research project involving research of a publishable standard which forms the basis of a thesis presented at the end of the year. As part of the unit you will present seminars on your research, and are trained in advanced scientific techniques. You will take part in an oral review of the thesis report. Candidates may commence the Honours year at the beginning of either the first or second semester.
Learning Outcomes:
- Have gained insight into the breadth and diversity of the biomedical sciences.
- Have the ability to pursue higher studies in selected disciplines of biomedical science; and
- Show communication skills in both oral and written presentation to a scientific audience;
- Have the capability to perform a variety of scientific procedures and techniques that are essential to the satisfactory completion and reporting of a research project;
- Be able to execute and analyse an appropriate set of studies;
- Be able to critically review the scientific literature in their discipline;
- Understand the processes involved in the design, development and implementation of a research project;
- Have acquired a range of technical skills appropriate to their discipline;
- Be able to write up scientific work in a potentially publishable way;
- Be proficient in computer-based data acquisition, analysis, presentation, and word processing;
Assessment Tasks:
- Seminars: 10% (Hurdle: None)
- Research report: 80% (Hurdle: Threshold)
- Literature review: 10% (Hurdle: None)

Subject: Advanced studies in biomedical science (BMS4200)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: The unit aims to develop analytical abilities and communication skills, as well as provide you with advanced knowledge in specific areas of Biomedical Science. You will undertake a module of statistics relevant to scientific hypothesis testing and provide a written critique of a scientific article. You will also undertake a component of work within your selected discipline that is unrelated to your specific research component and involves some level of advanced theoretical training. You may commence at the beginning of either first or second semester.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically review the scientific literature in their discipline;
- Explain the processes involved in the design, development and implementation of a research project;
- Apply simple statistical tests.
- Recognise the breadth of statistical tests and their applicability to common research methodologies;
Assessment Tasks:
- Discipline specific assessments (Critical literature analysis-2,500 words or 15 mins of Q+As): 60% (Hurdle: None)
- Statistics and data analysis (50 MCQs and assignment-SAQs): 40% (Hurdle: None)

Subject: Biomedical science industry research project (BMS4300)
Credit Points: 36
Overview: You will undertake a supervised placement within an industry setting. You will use the industry placement to inform development of a research question within the field, and conduct research to address this question. As part of the unit you will present seminars on your research, culminating in a final research report. This placement will help you develop and consolidate your workplace skills. Candidates may only commence the Honours year at the beginning of the first semester.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply advanced knowledge and technical skills including theoretical, experimental and computational approaches as appropriate for the field of research.
- Critically review and synthesise the relevant literature in the field.
- Demonstrate autonomy, well-developed judgement, adaptability and responsibility as a learner.
- Communicate ideas and results perceptively and effectively to professional audiences in oral and written formats.
- Explain the processes involved in the design, development and implementation of a research project.
- Demonstrate ethical, professional and personal responsibility and behaviours consistent with good research practice and professional codes of practice.
- Design and analyse an appropriate set of studies.
- Articulate a gap in existing knowledge or evidence and develop critical, creative and innovative solutions to address the research problem.
Assessment Tasks:
- Seminars (15 minutes): 10% (Hurdle: None)
- Research report (7,000 words): 70% (Hurdle: None)
- Literature Review (2,000 words): 10% (Hurdle: None)
- Reflective portfolio (1,000 words): 10% (Hurdle: None)
