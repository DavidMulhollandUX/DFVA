## Graduate Diploma in Professional Accounting - Bendigo
##### BG001B
##### 120 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
This Graduate Diploma in Professional Accounting course is designed for students who don’t have an undergraduate qualification in accounting and wish to advance their knowledge in professional accounting practice. The course provides the foundational knowledge required of an aspiring accounting professional. The course comprises a mix of accounting, finance and related business studies. It will equip students with the pre-requisite knowledge to study advanced accounting topics. This is an exit award only.

### Course features
### Career outcomes
Skilled accounting professionals are required for a diverse range of roles in commercial and public-sector organisations, including:

  * Financial accountant
  * Manage accounting records, financial statements and tax returns for individuals, companies, trusts and super funds.

  * Tax accountant
  * Examine taxes owed and prepare tax returns that comply with reporting and payment requirements.

  * Financial manager
  * Provide management with financial advice and formulate business and budget plans.

  * Forensic accountant
  * Perform forensic analysis on financial data and identify potential fraud or business revenue loss.

  * Business analyst
  * Understand how businesses run and use data to solve problems and improve processes.

Subject: ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES AND PRACTICES (ACC4001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Accounting principles are critical for ensuring consistent and reliable accounting practices. This unit develops students’ understanding of fundamental accounting principles and their application to various accounting processes and systems. Students will learn how to systematically identify and analyse business transactions, prepare financial statements, and evaluate businesses financial performance using relevant accounting tools. Students will also enhance their professional judgment and data analytics skills by exposure to real-world business scenarios.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and record business transactions, and prepare financial statements using double-entry accounting principles.
- Evaluate the accounting purpose, processes and systems within the Australian or an international regulatory environment
- Critically evaluate financial performance of firms to inform decision making using relevant information.
- Exercise professional judgement to solve accounting problems from ethical, social, and regulatory perspectives, and effectively communicate relevant information to a range of stakeholders

Subject: ACCOUNTING INFORMATION SYSTEMS (ACC5001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: The proliferation of digital technologies in business is resulting in a growing need for accounting and business professionals to cultivate their proficiency in information and communications technology (ICT) and business intelligence systems. This subject develops students’ knowledge of accounting information systems and business intelligence tools to measure, record and generate accounting information for analysis and decision-making within the framework of accounting principles and procedures. Students will learn how to critically evaluate and use these tools to record, manage, report, and communicate business processes and accounting information to various stakeholders, and determine how these tools can help organisations to effectively manage internal control risks.
Learning Outcomes:
- Work effectively in a team to produce high quality work
- Develop and employ accounting software application and/or an enterprise business intelligence tool to record, manage and communicate business processes to various stakeholders.
- Critically evaluate the role, use and impact of accounting information systems and technologies on organisation’s business operations, processes, data analysis and decision making.
- Critically evaluate the role and adequacy of ICT processes and controls in identifying, managing and reporting risks in organisations and improving organisational systems, processes and communications.

Subject: ECONOMICS FOR GLOBAL LEADERS (BUA4003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Economics impacts on all aspects of business and society. Key individual, organisational and government decisions around the allocation of scarce resources are at the core of economics, and an understanding of economic concepts, frameworks and language can greatly enhance a leader’s capacity to make sound decisions and influence the decisions of those around them. This subject develops students’ knowledge of economic concepts and provides the opportunity to apply this knowledge to real world situations. Students will apply economic concepts and frameworks to current issues of concern for organisations and society. Students will learn how to evaluate and apply critical aspects of economics such as the main market forces of supply and demand; the choices producers make in relation to their costs; revenues and profit; competitive and monopoly market structures and how these affect market prices; the use of game theory to make more strategic economic decisions; measuring a nation's income and the cost of living; the drivers of economic growth and the causes of unemployment.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply economic frameworks to business and social problems for policy and decision-making.
- Construct and present logical and persuasive economic arguments and communicate concepts professionally.
- Research and apply economic concepts to predict how changes in economic conditions may impact individuals, businesses and industries within market structures.
- Critically analyse economic data to explain relationships between economic variables and their impacts on policy, business and individuals.

Subject: STATISTICS FOR BUSINESS AND FINANCE (BUA4004)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will develop basic data-analysis skills to investigate real-life problems in business and finance. You will focus on basic concepts in statistics and probability theory, methods of statistical inference, measures of linear relationship between variables including linear regression analysis. There is a strong emphasis on the application of these techniques to practical problems in business and finance using Microsoft Excel. The contents of the subject are in line with the Chartered Financial Analyst (CFA) quantitative analysis curriculum. Successful completion of this subject will lay a solid foundation for the further study of data analysis in business and finance.
Learning Outcomes:
- Ability to present work in a professional manner and to communicate effectively to a professional audience
- Use statistical principles and research skills in a collaborative team environment to solve practical problems and communicate the findings to people with various viewpoints
- Apply probability theory in business and financial decision making
- Apply and interpret concepts of descriptive statistics to real life data and interpretation of results
- Conduct estimating linear regression models and testing hypothesis to aid decision-making

Subject: PRINCIPLES OF FINANCE (BUA4005)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will be introduced to the concepts, theories, and applications of finance. You will examine the role of the financial sector within the economy at large and the role of finance in the management of organisations. A particular focus will be on how investment and funding decisions are made within organisations, including an introduction to capital budgeting and project evaluation. This will lead to an introduction to a wide range of financial markets, including equity and debt markets. The mechanics of each market are examined, and the characteristics of the instruments traded in each market are explored. Consideration is then given to the concepts of risk and return and their application to portfolio theory and asset pricing models.
Learning Outcomes:
- Formulate the risk and return of individual financial assets and portfolios and describe the Capital Asset Pricing Model and its implications when evaluating financial assets' expected return and risk.
- Identify and review the fundamental concepts of capital structure theories and explain the critical factors which impact managerial decisions in developing capital structure and dividend policy
- Identify the financial manager's critical financial decisions, discuss the financial system's primary role, and explain how interest rates are determined.
- Compute bonds and share prices using the time value of money. Evaluate and explain the capital budget decision rules and apply these rules to a firm's investment decisions.

Subject: FOUNDATIONS OF COMPANY AND COMMERCIAL LAW (LST4001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will explore some of the core principles of contract law, corporations law and the law of negligence. You will discuss various business structures including the law relating to the incorporation of companies and a strong emphasis will be placed on important company law topics such as directors' duties, member rights and remedies, financing of companies and company insolvency. You will examine the purposes of these legal frameworks and analyse the practical operation of these legal principles in the business environment. This subject adopts a skills-based learning approach through teaching the basic techniques of problem-solving and promoting an understanding of the dynamic nature of the law in business and corporations. This is a core subject accredited by CA/CPA.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify legal principles, including case law and statute, relevant to company law and commercial law, applying them appropriately to problem-solving scenarios.
- Identify the key purposes and principles of contract law, the law of negligence and corporations law, including the legal status of corporations, and explain how these purposes and principles apply to commercial transactions.
- Demonstrate advanced knowledge of the key features of the Australian legal system.
- Critically analyse the ethical frameworks for the laws relating to data protection and privacy regulation in business and corporations.
