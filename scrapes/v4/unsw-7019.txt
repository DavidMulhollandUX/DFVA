## Description
The Nura Gili Indigenous Preparatory Program (Pre-Program) is a four-week residential program for entry to Business, Education, Law, Medicine, Science / Engineering and Social Work for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people. It is a program designed for Indigenous students who do not satisfy the minimum entry requirements for admission to university studies. The course has been designed to assess your academic potential and to provide a realistic environment within which you can develop the knowledge and skills to manage your undergraduate studies effectively. On successful completion of the program students may be offered a place at UNSW in an enabling program or undergraduate degree.

Students must complete their program within the allocated timeframe for their chosen course.

## Professional Recognition & Accreditation
The program is delivered as a residential program, a total of 116 class contact hours plus an expectation of approx 30 group/individual study hours across the first 3 weeks of the program. Some  disciplines (eg. Medicine) engage the students for more than this during the program.

Student enrolments will be Commonwealth-funded and students will not have to pay Commonwealth Support charges.

Subject: Pre-Business Program (Indigenous) (ATSI7001)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: The Indigenous Pre-Business course is an intensive four-week residential course for prospective Indigenous students wanting to enrol in an undergraduate degree in the Australian Business School at UNSW. The course has been designed to assess your academic potential and to provide a realistic environment within which you can develop the knowledge and skills to manage your undergraduate studies effectively. The Pre-Business course will engage you in learning activities associated with the following business disciplines: Accounting; Business Finance; Business Law; People and Organisational Management; Economics; Information Systems and Marketing. There is a strong focus on the acquisition of the study and learning skills required to undertake Business studies at the university level. Students are also given opportunities to apply their learning through case studies, written and oral presentations and site visits. Entry into the Indigenous Pre-Business course is based on a completed application form and an interview with staff from Nura Gili and the Australian School of Business. On successful completion of the course, measured through participation, assessments and a final interview, students may then be offered a place in a Business degree at UNSW. For further information please contact Nura Gili at UNSW.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate their ability to collaborate actively and respectfully in class and group activities
- Understand how to analyse and structure arguments within various business disciplines
- Understand the different components an academic task for a variety of business disciplines
- Understand a range of fundamental concepts within a variety of business disciplines.
- Understand how to structure, write, and orally present, academic information in various business disciplines
- Understand the importance of time-management and planning skills, and self-directed learning for success in a business discipline

Subject: Pre-Law Program (Indigenous) (ATSI7002)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: The Indigenous Pre-Law course is an intensive three-week residential course for prospective Indigenous students wanting to enrol in a Bachelor of Laws at UNSW. The course has been designed to assess students’ academic potential and provide a realistic environment for them to develop the knowledge and skills to manage their undergraduate studies effectively. The course focuses on teaching students a broad range of academic skills and legal knowledge. Skills include critical thinking, reading, writing and oral presentations, so that students are well prepared to undertake tertiary studies. The course is also directed at ensuring that students are equipped with foundational knowledge about the Australian legal system that will support their learning in first year law courses. Entry into the Indigenous Pre-Law course is based on a completed application form and lodgement of supporting documentation, which is then assessed by Nura Gili and Faculty staff. On occasion, applicants may be required to attend an interview as part of this assessment process. On successful completion of the course, measured through participation, assessments and a final interview, students may then be offered a place in a Law degree at UNSW. For further information please contact Nura Gili at UNSW.
Learning Outcomes:
- Reflect on different ways law is practised in the community
- Discuss basic legal reasoning and problem-solving techniques relevant to first-year law studies.
- Use basic tools to locate legal materials to support tertiary law studies
- Describe the origins, structures, and functions of the Australian legal system, including its historical and ongoing impact on Indigenous peoples and communities.
- Apply critical thinking, time-management skills, and communication skills in both academic and legal contexts to support success in future tertiary law studies.
- Use client interview skills to prepare and deliver submissions in a legal advocacy setting

Subject: Pre-Medicine and Health Program (Indigenous) (ATSI7003)

Overview: The Pre-Medicine and Health Program (PMHP) is an intensive academic program that runs for three weeks at the University of New South Wales in Sydney. The program will introduce you to some of the content covered in first year Medicine and Health Faculty undergraduate courses in Dietetics, Exercise Physiology, Medicine, Pharmacy, Physiotherapy and Optometry (Vision Science). - Explain the importance of interprofessional communication for effective multidisciplinary healthcare, in the context of First Peoples primary health care.
- Apply reflective skills to explore how your cultural, lived experiences and personal attributes will influence your professional identity and future practice as a health professional.
- Identify and describe the fundamentals of human structure and function in health and disease.
- Demonstrate effective academic written and oral communication and research skills.

Subject: Pre-Science and Engineering Program (Indigenous) (ATSI7007)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: The Indigenous Pre-Science/Engineering course is an intensive four-week residential course for prospective Indigenous students wanting to enrol in an undergraduate degree in the faculties of Science or Engineering at UNSW. The course has been designed to assess your academic potential and to provide a realistic environment within which you can develop the knowledge and skills to manage your undergraduate studies effectively. The Pre-Science/Engineering course will engage you in learning activities associated with the following disciplines: Mathematics, Chemistry, Biology, Psychology, Sustainable Engineering, Computer Science. There is a strong focus on the acquisition of the study and learning skills required to undertake studies at the university level. Students are also given opportunities to apply their learning through fieldwork, written and oral presentations. Entry into the Indigenous Pre-Science/Engineering course would require students to apply through Nura Gili. Students would have to: Attach documentation to confirm their Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander descent as part of the application process. Lodge an application with the Universities Admissions Centre (UAC) for the UNSW undergraduate degree program that they wish to study Complete the online Nura Gili, UNSW Indigenous Preparatory Programs application and attach all supporting documentation. On successful completion of the course, measured through participation, assessments and a final interview, students may then be offered a place in a relevant degree at UNSW. For further information please contact Nura Gili at UNSW.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop critical thinking skills around key concepts, challenges and developments in Science and/or Engineering
- Begin to work independently and in teams to explore their understanding of the fundamentals of biology, chemistry, psychology, sustainable engineering and computer science
- Verbally present information to peers and provide critical feedback to peers
- Generate questions for research and discussion
- Grow in their capacity to learn new and complex information and skills and reproduce it in a variety of formats
- Have developed lab skills and be able to produce a lab report
- Begin to develop skills in reflective writing describing accurate and neutral descriptions of own behaviours, strengths and weaknesses, emotions, and intentions.
- Develop further skills in core competency areas such as Mathematics
- Begin to identify questions and learning needs arising from lectures, tutorials and other teaching activities.
- Have developed skills in academic writing and information searching strategies
- Have an understanding and an awareness of individual skills and knowledge required to study Science and/or Engineering
