## Description
The fully online Master of Project Management is designed for professionals seeking to lead complex projects, programs and portfolios across a wide range of industries. Whether you are building on prior study or advancing your career, this program delivers comprehensive, practical skills for strategic project leadership.

Through a combination of core and elective courses, the program emphasises real-world application, preparing you to manage change, integrate ESG principles, and harness digital transformation. You will develop advanced capabilities in governance, stakeholder engagement and performance optimisation, while strengthening leadership and communication skills to guide teams and influence outcomes. The program equips you to align projects with broader organisational and industry objectives.

## Program Learning Outcomes (CILOs)
On successful completion of this program, students will be able to:

4. Demonstrate leadership, communication and collaboration skills to effectively manage diverse teams, engage stakeholders and align projects with strategic business objectives.
3. Identify, evaluate and mitigate project risks by applying governance frameworks, legal requirements and contract management principles to ensure compliance and accountability.
2. Critically analyse and apply best practices in scope, schedule, budget, quality and resource management to optimise project performance and organisational outcomes.
5. Adapt and integrate project management approaches in specialised contexts by leveraging emerging technologies, ESG principles and innovative practices to solve complex project challenges.
1. Apply advanced project management principles, methodologies and tools to plan, execute and evaluate complex projects across diverse industries.

## Admission Requirements
**Limitations on Recognition of Prior Learning**:
If students have completed a degree or specialisation in Project Management or a related discipline, they may be eligible to gain up to 24 UOC (equivalent to four courses) of RPL (advanced standing) of non-specific credit, if approved, and provided the eligible courses were undertaken within the last ten years in accordance with the UNSW RPL requirements.

**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
Must have completed a Bachelor degree or higher-level qualification in a cognate discipline.

Project Management cognate disciplines may include Management, Business, Commerce, Engineering, Information Technology, Construction Management, Architecture, Logistics and Supply Chain Management, Science, Social Sciences, Health Administration, Policy and Public Administration, Leadership and Organisational Development, Finance and Accounting, Environmental Studies, and Security and Risk Management, and other related fields.

Subject: Financial Management (ZZBU6002)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course introduces core concepts and principles associated with the financial management of organisations. Drawing on financial and management accounting and aspects of corporate finance, this course introduces students to core business skills including the ability to read and interpret financial statements, an understanding of sources of revenue and drivers of costs as well as insights into the ways that organisations are financed and how this impacts the business. Students will also be introduced to principles of extended reporting, such as integrated reporting, that seek to provide insights into the financial performance of organisations but also their social impact. Students will have the opportunity to apply the concepts covered in this course to real world examples and will gain an appreciation of how these concepts can be used to help shape business decisions. By the end of the course, students will feel confident in using accounting and financial information.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the contents of both internal and external financial reports.
- Research the financial performance and position of reporting entities.
- Communicate financial information to interested stakeholders in an ethical and professional manner.
- Apply analytical tools, techniques and frameworks to evaluate and critically examine the contents of both internal and external financial reports.
- Explain the objectives, concepts and relationships that underpin both internal and external financial reports.

Subject: Project Risk Management (ZZCA8301)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Effective risk management is critical to the success of projects and organisations in an increasingly complex and uncertain global environment. This course provides students with a foundational understanding of risk management principles, frameworks, and best practices, with a focus on their application in project management and enterprise environments. The course discusses the complex nature of the words 'uncertainty' and 'risk', and how their many definitions and interpretations impact risk management. The course covers aspects of risk management including why individuals and organisations must manage risks; the identification, classification and measurement of risks; the fundamental principles and frameworks for risk management; the tools and techniques used in the risk management process; the important role of risk culture and communication in managing enterprise risks; and current issues in risk management.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically evaluate a case study application of project risk management theories, frameworks, and standards.
- Conduct advanced qualitative and quantitative risk analyses and design integrated risk response strategies.
- Explain and evaluate the impact of risks on individuals and organisations, and evaluate strategies to measure and treat them effectively.
- Evaluate the effectiveness of risk governance, risk appetite frameworks, and senior-level governance oversight in aligning risk management with organisational objectives.

Subject: Corporate Governance and Leadership (ZZLJ8211)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course explores the primary legislative rules and legal principles that regulate the operation and governance of corporations, focusing on the legal responsibilities of leadership roles. Students will also be guided to develop legal reasoning, and in the application of legal principles to fact scenarios. Topics include foundational principles such as legal personhood and limited liability, the rights of shareholders and other stakeholders, decision-making, the appointment and removal of office holders, duties and remedies. Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse key principles of corporations law and governance.
- Evaluate the duties of directors and other leaders and the availability of remedies.
- Interrogate the regulatory and social responsibilities of corporations.

Subject: Commercial Contract Law (ZZLJ8212)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Contracts are an essential part of the daily conduct of business. In this course students will examine key elements of contract law and practice, including how and when contracts are formed, terms (express and implied), performance, what happens when contracts are breached, how they can be terminated, and remedies. Focusing on agreements in a commercial context, this course also explores the relationship between regulation and contractual freedom, and the interplay between contract law and related forms of legal obligation. Through exposure to case law and legislation, students will develop an understanding of sources of law and how to interpret primary legal materials. Students will also be guided to develop legal reasoning, and in the application of legal principles to fact scenarios. The course will be delivered online through asynchronous modules, online forums and synchronous online classes.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate the regulation of contracts in the context of business law.
- Analyse the key principles of contract law as they relate to the conduct of business.
- Identify the availability of remedies in statute, common law and equity.
- Apply key principles of contract law to hypothetical fact scenarios.
