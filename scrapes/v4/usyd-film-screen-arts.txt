The Master of Film and Screen Arts (MFSA) offers you a hands-on education in contemporary moving image production by teaching you how to develop a film project from concept to screen. It is ideal for professionals pursuing a career in the film, screen arts and digital media sectors, and for anyone wishing to engage with contemporary filmmaking and interactive media.

The course combines traditional and experimental aspects of film creation and production with new directions in the cinematic arts, incorporating emerging technologies in interactivity, three-dimensional imaging and virtual production.
The course structure requires you to complete a cohesive sequence of core units of study, supplemented by a range of selective units and your choice of an optional elective unit of study from a list of complementary fields of study. The capstone unit supports you to produce and disseminate a Final Project graduating film work, and unites the major themes of the course.

2 Attendance pattern
The attendance pattern for this course is full time or part time according to candidate choice.
3 Masters type
The masters degree in these resolutions is a professional masters course, as defined by the Coursework Policy.
4 Admission to candidature
(1)  Available places will be offered to qualified applicants in the order in which complete applications are received, according to the following admissions criteria.
(2)  Admission to the Master of Film and Screen Arts requires:
(a) a bachelors degree or higher award in visual arts, from the University of Sydney, or a qualification deemed by the faculty to be equivalent; and
(b) (i) the presentation of a portfolio of creative work demonstrating the ability to undertake advanced art practice and coursework,
(ii) that includes the presentation of a 1-2 minute video introduction.
(3)  In exceptional circumstances the Dean may admit applicants without these qualifications who, in the opinion of the faculty, have qualifications and evidence of experience and achievement sufficient to successfully undertake the award.
5 Requirements for award
(1)  To qualify for the award of the Master of Film and Screen Arts a candidate must complete 72 credit points, including:
(a) 30 credit points of core units of study; and
(b) a minimum of 24 and a maximum of 30 credit points of selective units of study; and
(c) 12 credit points of capstone units of study;
(d) a maximum of 6 credit points of elective units of study.
(2)  Candidates may apply to the course coordinator to substitute six credit points of electives with any other postgraduate coursework unit of study in the University. Permission may also be required from the coordinator of the chosen unit.
6 Transitional provisions
(1)  These resolutions apply to students who commenced their candidature after 1 January 2026.
(2) Students who commenced their candidature prior to 1 January 2026 will complete the requirements in accordance with the resolutions in force at the time of their commencement.

Subject: Chinese Media Studies in Global Contexts (MECO6929)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit introduces students to the systems, regulation and uses of Chinese media. Designed for all students, with or without knowledge of the Chinese language, it draws on theories and concepts of global media studies for understanding situations in mainland China, Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Chinese communities in other countries. Implications of media uses by the Chinese authorities, professionals, and ordinary citizens, in the context of globalized communication, are discussed with regard to a range of media, including the Internet.
Learning Outcomes:
- LO1 . understand the media systems, cultures and trends in mainland China
- LO2 . critically reflect on the influence of global and regional media on mainland China
- LO3 . appreciate how media in mainland China influences media beyond its border
- LO4 . think from a historical perspective about contemporary media systems and trends in China
- LO5 . recognise mediation processes in cultural globalisation
- LO6 . understand the changes in a chosen medium in China
- LO7 . connect personal media experiences with an informed understanding of media flow.

Subject: Digital Effects for Film and Video (MFDI9303)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit is designed to equip you with a conceptual understanding of, and technical expertise in, the use of digital effects for film and video projects. You will learn how to use software programs such as Adobe After Effects at an intermediate to advanced level, in order to understand how moving images can be transformed in combination with text, masks, animation, filters, effects and sound. Through a series of tutorials, screenings and practical studio tasks, you will develop and refine your After Effects authoring skills, culminating in the production of a studio project.
Learning Outcomes:
- LO1 . Conceive, plan and realise 2 short video works that demonstrates competency in compositing and engages with the contemporary media culture using Adobe After Effects and the Adobe Suite
- LO2 . Critically analyse, conceptualise and problem solve through the production process of a digital composited moving image work
- LO3 . Demonstrate appropriate and proficient use of Adobe after effects to realise the moving image project
- LO4 . Critically evaluate aesthetics, information & narratives in an ethical and culturally sensitive manner
- LO5 . Critically understand a range of different approaches to moving image practice and engage with related theoretical and cultural debates.

Subject: Hollywood vs. Netflix (USSC6919)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit examines the Hollywood studio film before and after the Internet. It studies Hollywood's power as a cultural symbol, its relationship to film production, cultures of reception, and digital distribution. This will include not only the analysis of feature films but also the ideas of filmmakers, theorists and media scholars. Questions of technology, authorship and audience will be a focus of the unit.
Learning Outcomes:
- LO1 . To analyse the impact of streaming media on studio film production and the cultural influence of Hollywood;
- LO2 . To critically assess key films, theories and discourses informing the evolution of American cinema in the twentieth century and twenty-first century;
- LO3 . To interrogate filmic narratives and myths foundational to Hollywood film production and spectatorship;
- LO4 . To engage Hollywood and independent American cinema as an emergent cultural, technological and political phenomenon
