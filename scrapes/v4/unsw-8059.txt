## Description
The Master of Mine Geotechnical Engineering is offered as a specialist postgraduate coursework qualification for individuals who currently, or wish in the future to, work within the mining industry with particular responsibilities in the field of ground /strata control. The program has been designed to cater for people with different backgrounds, including either engineering or scientific tertiary qualifications and/or relevant experience.

The program is particularly designed for people working at mine sites, or those who have access to a mine site for practical assignments. The course is offered in a flexible delivery format, with a large component available in a distance format, plus a limited number of face-to-face workshops/sessions. Assessments will include a number of practical, site-based assignments, including group and interactive work and presentations.

Students who have successfully completed the Graduate Diploma in Mine Geotechnical Engineering with a 65% WAM or higher can articulate to the Master of Mine Geotechnical Engineering.

Entry into the Master’s program requires access to mine site information, which is essential to complete the requirements of these programs and one of the following:

- completion of a 4-year Bachelor of Engineering degree (or equivalent) in Mining Engineering or Civil Engineering with a minimum of 1 year industry experience in Mining, or

- completion of a 3-year Engineering Science degree in a cognate discipline and a minimum of 2 years industry experience, or

- completion of UNSW program 5059 Graduate Diploma in Mine Geotechnical Engineering with 65 WAM.

Subject: Ground Control Principles and Practice in Underground Mining (MINE5050)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course explores the principles of rock reinforcement and ground support in underground excavations, focusing on the interaction between mining methods, excavation layouts, and reinforcement systems. You will learn about active and passive support mechanisms, support requirements for different excavation types, ground reaction curves, and load- and displacement-controlled support responses. The course also covers various ground support and reinforcement hardware, support system design, installation procedures, quality assurance, and time-dependent effects on support performance, including remedial options. A key focus is on risk-based ground support systems and their integration into ground control management, ensuring students understand how to optimise safety and stability in underground environments. Understanding ground support is critical for ensuring the safety, efficiency, and longevity of underground mining operations. This course equips you with the knowledge and practical insights needed to design, implement, and manage effective support systems in diverse geotechnical conditions, making it essential for those pursuing careers in mining, tunnelling, or geotechnical engineering.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply the principles of ground support installation and quality assurance.
- Evaluate and design ground support systems by selecting appropriate reinforcement hardware and related technologies for various geotechnical conditions.
- Analyse the interaction between mining methods, excavation layouts, and reinforcement systems to optimise ground support strategies.
- Explain the principles of rock reinforcement.
- Identify support requirements for different excavation types and mining methods.

Subject: Mining Geomechanics (MINE8140)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course is designed to introduce engineers and geologists to the major geomechanics components associated with mining operations, primarily underground. The course will cover the rock mass responses to excavations in both coal and metalliferous operations. It is, therefore, ideally suited to engineers or geologists who have an understanding and experience in the mining industry but are seeking to develop more specialist skills in the geomechanics field. The course content will include but not limited to the following components: Fundamentals of rock mechanics Overview of soil mechanics Rock mass classification Coupled rock behaviour Numerical methods in mine geomechanics Rock testing Managing risks in geotechnical engineering Geotechnical design principles and design acceptance The course is structured to provide an initial overview of basic principles applied to rock mechanics and geotechnical engineering, plus the major geomechanical properties and behavioural characteristics of rock material. This then leads to the application of these principles to the practical issues of site investigation, excavation design and ground reinforcement. An important component will be an emphasis on the interdependencies between geotechnical parameters and mine design/operational decisions and requirements. The link between geological and engineering disciplines is an important component in successfully managing these dependencies. The course will be presented through a series of lectures, facilitated discussion sessions and case studies, but will also include practical tutorial sessions and laboratory exercises.
Learning Outcomes:
- Show a good understanding of key numerical methods used in mining rock mechanics.
- Describe the basic mechanical properties of rock and how these are applied to analyse problems in mining geomechanics.
- Demonstrate a sound working knowledge of fundamental mechanisms and geotechnical principles within the context of practical mining applications.
- Recognise the role and importance of these principles in a comprehensive range of mining applications, both from a technical perspective, and from the risk and operational management perspective.

This research foundation provides a basis on which to design a solution that is robust and safe, cost effective and appropriate to the end-user. It is essential that this foundation reflects not only established thinking and practices but equally important, it should account for divergent and newly developing views as well as any limitations or weaknesses that underpin current understanding. The course emphasises the critical role of data interpretation and statistical analysis in geomechanics for effective design, study, and operational management. Practical examples and case studies will illustrate both effective and ineffective data use, highlighting the necessity of understanding data before applying it in design and numerical modelling. Assessment will consist of assignments designed to demonstrate practical competence and a deep understanding of the material. In-class tutorials will reinforce learning and provide hands-on experience, preparing students to develop and communicate effective geotechnical models and management plans.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and evaluate seismic monitoring technologies, data, applications, processing, and interpretation, while identifying potential challenges and implications, using real-world case studies.
- Assess and interpret geological and geotechnical data collected through various monitoring methods.
- Develop and communicate effective geotechnical models and management plans that incorporate data from various sources.
- Understand and explain the significance of geotechnical data in geo-engineering, including rock mass characterisation methods, data collection techniques, and ground stress, and apply these methods in practical scenarios.

Subject: Principles of Mining Excavations (MINE8860)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course explores the mechanics, technologies, and practical applications of rock fragmentation and excavation—critical aspects of mining and tunnelling. You will develop a deep understanding of rock breakage and fragmentation theories, rock mass properties, discontinuities, and stress states that influence excavation performance. The course delves into advanced excavation technologies, including full-face and partial mining machines, tunnel boring machines, and modern drilling systems. This course integrates theory with real-world applications, equipping you with the knowledge and skills to tackle the challenges of rock excavation using conventional and emerging mining and tunnelling technologies.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the contribution and influence of in-situ stress and stress redistribution on excavation stability and potential failure mode.
- Apply fundamental principles to design safe, efficient blasting and machine mining for optimal blasts, cutterhead efficiency, and economic productivity.
- Identify relevant requirements for the security, storage and handling of explosives.
- Select appropriate methods of rock excavations for given in-situ rock conditions.

Subject: Mine Slope Monitoring Technologies (MINE8940)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course covers the instrumentation and monitoring devices available to the mining industry to monitor and manage pit slope movement. Students will be able to detail the different types of slope movement typically observed in excavated, constructed and dumped slopes, as well as the various warning signs of instability. The course will discuss monitoring strategies (e.g. targeted, background monitoring), and the link to the range of monitoring instrumentation and tools available to geotechnical engineers to manage and track slope movement. Practical cases are used to demonstrate why monitoring is required in pit slope risk management and the advantages and limitations of each tool (e.g., accuracy, precision, data corrections). Practical application and deployment of available monitoring instrumentation and devices will also be demonstrated through case studies and in-class demonstration. This includes estimating time to failure from radar monitoring data and using monitoring data to reconcile numerical models. Risk management strategies are also discussed using Trigger Action Response Plans, Critical Control Registers, and Geotechnical Hazard Alerts. The intensive four-day course will include guest lectures from industry.
Learning Outcomes:
- Design a most efficient monitoring technique for any particular surface mining application.
- Determine the role and importance of these techniques in a comprehensive range of surface mining applications, both from a technical perspective, and from the risk and operational management perspective.
- Describe the principles of mine slope monitoring and identify the conventional and advanced mine slope monitoring technologies that are currently in use.
- Summarise and compare the appropriateness and effectiveness of different monitoring techniques.
