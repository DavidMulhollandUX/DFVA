Subject: Foundations in Microeconomics (ECON7000)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides students with a basic knowledge of the core economic principles. These principles will be explained and developed using a variety of examples involving businesses, governments, and households. Topics include supply and demand analysis, imperfect competition, producer and consumer theory, marginal analysis, welfare analysis, benefits and costs.
Learning Outcomes:
- Examine advanced economic concepts and theories.
- Critically assess economic policies related to real-world contexts.
- Explain policy discussions and decision-making processes.
- Evaluate contemporary economic challenges and their outcomes.
- Communicate economic ideas through written reports to both expert and general audiences.

Subject: Foundations in Macroeconomics (ECON7001)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This is an introductory course in Macroeconomics. This course covers important macroeconomic issues such as growth, inflation, unemployment, interest rates, exchange rates, technological progress, and budget deficits. The course will provide a unified framework to address these issues and to study the impact of different policies, such as monetary and fiscal policies, on the aggregate behavior of individuals.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply macroeconomic terminology correctly.
- Appraise the roles and functions of macroeconomic institutions.
- Use diagrams, simple equations and words to explain key causal relationships in macroeconomics.
- Analyse contemporary macroeconomic policy issues and their impacts on business and on society.
- Identify, compile and interpret macroeconomic data.

Subject: Economic Analysis of Strategy (ECON7070)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Game theory is the method economists employ to analyse strategic situations. One aim of the course is to teach you some strategic considerations to take into account when making your own choices. A second aim is to predict how other people or organizations behave when they are in strategic settings. We will see that these aims are closely related. We will learn new concepts, methods and terminology. A third aim is to apply these tools to settings from economics, business and finance. The course will emphasize examples.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and evaluate complex strategic interactions between decision makers in a variety of settings.
- Acquire a deep understanding of advanced game theoretic tools and models, and their applications in real-world scenarios.
- Develop the ability to make informed predictions about the behaviour of individuals and organisations in strategic environments, and understand the impact of their decisions.
- Apply advanced game theoretic concepts to real-world problems in economics, political science, and other relevant fields, and make recommendations based on your findings.
Assessment Tasks:
- In-semester Exam Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Report: Game Theory in the News: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- End-of-semester Exam In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week

Subject: Mathematical Techniques for Economics (ECON7150)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course will focus on the application of differential & integral calculus as well as matrix algebra to economic models. Simple unconstrained optimisation will be studied. The course will also introduce some aspects of multi-variate analysis.
Learning Outcomes:
- Represent and manipulate simple economic models algebraically.
- Use differential calculus, including partial derivatives and differentials, across a range of economic problems.
- Apply integration techniques to economic concepts such as consumer surplus and producer surplus.
- Apply methods of unconstrained and constrained optimization to simple economic problems.
- Use matrix algebra to solve sets of simultaneous equations and simple input-output models.

Subject: Advanced Microeconometrics (ECON7320)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course concentrates on mainstream models and estimation and inference methods that are widely used in most empirical investigations in applied microeconomics. The course has a topics-based structure, and theory and applications are closely integrated. Learning Outcomes:
- Outline the theoretical foundations of workhorse econometric methods.
- Apply econometric methods to real-world data and learn the correct way to report and interpret empirical results.
- Choose and implement appropriate econometric techniques to conduct estimation, prediction, and statistical inference.
- Examine the internal and external validities of econometric models applying to specific empirical problems.
- Implement econometric analysis using software packages like R.

Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides a comprehensive coverage of modern methods for analysing the productivity and efficiency of different types of decision-making units (e.g., individuals, firms, industries, regions, economies). Students learn how different assumptions concerning technologies, markets and firm behaviour can be used to guide the construction of proper productivity indexes. They then learn how these indexes can be exhaustively decomposed into measures of technical change, environmental change, and various types of efficiency change. Students learn how to estimate these components using data envelopment analysis (DEA), deterministic frontier analysis (DFA) and stochastic frontier analysis (SFA) methods. Students gain an understanding of why the estimation of these components is critically important for public policy-making. The course has a strong applied focus.
Learning Outcomes:

- define various measures of technical, mix, scale, allocative, revenue, cost and profit efficiency.
- explain the main assumptions that underpin piecewise frontier analysis and use DEA and FDH methods to estimate a piecewise-linear frontier
- explain the main assumptions that underpin deterministic frontier analysis and use GA, LS and ML methods to estimate the parameters of a deterministic frontier model
- explain the main assumptions that underpin stochastic frontier analysis and use LS and ML methods to estimate the parameters of a stochastic frontier model
- use DEA, LS and ML methods to estimate levels of technical, cost, revenue and profit efficiency.
- use DEA, LS and ML methods to decompose proper TFP indexes into measures of technical change, environmental change and various types of efficiency change.
- identify the policies that governments might use to target the different drivers of productivity change.

Subject: Knowledge and Evidence in Public Policy (POLS7114)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course frames public policy as a process where actors/agents use ideas and mental schemas to understand and influence the policy world. In doing so, it contrasts data-driven "rational" decision-making models with "post-positivist" approaches to decision-making based on narratives and interpretive schemas. The course begins with an introduction to the theory and conceptual tools for understanding knowledge and evidence in the public policy process. We ask what is 'useful' or 'appropriate' knowledge for various actors? And what does it mean to be debating, negotiating and 'muddling through'? We move on to explore the problems of conventional policy analysis in a complex world. We then apply these conceptual and theoretical tools to better understand how policy knowledges operate in practical situations. This is done through consideration of case studies drawn from such areas as health, welfare, and law and order (in Australia and elsewhere). The final part of the course takes a more 'practical' look at the actual uses of knowledge and evidence in public policy cases, with the assistance of 'practitioners'. We will invite guest lectures from senior policy practitioners to outline examples of the applied use of knowledge and evidence in various aspects of policy processes: advice; design; making; implementation; review; evaluation and audit.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify the different forms of knowledge and how they variously contribute to policy debates and policy innovation.
- Critically assess “problem framing” in the construction of particular policy strategies and documents.
- Critically assess how various forms of knowledge and evidence are used in the development of Reports by public inquiries.
- Analyse the processes used for policy evaluation and review.

- Policy Knowledge Analysis - 2 (800 words) Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
