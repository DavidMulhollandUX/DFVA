## Master of Financial Analysis - Melbourne (Bundoora)
##### LMFAN
##### 240 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
Master every aspect of the financial sector and apply advanced analysis skills to make informed investment decisions for organisations and individuals with La Trobe's Master of Financial Analysis.

Access professional investment platforms used daily by the world’s leading organisations in our trading room. Use powerful tools to analyse both historical and real-time data across international markets, conduct market research, create investment portfolios and respond to breaking news and events. Apply your knowledge in real-world scenarios with work placement and graduate opportunities with leading firms in Australia and around the world.

By completing La Trobe's Master of Financial Analysis, you will:

  * apply advanced analysis skills and digital tools to extract meaningful financial data to inform investment decisions
  * benefit from authentic guidance on current practice with guest lectures from industry experts
  * focus your expertise with specialisations in banking, fintech, international business or business research
  * complete subjects covering more than 70 per cent of Chartered Financial Analyst (CFA) examinations
  * graduate ready to apply for membership of the Financial Services Institute of Australasia (FINSIA) (subject to additional or ongoing requirements).

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Critically analyse and apply financial theories to solve financial and investment problems such as: valuing financial securities, constructing and managing international financial portfolios, funding projects and businesses and managing financial risks.
**2.**
Identify, analyse and interpret information from various sources using digital tools to provide informed advice and propose innovative solutions to financial and business problems.
**3.**
Critically reflect on yourself, your academic and professional practice, and the ethical basis of your financial decision-making.
**4.**
Persuasively explain your financial advice and solutions utilising a variety of digital and personal modes of communication independently and as part of a team.

Subject: ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES AND PRACTICES (ACC4001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Accounting principles are critical for ensuring consistent and reliable accounting practices. This unit develops students’ understanding of fundamental accounting principles and their application to various accounting processes and systems. Students will learn how to systematically identify and analyse business transactions, prepare financial statements, and evaluate businesses financial performance using relevant accounting tools. Students will also enhance their professional judgment and data analytics skills by exposure to real-world business scenarios.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and record business transactions, and prepare financial statements using double-entry accounting principles.

Subject: ECONOMICS FOR GLOBAL LEADERS (BUA4003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Economics impacts on all aspects of business and society. Key individual, organisational and government decisions around the allocation of scarce resources are at the core of economics, and an understanding of economic concepts, frameworks and language can greatly enhance a leader’s capacity to make sound decisions and influence the decisions of those around them. This subject develops students’ knowledge of economic concepts and provides the opportunity to apply this knowledge to real world situations. Students will apply economic concepts and frameworks to current issues of concern for organisations and society. Students will learn how to evaluate and apply critical aspects of economics such as the main market forces of supply and demand; the choices producers make in relation to their costs; revenues and profit; competitive and monopoly market structures and how these affect market prices; the use of game theory to make more strategic economic decisions; measuring a nation's income and the cost of living; the drivers of economic growth and the causes of unemployment.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply economic frameworks to business and social problems for policy and decision-making.
- Construct and present logical and persuasive economic arguments and communicate concepts professionally.
- Research and apply economic concepts to predict how changes in economic conditions may impact individuals, businesses and industries within market structures.
- Critically analyse economic data to explain relationships between economic variables and their impacts on policy, business and individuals.

Subject: PRINCIPLES OF FINANCE (BUA4005)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will be introduced to the concepts, theories, and applications of finance. You will examine the role of the financial sector within the economy at large and the role of finance in the management of organisations. A particular focus will be on how investment and funding decisions are made within organisations, including an introduction to capital budgeting and project evaluation. This will lead to an introduction to a wide range of financial markets, including equity and debt markets. The mechanics of each market are examined, and the characteristics of the instruments traded in each market are explored. Consideration is then given to the concepts of risk and return and their application to portfolio theory and asset pricing models.
Learning Outcomes:
- Formulate the risk and return of individual financial assets and portfolios and describe the Capital Asset Pricing Model and its implications when evaluating financial assets' expected return and risk.
- Identify and review the fundamental concepts of capital structure theories and explain the critical factors which impact managerial decisions in developing capital structure and dividend policy
- Identify the financial manager's critical financial decisions, discuss the financial system's primary role, and explain how interest rates are determined.
- Compute bonds and share prices using the time value of money. Evaluate and explain the capital budget decision rules and apply these rules to a firm's investment decisions.

Subject: DEBT SECURITIES (FIN5DBS)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will develop a working knowledge of the characteristics of fixed-income securities, the operation of debt markets, and the mechanics of trading these securities. You will also develop a thorough knowledge of the measurement of the yield of fixed-interest securities, the various relevant yield spread measures, and the term structure of interest rates. This will then be used in the valuation of fixed-interest securities, including the valuation of bonds with embedded options, structured securities, and international bonds. Specific attention will also be given to credit analysis and risk assessment and the measurement of interest rate risk.
Learning Outcomes:
- Value fixed-income securities, measure interest rate risk, and understand basic portfolio management strategy or fixed-income securities;
- Apply economic and financial theory to credit analysis for a pubic listed company.
- Compare and contrast the characteristics of various types of fixed income securities and markets;
- Explain in detail the measurement of the yield of fixed income securities and the term structure of interest rates;

Subject: FINANCIAL DERIVATIVES (FIN5DER)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: With the growing complexity of financial markets and the use of sophisticated instruments, expertise in derivatives is essential as market participants rely on derivatives to hedge risks, exploit arbitrage, and enhance returns, making it crucial to understand their pricing, application, and strategic use. This subject will introduce you to concepts and methods to price financial derivatives such as forwards, futures, swaps, and options on various underlying assets. You will learn about the uses of derivatives by various market participants such as traders, risk and portfolio managers and the application of strategies and techniques to hedge financial risk or take advantage of arbitrage. You will also learn about market cases, feasible uses, and limitations of derivatives and digital applications.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop and implement arbitrage and risk management strategies using various derivative instruments on various underlying assets, including stocks, bonds, interest rates, indexes, currencies, and others.
- Apply financial theories and analytical skills to price and value financial derivatives.
- Compare and contrast features of various derivatives and illustrate relevant market operations.

Subject: CORPORATE FINANCE (FIN5FMA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject is focussed on introducing you to the key goals and decision-making responsibilities of businesses and corporations. You will develop a thorough and advanced knowledge of the short and long term investment and financing decisions of a firm, including project evaluation and capital budgeting decisions; leasing, replacement and real option modelling; the relative merits of debt, equity, hybrid and derivative forms of capital and evaluation of the capital raising process; theoretical and practical decision-making relating to corporate capital structure and dividend policy decisions; the effect of leverage, taxes and other imperfections on the cost of capital and the value of the firm; and working capital and asset management. Related, and extension, topics that may also be considered include mergers and acquisitions, corporate restructuring and corporate governance applications.
Learning Outcomes:
- Employ practical and quantitative skills to manage the debt and equity capital of a firm, and to value these capital components and the overall firm in this context.
- Critically evaluate and analyse the suitability of specific corporate decisions and transactions for different firms in light of the relative impact on firm valuation and shareholder wealth by comprehending the accounting, financial and economic theories on which decision-making processes and the concept of firm valuation are founded.
- Structure and present an argument to a decision-making body (such as a CEO or Board of directors) recommending a specific corporate transaction or a restructuring or strategy action,
- Understand the operating and financial structures of business organisations and how these impact on the performance and valuation of these organisations.

Subject: FINANCIAL STATEMENT ANALYSIS (FIN5FSA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will gain an advanced appreciation of financial statement analysis from the point of view of the primary users: equity and credit analysts. The subject provides you with an understanding of how financial statements are constructed in order to separate the economic process that generates the numbers from the accounting process that at times obscures them. Each topic is focussed on a specific area of analysis and the effect of differences in accounting methods and estimates on reported financial conditions. The results of operations and financial ratios is highlighted. International accounting differences are discussed and relevant empirical research findings are considered.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate the analytical thinking required to apply accounting theory and forensic skills to determining the actual financial state of a company on the one hand and to analysing empirical and theoretical problems in a scholarly manner on the other.
- Analyse the differences which exist between accounting standards and their application in different countries, in particular the difference between US GAAP and IASB GAAP.
- Explain the accounting, financial and economic theory on which the practice of financial analysis is founded.
- Apply advanced financial analytical skills and appropriate digital tools, to extract meaningful information from financial statements to guide decisions.

Subject: INTERNATIONAL FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT (FIN5IFM)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Financial globalisation, a heightened geopolitical environment, economic uncertainties, and increased ESG focus present unique challenges and opportunities for investors and corporations worldwide. International Financial Management develops your conceptual and applied knowledge and skills to manage the critical value drivers of firms operating globally. You will examine the structure and operations of global financial architecture, evaluate the economics, institutions, policies, and uncertainties governing exchange rate behavior, forecast exchange rates, and measure and manage exchange rate risk exposures. You will be trained to critically evaluate the relative merits and risks associated with the strategic financial management decisions of multinational companies, including financing, investment, and asset allocation decisions in a competitive global economic setting.
Learning Outcomes:
- Assess foreign exchange behaviour - exchange rate regimes, exchange rate determination, international parity relations - and their implications for forecasting exchange rates and managing exposures.
- Design and manage strategic financial decisions, including country risk analysis, foreign direct investment, financing, asset allocation, and tax management in international settings to deliver sustainable value.
- Critically evaluate contemporary global financial systems and current issues in international finance that affect the global flow of funds and financial management functions of global firms.

Subject: PORTFOLIO MANAGEMENT (FIN5PMT)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject seeks to provide a structured and dynamic approach to portfolio management. This approach includes identifying investment objectives and constraints, researching ideas, forecasting exceptional returns, constructing and implementing portfolios, and observing and refining their performance. The approach draws on asset pricing theories, such as the Capital Asset Pricing Model, and a range of statistical tools for its structure and intuition. Particular attention is given to portfolio performance evaluation and risk management.
Learning Outcomes:
- Construct, manage and critically analyse the performance of a portfolio of financial assets consistent with the investment objectives.
- Apply financial theory and analytical skills to solving actual portfolio management problems and examining empirical and theoretical problems in a scholarly manner.
- Critically evaluate the relative merits of passive versus active portfolio management
