## Master of Professional Psychology - Bendigo
##### HMPPSB

You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
If you’re passionate about the importance of mental health, La Trobe’s accredited Master of Professional Psychology will give you the foundations for a rewarding career that supports others to develop resilience and good mental health.

Designed to meet the requirements of the Australian Psychology Accreditation Council (APAC) and the Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency (AHPRA) for a generalist fifth year in psychology, after you graduate from La Trobe’s accredited Master of Professional Psychology you’ll be eligible to undertake a one-year internship. After you successfully complete this supervised internship, you’ll be eligible to register for full registration as a general psychologist.

Gain essential tools that you’ll use every day, such as counselling, psychological assessment, assessment, formulation, and creating and implementing treatment plans. You’ll learn the latest best practice from experienced academic staff, as all your teachers have a wealth of recent clinical experience and have worked in a variety of settings such as public health, perinatal mental health, private practice, neuropsychological services and others.

You’ll benefit from both online and in-person learning. This flexible and accessible course includes just two weeks of intensive face-to-face classes at our Bendigo Campus in the first semester, so you can be based anywhere in Australia. Created to increase accessibility and equity, this course is designed to support you continuing your postgraduate study no matter where you live in Australia.

Get real-world skills working alongside industry professionals with our practice-based curriculum. You’ll gain clinical skills via simulation and role play in your first semester and an in-person placement in your second semester (located in your home state). Take advantage of La Trobe’s connections to hospitals, clinics and community health organisations around Australia when you work directly with patients in your second semester.

You’ll graduate confident, experienced and ready to take your psychology career to the next level.

You'll learn:

  * Cognitive behavioural therapy  * Learn about cognitive and behavioural models of psychological disorders. Develop the skills to apply cognitive behavioural therapy, such as assessment, case formulation and treatment planning.

  * Case reviews and discussion  * Observe cases and develop your practical skills through online seminars and discussion.

  * Psychological assessment  * Learn the concepts and techniques central to the assessment process. Understand psychometric theory and gain hands-on experience of assessment interviewing.

  * Professional placement  * Get hands-on experience when you complete a placement as a probationary psychologist in a relevant mental health environment, located in your home state.

  * Research in clinical practice  * Learn to source, evaluate and apply research findings to develop effective treatment plans for clients.

*Please be aware that this course includes mandatory in-person placement requirements. Students are expected to complete these placements in Australia.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Evaluate appropriate assessment and management strategies based on psychological research, clinical and community practice and relevant case studies
**2.**
Apply specialised practical skills with a critical understanding and mastery of the underpinning theory and evidence-base to work as a professional psychologist
**3.**
Employ high standards of professional integrity by competently identifying and responding to ethical and legal issues in professional psychology practice
**4.**
Apply advanced knowledge and skills in psychology to engage with a wide range of clients and colleagues across different service settings
**5.**
Demonstrate a flexible and reflective approach to professional psychology practice, research and service development
**6.**
Critically apply research skills in professional psychology to inform evidence-based practice
**7.**
Justify and sensitively communicate outcomes of psychological assessments, professional decisions and research in psychology to specialist and non-specialist audiences

Subject: FOUNDATION SKILLS IN COGNITIVE BEHAVIOURAL THERAPY (PSY5COG)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject content is based on the Cognitive Behavioural Therapy model (CBT). CBT is a relatively short-term intervention aimed at solving current problems through the evaluation of unhelpful thinking and behaviour. CBT has been shown to have considerable empirical effectiveness for a range of psychological presentations. You will gain an overview of cognitive and behavioural models of psychological disorders, with a particular focus on interventions for high prevalence disorders such as anxiety and depressive disorders. The role of individual case formulation and treatment planning as ways to tailor interventions to account for cultural differences and client diversity, will be emphasised. You will focus on the development of intervention skills relating to CBT, and review process issues in CBT interventions.
Learning Outcomes:
- Construct and appraise case formulations and intervention plans based on cognitive and behavioural models, accounting for cultural differences and client diversity.
- Assess the empirical base of CBT interventions including indications and contraindications.
- Identify and apply evidence based cognitive and behavioural intervention strategies for common presenting problems such as mild-moderate anxiety and depressive disorders.
- Understand cognitive behavioural models of a range of depressive and anxiety disorders.

Subject: ASSESSMENT AND TREATMENT OF CHILDREN AND ADOLESCENTS (PSY5NBC)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will learn about identifying, assessing, and treating child and adolescent mental health issues and neurodevelopmental conditions. You will learn to analyse child client presentations and identify appropriate therapeutic interventions using the biopsychosocial and developmental models. Other useful models and approaches to understanding the problems of children and adolescents - such as family systems theory, attachment theory, genetics, transdiagnostic psychology, and neurodevelopmental neurobiology - will also be introduced where relevant. During the tutorial each week, you will consolidate your knowledge and learn practical clinical and therapeutic skills in working with children and families.
Learning Outcomes:
- Consider the influence of context (family, peers, school, society) and role of diversity (e.g. cultural, linguistic, gender, and other) in navigating the assessment and treatment of young people.
- Discriminate, select, and apply appropriate evidence-based models, assessments, and interventions for child mental health and neurodevelopmental problems.
- Design an evidence-based treatment plan for mental health issues of childhood and adolescence; with consideration of the role of the psychologist within multidisciplinary health care.
- Identify and consider moral and ethical quandaries specific to the child and adolescent context.
- Describe child and adolescent mental health conditions and neurodevelopmental problems with respect to diagnostic criteria, clinical features, prevalence, aetiology, and support needs.

Subject: PSYCHOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT (PSY5PAX)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject provides in-depth training in the area of psychological assessment, integrating the scientist-practitioner roles of the professional psychologist. The focus is on concepts and techniques that are foundations for other subjects in the course. The assessment process, emphasising a problem-solving orientation, is examined, taking into account life-span and cultural issues. Psychometric theory, and its relationship to testing, is reviewed. Principles of assessment interviewing are outlined. The contribution of tests and testing to this process is considered in detail. Techniques of test administration and interpretation, with a particular emphasis on the Wechsler Intelligence Scales, are explored. Communication of psychological assessment outcomes, particularly through report writing, is given close attention. Lectures and seminar presentations will be augmented through self-directed experiential activities.
Learning Outcomes:
- Communicate effectively to clients, families, referral agencies, and other psychologists about the psychological assessment with understanding of how to incorporate assessment results into treatment planning.
- Write assessment reports to a professional standard and suitable for different referral agencies or contexts.
- Apply structured history- taking interview as the preliminary component of psychological assessments.
- Apply advanced understanding of the principles and theoretical background of psychological assessment with an understanding of issues relating to (i. reliability and validity, (ii. development and aging, and (iii. culture.
- Administer psychological tests taking into account issues of validity and reliability, and contextual issues related to development and aging, and cultural diversity.

Subject: PROFESSIONAL PSYCHOLOGY PRACTICUM B (PSY5PPB)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This practicum subject involves practical placement(s) at external placement agencies. Please be aware that this subject includes in-person placement requirements. Students are expected to complete these placements in Australia. You will begin to work as a provisional psychologist under the supervision of a Psychology Board of Australia Approved Supervisor. A total of at least 300 hours practicum experience, including a minimum 80 hours direct client contact and 40 hours of supervision, is required to achieve the required competencies and meet professional placement obligations. If at the final placement review, competencies are not met, you may be offered a set number of additional placement hours and/or supervision hours to work towards competency. Unscheduled online seminars are to prepare you for placement. You will need to watch these recorded seminars prior to commencing placement to be deemed 'compliant' on InPlace (and ready for placement) and to be able to apply for scholarships/bursaries. You will not be able to commence placement until all compliance requirements have been met. Online case analysis tutorials will support you in your casework and facilitate group case discussions designed to mirror case conferencing in the field. You will sit an Objective Structured Clinical Exam (OSCE) on case conceptualisation. The aim of the OSCE is to (1) determine if you have met the required level of competency at this stage of your training and (2) are ready to commence your internship.
Learning Outcomes:
- Competently communicate outcomes of psychological assessments and treatment decisions
- Exemplify professional integrity by identifying and discussing the ethical basis of psychology professional practice
- Apply assessment and treatment skills based on evidence-based professional psychology practice
- Communicate clearly and coherently with diverse clients and in the management of different types of practices
- Apply advanced clinical and/or professional skills to professional psychology practice

Subject: PSYCHOPATHOLOGY (PSY5PPY)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject begins the process of building clinical competence in assessment, diagnosis, and treatment of major disorders. You will also develop a rich appreciation of their psychopathology, including the subjective experience of clients. The Diagnostic and Statistical Manual (DSM) and International Classification of Diseases (ICD) classification systems are critically appraised and used, and you will be guided in developing skills in administering a diagnostic interview.
Learning Outcomes:
- Gain insight into, and empathy for, the personal experience of those who are affected by psychological problems
- Develop skill independently and collaboratively in conducting and appraising semi-structured diagnostic interviews that routinely consider differential diagnoses, age specific features, and common comorbidities
- Demonstrate an understanding of the notion of a taxonomy of mental disorders, with particular reference to the rationale, structure, content, and limitations of the DSM system
- Develop and apply a practical understanding of the symptoms and psychopathology of major psychiatric disorders
- Integrate cultural considerations in assessment and diagnosis
