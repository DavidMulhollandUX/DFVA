The Graduate Certificate in Entrepreneurship and Innovation is a pathway to studying the Masters program for students who have not completed a bachelor degree, and who wish to gain entry to the Master of Entrepreneurship and Innovation based on previous work experience (or a combination of post-secondary study and work experience). The program undertakes an interdisciplinary approach that utilises UQ's leading position in sciences and the commercialisation of those sciences, and allows for students to learn how to manage new ideas to maximise growth impact within a specialist field of study.

Subject: Creativity for Innovation and Design Thinking (EIBS7300)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Design thinking is a strategic, human-centred approach to innovation. In this course, you will use empathy, creativity, and experimentation to uncover needs, reframe opportunities, and co-create impactful solutions. Through real-world cases, user engagement, and peer collaboration, you will develop transferable skills that allow you to create meaningful value for humans in any context.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse complex problems by applying theoretical and practical knowledge of design thinking, creativity, and innovation.
- Generate creative ideas and solutions to improve current practice using a range of design thinking models and techniques.
- Persuade an audience to support design thinking processes and recommendations.
- Collaboratively validate potential solutions for customer satisfaction and well-being.
- Engage authentically and inclusively with diverse community stakeholders.
Assessment Tasks:
- Building Empathy Through User Journey Mapping: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Ideation, Prototyping, and Testing (Live Presentation) Team or group-based In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Individual Reflection via Real-time Online Interview Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Seminar 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: Idea Management (EIBS7301)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides an understanding of the importance of technological innovation for economic growth. It focuses on how businesses select and develop ideas, the research and development process, collaboration and open innovation, and the management of intellectual property.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the challenges in creating and implementing innovative ideas.
- Demonstrate a thorough understanding of how uncertainty impacts the innovation process.
- Demonstrate awareness of how ideas and other forms of intellectual property can be protected.
- Apply critical thought skills and course content to real case examples in collaborative teams and present your findings in oral and written form.
- Demonstrate knowledge and respect of ethics and ethical standards in relation to global idea management.
Assessment Tasks:
- Idea Management Examination Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Practical reflection on innovation: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Idea Management Strategy Team or group-based In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Seminar 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: Managing Innovation (EIBS7302)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: How should organisations and entrepreneurs decide what to innovate and when? This course introduces a strategic approach to innovation in the context of dynamic markets and increasing uncertainty, enabling you to understand innovation as a manageable process. It covers key frameworks and techniques to support decision¿making throughout the innovation journey, including innovating business models and technologies, managing innovation projects and portfolios, leveraging diverse sources of innovation, and formulating timely and effective innovation strategies.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse how organisations use innovation strategies to respond to dynamic markets, technological change, and uncertainty.
- Develop innovation strategies using appropriate innovation management frameworks, tools, and techniques.
- Adapt innovation strategies in response to changing market conditions, competitive pressures, and organisational outcomes.
- Evaluate innovation management approaches in relation to organisational contexts, stakeholder impacts, and changing business environments.
- Communicate evidence-based innovation insights and strategic recommendations using research, industry, and real-world innovation contexts.
Assessment Tasks:
- Blogging on innovation: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- In-class reflection on strategic innovation Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Self-written case study: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Seminar 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: Building Innovation Capability (EIBS7303)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: How do you lead in an era where the only constant is change? This course moves beyond innovation as a one-off event, focusing instead on building a sustainable innovation culture as a core strategic capability. Through collaborative problem-solving and live innovation challenges, you will develop the practical skills to identify, build, and lead innovation capabilities within startups, SMEs, and large-scale organisations. You will emerge with the strategic mindset and employability skills sought by modern organisations looking to harness creativity and scale their innovative processes in dynamic environments.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critique the foundations of innovation performance and the balance between flexibility and efficiency in dynamic environments.
- Collaborate effectively within diverse teams to develop informed strategic innovation decisions.
- Appraise the ethical implications and strategic risks associated with embedding innovation capabilities in modern organisations.
- Construct a professional portfolio of personal attributes and skills to drive future innovation leadership.
Assessment Tasks:
- In-Class Activities Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Group Report Team or group-based Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Individual Reflection: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Seminar 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: Entrepreneurial Leadership (EIBS7304)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: How can entrepreneurial leaders create meaningful change in complex and uncertain environments? This course develops your ability to lead innovation and systems change for more sustainable futures. You will explore regional economic transformation, build advanced leadership capabilities, and develop the self-awareness needed to lead across business, government, and community contexts.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse complex organisational and societal challenges using wellbeing polarity leadership and systems-thinking frameworks.
- Develop strategies for sustainable and adaptive change across diverse stakeholder contexts.
- Reflect on your leadership assumptions, behaviours, and patterns to support ongoing personal and professional development.
- Justify leadership insights and recommendations to diverse audiences using evidence-based argument.
- Collaboratively evaluate leadership resistance and competing perspectives in complex change contexts.
Assessment Tasks:
- In-Class Essay Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Wellbeing Polarity Leadership Presentation Team or group-based: 35%% (Hurdle: False)
- Personal Leadership Development: 35%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Seminar 3 Hours/ Week
