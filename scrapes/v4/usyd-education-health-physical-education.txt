The Bachelor of Education (Health and Physical Education) offers students a four-year full-time specialist professional qualification to teach in the key-learning area of personal development, health and physical education (PDHPE) and a second key learning area. The program will teach initial grounding in four HPE strands: health, physical education, sports science, and professional practice. Students also complete a second teaching area of specialisation.

While the course is dedicated mostly to teaching PDHPE in the secondary-school context, other examples of career opportunities include corporate training and human resource settings, community health, coaching, recreation, and sport.

## Bachelor of Education (Health and Physical Education)
To qualify for the award of the Bachelor of Education (Health and Physical Education) degree, students must complete 192 credit points in total, comprising:

108 credit points in core units of study, as set out in Table A for the Bachelor of Education (Health and Physical Education);
a 48 credit point major in Education Studies; and
a minor (36 credit points) or a second teaching area (24 credit points); and
an optional maximum of 6 credit points from Table O; and
where appropriate, elective units from Table S.

The enrolment pattern of requirements can be found on the
Health and Physical Education unit of study table
.

The available minors and second teaching areas are listed in the course resolutions.

Note that second teaching areas are administered by the School of Education and Social Work in compliance with the NSW Education Standards Authority to meet appropriate admission standards for teacher accreditation. The requirements for these second teaching areas are defined in the
schedule of teaching areas

Subject: Modern Hebrew, a Living Language (HBRW3610)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit is designed for students who have successfully completed HSC Modern Hebrew Continuers, HBRW2604, HBRW2632, or have reached a similar level of knowledge. In this unit students will advance their proficiency of using the four interlinked language skills of listening, speaking, reading, and writing. As well, engaging in a variety of text-types, students will gain a deeper understanding of issues of social, cultural and historical importance to Modern Hebrew speaking communities
Learning Outcomes:
- LO1 . increase your range of vocabulary and expressions you use in conversation both orally and in writing
- LO2 . demonstrate your skills in comprehending unseen written material on a topic you are familiar with
- LO3 . converse in everyday topics in easy Hebrew
- LO4 . use the online resources to compliment face to face learning and enhance self-study
- LO5 . reach a good working usage of the learners’ dictionary Rav Milon
- LO6 . reach a good working fluency and usage of the future tense form of the verb patterns pa’al- ain vav, regular root, lamed yud and, pi’el- regular root
- LO7 . conjugate the prepositions al (of/about), etzel (at), min (of/from), biglal (because of), bishvil (for), b (in), bli (without), kmo (similar to/as) and used it correctly both orally and in writing.
Assessment Tasks:
- Small tests throughout semester: 25%: Multiple weeks% (Hurdle: False)
- Participation Contribute to class discussion; consolidate use of recent language: 10%: Ongoing% (Hurdle: False)
- In-class assessment Three parts: Unseen text, short answers Grammar & syntax Essay 175 words: 25%: Week 08% (Hurdle: False)
- Oral presentation with written component Oral presentation; topic of choice: 15%: Week 12% (Hurdle: False)
- Semester in-class Assessment Three parts: Unseen text, short answers Grammar & syntax Essay 175 words: 25%: Week 13% (Hurdle: False)
