## Undergraduate Certificate in Cybersecurity  - On-Line
##### TU001O
##### 60 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
If you're interested in building your digital skillset or taking the first step towards a career at the digital frontlines, La Trobe's Undergraduate Certificate in Cybersecurity provides you with the foundational knowledge to upskill or pursue a new career in the emerging area of cybersecurity.

You'll develop the foundational knowledge in the areas of cybersecurity, coding and programming, ethical hacking, cryptography and computer networks. Gain an introduction into common technologies used to identify and mitigate threats including firewalls, intrusion detection systems, intrusion protection systems and honeypots. Learn how to protect data, systems, and networks from cyber threats as you build the interdisciplinary skills to prepare you for future studies and employment – such as communication, critical thinking, problem solving and collaboration.

Make study work around work and life commitments with flexible online learning, giving you the freedom to study anywhere. You'll gain the confidence to directly pathway into a Bachelor of Information Technology, Cybersecurity, or Computer Science, where you can build on your cybersecurity skills while expanding into various sectors of the IT industry.

You'll learn:

  * Cybersecurity principles  * Explore how to prevent unauthorised access of information and how to respond to a data breach or cyberattack.

  * Programming  * Learn the foundations of the Python programming language as a means of creating executable program code to design and implement working software solutions.

  * The human element of cybercrime  * Discover the human risks to cybersecurity from societal, industry, and organisational contexts, exploring how cyber criminals manipulate individuals to access confidential information.

  * Data and information security  * Explore the principles and strategies of information security and how to prevent unauthorised access, such as ethical and legal practices and the process of incident response and analysis.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Analyse the role of key cybersecurity technologies—including firewalls, intrusion detection/prevention systems, and honeypots—in mitigating digital threats.
**2.**
Analyse common cybersecurity threats and vulnerabilities, and apply the principles of confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA triad) to evaluate basic protective strategies.
**3.**
Evaluate how technical and human factors interact to shape cybersecurity posture.
**4.**
Develop foundational programming scripts using Python to automate basic tasks relevant to cybersecurity analysis and operations.
**5.**
Analyse the role of human behavior, psychological biases, and social engineering techniques in cybersecurity incidents, and propose basic strategies to enhance human resilience.

### Course features
### Career outcomes
The Undergraduate Certificate in Cybersecurity is a direct pathway into a Bachelor of Information Technology, Bachelor of Computer Science and Bachelor of Cybersecurity, providing students with the foundational knowledge in key areas of cybersecurity, such as coding and programming, ethical hacking, cryptography and computer networks.

With further study in La Trobe's Bachelor of Cybersecurity, students can work in cybersecurity roles across a range of industries, including technology, communications, education, finance, government, defence, healthcare, and energy and resources.

Subject: INTRODUCTION TO CYBERSECURITY (CSE1ICB)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In the modern Internet era, industries and organisations need to be prepared to defend against cyber threats and attacks. Stakeholders should be familiar with basic principles and best practices of cybersecurity to protect their businesses and personal information. In this subject, these principles and strategies for future cyber security are explored. Key topics include: information security; ethical and legal practices; mitigating cyber vulnerabilities; and the process of incident response and analysis. The subject introduces the broad discipline of cybersecurity and outlines how to ensure the privacy, reliability, confidentiality and integrity of information systems and mitigate against cyber threats and risks.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply and compare approaches to cyber risk management in addressing real-world digital security challenges.
- Demonstrate foundation skills in identifying malware, securing applications, systems, and networks
- Analyse emerging cybersecurity practices, regulations, and standards, and evaluate their implications for digital security.
- Work efficiently and effectively as a member of a team to investigate possible solutions such as cryptography to enhance data security, and web security, and digital forensics against cyber threats

Subject: HUMAN FACTORS IN CYBERSECURITY (CSE2HUM)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject examines cybersecurity through a human factors lens across societal, industry, and organisational contexts. Students explore how ethical, social, and psychological factors shape human behaviour and cyber risk, and how these vulnerabilities arise and are exploited. They analyse cybercrime business models and global threats, with a focus on how human factors contribute to attack success and organisational risk. Building on this, students apply human factors principles to design organisational cybersecurity solutions, including cultural, communication, and policy strategies aligned to relevant frameworks. Through this progression, students develop professional skills in evaluative judgement, teamwork, and communication to deliver practical, human-centred cybersecurity solutions.
Learning Outcomes:
- Assess the economic impact of and financial implications of investing in human factors in cyber security and propose cost-effective risk management strategies.
- Analyse cybercrime business models and threats impacting corporate social and ethical responsibilities, assessing the influence of organisational culture, leadership, and communication on cyber security practices.
- Investigate social implications of human factors in cyber security, considering digital divides, socio-cultural norms, and digital empowerment, to inform policies promoting equitable access and participation in secure environments.
- Analyse psychological factors influencing cybersecurity behaviours and personality characteristics to develop strategies for secure practices and organisational sustainability goals.
- Develop a comprehensive cyber security culture, education and engagement strategy within an organisational context, whilst employing professional skills in evaluative judgement, team building, negotiation, and high-level communication across diverse environments.
