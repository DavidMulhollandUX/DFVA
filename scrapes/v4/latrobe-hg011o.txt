## Graduate Diploma in Rural and Remote Nursing Practice - On-Line
##### HG011O
##### 120 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
Develop specialised rural and remote nursing knowledge and equip yourself with the skills required to lead and support health care in rural and remote and remote communities.

Learn how to coordinate culturally safe, up-to-date and evidence-based nursing care in rural and remote communities and develop the tools you need to make a real difference in rural healthcare.

Developed by experienced rural nurses in consultation with CRANA+, our industry-focused program based on the National Rural and Remote Nursing Generalist Framework will equip you with the skills and knowledge needed to become a leader in rural and remote health care.

In this course you'll:

  * Take your existing qualifications as a registered nurse and expand your knowledge and skills to support rural nursing practices.
  * Learn from experienced rural nursing academics who are also practicing clinicians.
  * Develop in-demand clinical skills and knowledge in digital health, urgent care, advanced life support, palliative care, rural aged care practice and remote emergency care.
  * Develop advanced work-based skills in communication, conflict resolution as well as effective teamwork and workplace culture.
  * Gain skills in leadership and management.
  * Have the flexibility of studying online, from anywhere.
  * Gain the skills required by registered nurses working in rural and remote settings as identified by remote health peak body CRANA+
  * Upon completion, use your qualification to apply for the Master of Nursing (Nurse Practitioner) when appropriately experienced , or a number of other master’s degrees in nursing, public health and leadership.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Communicate effectively with the community and as a member of a geographically dispersed multidisciplinary team.
**2.**
Perform independently and as a team member in a variety of clinical contexts within rural and remote healthcare settings in accordance with professional ethical standards.
**3.**
Apply advanced nursing practice knowledge and skills to contribute to informed clinical judgement for rural and remote nursing practice.
**4.**
Appraise and select digital health and telehealth care options to provide accessible, equitable and effective rural and remote nursing care.
**5.**
Generate evidence-based approaches to plan, implement and evaluate culturally appropriate person/client-centred rural and remote nursing care.
**6.**
Develop skills in leadership and management, including planning and implementing staff recruitment, performance management, and financial management relevant to rural and remote healthcare settings.

Subject: FOUNDATIONS OF URGENT CARE NURSING (NRS4001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Rural urgent care is a specialist rural nursing competency. This subject will equip you to assess and appropriately manage patients in the rural emergency setting. You will apply your knowledge to specialty population groups such as paediatrics and workers in primary industries and mining. You will learn to adapt your practice to low resource settings as well as being able to use the technology and equipment in contemporary rural health care environments. This subject will equip you to prepare and respond, as part of a multidisciplinary team, to high risk, infrequent situations. Apply Advanced Life Support (ALS) within the rural urgent care setting. Collaboratively stabilise, manage and transfer the acutely unwell patient out of the rural urgent care setting. Emergency maternity care for unplanned birth at term or prematurely. Urgent care emergency preparedness response in relation to natural disasters and pandemics. The subject has both theoretical and clinical components to support advanced skill development.
Learning Outcomes:
- Adapt a comprehensive urgent care plan to the resources available in a variety of rural and remote health care contexts
- Critically evaluate rural and remote urgent care nursing practice against research and current literature, applying a practice development framework for quality improvement.
- Initiate, plan and evaluate related clinical skills and equipment/technology to address patient care needs within the rural and remote urgent care setting.
- Manage care for identified speciality population groups such as bariatric, paediatric, obstetric and critically ill patients, as well as patients with difficult behaviours; or occupational/traumatic injury within rural and remote urgent care settings.

Subject: AGEING IN PLACE IN RURAL COMMUNITIES (NRS4004)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Taking a strengths-based perspective, this subject explores models of care for older people as they transition through home based, acute, sub-acute and residential care in the context of complex financial, regulatory, policy and political frameworks. In this subject you will develop your nursing practice to promote healthy ageing. You will assess, plan, deliver and evaluate person centred nursing care for older adults in rural health care settings. In this subject you will also develop your assessment skills in the management of dementia and delirium, frailty, geriatric syndromes, wound management and palliative care.
Learning Outcomes:
- Coordinate the transition of the older person through acute, sub-acute, community and residential care in the context of regulatory, policy frameworks relevant to rural health.
- Identify the signs of cognitive deterioration and impairment in older people and identify services and evidence based interventions for their care and referral.
- Demonstrate advanced communication skills to maintain positive therapeutic relationships with patients/residents, their significant others, and the multidisciplinary team.
- Apply advanced knowledge, skills and attitudes in the assessment and management of people at the end stages of life.
- Apply principles of wound prevention, wound assesssment and wound management, including the evaluation and referral of patients with non-healing wounds.

Subject: POPULATION HEALTH IN RURAL AUSTRALIA (NRS4006)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Using the principles of public health and a health promoting approach this subject focuses on the population health priorities of rural communities. Through an understanding of the wider determinants of health you will be able to identify the associations between environmental, social, structural, economic, cultural, biomedical, commercial and digital environments in which rural communities live, play, work and age and their impacts on health and wellbeing. You will use this knowledge to respond to the needs of individuals and communities to develop culturally safe and appropriate care.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate population health care for children, young people and their family in the rural context.
- Evaluate chronic health screening in a rural population. Identify strategies for improvement contextualised to rural Australia.
- Analyse health data and identify social determinants of health in a rural population. Select and justify the most important National Health Priority in this population and create a health promotion strategy to address it.
- Identify individuals and families at risk of, or undergoing, family violence and apply best practice models for support and referral.
- Evaluate health promotion material for cultural safety within minority rural populations.

Subject: ADVANCED CLINICAL EVIDENCE FOR NURSING PRACTICE (NSM5ACE)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Advanced Clinical Evidence for Nursing Practice aims to enhance your ability to source, synthesise, and apply evidence to inform clinical practice and improve patient care and outcomes. Throughout the coursework, you will develop foundational skills in searching, synthesising, critiquing, and referencing peer-reviewed literature. You will also explore various research methodologies. - Formulate ethically sound research questions which address evidence gaps within your practice area.
- Justify the selection and use of validated research tools to ensure methodological rigor and the integration of high-quality evidence into safe and effective consumer care.
- Utilise digital technologies and data analysis tools proficiently, while critically assessing the potential benefits and risks of discursive AI in nursing and midwifery research and practice.
- Critique how evidence translation impacts health outcomes, focusing on key barriers and the impact of underrepresented vulnerable populations in research literature.

Subject: FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT IN HEALTH SERVICES (PHE5FMH)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you are introduced to financial management in health services, in particular, systems of finance and budgeting as applied in healthcare.In addition, we review how non-monetary resources are recorded and explore the methods in which organisations secure and manage resources needed, and the financial implications associated with procurement. We explore the difference between organisational and management accounting.Topics covered include; accounting principles, basic analysis and management implications related to financial data and quantitative performance data, and basic techniques of quantitative decision making and operations research for dealing with health service resource utilization decisions. You are also introduced to costing and casemix funding in healthcare. In addition, we introduce the impact of procurement of goods and supplies by health services, including common procurement methods and an exploration of ethics in procurement, otherwise known as probity.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply conventions of basic principles of accounting, such as relevance, reliability, materiality and timeliness and appreciate implications of cash based and accrual based accounting individually and collaboratively
- Using evidence-based skills, analyze, evaluate and critique the implications of the applications of different methods of financing health service organizations, such as activity-based funding
- Critique and interpret the performance of a health service organisation using preexisting data and predict outcomes within the five major accounting categories of assets, liabilities, owner's equity, revenues and expenses individually and collaboratively
- Analyse and interpret between the information provided through a balance sheet, a profit and loss (or income) statement and a statement of cash flows and formulate these financial statements to provide commentary on a health service organisation's performance individually and collaboratively
- Explain the management and implications of procurement of goods and services by health services.
- Prepare and defend budgets for their organizations or department and apply basic principles of management accounting.

Subject: STRATEGY AND OPERATION MANAGEMENT (PHE5SOM)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will examine the strategic decisions, actions and leadership that determine the success of health service organisations. To achieve this, you will focus on the health care system and organisations from the strategic planning and management viewpoint, as well as covering marketing fundamentals and key components of operations management and research essential for decision analysis. This subject is designed for you to use much of the material previously covered in other subjects, as well as healthcare management experience to further develop the skills to deal with real life strategic and operational situations. Learning Outcomes:
- Critically appraise and recommend operations management tools and techniques to optimise service delivery and align operational systems with strategic objectives.
- Analyse and evaluate evidence-based operational reporting tools to support performance monitoring and strategic decision-making.
- Critically analyse the legislative, regulatory, and policy frameworks governing (influencing) health service delivery across public and private sectors, and assess their implications for strategic and operational decision-making.
- Design, implement, and critically evaluate strategic plans for health services, departments or organisations, integrating stakeholder needs, organisational goals, and contextual complexities.
- Evaluate and apply project management tools and evidence-based approaches to facilitate and sustain organisational change in healthcare settings.
