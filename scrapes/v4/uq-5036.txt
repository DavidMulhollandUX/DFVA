## Career Opportunities & Employment Outcomes
There is currently a demand for MRI physicists, MR Technologists, image processors, engineers, and biomedical engineers within Australia and internationally. Graduates have found employment in leadership positions in hospitals, private practice and research facilities. For a sample of the job opportunities in magnetic resonance, visit the International Society for Magnetic Resonance in Medicine (ISMRM), Australian Society of Medical Imaging and Radiation Therapy (ASMIRT), or visit an Allied Health locum agency website. Professional memberships: Completion of this degree will not fulfill requirements for clinical accreditation. Interested individuals can contact professional bodies such as the Australian Institute of Radiography (AIR) or the International Society for Magnetic Resonance in Medicine (ISMRM) for details on membership and accreditation requirements.

Subject: Magnetic Resonance Instrumentation (MRES7002)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Magnetic Resonance Instruments for clinical applications. Function & performance concepts for superconducting MRI magnets, RF & gradient coils. Spectrometer architectures. Technology implications of modern fast/advanced imaging sequences. Instrumental & patient induced artifacts & their amelioration.
Learning Outcomes:
- Different imaging modalities - Explain in general the medical imaging modalities used for diagnosis and treatment planning of patients.
- Different imaging modalities - Outline the different types of medical images obtained using various imaging modalities, such as CT, X-ray, PET, Ultra Sound, SPECT, Optical and MRI.
- Hardware requirements - Outline the primary components of an MRI static magnet.
- Hardware requirements - Define the x-, y- and z-gradient coils in an MRI scanner and their functionality.
- Hardware requirements - Describe the construction of various types of MRI RF coils and the manner in which these operate.
- Hardware requirements - State the physical limitations of each primary MRI hardware component.
- Hardware requirements - Understand the need for safety limits posed on MRI hardware.
- Hardware problems - Link different types of image artefacts to possible hardware causes.
- Hardware problems - State the image artefacts caused by impurities in the magnet, gradient coils and RF coil magnetic fields.
- Effect of magnetic fields - Describe the issues with magnetic fields and clinicians and how these affect their day to day work.
- Effect of magnetic fields - Describe the issues with magnetic fields and how these cause problems for patients.

- 3x Tutorial Questions Hurdle: 24%% (Hurdle: True)

Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week 1L

Subject: MR Safety and Monitoring (MRES7003)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Principal hazards of MRI environment and its effects on the human body and equipment. Physiological monitoring strategies examined from the origin of signals to integration with the imaging system. Considerations in planning for MR installations are reviewed.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the physical principles and biological effects of MRI electromagnetic fields, including the static field (B₀), RF field (B₁), and gradient fields, and their implications for patient and staff safety.
- Apply MRI safety screening and risk-assessment procedures to determine patient eligibility for MRI, including the evaluation of implants, pregnancy, foreign bodies, tattoos, and prior surgical history.
- Interpret and apply international MRI device labelling standards (MR Safe, MR Conditional, MR Unsafe) and manufacturer specifications to make evidence-based decisions for scanning patients with implants and medical devices.
- Implement safe MRI operational practices in relation to magnet siting, access control, zoning, emergency procedures, quench events, and cryogen hazards.
- Safely manage gadolinium-based contrast agents, including agent selection, dose administration, recognition of adverse reactions, and management of gadolinium retention risks.
- Perform and interpret physiological monitoring in the MRI environment
- Demonstrate professional and legal accountability in MRI practice by applying institutional policies, regulatory frameworks, and the roles of MRI safety personnel to ensure patient, staff, and facility safety.
Assessment Tasks:
- Assignment One: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Literature Review: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester examination Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week 1L

Subject: Magnetic Resonance Imaging: Fundamentals (MRES7100)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This unit of study explores the principles and methods that underpin Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI), a key molecular imaging technique. Topics covered include Physical principles of nuclear magnetic resonance (MR), underlying mechanisms of relaxation in MR and descriptions of the way in which pulse sequences are able to exploit relaxation to produce contrast. On completion of this unit, students will have a thorough understanding of the MRI methodology, and the molecular basis for endogenous contrast. The use of contrast agents to modify image contrast and target particular molecular features will be introduced. The factors affecting the accuracy and potential sources of artefact in MRI images will be explored.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate the basis of Magnetic Resonance Imaging.
- Evaluate the mechanism of MRI relaxations.
- Evaluate the basis of chemical shifts and fat suppression.
- Evaluate the process of MR data acquisition and creation of MR images.
- Evaluate the properties of spin echo and gradient echo sequences.
- Evaluate the strategies for generating MR image contrasts.

Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week 2L

Subject: MRI Pulse Sequence Construction & Image Contrast (MRES7400)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course aims to equip students with a comprehensive understanding of Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) sequences commonly used in clinical and research settings. Students will gain an in-depth understanding of the building blocks of MRI sequences, including radiofrequency (RF) pulses, magnetic field gradients, spatial encoding methods, and signal sampling. They will learn their characteristics and manipulation methods and how to combine them to form different sequences that produce images with various contrast weightings, such as proton density, T1-, T2, T2* and other contrasts. Starting from the basic gradient-echo and spin-echo sequences, students will explore the composition and parameters of various sequences and how to vary them to achieve different contrasts. More advanced and emerging sequences will also be explored, enabling students to confidently manipulate scanner parameters and generate desired MRI contrast. MR images are prone to artefacts from various sources, and identifying them is essential for MRI researchers and technicians. This course will introduce the different types of static and dynamic artefacts that commonly occur on MR images, explain their causes and the methods to eliminate or reduce them. The skills and knowledge attained in this course will prepare students of the program for later courses in advanced applications of MRI and practical operation of MRI scanners within the clinical environment.
Learning Outcomes:
- Create MRI sequences by applying knowledge of magnetic field gradients and RF pulses.
- Predict the effect of adjusting sequence parameters on image contrast.
- Explain the performance of advanced sequences in producing the desired contrast.
- Understand the practical implementation and limitations of imaging sequences.
- Recognise and explain image artefacts and suggest approaches for their correction.
