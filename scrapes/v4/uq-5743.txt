Subject: Groundwater Modelling and Management (CIVL4145)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The capacity to understand and predict the behaviour of groundwater is essential to many areas of engineering including: i) design and construction of foundations, ii) mining operation design and management, iii) water resource assessments and iv) contaminant transport modelling and pollution management. This course provides students with the necessary skills and knowledge in hydrogeology and groundwater modelling to allow them to effectively contribute to groundwater related projects in professional practice, including the design of bore fields and site dewatering systems, deriving solutions to water resource problems and providing information to inform environmental management and risk assessment activities. A range of different modelling approaches, from simple analytical methods to the application of dynamic physically based modelling packages, are explored. The process of model testing, calibration and sensitivity analysis are introduced along with methods for analysing and communicating simulation outputs. The course also covers aspects of data analytics and best practice in environmental modelling.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the physical and chemical mechanisms, processes and settings that influence groundwater flow and associated water quality, including the mathematical models that are commonly used to approximate these phenomena.
- Explain and apply the key elements of good practice for model development, testing and application in environmental water resource settings including the principles of model development, testing, sensitivity analysis, calibration and validation.
- Critically assess the suitability of different modelling approaches for a given problem, including the identification of the usefulness and limitations of different approaches in specific contexts.
- Apply a range of different models to simulate groundwater flow and contaminant transport to solve engineering problems with an appropriate level of professional competence, including the critical assessment of simulation results.
- Effectively interpret, summarise and communicate the results of an environmental modelling project, to a professional standard, in the form of a written report and an oral seminar.
- Successfully work within a team to synthesise and analyse data, develop and test various models and report that information in a clear, concise, timely and professional manner.
- Improve your ability to manage your learning, to reflect on your own learning and improve your study approach.
Assessment Tasks:
- Oral Seminar Presentation: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Groundwater Modelling Project: 45%% (Hurdle: False)
- Final Exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Workshop 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Advanced Soil Mechanics (CIVL4230)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course expands introductory studies of soil mechanics with topics such as constitutive modeling, continuum mechanics, tensor calculus, elasticity, plasticity, problematic soils, unsaturated soils, soft soils, and the use of geophysics. This course is useful for structural and geotechnical engineers designing civil works. This course is beneficial for advanced computer simulations and analyses in geotechnics.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply concepts for unsaturated soils to analyse mechanical, hydraulic, and environmental challenges.
- Create stress-strain models for clays and sands.
- Apply laboratory and field experiments to calibrate material models.
- Evaluate assumptions for the representation of the mechanical behaviour of soils.
- Analyse issues with soft and expansive soils.
- Evaluate hydraulic principles of unsaturated soils.
- Evaluate the impact of unsaturated soil mechanics in geotechnical engineering.
- Analyse results from electrical resistivity tomography for soil characterisation.
- Apply concepts of geophysics to provide solutions in geotechnical engineering.
Assessment Tasks:

Subject: Wind Engineering (CIVL4340)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Wind is ubiquitous in our daily lives. It loads the structures we live and work in, it transports pollutants through the atmosphere, and increasingly is becoming a dependable source of renewable energy. This course details the multi-faceted nature of wind in modern society and provides students with the tools to assess wind and its interactions with the built environment. The course will broadly cover: wind climate and resource assessment, environmental wind engineering, and structural wind engineering. Specifically, it will cover topics including; wind measurement techniques, statistical analysis of wind records, atmospheric boundary layer meteorology and models, turbulence, wind flow around and through buildings, wind loads on structures, wind-induced vibration and experimental and numerical wind modelling techniques.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe modern wind measurement techniques (including their advantages and disadvantages)
- Source, process and analyse a range of wind records
- Explain atmospheric boundary layer theories and implement models describing it and its interaction with terrain and topography
- Determine the wind loads on a range of different structures based on standards or wind tunnel results
- Calculate the dynamic response of simple structures to wind loads
- Undertake and analyse a wind tunnel experiment
- Work in a team to analyse and report wind tunnel test data
Assessment Tasks:
- Assignment 1: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Assignment 2 (Group assignment) Team or group-based: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Final exam Hurdle: 50%% (Hurdle: True)

Subject: Traffic Flow Theory and Emerging Technologies (CIVL4450)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Traffic flow theory is the foundation of daily traffic operation and controls. Transport Engineering is also rapidly changing as a result of emerging and disruptive technologies, such as connected and automated vehicles, car sharing and electric vehicles, which make transportation systems more dynamic and complex. This course provides future transport planners and traffic engineers with the capacity to develop and implement effective and efficient traffic management strategies. Students will gain essential knowledge of advanced and emerging issues in Transport Engineering so they can contribute to the design and management of future transport systems. The course focuses on advanced traffic flow theories and modelling as well as emerging topics such as automated vehicles, cooperative driving. The opportunities and challenges in the era of connected and automated vehicles are also explored. The course compliments other transport courses offered at the University of Queensland and extends material from courses such as CIVL2410/CIVL2420 and CIVL3420/CIVL3430. This course also prepares students for more advanced study in CIVL6415.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply microscopic models to simulate individual driving behaviours;
- Apply macroscopic models to simulate characteristics of traffic flow;
- Explain simulation outcomes of traffic models, and present the outcomes to the general public as well as the experts;
- Explain impacts of emerging technologies on Transport Engineering;
- Develop strategies to cope with impacts of emerging technologies on Transport Engineering.
