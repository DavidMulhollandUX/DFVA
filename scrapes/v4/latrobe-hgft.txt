Work based learning (placement) requirements 
**_Compulsory placements (Work Based Learning)_**  
During this course, you will participate in one or more supervised Work Based Learning (WBL) placements designed to allow you to practice newly acquired skills, behaviours and knowledge while integrating theory with practice. The placements will immerse you in professional work culture, enabling you to develop and further enhance your skills. WBL provides you with the opportunity to gain valuable industry experience at one or more of our local, interstate or international industry partners or agencies. WBL placements will generally be arranged by the Office of the Provost Placements and WBL Team at La Trobe University. The number of hours undertaken can vary considerably, depending on the requirements of the course. Students may incur additional costs and it may be necessary to travel or relocate to complete a specific placement. Please refer to the individual subject entries for more information.

Subject: FOUNDATIONS OF FAMILY AND SYSTEMIC PRACTICE  (FTH4001)
Credit Points: 30
Overview: This subject will provide you with a sound understanding of foundational family therapy concepts and skills to equip you to work effectively with both individuals and families. It will be delivered via weekly online (live streamed) lectures and small skills group learning across 12 weeks of Semester 1. FTH4001 will broaden and deepen your knowledge through experiential methods, including role play and in-class presentations, and opportunities to apply theory to your specific work context and role. Learning will be assessed through class presentations, reflective practice essays and case reports/reviews that focus on your current practice as well as observation of your skill development in vivo. You will work towards specified levels of skill as set out in defined competencies.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically evaluate and adapt systemic interventions to ensure respectful and inclusive practice that meets the needs of individuals and families from a range of cultural and diverse socio-cultural contexts.
- Identify and synthesise systemic theories, associated frameworks and apply a range of skills and techniques to inform family work practice.
- Develop an agenda and focus for family work and demonstrate a collaborative and respectful process in working with families.
- Contrast and compare a linear and systemic conceptualisation of families’ presenting issues identifying the implications

Subject: CONTEMPORARY APPLICATIONS OF FAMILY AND SYSTEMIC PRACTICE  (FTH4002)
Credit Points: 30
Overview: In this subject, systemic theories and practices are integrated and applied to more challenging family situations such as those involving conflict, trauma, grief and sociocultural diversity. Interwoven through all applications is a reflection on self and the influence of one’s own historical and current context. Alongside this you will engage in experiential exercises including role plays, developing requisite skills for conducting an initial family session.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate a systemic understanding of the impact of loss, grief and trauma as experienced by individuals and families.
- Integrate key ideas from a range of systemic and associated approaches to develop case conceptualisations and plans as appropriate to the needs and capacities of workers and clients within the mandate and context of the work.
- Synthesise and utilise personal learning about the influence of historical factors and current socio-cultural context on the worker's practice with individuals, families and other social and professional groups.
- Conduct a culturally sensitive, trauma informed, and effective single session family consultation.

Subject: FOUNDATIONAL FAMILY THERAPY MODELS (FTH5TPA)
Credit Points: 30
Overview: In this subject you are guided in the integration with your current skill and knowledge level with a more in-depth understanding of family therapy theoretical models relating to the family and other contexts. In lectures/seminars you will explore in greater depth the major family therapy and associated theories and practices with an emphasis on developing a theoretically informed collaborative case conceptualisation. Emphasis in this semester will be on using the theoretical frameworks provided to reflect on your own clinical skill development in the context of your personal and professional history and in formulating, setting and working on goals for improved competence. You will work towards specified levels of skill as set out in defined competencies.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop an appropriate systemic formulation of presenting difficulties drawing on family therapy and other relevant theoretical frameworks.
- Analyse and integrate factors pertaining to values, interpersonal style and capacities that impact on your practice within the therapist-client system, using defined theoretical frameworks.
- Execute a respectful, non-blaming, and effective initial family therapy session incorporating a reflecting team process.

Subject: FAMILY THERAPY MODELS AND APPLICATIONS (FTH5TPB)
Credit Points: 30
Overview: In this subject you will consolidate knowledge of family therapy theoretical models and integrate these into into your repertoire for analysing and intervening with families and in working on your own resources and constraints as a therapist. You will evaluate the appropriateness of the various models according to your assessment of presenting problems of families from across the life span and formulate interventions that fit with these assessments and your own developing style.
Learning Outcomes:
- Synthesise specialist knowledge relevant to evidence, developmental stages across the lifespan, sensitivity to culture and other diversity factors to develop appropriate case conceptualisations and therapeutic plans.
- Further integrate factors pertaining to values, interpersonal style and capacities as they impact on your practice within the therapist client system, using family therapy and other relevant theoretical frameworks to conceptualise and develop strategies to increase your therapeutic flexibility and effectiveness.
- Conduct interactionally based family therapy sessions incorporating a reflecting team process.

Subject: ACADEMIC INTEGRITY MODULE (LTU0AIM)
Credit Points: 0
Overview: The academic integrity module introduces academic integrity standards to inform and support students in avoiding plagiarism and academic misconduct. Students complete four parts that encompass academic misconduct and academic integrity decisions, including cheating, plagiarism, and collusion. Students learn about Turnitin (the text-matching tool) used at La Trobe University, and how to get help and support their development of referencing skills.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the importance of the acknowledgment and the referencing of sources
- Locate, select, and utilise information about La Trobe's approach to academic integrity to support appropriate acknowledgement of references used.
- Recognise and understand academic integrity responsibilities.
