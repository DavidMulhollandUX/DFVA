1. Applytools and frameworks to criticallyappraiseinformation quality and make explicit, judicious, and evidence-informed judgements and decisions in dynamic systems and complex environments;
2. Developplans for effective program implementation, risk management, service delivery, and management of public resources;
3. Applycommunication, negotiation, and contracting skills integral to working effectively with stakeholders in the public, private and community sectors;
4. IntegrateIndigenous ways of knowing, thinking, learning and perceiving in evidence-based decision-making and practice;
5. Innovateadaptive approaches and strategies to address complex problems and opportunities; and
6. Createpurpose-driven climates for people and teams to enact resilience, inclusion, well-being, continuous learning, high performance, and integrity.

Subject: Complexity, Judgement and Decision-Making (MGMT8101)
Credit Points: 3 units
Overview: This course is about individual and group information-processing, judgement, and decision-making in complex and dynamic work environments. It covers critical and analytical thinking, critical systems thinking, and the use of tools and techniques to de-bias decision-making and increase decision quality in the workplace. The course develops knowledge and skills for critical systems thinking, meta-cognition, and transdisciplinary approaches approach to complex issues and challenges in policy and practice.

This is a foundational course for other courses in the Master of Business Administration (MBUSA).
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the concept of complexity leadership, and the associated knowledge and skills required of leaders and their teams to navigate different types of complexity
- Describe the key barriers to effective decision-making and practice in organisations in the face of complexity
- Articulate the key features of critical systems thinking
- Identify the right methods to increase decision quality at the group and individual levels in the face of complexity
- Apply systems approaches to complex problem-solving.

Subject: Evidence-Based Practice (MGMT8102)
Credit Points: 3 units
Overview: This course introduces a structured framework for evidence-informed decision-making that increases decision quality and integrity by drawing on multiple sources of critically appraised information. Having a structured and repeatable approach to complex decisions enables integrity and has a number of important work-related outcomes, including improved leader-member relationships, and trust-based relationships with stakeholders and citizens. It helps leaders to avoid untrustworthy information and advice coming from inside and outside the organisation, and to engage in purposive inquiry with rigour.

This is a foundational course for other courses in the Master of Business Administration (MBUSA).
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the key features and skills requirements of evidence-based decision-making and practice
- Identify the benefits of evidence-based practice in management in terms of reducing risk
- Explain the importance of evidence-based decision-making and practice to leaders and organisations
- Differentiate the four sources of evidence to test claims and assumptions
- Formulate different types of questions to identify problems and solutions
- Appraise the trustworthiness of one’s own professional expertise.

Subject: Public Sector Resources Management (MGMT8106)
Credit Points: 3 units
Overview: This course builds on leaders' developed public sector capabilities. It develops knowledge and skills essential to servicing their financial and human resources accountabilities, from an internal agency perspective, and from an external perspective, whilst facing resource constraints. It augments learnings of Public Sector financial management, human resource management, and public sector budget cycles with more contemporary and transdisciplinary approaches.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the key drivers, processes, practices, and accountabilities integral to public sector resources management
- Identify the key opportunities and challenges relating to public sector resource management
- Evaluate different tools and frameworks used in public sector resources management, and their implications for value creation, sustainability, and strategic prioritisation.

Subject: Internal Consulting Capstone (MGMT8113)
Credit Points: 3 to 12 units
Overview: This capstone enhances leaders' organisational and business research skills in the context of a real-world complex organisational issue, problem, or opportunity.  It develops broader skills in evidence synthesis, knowledge generation, and translation. Integrating the decision-making and critical thinking skills developed throughout the program, MBA candidates will identify a real-world issue or opportunity warranting applied research with their sponsor, organisation, agency, or department. They will learn to write an evidence-based report for their organisation that details a set of practice recommendations and an implementation plan. This course, as such,  provides our MBA candidates with anintegrated learning experiencethat will help them demonstrate the value-add of their MBA education not only within their organisations, but to further advance career, professional, and personal development.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply an evidence-based decision-making and practice framework to integrate the theoretical and critical knowledge gained throughout the MBA program
- Critically analyse and integrate disciplinary information, knowledges, and evidences, from different sources and stakeholders using evidence-based tools and reasoning
- Effectively translate a set of integrated findings from their research into practical recommendations and action plans
- Ascertain important supportive conditions for the implementation of recommended evidence-based practices
- Effectively present an integrated and culminated learning experience together with the research findings, both orally and in writing.
