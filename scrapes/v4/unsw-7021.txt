## Description
The undergraduate certificate in Engineering develops your practical skills and improves your mathematical understanding. You will complete a selection of four courses, focusing on engineering design, computing, maths, and an elective in a chosen area of Engineering. These courses introduce you to some of the fundamental elements of Engineering practice, and equip you to apply that knowledge and skills to basic engineering problems.

On successful completion of the certificate, you can transfer your completed courses and continue your studies in the Bachelor of Engineering, subject to meeting the articulation requirements.

NOTE:

- This program does not have CRICOS registration; it is not available for study on a Student Visa.

Subject: Introduction to Engineering Design and Innovation (DESN1000)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this course, you will experience first-hand one of the major things that engineers do: designing and building creative solutions to problems. You will learn to think the way that engineers think, coming up with reasonable solutions to problems despite being limited by budget, time and resources, the requirement to also meet environmental and social objectives and, of course, the limitations of the laws of physics. This will help you appreciate engineering design's central ideas as an on-time, on-budget and fit-for-purpose solution to a poorly specified, open-ended problem. You will start to build critical skills for engineers that will be called upon repeatedly in your academic and professional lives, including concept development, critical thinking and evaluation skills, clear communication, research and information literacy skills and the skills involved in successfully functioning within a team environment to complete a given task. A key part of the course is a design project. During the first week, you will select a project to complete from a list of possible options offered. Once you have joined a project, you will be assigned to a team of around 5-6 students during the second week. With this team, you will work to practically solve your design problem for the rest of the term.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate the attributes of an effective team member, including the use of basic organisational and interpersonal tools.
- Apply foundational technical theory and skills to a design project.
- Demonstrate a systematic approach to design in response to a specified set of project requirements.
- Use foundational project management techniques to plan, execute and complete a design project.
- Explain designs using oral, written and visual forms of professional communication within the project context.
- Test the suitability of designs using analytical and practical validation methods pertinent to the project.

Subject: Electrical Circuit Fundamentals (ELEC1111)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Can you imagine your life without electricity, computers or mobile phones? Circuits are all around us. Electrical engineers are most commonly associated with the development of circuits, but they are not the only ones who know about or work with circuits. All engineers need to have a basic understanding of the relationship between electricity, electrical energy, electronic instrumentation, and measurements. Mechanical engineers use circuits, for example, when designing motors or controls for spacecrafts. Robotics is closely related to Computer Science, but it is hard to imagine doing anything in robotics without considerable knowledge of electrical circuits. ELEC1111 is an introductory course in Electrical Engineering. It provides an introduction to fundamental analogue electrical elements such as sources, resistors, capacitors, inductors and diodes, as well as fundamental circuit analysis techniques, including Ohm’s and Kirchhoff’s laws, nodal and mesh analysis and circuit theorems (superposition, source transformation, Thévenin & Norton equivalents). It then expands to fundamental analogue circuits including resistor-capacitor (RC) and resistor-inductor (RL) circuits and operational amplifiers, and AC analysis concepts, including phasors, impedances and AC power.
Learning Outcomes:
- Obtain the transient and steady state behaviour of first order circuits.
- Systematically analyse DC and AC electrical circuits with passive and active elements using circuit laws, theorems, and techniques.
- Apply phasors and sinusoidal steady state analysis to AC circuits.
- Demonstrate competency in constructing basic circuits, operating laboratory equipment, measuring and evaluating electrical quantities, and adhering to work, health and safety (WHS) regulations.
- Apply basic circuit design concepts and requirements in both practical and theoretical setups.

Subject: Infrastructure Systems Engineering (ENGG1400)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course introduces engineering students to optimisation, modelling, and systems engineering for the planning, design, and management of infrastructure systems. Through authentic engineering challenges drawn from various infrastructure sectors, students develop analytical and decision-making skills to address contemporary infrastructure problems. The course integrates systems thinking, operations research, network modelling, optimisation techniques, and project management within a project-based learning environment. Students apply mathematical models and computational methods to evaluate alternatives, optimise system performance, and support evidence-based engineering decisions while considering technical, economic, environmental, and societal objectives. A strong emphasis is placed on professional engineering practice through industry-inspired case studies, collaborative projects, and authentic assessments. Throughout the course, students develop competencies in solving engineering problems, applying systems thinking, exercising professional and ethical responsibility, communicating technical solutions effectively, and considering stakeholder needs in engineering decision-making. Students will gain practical experience in modelling, analysing, and optimising infrastructure systems using Python or Microsoft Excel Solver . To support their learning, students will be provided with example Python code and Excel Solver models, which they can adapt by modifying the relevant data inputs for their project. The use of either Python or Excel Solver is optional, and students may choose the tool that best suits their project and preferences. Teaching staff will provide instructions and guidance on the use of both tools.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply optimisation and operations research methods using computational tools to improve the performance, efficiency, resilience, and sustainability of infrastructure systems.
- Develop and justify evidence-based engineering solutions to real-world infrastructure problems by integrating optimisation techniques, mathematical modelling, economic analysis, and stakeholder considerations.
- Communicate engineering analyses, modelling assumptions, optimisation outcomes, and recommendations effectively to technical and non-technical audiences while demonstrating professional and ethical responsibility.
- Apply systems thinking and engineering principles to analyse infrastructure engineering problems using an integrative and holistic approach.
- Formulate quantitative models of infrastructure systems to evaluate alternative design and operational decisions under technical, economic, environmental, and societal constraints.

Subject: Surveying and Geospatial Engineering (GMAT1110)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Surveying and Geospatial Engineering deals with the science of determining relative positions of features above, on or below the surface of the Earth for a range of mapping, engineering and construction operations. The course combines lectures, workshops and outdoor practical exercises using modern electronic surveying equipment. Topics include levelling, orientation, distance measurement which combine into coordinate reference systems. Also traversing and control surveys, electronic detail surveys, construction setout, GPS/GNSS satellite positioning, geographic information systems (GIS) and sources of geospatial data such as laser scanning, remote sensing, aerial photogrammetry (UAVs and planes) and satellite imagery. Basic land law and cadastral surveying for NSW is introduced. The student will learn some basic surveying computations to critically assess spatial data quality. Students will be expected to have a good background in trigonometry and geometry from high school mathematics.
Learning Outcomes:
- Undertake basic survey computations from raw field observations to support a range of surveying and engineering applications such as levelling and terrain representation, area and volume calculations, traversing and construction set out.
- Demonstrate basic field surveying techniques including handheld GPS and GIS, levelling, and use of a total station to acquire raw field observations and set out of a minor structure.
- Demonstrate skill with various surveying instruments, forward planning for survey tasks, production of clear field notes and redundant field checks to ensure accuracy.
- Apply the theory behind the various surveying and geospatial techniques presented in this course and critically assess the quality of geospatial data.

Subject: Engineering Materials and Chemistry (MATS1101)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course introduces the fundamentals of Materials Science and Chemistry used in engineering. The different classes of materials used in engineering designs are introduced, and their basic structural, mechanical and functional properties are defined, and the mechanical properties are measured in laboratory activities. The relationships between a material’s underlying structure and its properties are explored. The ways in which materials fail within engineering settings are identified. The chemistry part of the course builds on an elementary knowledge of chemistry (equivalent to one year of high school chemistry, such as Year 11 chemistry, or CHEM1001 at UNSW) to explore the structure of atoms leading to an understanding of the properties of materials at an atomic and molecular level. This knowledge is applied to understanding chemical bonding and intermolecular forces which determine the engineering properties of materials. General principles of chemical equilibrium are developed and applied to chemical reactions involving acids and bases and organic molecules to understand the reaction materials can undergo. The course will consist of lectures, laboratories and tutorials split between the two disciplines: Materials Science and Engineering and Chemistry. This course is intended for engineering students to take early in their programs.
Learning Outcomes:
- Solve numerical problems related to mechanical behaviour and explain the mechanical properties of different types of materials.
- Interpret information and numerical data to explain and/or rationalise real-world phenomena by integrating multiple chemical principles.
- Conduct laboratory experiments in a safe manner and manipulate apparatus, perform quantitative and qualitative materials and chemical analyses, evaluate the accuracy and precision of measurements, and interpret results and observations.
- Describe relationship between a materials structure, properties and processing.
- Connect chemical concepts to real-world applications through a firm foundation in the fundamentals of chemistry for materials science.

- Apply communication and problem solving skills to identify ideal materials for design applications.

Subject: Sustainable Energy (SOLA1070)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Students will be introduced to the central concepts of energy and sustainability with a particular focus on sustainable energy systems. Energy efficiency will be introduced as an effective way in which to conserve our natural fuel reserves and reduce environmental damage in a cost-effective way. - Perform calculations based on energy conversion and transfer processes.
- Design an energy infrastructure by making decisions based on energy security (ability to meet demand), energy equity (affordability) and environmental sustainability.
- Identify the key economic and environmental sustainability indicators.
- Describe the operation of a range of renewable energy technologies.
