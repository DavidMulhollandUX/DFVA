UQ's Bachelor of Social Science will build your in-depth knowledge of these key social issues, and will empower you with practical and transferrable skills to address them ¿ skills that are sought after by employers. It is the degree for social change makers.

It provides diverse skills, values and knowledge from policy evaluation, social impact assessment, social research, to communication, and cross-cultural awareness. Students are also guided in developing a strong ethical compass to understand and respond to critical social issues.

Subject: Introduction to Social Research (SOCY2019)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course introduces students to the foundations, tools, and techniques of social research. It does this by embedding questions of method into a broad framework of research design and analysis so that research is approached as a holistic enterprise in which theory, method and analysis are interrelated. Rather than simply offering a suite of different qualitative and quantitative research methods for students to trial, this course takes as its starting point the kinds of questions researchers pose about the social world and the various methodologies and methods that inform these questions. It also encourages students to be critical about their research practice and to consider the ways that research has supported the project of settler colonialism in its treatment of Indigenous peoples and communities. Students will be introduced to Indigenous perspectives on research, not only as subjects of research by non-Indigenous researchers, but as skilled researchers with their own ways of knowing, being and doing. The course also exposes students to the affordances of Generative AI in research and equips them with the skills to become ethical and critical users of GenAI across the research process. On completion of this course, students will be skilled in the art of reflexive, ethical and methodologically rigorous social research.
Learning Outcomes:
- Design methodologically coherent research across all stages of the research process
- Demonstrate a growing capacity to undertake research with Indigenous peoples and communities guided by Indigenous values and priorities
- Make ethical choices in the design and conduct of social research
- Apply and transfer social research skills in a variety of contexts and domains
- Demonstrate critical use and appraisal of Gen. AI in the research process
Assessment Tasks:
- Assignment 1 Problem Statement: 30% 800 words% (Hurdle: False)
- Assignment 2 Article Review: 30% 1,000 words% (Hurdle: False)
- Assignment 3 Exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 40% approximately 1,000 words% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Workshop 2 Hours/ Fortnight

Subject: Introducing Quantitative Research (SOCY2339)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Quantitative research methods are the most commonly used research methods in the social sciences. They are widely used by academic researchers, government agencies, private companies, and community organisations. This course provides an introduction to quantitative methods in the social sciences. It emphasises how social scientists use simple quantitative techniques to investigate research questions coming from social science theory, prior research and applied problems. The course focuses on the link between theory and research in social science, the logic of quantitative empirical analysis, and techniques for describing quantitative data and drawing inferences (generalisations) about larger populations. The course also introduces Jamovi statistical software for simple quantitative analysis.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the stages of quantitative research
- Understand how social theories are related to social scientific concepts
- Be able to calculate and interpret basic univariate descriptive statistics
- Be able to construct and interpret two and three-way cross-tabulations
- Understand the logic of classical statistical inference
- Be able to make statistical inferences based on a sample mean/proportion or comparison of means/proportions
- Be able to carry out a chi-squared test of statistical independence for tabular data
- Be able to construct and interpret a correlation coefficient
- Appreciate the logic of bivariate linear regression
- Be able to use Jamovi statistical software to access and manipulate survey data in preparation for quantitative analysis
- Be able to use Jamovi statistical software to carry out simple descriptive and inferential statistical procedures
Assessment Tasks:
- Mid-Semester Exam (central) Identity Verified In-person: 35%% (Hurdle: False)
- Assignment 2 - Applying Inferential Statistics Online: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
- Review Tests Online: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week

Subject: Qualitative Social Research (SOCY3329)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Learn sought after skills in qualitative social science research through doing. Covering research design and practice, the course introduces the methods, methodology and epistemology of various analytical approaches: thematic analysis, critical discourse analysis, visual analysis, yarning and participatory action research. The course also explores many contemporary theoretical approaches and debates including interpretivism, social constructionism, post-positivism, feminism, Indigenous methodologies and post-colonial approaches.
Learning Outcomes:
- distinguish the assumptions, theories and concepts which have been developed by various approaches to social inquiry, from post-positivist to interpretivist, critical, participatory and Indigenous methodologies;
- identify the specific concepts, methods, data and approaches relevant to the qualitative exploration and analysis of particular issues, problems and social phenomena;
- generate original research questions informed by relevant theories and concepts, and identify the corresponding methods for ethically generating data to answer these questions;
- identify and evaluate the strengths and limitations of research techniques in qualitative inquiry;
- describe and apply sociological knowledge and understanding through oral and written means of expression;
- collaborate effectively as a member of a social research team.
Assessment Tasks:
- Thematic Analysis Report Hurdle Team or group-based: 35% 1,500 words% (Hurdle: True)
- CDA Proposal & 'Show Your Work' Write Up: 35% 1,500 words% (Hurdle: False)
- Yarning Methodology Essay Hurdle In-person: 30% 1,000 words% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 1.5 Hours/ Ad Hoc Workshop 1.5 Hours/ Week

Subject: Society, Challenges and Solutions: Introduction to the Social Sciences (SOSC1000)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Society, Challenges and Solutions is a multidisciplinary social science course with an applied approach to the study of human society. This course examines diverse aspects of social life from multiple perspectives, incorporating analytical and methodological strengths in a wide range of social science disciplines, including sociology, anthropology, political science, public policy, psychology and human geography. Through real cases of contemporary issues and social problems, students will explore a wide range of topics that shape both Australia and the world today - the changing nature of work and education, the environment and health, community and development. Using a blend of text, video, and online materials, students will learn the skills for independent study and collaborative investigation. Upon the successful completion of this course, students will gain critical knowledge on Australian and global social issues, relevant policies, and possible solutions. Through engaged peer learning, students will learn to think about and reflect on social issues based on evidence, develop proposals for action, and practice effective oral and written communication.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse human actions, issues, and social phenomena using major social science concepts, methods, and cross-cultural perspectives.
- Evaluate how social structures shape individual and collective experiences across diverse cultural and global contexts.
- Critically assess scholarly social science sources and construct evidence-based arguments using systematic empirical inquiry.
- Communicate complex social science ideas effectively through collaborative work, presentations, and academic writing that integrates textual and visual materials.
Assessment Tasks:
- Mini quizzes Online: 10% allocated across two separate quizzes (i.e., 5% each)% (Hurdle: False)
- Rewrite (and reframe) the headlines In-person: 45% 600 words% (Hurdle: False)
- 'Know where you are': group presentation & essay Hurdle Team or group-based In-person: 45% 2000 word group essay + 5-6 min group presentation% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week

Subject: Research, Planning and Design (SOSC3201)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course is only available to BSocSc students and is recommended to be completed in the final year of study. It enables students to apply and extend core research skills developed in earlier courses through an applied group research project conducted with an industry partner. Students become skilled in project planning, team work and industry liaison.
Learning Outcomes:
- Prepare for applied research projects, including liaising with stakeholders, evaluating published research, and designing ethical research.
- Engage actively, effectively and professionally in teamwork.
- Communicate research under development to various audiences.
- Identify and articulate the ethical implications of social research methods, data, analysis and reporting.
- Utilise AI for social research in an ethical, effective and responsible manner.
Assessment Tasks:
- Annotated Bibliography Hurdle: 25%% (Hurdle: True)
- Group Ethics Application Hurdle Team or group-based Online: 25% + Peer Assessment*% (Hurdle: True)
- Group Research Project Proposal Sequence Hurdle Team or group-based Online: 50% + Peer Assessment% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Seminar 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: Project (SOSC3202)
Credit Points: 4
Overview: This course involves the conduct of original research with an industry partner. It is the companion to SOSC3201 and both should be completed in consecutive semesters in the third or final year of study. The course integrates and applies skills acquired in core courses, and majors, through applied team research projects with an industry partner. Students identify, plan, implement and evaluate a research project to address the problem, and disseminate research findings through written and oral reports.
Learning Outcomes:
- Plan and manage a small-scale social research project
- Work effectively in team environments
- Conduct data collection using methods relevant to your research questions and aims
- Understand and address ethical issues relating to social research as they arise in the context of conducting research
- Analyse, synthesise and present empirical research data to mount a convincing argument
- Write research reports and outputs for different audiences
- Reflect critically upon your learning and your achievements
- Transfer your knowledge and skills to diverse professional employment contexts
Assessment Tasks:
- Research Project Reflection and Methods Summary Exam Identity Verified In-person: 30% Individual% (Hurdle: False)
- Summary Research Findings Team or group-based: 10% Group% (Hurdle: False)
- Group Video and Showcase Presentation Team or group-based: 25% Group% (Hurdle: False)
- Research Report Team or group-based Online: 35% Group% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Seminar 3 Hours/ Week
