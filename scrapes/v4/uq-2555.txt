## Career Opportunities & Employment Outcomes
Upon graduation, students will have diverse employment opportunities available to them. The combination of design and information technology skills opens doors to a wide range of industries and roles. Graduates can pursue careers in digital communication, human-centred design, information architecture, software development, data analysis, user experience design, and many other fields that require a blend of creative problem-solving and technical expertise. Additionally, the program's emphasis on teamwork and communication skills equips students with the collaborative abilities sought after by top companies in the industry, enhancing their employability and professional growth prospects.

Subject: Sustainability for Global Citizenship (ADPS1000)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This first-year interdisciplinary course equips students with knowledge and skills to engage with sustainability as a holistic and interdisciplinary concept. The course explores the evolution of sustainability, integrating environmental, economic, technological, and socio-cultural dimensions to foster an interconnected approach to global challenges. Through active learning and cross-cultural inquiry, students will reflect on personal and collective assumptions, developing an appreciation for diverse worldviews and the role of architecture, design, and planning in shaping sustainable futures. The course prepares students as global citizens to actively participate in a professional community, fostering a sense of responsibility, adaptability, and leadership in addressing sustainability challenges.
Learning Outcomes:
- Examine the role of sustainability frameworks in addressing contemporary environmental and societal challenges through interdisciplinary thinking and practice.
- Critically analyse the evolution of sustainability as a holistic concept that enables an interconnected view of environmental, economic, technological, and socio-cultural systems.
- Lead open, appropriate and effective discussions on the impacts and influences of architecture, design, and planning on sustainability outcomes.
- Examine personal and others’ perspectives and assumptions to inform a cross-cultural inquiry into sustainability issues.
- Express awareness of and respect for the values and knowledges of First Nations peoples including the social, political, and historical dimensions of colonialism in Australian and global contexts.
- Articulate an international perspective on the disciplines of architecture, design and planning to enable active contribution in a global community.
Assessment Tasks:
- Assessment 1 Examination Hurdle Identity Verified: 40%% (Hurdle: True)
- Assessment 2: Interactive Oral Assessment Identity Verified In-person: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Assessment 3: Graphic Report: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week

Subject: Digital Visual Communication (ADPS1001)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Digital visual communication skills are highly sought after across a variety of professional fields, including the creative sector, the built environment, business, and education. This foundational design course will equip you with the methods and tools required to effectively communicate ideas visually through the creation of digital images and assets. You will learn to apply fundamental conventions, principles and techniques of graphic design in the digital context, gaining proficiency in leading industry software Adobe Photoshop, Illustrator, and InDesign, including the latest generative AI features. You will apply creative thinking and foundational design methods to respond to design briefs and learn to articulate design propositions through the creation of raster and vector images, visualising information with engaging infographics, and designing impactful posters and portfolios.
Learning Outcomes:
- Recognise key theories and conventions of visual communication and how meaning is constructed through images.
- Develop and iterate upon design ideas in response to design briefs using foundational design methods.
- Implement foundational sketching techniques for design ideation and information visualisation.
- Implement visual communication principles to develop, document, and showcase creative concepts in both digital and print media.
- Identify and use appropriate digital software to communicate design outputs.
- Construct vector and raster imagery to visually represent various types of information and articulate design ideas.
- Critically evaluate visual communication approaches, taking into account purpose, audience, context, and medium.
Assessment Tasks:
- Assessment 1: Visual Language and Communication Portfolio: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Assessment 2: Visual Storytelling and Influential Imagery Portfolio Identity Verified: 30%% (Hurdle: False)

Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week Practical 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: Industry Placement (ADPS3300)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides the opportunity for students to learn in a workplace environment. Students are responsible for finding and arranging a professional work placement, subject to approval, that provides meaningful work experience in the profession. Students are required to complete between 100 hours and 130 hours of placement in the academic term for which the student is enrolled. The course will require attendance, participation, observation, critical reflection, and report writing on workplace activities and experiences. The emphasis of critical reflection and report writing will be on identifying and describing aspects of professional relevance incorporating: professional practice; collaboration and teamwork; work place health and safety; professional ethics; and other aspects of the work place experience. Information about enrolling in a placement course, including registering your interest and application deadlines, can be found here: https://www.eait.uq.edu.au/prepare-for-your-placement
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate an improved understanding of a technical, administrative, and personal skills and knowledge required to contribute effectively in a professional setting.
- Acquire practical skills and experience in professional workplace including working with others and working independently.
- Build an improved awareness of the sectors, disciplines and interests which contribute to and influence practice in the disciplines of architecture, design or planning and their ability to meet sustainable development goals.
- Identify and analyse integrity and ethics issues in professional practice, and the role of ethical standards in the profession.
- Use graphic, written and verbal presentation skills to communicate the placement experience.
- Reflect on your own approach to professional practice taking into account your personal values and ethics, completed course work, placement workshops and workplace experience.
