## Description
The Graduate Diploma in Mining Engineering offers foundational knowledge and practical skills for professionals already working in the mining sector or individuals looking to transition into the industry. This postgraduate qualification is designed for those who want to advance their expertise in mining engineering while continuing their current professional roles.

The Graduate Diploma in Mining Engineering will be awarded after successful completion of 48 Units of Credit (UOC). The program can be undertaken either full-time or part-time, providing flexibility to accommodate the schedules of working professionals. Most courses are delivered in a modular format, either face-to-face or online, in intensive block periods. This delivery mode is particularly well-suited for employees of mining companies and associated service providers, allowing them to balance their studies with their work commitments.

The curriculum covers a broad range of essential mining engineering topics, from mine design and process optimization to safety and environmental management. Students will gain a solid understanding of both the technical and managerial aspects of the mining industry, while also developing critical research skills that will prepare them for leadership roles. The program provides a comprehensive overview of the field, making it an ideal option for professionals who wish to specialize in mining engineering without committing to a full Master's degree.

This qualification not only enhances career prospects but also serves as an excellent pathway for those wishing to further their education, with the option to continue into the Master of Mining Engineering if desired.

## Admission Requirements
**Limitations on Recognition of Prior Learning**:
Recognised prior learning (RPL) will be considered for a maximum of 24 UOC towards the Graduate Diploma in Mining Engineering upon application to the Program Authority and according to the UNSW RPL Procedure. Evidence will be required for equivalent learning including duration of a course provided by a recognised institution, and course content and assessment.

All courses completed towards the Graduate Certificate in Mining Engineering at UNSW will be recognised for the purpose of advanced standing for the Graduate Diploma in Mining Engineering.

**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
Entry to the UNSW Graduate Diploma in Mining Engineering requires one of the following:

- successful completion of the UNSW Graduate Certificate in Mining Engineering with 65 WAM, or

- a Bachelor degree in a related cognate discipline such as (Chemical, Electrical, Environmental, Mechatronics, Mechanical, Metallurgy, Petroleum) or physical Science degrees (Geology, Physics, Chemistry) with a minimum of one (1) year industry experience in Mining.

The Graduate Diploma in Mining Engineering can be an entry point for articulating to a Master of Mining Engineering, with a program WAM of 65.

Learning Outcomes:

- Describe the basic mechanical properties of rock and how these are applied to analyse problems in mining geomechanics.
- Demonstrate a sound working knowledge of fundamental mechanisms and geotechnical principles within the context of practical mining applications.
- Recognise the role and importance of these principles in a comprehensive range of mining applications, both from a technical perspective, and from the risk and operational management perspective.

Subject: Principles of Mining Excavations (MINE8860)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course explores the mechanics, technologies, and practical applications of rock fragmentation and excavation—critical aspects of mining and tunnelling. You will develop a deep understanding of rock breakage and fragmentation theories, rock mass properties, discontinuities, and stress states that influence excavation performance. The course covers the fundamentals of blasting mechanics, including types of explosives, initiation systems, and blast design for both underground and surface mining. It also examines blast hazard management, vibration control, and the latest advancements in blasting technologies, including alternatives to conventional blasting. Drilling methods and equipment are also explored, with a focus on production drilling techniques, drill bits, accessories, and performance considerations. In addition, you will study the principles of coal and rock cutting mechanics, cutting tool performance, and rock cuttability assessment. The course delves into advanced excavation technologies, including full-face and partial mining machines, tunnel boring machines, and modern drilling systems. This course integrates theory with real-world applications, equipping you with the knowledge and skills to tackle the challenges of rock excavation using conventional and emerging mining and tunnelling technologies.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the contribution and influence of in-situ stress and stress redistribution on excavation stability and potential failure mode.
- Apply fundamental principles to design safe, efficient blasting and machine mining for optimal blasts, cutterhead efficiency, and economic productivity.
- Identify relevant requirements for the security, storage and handling of explosives.
- Select appropriate methods of rock excavations for given in-situ rock conditions.

Subject: Space Resources Engineering (MINE8870)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In-situ resource utilisation (ISRU) — the concept of using local materials in space to support human activity - is widely recognised as one of the most transformative ideas for the future of space exploration. It underpins the emerging field of space mining, which has so far primarily focused on overcoming engineering challenges. Many scientists agree that, in the near future, we may begin extracting resources from off-Earth bodies such as asteroids, comets, the Moon, Mars, and its moons. These celestial bodies represent some of the most distant and untapped sources of materials ever considered for human use. For humanity to establish a sustained presence in deep space, including potential colonisation, it will be essential to produce key materials and products locally - making ISRU a critical enabler of space settlement. To succeed, space mining operations will need to function autonomously or be remotely operated from orbiting stations, spacecraft, or Earth. The uncertainties and technical challenges involved are significant and will require the development of new knowledge and capabilities. As a result, mining expertise will play a central role in enabling ISRU, and engineers will need to acquire new or specialised skills to meet the demands of this frontier. In this course, students will be taken through the engineering lifecycle of new technologies and encouraged to think about how their unique skills can be applied to these projects. Subject: Mine Water and Waste Management (MINE8910)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Mine water and waste management are critical issues for safe and efficient underground and pit mining operations and to minimise environmental impacts at all stages from feasibility assessment to mine closure. Leading practices for mine site water accounting and assessment will include depressurization and dewatering, geotechnical stability, sustainable water supplies and surface diversions, discharge and pump system management. Fundamentals of hydrogeology and hydrogeochemistry for mining will include monitoring and management of groundwater storage, salinity and acid mine drainage; potential contaminant migration processes; geochemical reactive transport modelling and tracing methods. The course will focus on aqueous aspects of mine water including engineered seepage barriers, water recycling and treatment options, with an overview of waste rock and tailings management.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply this knowledge to critically review water and waste management practices at a mine and to creatively solve problems in a manner that is responsive to change.
- Identify and assess information requirements for depressurization and pumping operatons, sustainable water yields and diversions, and the safe management of waste rock and tailings. Skills to locate, critically evaluate and use information are to be developed in an interdisciplinary context.
- Undertake presentations that develop essential communications skills and collaborative team work. Team work skills are to recognise diversity and the importance of working within an international community.
- Demonstrate knowledge of water and waste management practices for mining from feasibility assessment, operations to closure. This knowledge is to demonstrate ethical practice and social responsibility in an international context.

Subject: Uranium Mining Fundamentals (MINE8930)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Uranium Mining Fundamentals will cover all stages of mining, from exploration of ore geology to mine feasibility and design and mine operation. Global and Australian uranium potential and leading practices for mining uranium will be evaluated and critiqued. Mining methods considered for efficient and safe extraction of uranium will include open cut, underground and In Situ Recovery (ISR) leach methods. The fundamentals of mine geomechanics, including slope stability and ground support requirements, will be presented as part of mining method selection, along with consideration of grade control and ventilation for safe underground mining. Fundamentals of milling and chemical processing of uranium ore will be presented, focusing mostly on production to the yellow cake stage (uranium oxide powders). Technical aspects of mining and milling operations are complimented with consideration of health and safety, economic, community, environmental and regulatory issues associated with uranium mining, presented within a risk management framework.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop a plan for a hypothetical mining operation, integrating exploration, mining, processing, safety measures, adherence to regulations, and waste management strategies.
- Analyse the potential risks and hazards associated with uranium mining and evaluate how legislation mitigates these risks.
- Describe relevant terminologies and concepts related to uranium mining operations, covering from assessing feasibility to the operational stages, including associated exploration and milling practices.
- Assess information requirements for designing a uranium mine as an open cut, underground or ISR (in situ recovery), and for safe and efficient mining and milling operations, including consideration of ground support and ventilation.

Overview: This course covers basic principles of coal and metalliferous mine ventilation and underground environmental control.
