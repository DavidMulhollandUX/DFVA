Subject: Research Methods (SCIE4000)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course equips students with fundamental and transferable skills in conducting research both within and outside of their disciplinary areas through a series of workshops designed to build on their scientific research knowledge. Opportunities will be provided for students to gain first-hand experience of research activities outside of their own disciplinary area in the Faculty of Science through field trips and site visits, and students will be required to work in multidisciplinary groups for workshop activities. This course is designed to complement and enhance students' experience of their own research project. Students should complete this course in their first semester of Honours year.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop a research-focussed approach to the solution of a new problem, beginning with the development of research questions.
- Apply project management techniques to plan and carry out a research project in the allocated time frame, within the available resources and in a safe and ethical manner.
- Competently search the scientific literature to critically evaluate previous research.
- Offer clear and logical explanations of scientific concepts that may be unfamiliar to the non-specialist through both oral and written communication.
- Incorporate Indigenous perspectives in research in a respectful and meaningful way.
- Apply fundamental data analysis tools to analyse quantitative and qualitative results.
- Be familiar with the contexts of scientific research, and the various research activities that take place within the Faculty’s schools and research centres.
Assessment Tasks:
- Worksheets Hurdle: 48% - This weight is indicative only. This is a pass/fail course.% (Hurdle: True)
- 3MT-style presentation Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 18% - This weight is indicative only. This is a pass/fail course.% (Hurdle: True)
- Research pitch Hurdle: 20% - This weight is indicative only. This is a pass/fail course.% (Hurdle: True)
- Development documentation Hurdle Team or group-based: 14% - This weight is indicative only. This is a pass/fail course.% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Workshop 2 Hours/ Week This course contains a series of workshops (up to two hours each) and tasks requiring self-paced learning and group work

Subject: Veterinary Technology Honours Practicum (VETS6622)
Credit Points: 10
Overview: This course provides hands-on, advanced training for veterinary technologists. Students will be based in approved, referral veterinary practices or other animal/technical environments (e.g. zoos, research institutes, government agencies). They will work as part of a para-veterinary team in managing clinical or professional caseload and activities, under the guidance of UQ staff and and/or external preceptor. This Work Integrated Learning approach to training will prepare the student for an advanced level of professional practice, as well as enhancing their employment prospects through acquisition of contextually appropriate skills and competences. Students commencing in Semester 1 full-time enrol in VETS6621 for 2 consecutive semesters. Students commencing in Semester 2 full-time enrol in VETS6622 for 2 consecutive semesters.
Learning Outcomes:
- Work at an advanced level of efficiency and effectiveness in a veterinary or veterinary-related environment.
- Successfully integrate and demonstrate advanced knowledge and skills in the chosen discipline to the satisfaction of the invigilator/mentor/supervisor.
- Develop an understanding of industry expectations and opportunities available in relation to skills and knowledge attained during the Practicum.
- Demonstrate understanding of ethical responsibilities associated with professional practice in the workplace.
- Effectively apply information and compile case/project/other reports accurately and professionally.
- Apply reflective techniques towards developing a practice of continual professional self-improvement.
Assessment Tasks:
- Work Integrated Learning Placement Performance Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: PASS/FAIL% (Hurdle: True)
- Mastery of Advanced Skills Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: PASS/FAIL% (Hurdle: True)
- Caselog Hurdle In-person Online: PASS/FAIL% (Hurdle: True)
- Assessment Sequence: Clinical Principles Questions Online: 45% (15% each)% (Hurdle: False)
- Clinical Case Report: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Case Presentation Identity Verified In-person: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Contact 30 Hours/ Semester 30C

Subject: Honours Research Project in Veterinary Technology (VETS6631)
Credit Points: 4
Overview: This course allows veterinary technicians to undertake their own research project under the supervision of an experienced academic. With the help of their supervisor, students will design a study, collect and analyse data and write up their study as a small thesis. They will also present their study as a seminar to their peers and academics. This will provide the student with an appreciation of the research process and the concept of evidence-based medicine that underpins clinical practice. Students also receive training in research skills that enables them to pursue further research study. Students commencing in Semester 1 full-time enrol in VETS6631 for 2 consecutive semesters. Students commencing in Semester 2 full-time enrol in VETS6632 for 2 consecutive semesters.
Learning Outcomes:
- Have the ability to perform self-directed, original and independent research
- Display skills in time management, problem solving and organisation
- Have a sound understanding of the selected field of research and highlight areas which require further research and innovation
- Have the ability to critically evaluate other studies in this area of research as well as critically evaluate and highlight any problems in this study
- Display skills in experimental design and appropriate statistical analysis and able to draw valid conclusions from the analysis
- Convey and communicate ideas in a number of different ways (verbal and written)
- Be able to work as part of a functioning laboratory and part of a university system and be able to work effectively with others as part of a cohesive research team
Assessment Tasks:
- Learning Contract Hurdle Identity Verified: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- Project Proposal Seminar Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 10%% (Hurdle: True)
- Honours Seminar Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 25%% (Hurdle: True)
- Honours Thesis Hurdle: 50%% (Hurdle: True)
- Supervisor Evaluation Identity Verified: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Contact 12 Hours/ Fortnight 12 C/F

Subject: Honours Research Project in Veterinary Technology (VETS6632)
Credit Points: 4
Overview: This course allows veterinary technicians to undertake their own research project under the supervision of an experienced academic. With the help of their supervisor, students will design a study, collect and analyse data and write up their study as a small thesis. They will also present their study as a seminar to their peers and academics. This will provide the student with an appreciation of the research process and the concept of evidence-based medicine that underpins clinical practice. Students also receive training in research skills that enables them to pursue further research study. Students commencing in Semester 1 full-time enrol in VETS6631 for 2 consecutive semesters. Students commencing in Semester 2 full-time enrol in VETS6632 for 2 consecutive semesters.
Learning Outcomes:
- Cultivate and demonstrate the skills, knowledge and attitudes required to perform self-directed, original, independent, ethical research.
- Apply skills in communication, time management, problem solving and organisation to meet project milestones and work effectively in a research team.
- Objectively analyse current knowledge in your field and use innovative and novel approaches to address gaps in knowledge and/or revise methodologies.
- Apply detailed understanding, and demonstrate developing mastery of your research field to evaluate literature and optimise project design, critically and objectively.
- Utilise appropriate experimental design and statistical analysis to critically analyse and draw accurate conclusions from your research.
- Summarise and communicate your research effectively to a broad scientific audience, in written (thesis presentation) and verbal (seminar presentation) formats.
