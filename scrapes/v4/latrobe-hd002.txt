### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Apply knowledge and skills to demonstrate understanding and competency of critical academic skills essential to successful university learning.
**2.**
Identify and discuss fundamental knowledge related to health disciplines relevant to future university studies.
**3.**
Apply a range of professional communication skills to communicate with clients and other health care professionals.
**4.**
Identify the key principles of evidence based practice as it relates to the provision of health care.
**5.**
Recognise and illustrate key human bioscience concepts and systems.

Credit Points: 0
Overview: Wominjeka La Trobe is a short one-hour compulsory online subject that introduces all La Trobe students to Indigenous Australian history, culture and customs. A rich and relevant cultural heritage education is important for everyone. This topic highlights the link between Indigenous knowledge and values and broader cultural literacy. - Demonstrate knowledge of and respect for Indigenous Australian perspectives and experiences, history, culture and customs, including an appreciation of the local people and country

- Understand the need for and value of appropriate and effective intercultural communication

Subject: ANATOMY FOR PHYSIOTHERAPY A (ANA1003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will be introduced to foundational concepts and terminology for the study of human anatomy and biomechanics. These concepts will then be applied in detail to the study of the lower limb, as relevant to physiotherapy. Teaching will include both online learning and face-to-face teaching sessions including practical classes involving cadaver materials and clinical anatomy. You will be required to work together in small groups, to take responsibility for your own learning and understanding of anatomy so that you are able to identify anatomical structure and function on a living body as you would in a clinical physiotherapy context.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify musculoskeletal structures within the lower limb through surface anatomy.
- Apply relevant anatomical principles to describe normal structure and function of body systems within the lower limb, including its role in support, locomotion and posture.
- Apply observational and communication skills, individually or as part of a team, using appropriate anatomical and lay terminology for use with clients or other health care professionals, to make accurate observations of anatomical structures or events and infer their relationship to function using cadaveric material, anatomical models, the living body or medical imaging.
- Apply foundational biomechanical principles, including the concepts of force, motion and leverage, to analyse human movement within the lower limb.

Subject: HUMAN BIOSCIENCES A (HBS1HBA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject introduces you to human physiology - how the body works. You'll learn how the body is organised and the basics of cell structure and function, and how processes in the body are controlled. This will provide the foundation for the study of the major body systems important for support and movement (muscular system); regulation, integration and control (nervous and endocrine systems); fluids and transport (blood, heart, cardiovascular and immune systems); energy, maintenance and environmental exchange (respiratory, digestive and renal systems, metabolism); and the continuity of life (reproductive systems).
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the basic physiology of major organ systems of the human body.
- Apply knowledge of physiology concepts to clinical and disease states.
- Demonstrate oral and written communication skills.
- Demonstrate the skills for self-managing, reflection, and successful learning at university.
- Describe the structure and function of body cells, tissues, and organs.
- Apply specialist terms and language associated with physiology and anatomy to describe the human body.

Subject: FROM BAD SCIENCE TO GOOD HEALTH (HLT1001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: We are increasingly faced with controversial debates occurring in our media, with different groups arguing their viewpoints, and the scientific evidence is often swamped by misreporting or distorted evidence. This subject uses a case-based approach to examine the evidence underpinning current topics of debate in healthcare, and the motivations behind research and reporting of research, including the scientific process, peer-review, conflict of interest confirmation bias and cultural identity. This subject introduces skills in research that minimize bias, and are important in understanding and analysing evidence, including systematic approaches to acquiring evidence, critical appraisal of the literature and descriptive and inferential statistics.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify psychological characteristics and reasoning fallacies that contribute to our susceptibility to believe non-evidence based health claims.
- Effectively search for evidence of the effects of a health product or treatment and evaluate the presence of characteristics that are necessary for a fair test of effectiveness.
- Examine factors that drive the promotion of health products and treatments, question the plausibility of these claims, and understand the importance of evidence-based healthcare
- Communicate effectively and with respect for diversity in opinions to explain complex concepts in understanding evidence-based health.

Subject: PHYSIOTHERAPY SKILLS AND REASONING 1 (PTY1001)
Credit Points: 30
Overview: In this subject, you will be introduced to the profession of physiotherapy. You will learn about the determinants of health, their influence on the wellbeing of individuals, communities and populations, and the role of a physiotherapist in promoting health and wellbeing for people from a variety of backgrounds. You will begin to create your professional identity, critical thinking and curiosity as a healthcare practitioner and reflect on personal and professional behaviour as governed by codes of conduct and relevant legislation. You will integrate your developing knowledge of anatomy, physiology and biomechanics principles to develop skills relating to physiotherapy for lower limb conditions, including how to assess clients, develop client-centred problem lists, and perform basic treatment for soft tissue injuries. You will learn strategies to communicate effectively and respectfully with clients from a variety of backgrounds, and clinical reasoning skills to interpret information to support the planning of tailored and culturally responsive physiotherapy care.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe and demonstrate basic treatment approaches for soft tissue injuries, with a focus on the lower limb.
- Reflect on personal and colleagues' professional behaviour based on relevant legislation, ethical standards and codes of conduct.
- Apply clinical reasoning to analyse and interpret physiotherapy assessment findings and develop problem lists for lower limb presentations.
- Identify the determinants of health and explain how they influence health and wellbeing of individuals, communities and populations.
- Demonstrate and apply effective subjective and objective physiotherapy assessments that are culturally responsive and relevant to specific clinical contexts, with a focus on the lower limb.
- Demonstrate professional practice and communication skills that are applicable to physiotherapy management of lower limb conditions and important to culturally safe practice, including verbal, interpersonal, reflective and written skills.
Workload:

Subject: PHYSIOTHERAPY SKILLS AND REASONING 2 (PTY1002)
Credit Points: 30
Overview: In this subject you will further develop your knowledge of physiotherapy in Australia, including the history of the profession, scope of practice and funding models. You will learn the importance of managing your own physical and mental wellbeing as a health professional. You will lay foundations for key physiotherapy principles including cultural safety, evidence-based practice and interprofessional collaborative practice. You will undertake work integrated learning that will provide real-world context for your exploration of these professional themes. You will integrate your developing knowledge of anatomy, physiology and biomechanics principles to develop skills relating to physiotherapy for upper limb and spinal conditions, including how to assess clients, develop client-centred problem lists, and perform basic treatment for soft tissue injuries. You will continue to refine your communication and clinical reasoning skills, to support the planning of tailored and culturally responsive physiotherapy care. This subject includes professional learning, practical experiences or portfolio activities which contribute to the completion of the Career Ready AdvantageTM award.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply clinical reasoning to analyse and interpret physiotherapy assessment findings and develop problem lists for upper limb and spinal presentations.
- Demonstrate professional practice and communication skills that are applicable to physiotherapy management of upper limb and spinal conditions and important to culturally safe practice, including verbal, interpersonal, reflective and written skills.
- Demonstrate and apply effective subjective and objective physiotherapy assessments that are culturally responsive and relevant to specific clinical contexts, with a focus on the upper limb and spine.
- Describe and demonstrate basic treatment approaches for soft tissue injuries, with a focus on the upper limb and spine.
- Apply concepts of cultural safety, evidence-based practice, interprofessional collaborative practice, and managing your own physical and mental health, as they relate to your role as a future physiotherapist.
- Discuss physiotherapy practice in Australia, including scope of practice and funding models.
