## Graduate Diploma of Clinical Trials
##### M5036
##### 48 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
Graduate Diploma of Clinical Trials is a comprehensive program designed to equip you with the essential knowledge and skills required to design, conduct, and analyse clinical trials. This course is particularly beneficial for healthcare professionals, researchers, and those looking to transition into the clinical trials industry.

Clinical trials are the backbone of evidence-based medicine, playing a pivotal role in the development of new therapies, drugs, and medical devices. Clinical trials contribute to scientific knowledge and medical advancements, addressing unmet medical needs and leading to innovative healthcare solutions. They are essential for advancing medical research, enhancing patient care, and ensuring that healthcare interventions are based on solid scientific evidence.

This course prepares health professionals to contribute effectively to this vital field, ensuring the continuous improvement and innovation in healthcare.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Demonstrate autonomy, expert judgement, adaptability and responsibility in the conduct of clinical trials.
**2.**
Communicate essential principles underpinning the safe and ethical conduct of clinical trials.
**3.**
Apply specialist knowledge and skills in a clinical trial setting.
**4.**
Formulate clinical trial operations that align with The International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use (ICH) - Guideline for Good Clinical Practice (GCP) ICH-GCP frameworks.
**5.**
Design new, and critical appraise existing, clinical trials and clinical trial methodology.

Subject: Demographic methods (MPH5214)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit gives you a solid foundation in formal demography, where quantitative data and techniques are used to calculate demographic measures. Topics include sources of demographic data and assessment of their quality; calculation and interpretation of basic measures of fertility, mortality and migration; population dynamics, growth and ageing; life tables; Indigenous demography; and population projections. Learning activities include hands-on calculation and interpretation of demographic measures using real-world data. You are expected to be familiar with Microsoft Excel.
Learning Outcomes:
- Choose appropriate demographic methods to calculate demographic measures.
- Interpret demographic measures in the context of changing populations.
- Calculate and graphically present demographic measures. .
- Identify, locate and critically evaluate quality of different types of population data.
- Effectively communicate demographic measures and their interpretation in written, tabular and graphical form to a lay or policy audience.
Assessment Tasks:
- Population projections (1,500 words or equivalent): 25% (Hurdle: None)
- Lexis diagrams and life tables (900 words or equivalent): 15% (Hurdle: None)
- Migration and family formation (1,500 words or equivalent): 25% (Hurdle: None)
- Standardisation (600 words or equivalent): 10% (Hurdle: None)
- Indigenous demography and models of population structure (1,500 words or equivalent): 25% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 6 hours of teacher directed learning activities and 6 hours of self-directed study per week. 12 hours of self-directed study per week.

Subject: Principles of health care quality improvement (MPH5267)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this unit you will learn how historical, political & social factors impact on quality measurement in health care; the relationship of industrial and health care quality monitoring; epidemiological and statistical quality measurement principles; the strengths and limitations of current monitoring techniques; different sources of health care quality data; principles of clinical indicator programs; adverse event monitoring, satisfaction surveys and benchmarking; and the relationship between evidence based medicine, clinical practice guidelines and quality improvement. You will appreciate the complexities in designing, implementing and evaluating quality improvement programs in the clinical setting. The content aligns well with other management units in the program such as MPH5266 (Clinical Leadership and Management) and MPH5020 (Introduction to Biostatistics and Epidemiology). It is a core unit of the Graduate Diploma and Master of Health Services Management.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the key driving forces that underpin the need for healthcare quality improvement.
- Contribute to debates on safety and quality problems and potential solutions.
- Formulate practical solutions to safety and quality problems leading to improvement in a healthcare context.
- Apply safety and quality literature, research findings to healthcare practice.
- Apply quality improvement tools and methods to healthcare systems, processes and outcomes.
Assessment Tasks:
- Written report (2,400 words): 40% (Hurdle: Threshold)
- Critical reflections (5 x 480 words each. Total 2,400 words): 40% (Hurdle: Threshold)
- Letter to the editor (Letter x 600 words and supporting rationale x 600 words): 20% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 15 contact hours over 2 block day On average 10 hours per week to participate in online tasks and complete assignments

Subject: Financial management in health care (MPH5268)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The provision of effective healthcare is an essential component of contemporary society, however with people living longer and community expectations of and demand for healthcare increasing at an unprecedented rate, the financial costs of healthcare are growing at an ever increasing rate. Together with rapid advances in clinical capabilities and technological sophistication, meeting these social and financial challenges has never been more challenging. In this unit you will examine the basic accounting, financial reporting, budgeting and financial business planning concepts applicable to the healthcare sector, and how they are used by healthcare professionals to aid strategic, operational and service delivery decision making.
Learning Outcomes:
- Interpret operational and capital budgeting processes, and critique their importance in submissions for major items of capital expenditure
- Apply accounting terminologies, concepts and processes that underpin the preparation of key financial statements
- Appraise the various internal management reports typically used in health care organisations and evaluate their use in the day to day management of organisational sub units
- Analyse the statutory financial statements prepared and used by public and private sector health care providers
- Critically analyse the importance of business planning and its role in organisational growth and development
- Review the financial reporting requirements of the regulatory framework that applies to the healthcare sector and assess how they impact the preparation and use of financial reporting systems
Assessment Tasks:
- Case study analysis and reflection (2,400 words): 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Report (1,400 words): 40% (Hurdle: Competency)
- Essay (1,200 words): 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Audio visual presentation (10 mins): 10% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 12 hours of self-directed study per week, plus 3 block days.

Subject: Practical data management (MPH5277)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Managing health data has never been more challenging or significant. Data now plays a pivotal role in health care provision, discovery and research. As health data becomes more widely accessible, it is essential that health data users can identify high quality data and understand the implications of poor data management. In this unit, you will explore health data management using a practical, skills-based approach. You will be guided through the process and pitfalls of health data management from project inception to final data analysis for a hypothetical study. Practical skills will be developed through the completion of weekly data management tasks and coding exercises. Topics covered include: data management for project set-up, data collection design and pitfalls, data querying and cleaning, handling tricky data, interim data reporting and preparing for data analysis.
Learning Outcomes:
- Design and construct interim data reports
- Apply new knowledge of data quality and monitoring to synthesize an analysis data set
- Apply knowledge and skills of user experience to create a data set handbook
- Interpret the role of data management in identifying and supporting operational KPIs
- Develop and implement a data management workflow from a project protocol
- Summarise the barriers to data quality and the impact upon analysis
Assessment Tasks:
- Data set portfolio (1,500 words, plus data dictionary): 25% (Hurdle: None)
- Operational data report (1,500 words, plus data tables): 25% (Hurdle: None)
- Weekly coding exercises: 50% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 6 hours of teacher-directed learning activities per week, and 6 hours of self-directed study per week, plus 3 on-campus Block Days.
