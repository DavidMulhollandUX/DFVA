## Bachelor of Social Work (Honours) - Bendigo
##### HHSWB

You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
As a social worker, you'll provide support and assistance to people and families in need. La Trobe's Bachelor of Social Work (Honours) gives you the clinical knowledge and hands-on skills to launch your career.

You'll learn from world-renowned academics about promoting human rights and the importance of social justice. Our diverse range of subjects provide foundations in both psychology and sociology. Two 14-week placements enable you to put your knowledge and skills into practice and gain professional experience within leading health and welfare organisations in Victoria.

You'll learn:

  * Sociology and society  * Discover the cultural changes that shaped Australian society and how individuals connect with each other.

  * Introductory psychology  * Study they key areas of psychology and explore how diverse contexts affect individuals in different ways. Discover how your brain helps to create your behaviour, and how your brain and behaviour change across your lifespan.

  * Human services  * Explore the values, ethics and practice of social work and human services.

  * Social work and inequality  * Study the issues of inequality and their impact on people's wellbeing.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Critically evaluate knowledge and social work practice using appropriate technologies to support continued professional development and ethical practice
**2.**
Apply complex theoretical and technical social work knowledge and skills to critically analyse multifaceted situations to empower individuals, groups and communities
**3.**
Advocate for progressive social change including global political concerns around environmental challenges particularly as they impact human rights, social and economic justice for those most vulnerable
**4.**
Articulate Western and First Nations worldviews with a critical appraisal of ongoing impacts of colonisation and identify benefits of First Nations relational ways of knowing for all
**5.**
Design, implement and evaluate consumer and community informed research, projects and programs and creatively influence innovative future practice, social reform and policy
**6.**
Articulate a professional social work identity, both independently and as a member or leader of a team in a multidisciplinary work environment
**7.**
Plan, execute and interpret the findings of project work or research with independence
**8.**
Communicate knowledge, research and practice effectively to a wide variety of audiences using a variety of media
**9.**
Practice Social Work in a holistic, ethical and critically reflective manner

### Course features
### Work based learning (placement) requirements
Compulsory placements (Work Based Learning)
During this course, you will participate in one or more supervised Work Based Learning (WBL) placements designed to allow you to practice newly acquired skills, behaviours and knowledge while integrating theory with practice. The placements will immerse you in professional work culture, enabling you to develop and further enhance your skills. WBL provides you with the opportunity to gain valuable industry experience at one or more of our local, interstate or international industry partners or agencies. WBL placements will generally be arranged by the Placements Operation Team or Experiential Learning Team at La Trobe University. The number of hours undertaken can vary considerably, depending on the requirements of the course. Students may incur additional costs and it may be necessary to travel or relocate to complete a specific placement. Please refer to the individual subject entries for more information.
### Work integrated learning opportunities
Industry informed curriculum, Industry-informed projects, Career development learning, Professional simulations
### Career outcomes
Graduates can find employment in private industries and the public sector, including:

  * Community health centres and hospitals
  * Work alongside other health professionals to provide practical support, counselling, information and emotional support to clients.

  * Government departments and non-government agencies
  * Provide case management, counselling and advocacy for clients, including children and families.

  * Mental health and disability services
  * Provide recovery-oriented practice and support, advocate for policy changes, raise awareness on social issues and connect clients with human services.

- Describe guiding principles, philosophies and values that contribute to respectful and inclusive delivery of health and human services to people with developmental disabilities.
- Identify and describe the capabilities of people with varying degrees of physical and/or cognitive disabilities, and identify the supports they require.
- Compare the roles of various allied health and human services professionals in supporting the health, wellbeing and social inclusion of people with developmental disabilities.
- Explain what it means to have a developmental disability.

Subject: SOCIETY AND CULTURE: AN INTRODUCTION TO SOCIOLOGY (SOC1SAC)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Every society has its own dominant norms, expectations, and assumptions about the ‘good life’ and the ‘right’ ways to behave in our everyday lives. In this engaging and accessible introductory subject, open to all students, we challenge these views by demonstrating how ‘common sense’ claims are always open to debate. For example, is the ‘Australian Dream’ a reality? Is Australia really ‘the Lucky Country’ and an egalitarian society, where everybody gets ‘a fair go’? Can anybody succeed in Australia, and who is left behind? This subject examines these fundamental questions about inequality, power, and our different ‘life chances’, as well as the question of how we sustain social cohesion and meaningful lives amid ongoing cultural, political, economic, and technological change. We explore this through exciting and relevant case studies on consumption, technology, health, gender, sexuality, race, ethnicity, class, status, age, migration, education, religion, popular culture and more. By questioning taken for granted assumptions about Australian society, we can exercise our ‘sociological imagination’ to see our social worlds in new ways. These analytical capabilities in seeing possibilities for change are crucial in practically all vocations. This subject thus not only enables you to see your daily life in new ways, but will equip you with valuable research, problem-solving, and critical thinking skills vital for future employment.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate a sociological understanding of social relationships and institutions, patterns of social diversity and inequality, and the processes that underpin social change and stability
- Adapt sociological theories, concepts and evidence to analysis of local and global issues
- Evaluate diverse sociological evidence, positions and arguments
- Critically review, analyse, summarise and synthesise sociological scholarship including quantitative and qualitative data

Subject: BEING A SOCIAL WORKER (SWP1BSW)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this first year subject, you will be provided with an overview of the theoretical framework that guides the La Trobe social work course. You will be introduced to the key aspects of how social work is understood, particularly what it means to be a social worker and how social work is expressed across its many domains of practice (from work with individuals to community work and advocacy) and fields of practice (such as child and family wellbeing, health and disability). You will explore the historical development of social work including contested ideas about how social work is practiced, as well as locating yourself in respect of your own values and beliefs; and how these fit with social work's ethical and values framework. You will explore the concept of social justice from a critical, First nations and green social work perspective. You will also be oriented to university study, specifically the development of research, writing and oral communication skills as well as working collaboratively with other students. This subject includes professional learning, practical experiences or portfolio activities which contribute to the completion of the Career Ready AdvantageTM award.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify LTU social work’s core theoretical framework and begin to engage with critical social work theory and how it applies across multiple social work practice domains.
- Describe personal values and how these fit with the social work profession and identity as a social worker, including the concept of social justice.
- Demonstrate a beginners understanding of the historical development of social work and the contested debates, such as the influence of colonisation, that have underpinned the discipline's development.
- Demonstrate research, oral and written communication and teamwork skills required for first year social work study.
- Identify how sociological and psychological perspectives relate to social work

Subject: REFLECTIVE COMMUNICATION AND SOCIAL WORK (SWP1REF)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Social workers and educators aim to empower others and to help create the conditions for people to thrive. This involves working for a socially just and equitable society and also helping people to better manage the day to day challenges they inevitably confront in their lives. Yet we all experience challenges in life and there will be times we need to deal with problems that seem overwhelming and outside our control. SWP1REF takes as its premise that working to help other people involves first understanding ourselves and building self-awareness and personal skills to deal with our own life situations. In this subject you are introduced to the concept of empowerment and the role this plays in enabling health and wellbeing even in the most challenging and difficult situations. The subject draws from an evidence based Aboriginal Family Wellbeing empowerment program that has been integrated within a wide range of health human services across Australia and internationally. Students will experience the program as both a participant and a facilitator. This subject includes professional learning, practical experiences or portfolio activities which contribute to the completion of the Career Ready AdvantageTM award.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate skills in creative collaboration and teamwork
- Conceptualise and analyse the concepts of empowerment and wellbeing.
- Utilise critical reflection to enhance communication in personal and professional spheres
- Critically reflect on personal values, knowledge and skills.
- Apply group work intervention informed by First Nation relational ways of knowing

Subject: INTRODUCTION TO DIVERSITY (SWP2IDV)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will explore and analyse social work practice in a diverse society. Of particular consideration will be how a sense of 'otherness' or 'difference' is created and maintained. Three broad arenas of 'difference' will be featured from amongst the many forms of social and cultural diversity. Learning Outcomes:
- Explain how social structures, worldviews, international and national development and policy responses impact on the experiences of individuals and groups in terms of marginalization and othering.
- Compare and contrast social work theories and practice when working with individuals and families, in organizations and communities in terms of working with difference
- Identify how personal experiences influence understanding of what 'difference'; means and how it may contribute to creating a sense of 'difference' or 'otherness'
- Describe possible strategies for engaging in culturally competent practice with diverse populations.

Subject: THEORIES FOR SOCIAL WORK (SWP2TSW)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will further explore La Trobe University Social Work's core theoretical framework of critical social theory and how it informs social work practice. You will also be introduced to a range of other theories used by social workers to situate their experiences and the experiences of those they work with in a particular social context and physical environment. You will be taught to develop a beginning capacity to understand and engage with different ideas about the world and how it is perceived, how to understand social issues and human behaviour and how to engage with these in practice. More specifically you will explore various discourses (beliefs about the world and related values), explanatory theories (which seek to explain social issues and experience of them) and practice theories and approaches (how explanatory theories are expressed in practice).
Learning Outcomes:
- Illustrate how theories can reinforce each other (e.g. ecofeminism or provide useful contrasts (e.g. psychodynamic compared to strengths).
- Explain and discuss key discourses, explanatory theories and practice theories and approaches in social work
- Review range of social work practice theories
- Distinguish critical theory in social work and the difference between discourses, explanatory theories, practice theories and practice approaches.

Subject: FIELDS OF PRACTICE A (SWP3001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will be introduced to the theory and knowledge base of two fields of social work practice, child & family welfare and family violence. Your introduction to social work practice in these fields will recognise the importance of respect for and enabling human rights, and taking a person centred approach. You will be introduced to the key debates, policies, programs, service delivery and skills required to undertake beginning level practice in both fields.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critique the success of policy developments in child protection and family violence and the drivers that have led to policy shifts.
- Analyse and evaluate the construction of child protection and family violence as social problems to apply practice frameworks and interventions.
- Synthesise and apply research outcomes and relevant psychosocial knowledge and skills to case management
- Analyse the conceptualisation of child protection and family violence practice over time including the values that underpin these conceptualisations.

Subject: SOCIAL WORK SKILLS FOR PRACTICE (SWP3APB)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: You are expected to play an active role in group-based learning and participate in exercises to develop social work skills: A client here may be an individual, family, group, colleague or community member. Fundamental to this subject is the development of a critical awareness of self, including your style as a worker and the impact of this style on others. The key aspects of this subject are group work, with a focus on individuals roles within the group; and casework practice, with an emphasis on exploring your styles in the application of world views and theories to interviewing skills including assessment, intervention plans and case recording. Professional accreditation requires a minimum level of fieldwork practice. Your attendance at tutorials in this subject will contribute to meeting this requirement for professional recognition as a social worker.
Learning Outcomes:
- Formulate clear and effective social work interviews, to develop intervention plans and appropriate documentation.
- Apply, with judgment, a critical social work lens to practice and apply other theories to practice when using these skills with individuals, families, groups or communities.
- Analyse theoretical concepts and social work practice skills to distinguish effective group work including group facilitation.
- Reflect on a First Nations perspective on ways of knowing, being and doing.
- Appraise personal ideas and values in relation to theory to improve client outcomes.

Subject: INCLUSION IN COMMUNITIES (SWP3IIC)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will focus upon critically reflective social work practice at community and societal levels and explore the conceptual and theoretical underpinnings of community development including principles such as participation, human rights and empowerment. This broad focus will cover social, economic and environmental justice and political and cultural awareness and how these impact on individual and community development. The emphasis for creating social change is grounded in understanding the impact of ideologies and on strategic interventions, incorporating a strengths perspective, community organisation, social action, social policy and planning approaches. You will examine and develop skills in community development as a framework for engaging people, communities, and organisations in promoting community building and social well-being.
Learning Outcomes:
- Appraise community development approaches including contextually appropriate strategies and techniques.
- Compare and analyse communities using a critical theory perspective and First Nations worldviews to understand and assess their relevant contexts.
- Distinguish and examine the key roles and skills necessary to engage in effective community development processes and initiatives in a variety of contexts
- Critically review personal values, knowledge and skills and how these impact on working with communities and related groups

Subject: PROFESSIONAL PRACTICE A (SWP3PPA)
Credit Points: 45
Overview: In this subject you will be provided with experiential learning opportunities to further integrate theory with practice, to demonstrate appropriate professional relationships, and gain working knowledge of organisational contexts. Through this professionally supervised practice experience you will be provided with constructive feedback on your performance and will be challenged to critically self-reflect on your effectiveness. You will have opportunities to further develop skills in basic interviewing, assessment and intervention processes required for practice along with skills in research. Professional competencies will be developed across nine key learning areas and will be assessed against the standard of competency at a beginning level with support. Professional accreditation requires a minimum level of fieldwork practice. Your attendance at workshops and professional placements in this subject will contribute to meeting this requirement for professional recognition as a social worker. This subject includes professional learning, practical experiences or portfolio activities which contribute to the completion of the Career Ready AdvantageTM award.
Learning Outcomes:
- Examine the impact of complex organizational contexts on practice.
- Critically review professional knowledge, values and skills to employ as a responsible and adaptable practitioner.
- Combine social work knowledge, values and skills to solve a range of complex social work problems
- Appraise professional knowledge and skills in the context of the Australian Association of Social Workers Practice Standards with accountability as a practitioner.

Subject: FIELDS OF PRACTICE B (SWP4001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will have the opportunity to develop your knowledge and skills base in a specialty field of social work practice. There will be four streams in this subject, which is offered online. This subject builds on prior knowledge and experiences gained throughout the course and is designed to enable you to enrich and add breadth and depth to your understanding of the chosen field of practice. You will have the opportunity to further develop your appreciation of the importance of respect for, and enabling of, human rights and taking a person-centred approach. You will be introduced to the key debates, policies, programs, service delivery and skills required to undertake beginning level practice in the chosen specialty area/field of practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and analyse key policy developments in the specialty area and become familiar with the drivers that have led to these policy shifts
- Apply social work research evidence to contemporary social work discourse related to the specialty area
- Critically review the various ways in which knowledge and practice in this specialty area has evolved.
- Critically review personal and professional values and ethics, psychosocial knowledge and skills, and their relation to social work practice in the specialty area
- Apply social work theoretical and practice frameworks to contemporary practice in the specialty area

Subject: SOCIAL WORK SKILLS C (SWP4APC)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will build on the knowledge and skills acquired in previous subjects and in your first field placement. In this final year of study, you will focus on extending your ability to integrate a critical theory perspective as well as other key theoretical approaches to social work practice at a deeper level. On completion, you will have been provided with the opportunity to work more confidently with individuals and families experiencing complex problems. You will further analyse practice using critically reflective processes, knowledge and skills. You will actively engage in seminar groups including role plays and will be expected to play an active part in group development and maintenance. Professional accreditation requires a minimum level of fieldwork practice. Your attendance at workshops in this subject will contribute to meeting this requirement for professional recognition as a social worker. This subject includes professional learning, practical experiences or portfolio activities which contribute to the completion of the Career Ready AdvantageTM award.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply social work knowledge and skills to demonstrate well-developed judgement, knowledge adaptability and responsibility as a social worker
- Integrate and apply social work theory and knowledge when undertaking an assessment, intervention, interview or occasion of service in complex social work practice situations.
- Critically analyse and describe personal knowledge, skills, beliefs and values as they relate to one's own future social work practice and determine areas for further development in direct social work practice with individuals and families.

Subject: SOCIAL POLICY & ADVOCACY (SWP4SPA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will further explore the knowledge and skills learnt in previous years, required to make an informed and active contribution to social policy development and advocacy. Active engagement in policy processes and advocacy is a core requirement of all social workers, and you will be taught to develop competence, confidence and commitment to this domain of practice. Your theoretical knowledge of political institutions and processes of policy making, political ideology, social welfare history, policy analysis, and policy advocacy will be explored. You will be taught practical skills relevant to policy analysis, advocacy and report writing. The knowledge and skills developed through the subject will be integrated with broader social work knowledge and values and First Nations principles to inform the critical analysis of contemporary social policy and the formulation of proposals for more appropriate and just policy responses.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse contemporary social policy using policy analysis frameworks and social work values
- Apply relevant knowledge to inform policy development and change
- Critically review the historical, institutional, ideological and environmental contexts that shape Australian social policies
- Integrate knowledge of policy and advocacy theory to formulate alternative policy responses and advocacy strategies.
