## Master of Art Therapy - Melbourne (Bundoora)
##### HMART
##### 240 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
Prepare for a unique career combining art and counselling in clinical, therapeutic, or community settings with the only postgraduate specialist degree of its kind in Australia, La Trobe's accredited Master of Art Therapy.

Get the right mix of technical and people skills to meet both industry and client needs with evidence-based learning aligned with global benchmarks of art therapy practice. Learn from leading practitioners, academics, and researchers in immersive and interactive face-to-face workshops in our purpose-built art therapy studio. Benefit from our long-established industry partnerships and work-integrated learning opportunities with prestigious organisations like the Peter MacCallum Cancer Centre, Austin Health, and The Royal Children's Hospital.

By completing La Trobe's Master of Art Therapy, you will:

  * master a balance of theoretical and practical approaches, including a critical awareness of current issues in art therapy practice
  * gain expertise across clinical mental health, counselling, treatment planning, program design and evaluation, leadership, communication and cultural sensitivity
  * understand the different approaches to art therapy for adults, children and groups
  * graduate ready to apply for membership with the Australian Counselling Association (ACA) and the Australian, New Zealand and Asian Creative Arts Therapies Association (ANZACATA).

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Apply critical thinking and judgement to complex practice issues informed by theory and research literacy
**2.**
Contribute as an effective practitioner with strong communication skills within a multidisciplinary team and broader family and consumer contexts
**3.**
Practice in a reflexive and flexible professional manner, aware of the responsibilities and boundaries of role
**4.**
Practice with advanced integrated specialised skills applicable with a wide range of clients, based in contemporary theory and evidence-based knowledge in art therapy
**5.**
Practice with integrity, informed by an awareness of culturally and socially attuned values and issues, cognisant of ethical and legal implications
**6.**
Design a systematic intervention model of practice and its evaluation, appropriate to a specific client population

### Course features
### Work based learning (placement) requirements
Compulsory placements (Work Based Learning)
During this course, you will participate in one or more supervised Work Based Learning (WBL) placements designed to allow you to practice newly acquired skills, behaviours and knowledge while integrating theory with practice. The placements will immerse you in professional work culture, enabling you to develop and further enhance your skills. WBL provides you with the opportunity to gain valuable industry experience at one or more of our local, interstate or international industry partners or agencies. WBL placements will generally be arranged by the Office of the Provost Placements and WBL Team at La Trobe University. The number of hours undertaken can vary considerably, depending on the requirements of the course. Students may incur additional costs and it may be necessary to travel or relocate to complete a specific placement. Please refer to the individual subject entries for more information.
### Career outcomes
After graduation, you’ll be qualified to practice as an art therapist in a range of settings, including:

  * hospitals and other health and wellbeing providers
  * community outreach and crisis centres
  * schools and universities
  * aged and palliative care
  * correctional facilities
  * rehabilitation and pastoral services
  * museums and galleries.

The unique mix of knowledge and skills in art therapy means graduates often create a job for themselves but common roles include:

  * art therapist, counsellor or facilitator
  * case manager
  * senior clinician.

Subject: ART THERAPY PLACEMENT AND SUPERVISION A (PHE4005)
Credit Points: 30
Overview: In this subject you will participate in group supervision and complete work-based learning as a trainee Art Therapist over 250 hours plus a minimum of 22 hours of small group supervision totaling 272 hours. The 250 placement hours includes 90 hours of direct client contact. Attaining the requisite placement and supervision hours will enable you to meet the professional registration requirements of the Australian, New Zealand and Asian Creative Arts Therapies Association (ANZACATA) and the Australian Counselling Association (ACA). In addition to work based learning and supervision classes, you will participate in a range of activities to prepare for placement, including an expression of interest interview to support the placement allocation process, a Placement Preparation Workshop, and 7 individual workshops during the semester. Placement and Supervision is aimed to develop your skill base as a health professional in a clinical or community-based setting. You will be expected to deliver art therapy services that are tailored to the needs of the client group and agency framework, in collaboration with your site supervisor, agency colleagues and clients you will develop initial clinical practice skills including writing progress notes, application of ethical and legal principles and professional self care. To develop clinical competencies as a trainee art therapist, you will reflect upon clinical experience through art making and discussion with peers during small-group supervision class. Here you will explore strategies for managing this initial period of professional development within your placement setting, alongside other trainee art therapy students and facilitated by an art therapist-educator with clinical expertise. You will share and present material from your work with individual clients and groups, to begin to develop an understanding of evidence-based art therapy practice, clinical reasoning, client experience, therapeutic process, and the therapeutic relationship.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and implement evidence-based art therapy approaches responsive to client needs and service setting.
- Apply interpersonal skills and techniques that build effective, ethical therapeutic alliances with clients.
- Articulate clinical reasoning through documentation and synthesis of art therapy observations.
- Examine clinical strengths and limitations through reflective art making and supervisory feedback.
- Demonstrate professional practice skills as an art therapy trainee within a team, including accountability, integrity, and professional conduct.

Subject: ART THERAPY COUNSELLING SKILLS (PHE4ASA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject will provide you with foundational, intermediate and advanced art therapy counselling skills. Theoretical and experiential learning opportunities will build an effective and well-rounded counselling skill set for work as an art therapist. Emphasis is given to developing key therapeutic skills, including building the relationship, responding to client content and emotions, the intentional use of art-making in therapy, navigating challenges and complexities, embracing diversity and difference, among other competencies. A strengths-based and humanistic approach will be front of centre, but other models and theories will be harnessed to offer understandings of the variety of ways to work with clients. Hands on approaches will develop practical skills, including through role plays, peer work and group discussions. You will be offered many opportunities to think critically about stepping into a role as an art therapist.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate foundational art therapy counselling competencies, through the development of empathic observational skills, reflective listening and establishing therapeutic presence in art therapy
- Demonstrate reflexive capacities through developing a creative practice to support professional development and growth as a trainee art therapist
- Effectively communicate theoretical understandings of the foundations of the art therapy process in writing
- Critically examine the unique therapeutic processes and relational dynamics present in the application of art therapy

Subject: GROUP ART THERAPY (PHE4ASB)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject students will explore group art therapy through theoretical and experiential learning. The focus is on learning about experiential group art therapy, gaining an understanding of art making within a group context, an understanding of group development and the group dynamics process and issues of visual and verbal self disclosure. The impact of image making in groups on the individual student, the group process and the learning about group art therapy practice will be examined.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically analyse and appraise group art therapy literature and practice, including an advanced sense of best practice standards informed by the specific needs of diverse client populations.
- Demonstrate a professional capacity to engage in the experience of a training group and subsequently identify and explain relevant proficiencies in relation to self as a member of a learning group and self as a trainee art therapist.
- Demonstrate a reflective and relational capacity to understand group dynamics, through the design and implementation of practical group facilitation experiences.
- Effectively communicate a nuanced conceptualisation of the historic development of and contemporary practice of group art therapy through academic writing.

Subject: ART THERAPY WITH ADULTS (PHE4ATA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will learn the stages of adult development, examine and critique the diagnosis of a range of mental health disorders according to the DSM 5-TR, and develop skills in art therapy assessment appropriate to these populations. You will also develop an awareness of considerations of working with clients from cross-cultural and diverse backgrounds and identities. You will also demonstrate knowledge and increased art therapy skills in working with diverse populations of adults.

- Critically analyse and integrate cross-cultural perspectives on mental health and the application of art therapy in adult populations, using existing academic evidence.
- Identify and articulate key elements in adult mental health assessment, particularly using art therapy tools, in practical demonstrations.
- Communicate clinical information effectively in written and oral forms, individually and in groups.
- Work independently and in groups to research and present art therapy findings, evidenced in group projects and assignments.

Subject: ART THERAPY WITH CHILDREN (PHE4ATC)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will develop an understanding of theories and practices of art therapy with children and adolescents. You will learn about theories of typical and atypical child development and how children systematically develop the ability to express themselves using visual art. Art therapy assessments and interventions appropriate for use with children and adolescents will be presented and critiqued, with a focus on child-centered and culturally responsive practice. This will include working with the system of adults in a young person’s life, such as diverse families, welfare systems and school settings. Through the acquisition of art therapy clinical skills, you will gain the ability to practice art therapy with children from diverse cultures in various settings.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse child development theories and, in particular, graphic development.
- Evaluate subjective and objective information in child observation to generate fundamental self-awareness as a trainee art therapist.
- Critically analyse theories and approaches of art therapy assessment and intervention for a range of mental health issues, attachment patterns, ability needs, cultural differences and the impacts of trauma which present in childhood and adolescence.
- Critically engage and reflect upon art-making and play typical of childhood to develop self-reflective skills as a trainee art therapist.

- Critically review the psychological underpinnings of art therapy theories and evaluate their differences.

Subject: ART THERAPY LITERATURE REVIEW (PHE5ATL)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will examine, discuss and critique the literature from art therapy and research methods as it relates to art therapy practice. Diverse research methodologies will be presented and you will strengthen your critical appraisal skills as you analyse texts for quality, strengths and weaknesses. You will learn how to identify key themes across the research related to a chosen client population, and to write a literature review that examines this current evidence. Using what you have learned through this process, you will identify a gap or area of interest that will inspire a program proposal for a client population. Both assessment tasks in this subject (a literature review and program proposal ) are designed to support and further develop your capacity to think critically about client needs and how their needs intersect with the art therapy evidence base. The program proposal will form the foundation for the Final Project undertaken in Semester 2 (PHE5AFP).
Learning Outcomes:
- Complete a critical review of the art therapy literature on a specific topic to analyse an evidence base
- Develop an art therapy research proposal for a specific clinical setting and population including resourcing, and evaluation methods.
- Critically analyse a piece of research for its overall quality and contribution to an evidence base.
- Critically review relevant theory and practice models with an awareness of their theoretical frameworks, methodological orientation and argue their respective strengths and shortcomings.
- Critically reflect on researcher bias and relevance of a text to art therapy practice.

Subject: OPEN STUDIO AND POST-TRAUMATIC GROWTH (PHE5OST)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will learn about theoretical components of open studio practices art therapy, its frameworks of practice and also the concept of post traumatic growth.The subject will develop an understanding of how post traumatic growth can be supported in the open studio format of art therapy along with experiential learning through experiencing and hearing from trained art therapists about how they run their open studio workshops. In particular, you will learn about the role of art materials, processes and product in how they integrate with trauma informed theory to facilitate post-traumatic growth. The central vehicle for this clinical application will be the Open Studio method. You will be equipped with skills in facilitating this approach for clients in clinical settings as well as for people affected by traumatic issues in the general community.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply advanced art therapy interventions, including suitable use of materials to reduce trauma impacts for individuals and communities
- Differentiate the various forms of trauma relevant to different contexts and how these impact post-traumatic growth
- Articulate and critique the structure, form and purpose of the Open Studio method of art therapy
- Working collaboratively and independently, reflect and formulate an Open Studio art therapy program for professional practice.
- Demonstrate advanced skills in the application of the Open Studio method.
