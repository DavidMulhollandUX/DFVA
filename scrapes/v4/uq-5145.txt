UQ is the only university in Queensland, and one of only six universities in Australia, to offer a Master of Audiology Studies program.
Communication is vital to the quality of life of all individuals in our society. Audiologists play an essential role in improving hearing health and well-being in adults and children. Audiologists make a true difference to the lives of others and are in high demand due to their specialist skills in helping people with hearing difficulties.
Study the Master of Audiology Studies at UQ and you will learn from international leaders in the field with course content based on the latest research and developments in audiology. The program integrates the theoretical foundations of audiology with practical clinical skills in preparation for a career as a clinical audiologist.
UQ is known worldwide for the quality of its audiology graduates, the commitment of its teaching staff, and its strong research focus. As a graduate of the Master of Audiology Studies, you will be in high demand with employers due to your in-depth knowledge and hands-on, practical skills in audiology.

Subject: Clinical Audiology I (AUDL7111)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Acquire basic clinical skills through an introduction to audiological test procedures.
Learning Outcomes:
- Collect and synthesise case history, goals, expectations, needs and priorities, and information from other relevant sources to plan and conduct valid assessments by selecting the tools, equipment, and techniques that will address the needs of the non-complex client.
- Comply with relevant guidelines, standards, legal obligations, relevant workplace health and safety legislation, and workplace policies including compliance with infection control and risk management, in conducting hearing screening and assessments.
- Critically evaluate assessment results using knowledge, clinical judgement, test calibration and reliability considerations to formulate valid conclusions regarding the site of lesion and functionality of non-complex ear, hearing, and associated conditions.
- Synthesise information from a range of sources to develop recommendations for intervention, including audiological therapy, technology, modifications to the environment, and/or further referrals in line with the non-complex client’s goals and needs.
- Generate accurate and complete reports, case notes, and other forms of communication that conform with medical and legal requirements and appropriately communicate assessment results with client to facilitate informed decision making.
- Obtain informed consent from the client after appropriately communicating assessments and procedures, and collaborate with the client, significant others, professionals, and organisations to achieve the best outcomes for the client utilising the principles of client- and family-centred care.
Assessment Tasks:
- Practical reports: 36% 6% for each practical report% (Hurdle: False)
- Clinical masking test Identity Verified In-person: 14%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Theory exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 50% MUST PASS% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 3 Hours/ Week Practical 1.5 Hours/ Week

Subject: Professional Practice in Audiology I (AUDL7112)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Acquire professional practice skills through an introduction to professional behaviour, and working with and advocating for the needs of diverse communities in audiological settings.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify the foundational professional behaviour, communication, and collaboration requirements of audiological practice, reflect on personal strengths and areas of development, and engage with feedback to ensure continuous improvement.
- Adapt communication to meet the specific needs of each client, including Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples and those from other culturally and linguistically diverse backgrounds.
- Consider the culture and communication preferences of the Deaf community and identifies the need for an interpreter, where appropriate.
- Explain the importance of person- and family-centred practices for the provision of effective audiological care.
- Describe the workings of the Australian healthcare system (e.g., NDIS), including the range of professional roles in health-related settings.
- Identify barriers that can impede or prevent access to services and resources by those with ear, hearing, and associated conditions; and strategies to advocate for and partner with people within these communities.
- Identify strategies for raising the awareness of the communication needs of those who have, or are at risk of developing, ear, hearing, and associated conditions.
Assessment Tasks:
- Weekly in class quiz Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Critical reflective essay: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Advocacy project Team or group-based: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week 2L

Subject: Foundational Audiological Science (AUDL7113)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Examine the foundation sciences of acoustics, psychoacoustics, anatomy, and physiology that underpin the practice of clinical audiology.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain how sound is generated, transmitted, and measured.
- Explain how humans hear and common causes of hearing impairment across the lifespan.
- Describe how humans maintain balance and common causes of balance impairment across the lifespan.
- Describe how humans receive and express speech and language, and common causes of speech and language impairment, across the lifespan.
- Describe cognitive processes associated with hearing and common causes of cognitive impairment across the lifespan.
Assessment Tasks:

- Tutorial quizzes In-person: 20% (5% per Tutorial quiz)% (Hurdle: False)
