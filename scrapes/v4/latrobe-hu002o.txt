## Undergraduate Certificate in Psychological Science - On-Line
##### HU002O
##### 60 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
If you're passionate about understanding the human mind and how it drives behaviour, La Trobe's Undergraduate Certificate in Psychological Science provides you with the foundational knowledge in psychological science and the interdisciplinary skills to upskill, prepare you for future studies, or transition into a career within the field of psychological science.

Explore foundational subjects that will introduce you to core areas of psychology, including brain and mind, people and culture. Expand your understanding of the world by exploring the psychology of individuals, groups, communities and populations, and propose your own research question in an applied area of psychology. Graduate with the insights needed to understand human behaviour in culturally diverse settings.

Make study work around work and life commitments with part-time online learning, giving you the freedom to study anywhere. You'll gain the confidence to directly pathway to the Bachelor of Psychological Science, where you'll continue to build your knowledge in areas of clinical, health, developmental and social psychology.

You'll learn:

  * Applied psychology  * Learn how psychology is applied to different areas and health-related settings, such as individual and public health, sports, forensic, and community-based psychology.

  * Psychology of people and culture  * Understand how personal experiences, including emotions, motivation, intimacy with others, and health behaviours are shaped by cultural understanding and social expectations in society.

  * Scientific foundations of psychology  * Explore the philosophical, historical and methodological foundations of the discipline of psychology, critically examining theories in psychological science.

  * Understanding the brain and mind  * Discover the role of the brain in creating behaviour, including how the brain influences behaviour change across the lifespan and how we process and understand information from our senses.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Communicate and explain information and ideas in psychology using a scientific approach.
**2.**
Describe and apply introductory principles, theories, and concepts in psychological science.
**3.**
Describe cultural differences and diversity in beliefs, practices, and behaviour.
**4.**
Reflect on one’s own behaviour and consider the behaviour of others within a broader social context.

Subject: APPLIED PSYCHOLOGY (PSY1APP)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will learn about how the discipline and practice of psychology can be applied in different ways (e.g., to promote mental health, physical health, social participation and inclusion), and in different areas including individual and public health, sports, forensic, and community-based psychology. You will consider how psychological theories and evidence can be applied to a range of psychology and health-related fields and be introduced to key practical skills relevant to these fields. You will create a personal development plan, which will involve self-reflection and evaluation of existing skills, skills to be developed, and strategies to developing self- efficacy, self-confidence, and self-esteem, with a view to your future career goals.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe how psychology theory and practice can be applied in a range of professions and fields
- Evaluate the career options for psychology graduates and apply the insights gained to personal career plans.
- Work collaboratively to produce an integrated presentation and communicate with an audience.
- Demonstrate the ability to reflect on one’s own behaviour, and consider the behaviour of others within the broader social context while being mindful of diversity and professional conduct

Subject: INTRODUCTORY PSYCHOLOGY: PEOPLE AND CULTURE (PSY1PAC)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will be introduced to key areas of psychology with a socio-cultural perspective. People share knowledge with others in society. The shared knowledge (i.e., culture) gives meanings to people’s lives as well as influencing their everyday behaviour, the sense of who they are, their personal relationships and psychological wellbeing. We will discuss psychology of individuals in diverse contexts to understand how personal experiences, including emotions, motivation, intimacy with others and health behaviours are shaped by cultural understanding and social expectations in those contexts.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate sensitivity and knowledge of diversity in cultural beliefs, practices, and communication styles.
- Identify appropriate information sources to develop logical, well-supported, and appropriately referenced written arguments based on empirical evidence.
- Critically reflect on psychological assessment tools within a socio-cultural context
- Apply ethical guidelines governing appropriate academic conduct.
- Apply an understanding of socio-cultural perspectives of psychology to human behaviour and experiences

Subject: SCIENTIFIC FOUNDATIONS OF PSYCHOLOGY (PSY1SFP)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will be introduced to the philosophical, historical and methodological foundations of the discipline of psychology. You will critically examine how theories are developed and used in psychological science and examine how they are operationalised in experimental contexts. As part of a team, you will propose your own research question in an applied area of psychology and present this enquiry to your class, and as an individual you will analyse, present and discuss the results in a written report.
Learning Outcomes:
- Work collaboratively with peers and independently to identify and use appropriate information sources to support an oral and written argument with appropriate formatting and referencing schemes acceptable to international standards, and demonstrate appropriate ethical conduct (e.g. avoiding plagiarism and collusion) in line with professional expectations.
- Design a basic research study on a behavioural phenomenon and develop a logical and well supported conclusion based on empirical evidence, including potential solutions to resolve issues such as cultural bias.
- Critically evaluate the research design, analysis and interpretation of a basic investigation in psychology, including identification and description of potential cultural biases arising from psychological research and assessment methods.
- Apply an understanding of the historical, philosophical and methodological foundations of psychology, including the ethical guidelines governing psychological practice and research to contexts beyond those of psychology itself.
