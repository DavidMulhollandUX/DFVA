## Master of Communications and Media Studies
##### A6003
##### 96 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
There has never been a more important or exciting time to study the mediated world.

This course builds your understanding of the changing contexts of media and communication practices. You will gain insight into the key issues affecting the communications and media industries and you will examine these developments from cultural, political and theoretical perspectives. You will be challenged to communicate your new knowledge and insights with impact and purpose, accommodating a range of media formats and potential audiences.

This course develops an advanced understanding of the contemporary communications and media environment. It is suitable for those pursuing careers in a wide range of communications and media roles, including in the areas of communications, media relations, social media, digital engagement, public policy and public relations.

You will have the opportunity to engage with local practitioners and industry experts via guest speakers, case studies, internships and site visits. You will also have the opportunity to conduct in-depth analysis of contemporary media industries, issues and organisations.

### Structure
The course is structured in five parts: Part A.  Foundation studies, Part B. Core studies, Part C. Applied studies, Part D. Professional practice studies and Part E. Elective studies.

A research pathway is also available subject to meeting eligibility requirements.

Part A. Foundation studies

These studies provide you with an introduction to communications and media studies.

Part B. Core studies

These studies draw on best practices within the field of communications and media studies to further your understanding of communications and media industries, issues and practices, both locally and globally. Studies focus on the challenges posed by digital disruption, globalisation and on methods of research and analysis for the contemporary media and communications environment.

Part C. Applied studies

The focus of these studies is professional or scholarly work that can contribute to a portfolio of professional development. 

Part D. Professional practice studies

Designed in association with industry experts, select from this suite of professional enhancement offerings to build essential competencies for your career pathway.

Part E. Elective studies

These studies provide you with the opportunity to undertake a program of coursework study tailored to your own interests. Units not taken in Part B, Part C and Part D are also available as elective options.

Master's entry points

Depending on prior qualifications you may receive entry level credit (a form of block credit) which determines your point of entry to the course:

  * If you are admitted at entry level 1 you complete 96 credit points, comprising Part A, Part B, Part C, Part D, and Part E.
  * If you are admitted at entry level 2 you complete 72 credit points (Caulfield Campus), comprising Part B, Part C, Part D (6 credit points) and Part E (30 credit points).
  * If you are admitted at entry level 2 you complete 72 credit points (Malaysia Campus), comprising Part B, Part C, Part D (6 credit points) and Part E (30 credit points).
  * If you are admitted at entry level 3 you complete 48 credit points, comprising Part B, Part C and Part E (12 credit points).

Note: If you are enrolled at the Malaysian campus you are admitted at entry level 2 or 3.

Note: If you are eligible for credit for prior studies you may elect not to receive the credit and complete one of the higher credit-point options.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
investigate challenges and opportunities for the communications and media industries and assess their impacts on wider contexts, including culture, politics, business, and everyday life;
**2.**
work effectively, thoughtfully and professionally as peers and team members, able to lead, collaborate and critique;
**3.**
undertake advanced independent research employing research methodologies and ethics.
**4.**
critically analyse and reflect on important trends, issues and challenges shaping the contemporary communications and media environment;
**5.**
skilfully communicate insights into the communications and media industries to a range of specialist and non-specialist audiences in written, verbal and media formats;

### Progression to further studies
Successful completion of the Research pathway in this course may lead to eligibility in a Higher Degree by Research.

Master's accelerated pathway

### AHEGS
The Master of Communications and Media Studies is a postgraduate course. It is taught in English and normally takes two years of full-time study or four years of part-time study. The course focuses on challenges posed by the emergence of digital media, globalisation and increasing levels of cross-cultural exchange. Graduates will demonstrate a sound knowledge of diverse aspects of communication and media including policy, content and technology change and will articulate and apply an understanding of the world view of communications and media as it applies to politics, public administration and business. The Master of Communications and Media Studies is a Level 9 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Communications and media management (AMG5004)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: You will learn through this unit that media and communications is not just situated in the domain of creative endeavours, but that successful communication and media management is crucial to the growth and survival of any organization. This unit will focus on developing media strategies for both internal and external contexts, such as promoting safer workplace environments, or engaging with external stakeholders in times of crisis. You will be introduced to a variety of media processes that require expertise and active management in any organization.
Learning Outcomes:
- Produce reports, policy briefs, and other works that positively promotes a safer, more equitable, and ethical working environment;
- Analyse the complexities of internal and external communications in order to gain a leadership perspective of a professionally and socially diverse workplace;
- Develop media strategies to productively engage with external stakeholders, the public, and the media particularly in times of crisis;
- Collaborate with others to identify and utilize collective skills and expertise to maximize impact of media strategies, as well as develop and utilize task management skills
Assessment Tasks:
- Case study: 50% (Hurdle: None)
- Presentation: 50% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. A unit requires on average three/four hours of scheduled activities per week. Scheduled activities may include a combination of teacher directed learning, peer directed learning and online engagement.

Subject: Emerging media industries in Asia (AMG5009)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: In this unit, students draw on their exposure from the MCMS, where they learn about different facets of the media and creative industries, supported by industry exposure in this capstone unit. They draw on their own locations and use this as an opportunity to learn about and experience the emerging industries in the media and creative economy, allowing them to build networks in the industry they see themselves working in. They submit a project report at the end of the unit, reflecting their understanding of the emerging media industries.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically analyse and evaluate the emerging media industries in Asia;
- Apply learning from units across the MCMS to their industry practicum;
- Produce a professional industry-facing report and presentation.
Assessment Tasks:

Subject: Monash Innovation Guarantee (MON5750)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The MIG is an intensive unit designed to equip you with the holistic skills and mindsets required to lead and implement viable innovation solutions in industry. You will work on a purpose-driven solution to a real-world challenge facing a commercial or social enterprise, a government or UN agency, or a start-up. Industry and academic experts will coach and guide you and your interdisciplinary team through a three-week innovation process involving analysis, ideation, design, testing, validation, and pitching. During the program, you will build your employability skills and broaden your professional networks. The MIG provides a solutions-focused experience to transform your capacity to drive positive change in the workplace through multi-stakeholder collaboration and innovation. It provides a basis for understanding how to intervene in contemporary global issues facing industry, and the implications for society and your career.
