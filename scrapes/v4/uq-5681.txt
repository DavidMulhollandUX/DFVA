Subject: Digital Storytelling (COMU7025)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Stories are powerful, and they are everywhere. And, through digital media, we have more opportunities to make, share, and consume stories than ever before. In this course, theory and practice are blended to encourage students to consider the many ways in which stories may be told and received using digital media. Students develop the skills to critically analyse digital stories through narrative and media analysis. They also develop the skills to create their own digital stories for education, entertainment, and profit.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate knowledge of relevant theories and conventions related to narrative structures.
- Critically evaluate digital narratives in multimedia forms.
- Produce short digital stories that effectively and affectively communicate intended messages.
Assessment Tasks:
- Essay: Effect, Affect, Interpretation: 30% 1500 words% (Hurdle: False)
- Digital Story Presentation and Conversation Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: True)
- Final Project: Digital Story: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Digital Media Theory and Practice (COMU7304)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: COMU7304 will guide you to understand, critically evaluate, and apply digital media theories and professional tools employed in contemporary communication practice. You will plan and execute a digital content strategy based on the examination of the impact of social platforms and AI on communication practice, social listening, and the effective use of transmedia content strategies for real-life clients.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate knowledge, understanding, and critical reasoning capabilities to evaluate the role played by digital media, social platforms, and AI in interpersonal and professional communication and relations, as well as some of their social, cultural and ethical implications in various settings.
- Create, evaluate, and execute data-driven content strategies and specific assets tailor-made for clients' specific communication needs to connect with audiences and build and manage engagement, expertise and community.
- Refine individual and group work ethics and skills to design and produce quality digital content strategies that consider cultural, ethical, sociopolitical and/or legal implications.
Assessment Tasks:
- In-Class Writing Task In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Digital Content Strategy Team or group-based: 30% 1800 words% (Hurdle: False)
- Multimedia Portfolio Team or group-based In-person Online: 40% 2000 words% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 1.5 Hours/ Week Workshop 1.5 Hours/ Week

Subject: Nonfiction for Publication: Think Pieces, Features and Podcasts (WRIT7055)

Overview: This course develops your critical understanding of how creative nonfiction authors make money from their writing, as freelancers and as entrepreneurs. We focus on three contemporary forms that early-career writers can master and make money from: i) the think piece ii) the feature article iii) the podcast episode. This course will help you develop the skills you need to succeed in the competitive world of nonfiction writing and publishing. Join us and take the first steps towards developing a portfolio career as a creative nonfiction writer.

- Critically analyse and synthesise advanced stylistic, narrative and rhetorical approaches in contemporary creative nonfiction, situating them within broader theoretical, cultural and industry contexts.

- Plan, research, draft, revise and edit complex nonfiction works with industry-level precision, demonstrating advanced editorial judgement, reflective practice and adaptability.
- Provide relevant, theory-informed feedback on peer writing, demonstrating high-level workshop literacy, collegial communication and the capacity to contribute to professional editorial processes.
- Evaluate emerging trends, technologies and debates in creative nonfiction and digital publishing, applying insights to complex creative, cultural and/or industry challenges.
Assessment Tasks:
- Blog/newsletter post: 10% 250-500 words% (Hurdle: False)
- Think piece: 20% 800 words% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Writing the Speculative Short Story (WRIT7075)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The speculative short story is one of the most provocative and intriguing narrative forms available to writers and readers: it hits like a gut punch; it should astonish, mystify, amaze, wound and console¿all in the space of a few pages. In this course, you will develop the skills necessary to write compelling and thought-provoking speculative short stories. Through readings, discussions, and writing exercises, you will explore the various sub-genres of speculative fiction, including science fiction, fantasy, horror, and magical realism. Topics covered may include discovering and developing prompts, narrative structure, worldbuilding, and thematic elements. You will also analyze important works of speculative fiction and their publishing contexts to gain a better understanding of the genre's conventions, techniques and markets. By the end of the course, you will have written and received feedback on a speculative short story of your own and prepared it for submission.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and critique examples of speculative short fiction for form, genre, and technique
- Write a complete speculative short story using knowledge about the form, skills and technique
- Understand the market for speculative short stories and how to pursue publication
Assessment Tasks:
- Critical Commentary: 30% 600-800 words% (Hurdle: False)
- Workshopping and Seminar Engagement Identity Verified: 20% 400 words% (Hurdle: False)
- Short Story: 50% 2500-2800 words% (Hurdle: False)
- Draft Submission and Presentation Hurdle Identity Verified: Pass/Fail - 20 minute presentation% (Hurdle: True)

Subject: Writing and Publishing for Children and Young Adults (WRIT7090)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course considers the vibrant segment of the trade publishing market that is children's and young adult writing, both from the perspective of the publishing professional and the creative writer. While the conditions of publishing have seen considerable disruptions and reconfigurations since the turn of the 21st century, the children's and young adult market has remained reliably significant in both economic and artistic terms. The course will consider the ways in which writing for children and young adults may differ from writing for other audiences; the particular ways in which these texts are produced, marketed, and circulated; and how authors and publishers relate to younger readers.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically evaluate and refine creative work through iterative drafting and workshopping, demonstrating autonomous decision-making and sophisticated revision strategies.
- Integrate research, theory, and industry knowledge into creative practice, including awareness of contemporary MG/YA publishing contexts, ethical issues, and genre conventions.
- Articulate and justify creative decisions using reflective, research-informed commentary aligned with Masters-level scholarly and professional practice.
- Produce original, sustained creative work for the MG or YA market that demonstrates advanced control of essential craft techniques and audience positioning.
Assessment Tasks:
- Workshopping, Annotated Draft Portfolio & Critical Reflective Essay Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 55%% (Hurdle: True)
- Writing Portfolio: 45%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Seminar 2.5 Hours/ Week

Subject: Advanced Writing: Grammar, Syntax and Style (WRIT7260)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course presents contemporary grammatical and stylistic concepts and strategies that will enhance students' ability to revise and edit their writing.
Learning Outcomes:
- confidently identify grammatical features and patterns of contemporary written English
- recognise grammatical choices that affect clarity, cohesion, and style
- use your explicit knowledge of English grammar to reflect on and revise a variety of texts at both the structural and functional levels
- integrate grammatical conventions to adapt, refine and polish writing for specific audiences, genres, and purposes.
Assessment Tasks:
- Discussion Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Supervised tutorial problem set – structural and stylistic analysis Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Editing task Online: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 1.5 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1.5 Hours/ Week

Subject: Dissertation (WRIT7300)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: A supervised dissertation (10,000 words) on a writing, editing, and publishing topic. WRIT7310 is for part-time students commencing in semester 1. WRIT7320 is for part-time students commencing in semester 2.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify a research topic in the field of WEP or Communication
- Plan and carry out research in the field of WEP or Communication
- Access, evaluate, and critique primary and secondary sources
- Develop an argument critically and clearly
- Organise and present written research at a sophisticated level, in a form appropriate to the intended audience
Assessment Tasks:
- Milestone requirements Hurdle Identity Verified: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- 10,000-word dissertation: 100%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Workshop 3 Hours/ Ad Hoc
