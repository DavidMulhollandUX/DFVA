## Graduate Certificate of Business Administration (Digital)
##### B4009
##### 24 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
In the age of disruption, strategic leaders face relentless pressure to keep pace with an increasingly digital and dynamic business environment. The Graduate Certificate of Business Administration (Digital) provides knowledge and skills you need now, coupled with some of the strategies and methods you need to stay in the know as an uncertain future unfolds.

The Graduate Certificate of Business Administration (Digital) is designed to introduce you to operational knowledge, organisational and professional skills required to thrive in an increasingly dynamic and uncertain business environment.

### AQF level
Level 8 - Graduate Certificate
### Post-nominals
GradCertBusAdmin (Digital)

### Structure
This course is structured in three parts: Part A. Operational knowledge, Part B. Organisational skills, Part C. Professional advancement.

Part A. Operational knowledge 

These units provide ample coverage of theory, models and contemporary practice across the key business functions of contemporary organisations. They are designed to provide foundational knowledge of each subject area and extend the knowledge to contemporary practices that emphasise digitalisation, the strategic use of data, virtual teamwork, design thinking, and agile project methods.

Part B. Organisational skills 

The focus of these units is to develop deep understanding and advanced practice in the three key organisational skills required by strategic managers: strategic management, leadership and innovation. Each of these skillsets is examined in the context of a contemporary movement: the emergence of new organisational forms, sustainable development and digital transformation. In contrast to the other nine MBA (Digital) units, the Organisational Skills units will make significant use of synchronous learning (24 hours of synchronous learning in each unit).

Part C. Professional advancement

The focus of these studies is to develop the strategies and tactics necessary for you to thrive in an increasingly digital, dynamic and uncertain business environment. The units include methods for maintaining currency through lifelong learning, building your professional communities, and honing your direction and purpose for career advancement.

### Requirements
The course comprises 24 credit points structured into three parts:  Part A. Operational knowledge (12 credit points), Part B. Organisational skills (6 credit points) and Part C. Professional advancement (6 credit points).

Part A. Operational knowledge (12 credit points)

You must complete two units (12 credit points) from the following:

  * MBO5001 Consumers, brands and marketing management

  * MBO5002 Control, performance and accounting management

  * MBO5003 Operations, logistics and supply chain management

  * MBO5004 Value, risk and financial management

  * MBO5005 People, culture and change management

  * MBO5006 Governance, continuity and crisis management

Part B. Organisational skills (6 credit points)

You must complete one unit (6 points) from the following:

  * MBO5007 Strategic management for contemporary organisations

  * MBO5008 Leadership for sustainable development

  * MBO5009 Innovation and change for digital transformation

Part C. Professional advancement (6 credit points)

You must complete one unit (6 credit points) from the following Professional Skills units:

Professional Skills

  * MBO5010 Currency and lifelong learning

  * MBO5011 Direction, purpose and career advancement

  * MBO5012 Connection, communication and community

### Progression to further studies
If you successfully complete the Graduate Certificate of Business Administration (Digital) you may be eligible for admission at entry point 2 into the Master of Business Administration (Digital) (B6029), provided you have satisfied the appropriate entry requirements.

### AHEGS
The Graduate Certificate of Business Administration (Digital) is a postgraduate award. The course is taught in English. The course takes 0.7 years of part-time study to complete. Graduates have an understanding of operational, organisational and professional skills and knowledge relevant for dynamic business contexts. The Graduate Certificate of Business Administration (Digital) is a Level 8 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Consumers, brands and marketing management (MBO5001)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit explores contemporary practice of marketing in light of an increasingly digital and dynamic business environment. It introduces the key concepts, frameworks, and theoretical foundations necessary for leaders without marketing backgrounds to work effectively with the organisation’s marketing professionals. Specifically, the unit provides critical focus on consumers and brands, highlighting progressive practice in digital marketing, such as the use of marketing analytics and customer experience mapping. To deepen and extend understanding, the unit then introduces a series of forces - technological, social, environmental, economic, and geopolitical – which will shape the practice of marketing in the future. Informed with foundational knowledge and enlightened by future directions, this unit prepares leaders to understand the organisation’s marketing function in light of contributions it should make to strategic performance, today and in the future. As one of six operational knowledge units, it also provides ample opportunity to apply your learning to professional practice and build a portfolio of projects relevant to your career.
Learning Outcomes:
- demonstrate understanding and application of contemporary marketing theory, models and frameworks to work effectively with an organisation’s marketing professionals
- examine the potential impact of technological, social, environmental, economic, and geopolitical forces on the future practice of marketing.
- evaluate the integration of digital advancements within an organisation’s marketing systems and management
Assessment Tasks:
- 1 - Written: 40% (Hurdle: None)
- 2 - Written: 60% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 150 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Independent study may include associated readings, assessment and preparation for scheduled activities. You are expected to complete all pre-class activities prior to your scheduled class, and post-class activities should be completed after your scheduled class. Learning activities may include a combination of teacher directed, peer directed and online engagement activities.
