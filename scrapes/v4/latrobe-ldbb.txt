### Overview
La Trobe’s Diploma in Business is your supported pathway into a purpose-driven business degree focused on responsible AI, sustainability, and innovation for a rapidly evolving digital economy.

Build your knowledge and skills with core foundational subjects in marketing, management, economics, accounting, and finance. You will be equipped with the ability to analyse and apply business information and communicate and work effectively with others to solve business challenges. You can select elective subjects to broaden your business exposure or undertake subjects forming part of a planned discipline major. 

After successfully completing the Diploma, you’ll earn up to one year’s worth of course credit (also called advanced standing) if you wish to transfer into a Bachelors degree for the subjects you study.

### Career outcomes
The Diploma in Business is a direct entry pathway into a number of La Trobe business degrees, but it can also prepare you for administrative and entry level positions. You could work in accounting, communications, compliance, IT, marketing or management in the public or private sectors.

Possible roles include:

  * Human resource adviser
  * Provide support to businesses when it comes to staffing, HR policies and programs.

  * Finance officer
  * Manage receipts and disbursements for a business, prepare related reporting and compliance documentation, and monitor overall business performance.

  * Executive assistant
  * Support leaders, managers and other professionals in their roles. Perform administration and organisational tasks.

  * Office manager
  * Organise and oversee the functions and resources of an office or team, including administrative duties and staff.

Subject: ACCOUNTING FOR BUSINESS (ACC1001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject introduces you to the nature of accounting and its role in business. Accounting for Business develops students’ understanding of the language of business through the analysis of financial transactions and statements to assess a business’s financial health and performance, and apply this understanding to decision making in a variety of roles. You will also learn the ethical requirements underpinning financial statements and how to interpret financial statements. Additionally, you will understand a budget and its importance to the business as well as the information required to make strategic and tactical decisions from a non-accountant user’s perspective.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and interpret financial statements and discuss implications for business decision-making
- Explain the importance of accounting reporting and ethical and corporate governance issues as they apply to managerial decisions
- Develop teamwork skills to prepare and apply Budgets, Variances, and Cost-Volume-Profit analysis in business decision-making
- Understand the interaction of decision-making across various organisational functions on financial performance

Subject: DATA FUNDAMENTALS FOR BUSINESS DECISIONS (BUS1003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject equips you with the skills to transform raw data into actionable insights that drive informed business decisions. You will learn key Excel functionalities crucial for data manipulation, analysis, and visualisation. The statistical component covers key concepts such as descriptive statistics (mean, median, mode, variance, standard deviation), probability distributions, hypothesis testing, and regression analysis. Emphasis is placed on interpreting statistical outputs and applying findings to real-world business scenarios. You will gain skills in designing dashboards that summarise complex data sets for managerial review and strategic planning. Overall, you will be equipped with the foundational knowledge and skills to harness data as a strategic asset in driving business success.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply Excel proficiency using fundamental statistical methods to evaluate business problems
- Use appropriate software and statistical techniques to present business data to inform decision-marking
- Apply statistical analysis and hypothesis testing techniques to economic, financial and business data to draw conclusions

Subject: MARKETS, POLICY AND THE ECONOMY (BUS1BUE)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will undertake research into current economic issues and apply economic concepts and frameworks to critically analyse problems and suggest appropriate public policy responses to them. You will learn how the forces of demand and supply coordinate the behaviour of individuals and businesses in the market and how government policy settings affect those market outcomes. You will study the decisions that businesses make in determining how much to produce and at what cost, and analyse how the market structure and public policy settings affects firms’ choices. From an economy-wide perspective, you will investigate how to measure economic activity, inflation and unemployment, and you will examine the factors that influence these variables and the impacts on individuals, businesses and governments.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate the relationships between macroeconomic variables and their impacts on businesses, individuals, and society.
- Use models to predict and analyse the impacts of changing market conditions and/or public policy responses and their impacts on society
- Research and analyse current issues and/or events in the economy by integrating data and other information sources
- Explain fundamental economic concepts with written communication incorporating diagrams, equations, and other media

Subject: INTRODUCTION TO FINANCE  (FIN1002)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject provides an introduction to the principles and practices of corporate finance, focusing on how financial managers create value through investment and financing decisions. Key topics include the scope of corporate finance, financial statement and cash flow analysis, the time value of money, the valuation of bonds, shares, and options, and the trade-off between risk and return. You will examine the application of portfolio theory, the capital asset pricing model (CAPM), and market efficiency theory in explaining the behaviour of capital markets. Capital budgeting techniques are introduced to evaluate long-term investment decisions under conditions of risk and uncertainty. Through applied analysis and problem-solving, you will develop the ability to interpret financial information, value securities, assess risk and return, and apply decision-making frameworks to real-world business finance scenarios.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate business and investment risk and return applying portfolio theory and the capital asset pricing model (CAPM), including the application of market efficiency theory to explain the behaviour of capital markets.
- Apply capital budgeting techniques and decision criteria to evaluate investment projects, incorporating cash flow estimation, risk assessment, and sensitivity analysis to support sound business finance decisions.
- Identify the various sources of financing available to businesses and apply appropriate theoretical and quantitative considerations in determining the most suitable financing source given business circumstances.
- Explain the scope and role of corporate finance, and apply fundamental concepts—including financial statement analysis, the time value of money, security valuation, and dividend policy—to understand the financial environment and support informed business decision-making.

Subject: ACADEMIC INTEGRITY MODULE (LTU0AIM)
Credit Points: 0
Overview: The academic integrity module introduces academic integrity standards to inform and support students in avoiding plagiarism and academic misconduct. Students complete four parts that encompass academic misconduct and academic integrity decisions, including cheating, plagiarism, and collusion. Students learn about Turnitin (the text-matching tool) used at La Trobe University, and how to get help and support their development of referencing skills.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the importance of the acknowledgment and the referencing of sources
- Locate, select, and utilise information about La Trobe's approach to academic integrity to support appropriate acknowledgement of references used.
- Recognise and understand academic integrity responsibilities.

Subject: MANAGEMENT AND ORGANISATIONS (MGT1004)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: The subject introduces the theories of management and organisations that underpin contemporary management practices. In order to develop an appreciation of organisations as complex and multifaceted, you will study the management of different kinds of organisations - from government to private, from small to large, from local to multi-national, from start-ups to mature firms, from technical production to political advocacy. You will learn about the roles and skills of managers; organisations and cultural diversity; globalisation; technology and the changing nature of work; organisational design; introduction to human resource management; motivation and ethics and social responsibility.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe key management functions, roles, and skills, and explain how they support effective decision-making
- Identify current trends and explain how change influences management practices in a global context
- Explain and evaluate management theories, principles and concepts that support effective organisational management

Subject: PRINCIPLES AND PRACTICES OF MARKETING (MKT1MDP)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Marketing refers to the activity, set of institutions, and processes for creating, communicating, delivering, and exchanging offerings that have value for consumers, customers, clients, partners, and society at large. In today’s competitive business environment, it is important to understand the role of marketing in society, the marketing process and the marketing related decisions that businesses must take to satisfy the needs and wants of customers, and create value for stakeholders. This subject covers the principles of marketing. You will learn the role of marketing in business and society, and how marketing is based on an understanding of customer value. You will gain knowledge of core marketing principles including scoping and analysing the marketing environment, marketing research, consumer behaviour, and managing the marketing mix to influence consumers. In addition to marketing-specific knowledge you will also develop transferable employability skills in areas including communication, critical analysis and creative thinking, and working collaboratively.
Learning Outcomes:
- Individually and/or collaboratively develop marketing strategies in different business environments and contexts
- Evaluate market information, individually and/or collaboratively, to develop segmentation, targeting, differentiation and positioning strategies within culturally diverse business environments
- Explain marketing concepts and interpret their application, individually and/or collaboratively, within real-world business scenarios
