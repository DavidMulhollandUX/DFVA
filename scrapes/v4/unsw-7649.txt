## Description
This Graduate Certificate is a qualification for professionals seeking to transition fields, retain and expand knowledge, and refresh skills.

It provides a foundation in cyber security literacy and background technical knowledge plus applied cyber security unit, and provides a security mindset and understanding of the current state of the field.

This program is designed to be delivered fully online, in intensive terms, to provide a fast-paced pathway.

It is also an early exit option for those enrolled in the linked Master of Cyber Security or Graduate Diploma of Cyber Security, and a pathway to continue studying in the same.

Subject: Cyber Threats and Crime (ZZCA9222)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The Internet drives economic growth and enables new ways to connect and co-operate. As with most change, increasing our reliance on cyberspace brings new opportunities but also new threats. While the internet fosters open markets and open societies, this can also make users more vulnerable to criminals, activists, and foreign intelligence services who want to harm us by compromising or damaging our critical data and systems. Cybercriminal attacks against Australian organisations are not a matter of “if” but of “when”. In this new era of unavoidable attacks and breaches, cybersecurity professionals have to duty to ensure their organisations are resilient, and to be adequately prepared when an incident unfolds. This course outlines, from a holistic perspective, the current and emerging trends in cybercrime and how they are being countered from an Australian perspective. It also introduces students to contemporary trends in cyber security strategy and demonstrates how they can be used to increase an organisation’s resilience and preparedness.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically analyse the data and effect of online crime and the impact it has on government, businesses and individuals.
- Synthesise current trends to predict future trends in cyber crime and terror, and effectively communicate appropriate actions and controls.
- Critically evaluate the current legislation that impact on computer crimes and law enforcement and corporate responses to this legislation.
- Identify and describe the different categories of both historical and contemporary computer crime.
- Explain the strengths and weaknesses of current controls to minimise the impact of cyber crime and terror.

Subject: Principles of Security Engineering (ZZEN9203)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course introduces the principles of the emerging field of Security Engineering, applying engineering principles and practices to the design, operation, and assurance of secure systems. Students will engage in a systematic, analytic and reflective approach to designing and assuring reliable secure systems from unreliable insecure components, as well as understanding how systems can fail and the nature of the cryptographic components from which modern digital systems are constructed.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate an understanding of the nature of characteristic cybersecurity attacks, including the nature and characteristics of the vulnerabilities they exploit, and analyse the range of mitigation measures possible.
- Demonstrate an understanding and make appropriate judgements about when to apply the major principles of secure design operation and assurance, their characteristics strengths and weaknesses.
- Analyse real world design, operation and assurance scenarios and then apply a security engineering approach to make appropriate decisions.
- Apply fundamental cryptographic primitives (including confidentiality, integrity, and authentication) and protocols to achieve them and explain their weaknesses and appropriate use. Be able to apply information theoretic concepts such as information and work measures, zero knowledge, randomness, and privacy to design appropriate solutions for real world cryptographic and security problems, or recognise when no effective solutions are likely to be feasible.
- Analyse their prior approach to security in everyday life, and reflect on how a security mindset changes this.

Subject: Fundamentals of Coding: C and Assembler (ZZEN9216)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Learn how to program to C and low level assembly language. This course provides an introduction to programming in C and a low level understanding of the underlying operation of modern computers. C is introduced slowly and at each step we see how C programs are compiled into corresponding machine code and the details of how the computer actually then executes the program. This course is suitable for those who have never programmed before, or those who have programmed but are not familiar with C or assembly, or who would like a refresher from the point of view of cyber security.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop an understanding of the principles of secure coding and given a C program be able to identify common security vulnerabilities in it.
- Design, write and debug programs in C
- Be able to write programs in a simple assembly language.
- Effectively collaborate with others in accordance with modern software design practices including pair programming and team review.
- Explain how C programs are compiled, linked, and executed including memory allocation, stack frames, system calls, the role of static and dynamically linked libraries and how to inspect this using a debugger.

Subject: Cyber and the Law (ZZLJ9223)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The goal of the course is to provide a legal orientation for cyber security professionals. Students are not assumed to have a legal background. The course offers an introduction to legal and policy issues in cyber security. It introduces students to relevant core legal concepts, including understanding the role of legislation and case law as well as other forms of regulation, including technical standards. It will guide students in understanding the ‘map’ of law and regulation relevant to cyber security and the role of these in Australia’s cyber security policy. The course helps cyber security professionals become familiar with legal issues they are likely to encounter and how to manage them in different circumstances - during normal operations, in responding to an incident, and in the aftermath of an incident. After completing the course, students will be aware of and be able to manage core compliance obligations as well as be in a good position to interact meaningfully with legal advisers in more complex scenarios. The focus is on Australian law, but comparative and international material is included where relevant, particularly where it impacts Australian industry.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify key legal requirements and how these and other forms of regulation impact on cyber security professional practice.
- Discuss the limits of law, both generally and in specific instances, and be able to argue for reform in particular areas.
- Explain the role of key players in regulation for cyber security and be aware of the circumstances in which engagement with regulators is required or advisable.
- Analyse and respond to legal issues arising in the contexts of cyber security operations, incident response and incident fall-out.
