## Graduate Diploma in Education - Melbourne (Bundoora)
##### EG001
##### 120 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
Advance your career and stay up-to-date with current best practice with La Trobe’s Graduate Diploma in Education. You’ll develop your networks and grow your career as an educator and leader.

Deepen your foundations with core subjects on educational leadership, how humans learn and digital technologies. Then add a specialisation of your choosing, such as curriculum design, or inclusion and diversity.

This flexible course is designed for people who’re working. Many subjects are delivered in intensive or blended modes, making it easier to fit study into your life. You can also study at your own pace, undertaking one to four subjects per semester.

If you’d like the option to further extend your skills, the Graduate Diploma in Education can also provide a fully credited pathway to the Master of Education.

You'll learn:

  * Contemporary educational practice  * Develop your understanding of educational best practice.

  * Diversity  * Explore diversity in educational contexts and plan for socially just and inclusive education.

  * Research  * Explore educational research and learn how to incorporate it into your practice.

  * Learning sciences  * Learn about the science of language and reading and how to apply this across primary and secondary classrooms.

  * Leadership and team building
  * Learn how to evaluate current practice and manage diversity in educational organisations.

Subject: HOW HUMANS LEARN (EDU4001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will explore the educational psychology underpinning human learning, including topics such as information processing theory, schema theory, memory consolidation, and executive control. You will take a neurodevelopmental perspective, focusing on the school years. There will be a particular focus on Sweller’s Cognitive Load Theory and Dehaene’s Four Pillars of Learning. You will learn how to use established principles of cognitive science and contemporary behavioural psychology to design learning experiences for maximum impact and to discern pseudoscientific educational approaches from those that align with empirically supported theories. You will also build your confidence to share what you learn in this subject with your teaching colleagues and/or parents to maximise your professional impact.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate understanding of current evidence-based approaches to analysing and designing learning experiences.
- Differentiate scientifically-aligned educational theories and practices from those for which the evidence-base is weak or non-existent.
- Demonstrate strong knowledge of the development of executive functioning in children and adolescents, and information processing models of learning, including selective attention, working memory, and Cognitive Load Theory.

Subject: UNDERSTANDING AND BUILDING LEADERSHIP CAPABILITIES (EDU4008)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: The content is tailored to both current school-based leaders and principals, as well as aspiring leaders looking to attain leadership roles in education. Demonstrate expert, specialised and technical knowledge in broad educational areas of implementation to address the needs of diverse stakeholders across a range of educational contexts. Analyse the relationship between leadership practices and capacity building, workplace culture, strategy, people and structure as a means of reaching organisational goals and learning outcomes. Critically evaluate sources of leadership issues and identify strategies to effectively address, manage and develop both personnel and stakeholder productivity in educational organisations.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse situations thoroughly before making decisions and act decisively in choices including taking responsibility for outcomes.
- Analyse and evaluate current research and contemporary literature in leadership and implementation science, including the roles of communication skills; self-leadership; gender issues; ethical issues; and change and innovation.
- Reflect critically about their own leadership approaches and capabilities.
- Identify, discuss and suggest viable approaches to leadership strategies for future action that demonstrate appropriate behaviours and attitudes.

Subject: UTILISING EMERGING TECHNOLOGIES FOR EDUCATIONAL PURPOSES (EDU4009)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Future-focused educators and leaders will be equipped to critically examine and practically engage with the emerging technologies shaping contemporary education, with a particular emphasis on artificial intelligence (AI). Students will explore the ethical, pedagogical, and systemic implications of these technologies across diverse educational contexts. In addition to interrogating the challenges—such as bias, surveillance, and automation—students will engage with real-world use cases to understand how AI and related tools can be responsibly and effectively used to improve teaching, communication, leadership, and organisational efficiency. The subject supports educators and leaders to become informed, reflective, and strategic in their use of technology, ensuring its alignment with inclusive and ethical educational practices. The subject supports students to become informed decision-makers who can harness technology with integrity, creativity, and purpose in diverse educational contexts.
Learning Outcomes:
- Compare and contrast AI use cases across different educational contexts, analysing their implications for teaching, equity, and school operations.
- Describe the potential benefits and challenges of utilising AI into learning and teaching environments.
- Assess the effectiveness, efficiency, and ethical soundness of a selected AI or digital tool used in education.
- Analyse the impact of emerging technologies on different student populations and/or educational contexts.

Subject: STRATEGIC COMMUNICATION FOR EDUCATION (EDU4010)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Effective communication is at the core of successful educational leadership, shaping the dynamics between school leaders, teachers, students, parents, the wider community and stakeholders. Explore how strategic communication enhances decision-making to foster creative inclusive environments that support shared goals and vision. Utilise diverse communication channels, such as digital platforms and face-to-face interactions, to actively engage stakeholders, address concerns, and celebrate achievements. Develop effective communication techniques to manage change, resolve conflicts, and align organizational priorities to meet stakeholder expectations. Analyse the political, cultural and economic contexts within which contemporary communication and reputation management occur.
Learning Outcomes:
- Contextualise the evolution of contemporary communication to enhance decision-making, foster collaboration, and build trust among stakeholders.
- Examine the role of strategic communication in different professional education sectors and contexts
- Apply communication theories to independent and collaborative projects across a range of media.
- Identify challenges and opportunities for strategic communication practice in different educational contexts
- Critically examine internal and external communication engagement

Subject: EDUCATIONAL SYSTEMS (EDU5032)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Develop your understanding of education systems in Australia and internationally, including how they have developed over time, are organised and the social, political and economic contexts that influence them. Explore the complex Australian education system, including early childhood, primary, secondary, tertiary, Vocational and developmental education, and the services that support and regulate them. - Critique education systems, policy and management in contemporary society
- Critically analyse education systems in Australia, and the social, political and economic contexts that influence them.

Subject: LEARNING ENVIRONMENTS: APPLICATIONS OF THE LEARNING SCIENCES (EDU5033)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: The Learning Sciences is an interdisciplinary field, which investigates how people learn (EDU4001) and how learning environments are designed to support effective, inclusive, and enduring learning. In this subject, you will engage with foundational theories and emerging research drawn from cognitive science, educational psychology, sociology and neuroscience to better understand how educators create environments for learning. Through critical engagement with research and real-world examples, students will explore principles that underpin evidence-based teaching and learning in contemporary educational settings. By the end of the subject, students will be equipped to critically evaluate educational practices through a learning sciences lens.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse the role of classroom routines, engagement strategies, and feedback mechanisms in designing learning environments.
- Identify one’s own assumptions about teaching and learning in light of current research in the Learning Sciences.
- Interpret and apply cognitive theories to real-world teaching scenarios.
- Apply interdisciplinary insights from the Learning Sciences to design a professional learning session.

Subject: LEADERSHIP, DIVERSITY AND INCLUSION (EDU5LDI)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject introduces you to some of the major issues that dominate today's thinking about organisational leadership. It provides you an opportunity to explore and critique the need for leaders in education and other organisations to implement suitable leadership strategies and approaches that will effectively respond to the diverse management and leadership needs of today's complex and culturally diverse organisations. Participants examine the context of the changing demographics of today's organisation and the eminence of globalisation, along with the need for paradigmatic-shift in conventional leadership mindsets and practices towards diversity and inclusion. Relevant theories of diversity and inclusion are investigated including: theory of equity and attribution, as they relate to ethnicity, race, religion, gender, sexuality and age. You will demonstrate a fact-based understanding of existing case studies and models of best leadership practices, and a critical awareness of relevant factors influencing leadership effectiveness.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify key issues affecting diversity and inclusion in contemporary workforce with particular reference to education.
- Identify contemporary leadership capabilities that contribute to effective diversity and inclusive practices in education and develop a personal critical perspective on models of best leadership practices.
- Critically examine how prevailing leadership practices respond to local contexts of diversity and inclusion in Australia and in various international contexts.
- Critically examine the terms diversity and inclusion, equity and attribution and related theories.
