## Description
The Graduate Certificate provides foundational capability through core courses in artificial intelligence (AI) fluency, responsible AI practice, and AI strategy. The program also offers opportunities to deepen these foundations through either a technical lens, developing practical programming skills, or a more philosophical perspective, examining the conceptual and ethical questions that shape how we think about AI. Designed for professionals with interest or some exposure to AI but limited formal knowledge, the courses build confidence in evaluating AI tools and their implications, applying ethical and governance principles to organisational decisions, and designing and communicating strategic uses of AI for diverse stakeholders.

## Program Learning Outcomes (CILOs)
On successful completion of this program, students will be able to:

1. Make informed and effective selection and application of knowledge in a discipline or profession, in the contexts of local and global business.
2. Define and address business problems, and propose effective evidence-based solutions, through the application of rigorous analysis and critical thinking.
4. Develop and be committed to responsible business thinking and approaches, which are underpinned by ethical professional practice and sustainability considerations.
3. Harness, manage and communicate business information effectively using multiple forms of communication across different channels.

## Program Structure & Degree Requirements
Students must complete 24 Units of Credit (UOC) structured across the following academic components:

### Core Courses (18 UOC)
Students must complete 18 UOC of the following courses.

- **ZZBU0032**: Responsible AI for Business and Society [6 UOC]
- **ZZBU0033**: Developing Strategy for AI [6 UOC]
- **ZZBU0031**: AI Fluency [6 UOC]

### Prescribed Electives (6 UOC)
Students must complete 6 UOC of the following courses.

- **ZZBU0034**: Computational Thinking and AI [6 UOC]
- **ZZBU0036**: Risk Management and Governance in the Age of AI [6 UOC]

## Progression & Academic Rules
Students who successfully complete the Graduate Certificate may articulate into the Graduate Diploma in AI Leadership and Strategy or the Master of AI Leadership and Strategy with full credit for courses completed.

Subject: Responsible AI for Business and Society (ZZBU0032)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly embedded in business operations and societal decision-making. While AI enables innovation and efficiency, its use introduces ethical challenges, organisational responsibilities, and broader societal implications. AI-enabled decisions influence fairness, accountability, transparency, employment, and public trust. This course examines the ethical, social, and organisational challenges created by AI-enabled decision-making in business and society. Students explore responsible AI principles and evaluate how organisations apply these principles in practice. Through real-world cases, students analyse stakeholder impacts, assess value-based trade-offs, and examine how AI reshapes accountability and organisational responsibility. The course emphasises ethical reasoning, societal impact, stakeholder analysis, and structured judgement as foundations for responsible organisational decision-making. This course aims to develop students’ ability to evaluate AI-enabled practices through ethical, organisational, and societal lenses; build structured judgement in assessing trade-offs and unintended consequences; strengthen organisational responsibility analysis; and enhance professional communication of responsible AI recommendations.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain how AI-enabled decision-making influences business processes, organisational accountability, and societal outcomes.
- Apply ethical reasoning, stakeholder analysis, and value-based trade-off evaluation to assess AI use in real organisational contexts.
- Identify organisational vulnerabilities and unintended consequences associated with AI adoption.
- Develop and communicate structured, ethically grounded recommendations to support responsible and sustainable AI use in business and society.

Subject: Computational Thinking and AI (ZZBU0034)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course develops programming literacy and structured computational thinking for business contexts. Students engage with programming and related tools to understand how business problems are translated into data processes, logic structures, and automated workflows within organisational systems. The course introduces foundational concepts such as data representation, control logic, modular design, and process decomposition, enabling students to interpret how artificial intelligence-enabled systems are constructed and monitored. Students focus on evaluating, adapting, and validating code — including code generated with artificial intelligence tools — and assessing the robustness, limitations, and organisational implications of computational solutions. The emphasis is on understanding how software systems think and operate in practice, equipping students to collaborate effectively with technical teams and exercise informed oversight of artificial intelligence-enabled processes.
Learning Outcomes:
- Communicate computational reasoning and system logic clearly to non-technical stakeholders.
- Evaluate and validate computational outputs for accuracy, robustness, and organisational relevance, including when using artificial intelligence-assisted development tools.
- Explain core programming and computational concepts relevant to business problem-solving.
- Analyse how business problems are translated into structured code, data processes, and automated workflows.

Subject: Risk Management and Governance in the Age of AI (ZZBU0036)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course examines organisational risk management and governance challenges associated with artificial intelligence adoption. Students apply established risk management frameworks, regulatory standards, and governance models to identify, assess, and mitigate artificial intelligence-related risks, with particular attention to compliance and accountability. The course emphasises formal governance structures, risk controls, regulatory compliance, and escalation pathways.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and evaluate AI-related risks
- Apply risk management frameworks to mitigate AI-related threats
- Design organisational policies for responsible AI use
- Collaborate to evaluate competing risk perspectives and produce joint governance recommendations
- Analyse and compare governance models and regulatory approaches across jurisdictions
