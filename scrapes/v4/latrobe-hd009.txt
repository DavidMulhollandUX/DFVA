### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Recognise and illustrate key human bioscience concepts and systems as they apply in the allied health disciplines.
**2.**
Identify and discuss fundamental knowledge related to a range of allied health disciplines relevant to future university studies.
**3.**
Apply a range of professional communication skills to communicate with clients and other health care professionals.
**4.**
Apply knowledge and skills to demonstrate understanding and competency of critical academic skills essential to successful university learning.
**5.**
Identify the key principles of evidence based practice as it relates to the provision of health care.

Overview: Wominjeka La Trobe is a short one-hour compulsory online subject that introduces all La Trobe students to Indigenous Australian history, culture and customs. A rich and relevant cultural heritage education is important for everyone. This topic highlights the link between Indigenous knowledge and values and broader cultural literacy. Subject: INTRODUCTION TO HUMAN ANATOMY (ANA1001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will explore the fundamentals of human anatomy. You will develop a strong foundation in anatomical terminology, including concepts such as anatomical position, planes, directional terms, and movements. Through focused study of the major body systems—particularly the musculoskeletal and nervous systems—you will learn to apply anatomical knowledge to real-world scenarios and communicate effectively using precise anatomical language.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate how the nervous system integrates sensory and motor control, and the effects of its dysfunction/impairment.
- Describe the anatomical structure and function of major body systems using the appropriate anatomical terminology.
- Analyse the role of the musculoskeletal system in facilitating movement and assess the impacts following injury.

Subject: HUMAN BIOSCIENCES A (HBS1HBA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject introduces you to human physiology - how the body works. You'll learn how the body is organised and the basics of cell structure and function, and how processes in the body are controlled. This will provide the foundation for the study of the major body systems important for support and movement (muscular system); regulation, integration and control (nervous and endocrine systems); fluids and transport (blood, heart, cardiovascular and immune systems); energy, maintenance and environmental exchange (respiratory, digestive and renal systems, metabolism); and the continuity of life (reproductive systems).
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the basic physiology of major organ systems of the human body.
- Apply knowledge of physiology concepts to clinical and disease states.
- Demonstrate oral and written communication skills.
- Demonstrate the skills for self-managing, reflection, and successful learning at university.
- Describe the structure and function of body cells, tissues, and organs.
- Apply specialist terms and language associated with physiology and anatomy to describe the human body.

Subject: FOUNDATIONS IN SPEECH PATHOLOGY (HCS1FSP)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will be introduced to the concepts of communication, eating and drinking and develop an awareness of the individual and social factors that shape human communication, eating and drinking across different populations and contexts. You will learn about the human right to communicate and the integral role that communication, eating and drinking play in the formation of individual identity. You will learn about the role of speech pathology in enabling communication, eating and drinking by exploring speech pathology practice in a range of contexts, including health, disability and education. You will also be introduced to the role of research literature in advocacy, support and the delivery of innovative, evidence-based speech pathology services. This subject includes professional learning, practical experiences or portfolio activities which contribute to the completion of the Career Ready AdvantageTM award.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the scope of speech pathology practice and its role in enabling communication, eating and drinking
- Explain how communication, eating and drinking inform identity and work as a team to compare and contrast the factors that influence others' communication, eating and drinking abilities.
- Describe the foundational concepts of communication, eating and drinking
- Explain the individual and social determinants on communication, eating and drinking
- Explain the role of communication in the preservation of human rights.

Subject: ANALYSING AND COMBINING WORDS (HCS2ACW)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will investigate the structure and function of words. Focusing on Australian English, you will explore the production and description of speech sounds and how these sounds work together as a system. - Demonstrate preclinical competence in analysis of the syllable and foot structure of English words.

Subject: HEALTH AND WELLBEING OF PEOPLE WITH DEVELOPMENTAL DISABILITIES (HLT1HDD)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will be introduced to people with developmental disabilities and hear directly from them about their lives. Learning about them as individuals, and also about their experiences, will develop your understanding of the roles played by health and human services professionals in supporting their health, wellbeing and social inclusion. You will be introduced to current principles and values that are key to positive interactions between people with developmental disabilities and health and social service providers. You will also develop an understanding of reflective practice and the National Disability Insurance Scheme, and explore the role you will play in your future profession, and the roles of other professions in contributing to the health and social inclusion of people with developmental disabilities. Your learning will be assessed through written tests, and contribution to a group discussion.
Learning Outcomes:
- Recognise and reflect on your own reactions to people with developmental disabilities.
- Demonstrate sound writing skills that are appropriate to various media to communicate your critical thinking.
- Describe guiding principles, philosophies and values that contribute to respectful and inclusive delivery of health and human services to people with developmental disabilities.
- Identify and describe the capabilities of people with varying degrees of physical and/or cognitive disabilities, and identify the supports they require.
- Compare the roles of various allied health and human services professionals in supporting the health, wellbeing and social inclusion of people with developmental disabilities.
- Explain what it means to have a developmental disability.

Subject: RESEARCH AND EVIDENCE IN PRACTICE (HLT1RAE)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject is an introduction to the use of research-based evidence in professional health care practice. Working in interprofessional teams and using a range of case scenarios, you will develop research skills in areas relevant to your field of practice. Through online activities and workshops, you will learn about the role of evidence-based practice in health. Areas of study include systematic approaches to acquiring evidence, critical appraisal of the literature, interpretation of research design, descriptive and inferential statistics and assessment of research outcomes. You will learn how an evidence-based approach in health informs clinical practice. You will develop research skills to determine the most appropriate intervention techniques for application in a given clinical population, while understanding the complex interaction between social, economic and environmental influences that contribute to sustainability thinking in health research.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically appraise some components of the validity of health research evidence
- Explain the relative merits and uses of the different types of evidence used in practice
- Communicate research outcomes appropriate to peers
- Develop an effective search strategy to address practice based research questions

===== SOURCE: https://handbook.latrobe.edu.au/subjects/2026/MIC2MAH =====
Subject: MICROBIOLOGY FOR ALLIED HEALTH (MIC2MAH)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject will suit anyone that has an interest in basic medical microbiology and the potentially pathogenic microbes around us. You will be introduced to your normal flora, medically important bacteria, viruses, and fungi and the ways that we can control and prevent infection by these organisms in a health care setting. In practical classes you will use culturing techniques and biochemical testing to identify microbes and understand the effectiveness of hand washing in stopping the spread of pathogens throughout a population. This subject will teach you to recognise the general characteristics of pathogenic microorganisms that cause disease in humans and the steps involved in disease transmission.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe how to isolate and identify non-pathogenic and pathogenic microorganisms as part of diagnostic strategies and interpret the findings
- Apply knowledge of infection control and tools for optimising disease prevention to identify appropriate treatments for infectious diseases in clinical scenarios.
- Apply fundamental knowledge of cell biology and patient genetics to inform disease treatment and outcomes.
- Explain and communicate the key principles regarding microorganisms that cause disease in humans to patients and clinicians, individually or as part of a small team.

Subject: EYE ANATOMY AND PHYSIOLOGY (ORT1EAP)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Students will study the anatomy and physiology of the internal and external structures of the eye, including the physiology of intraocular pressure, accommodation and pupil responses. The process of visual image formation will be explored in terms of specific aspects of retinal anatomy and physiology introducing students to the concepts of visual acuity, contrast sensitivity and colour vision. The anatomy of the visual pathway from retina to cortex and its relationship to basic visual defects will be introduced as part of this subject.
Learning Outcomes:
- Discuss the relationship of ocular anatomy and physiology to sight
- Demonstrate the assessment of contrast sensitivity and colour vision on a patient and interpret the preliminary findings
- Describe the normal structure and function of the eye and the anatomical organisation including the clear media and internal and external structures
- Describe the processes of normal visual acuity, contrast sensitivity and colour vision
- Define and correctly use anatomical and physiological terms as they relate to the eye
- Describe the normal visual pathway from the retina to the visual cortex using correct anatomical terminology

===== SOURCE: https://handbook.latrobe.edu.au/subjects/2026/ORT1ORS =====
Subject: OPTICS AND REFRACTIVE STATE (ORT1ORS)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, students are introduced to the optical principles of the refractive system of the eye. The physics of light and the principles of physical optics are integrated with the ocular system, including its refractive errors and physiological defects. Emphasis will be placed on developing students' abilities to propose causes of the various refractive errors and their respective optical management. The theoretical content and the clinical investigatory skills of lens measurement, vision assessment and optical prescription reporting will also be taught.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain how the normal healthy eye can compensate for optical errors to improve vision.
- Describe the different types of refractive errors of the eye, their causes and patients symptoms
- Describe the underpinning optical principles in managing a patient's refractive error, relating the patient's visual requirements for normal activity.
- Analyse physical data and explain the behaviour of light
- Perform vision testing and communicate results to others.
- Explain to patients how their glasses will correct their refractive error.

Subject: CLINICAL ORTHOTICS 1 (POR1COF)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will explore the evidence that underpins the orthotic management of the foot in clinical practice. This will include principles, clinical assessment, prescription considerations, treatment processes, and a biomechanical basis of orthotic management for a range of pathologies affecting the foot. Evidence-based orthotic treatment of various foot conditions will be presented in the context of current clinical practice. This includes applied anatomy, pathology and biomechanics relevant to orthotic management and interdisciplinary practice. Both traditional and digital fabrication techniques for orthotic management will be presented and practiced.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate knowledge and practical skills in understanding pathomechanics, gait deviations, orthotic mechanics and design principles for managing common foot pathologies
- Explain treatment procedures for optimal orthotic treatment of clients with common foot pathologies using both traditional and digital techniques
- Select and justify the use of foot orthoses to suit common foot pathologies
- Apply clinical and research evidence in the orthotic management of common foot pathologies
