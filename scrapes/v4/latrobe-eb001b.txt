## Bachelor of Outdoor and Sustainability Education - Bendigo
##### EB001B
##### 360 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
La Trobe’s Bachelor of Outdoor and Sustainability Education takes you beyond the classroom. Get hands-on outdoor experience and build a deeper understanding of sustainability and environmental education.

Enjoy opportunities to build and enrich your outdoor adventuring skills while you explore nature. You could spend more than 70 days on field trips* and extended outdoor journeys while building a vital understanding of research on climate change and experiential learning. From bushwalking to kayaking, you’ll prepare to lead groups while they explore outdoor environments and you develop your skill in outdoor learning.

You’ll also be ready to help foster respect and appreciation for the world around you. Explore the social and scientific aspects of sustainability, including Indigenous understandings of our environment, and learn how to manage and teach outdoor education programs.

Whether you want to guide adventures or educate the community, we’ll help you be ready for your future. You’ll develop the skills and confidence to lead, teach and communicate in any situation.

You'll learn:

  * Natural environment knowledge and sustainability  * Learn about Australia’s diverse range of native flora and fauna and our complex relationship with it.

  * Educational skills  * Explore how to successfully create and deliver educational experiences about sustainability and the natural environment.

  * Outdoor adventure guiding  * Explore the outdoors and build your skills in leading groups on adventure experiences.

  * Outdoor safety and risk management  * Discover how to safely plan for leading groups of people in natural environments.

  * Leadership, teamwork, and communication  * Learn by experience as you develop strong transferable skills that are applicable in any career.

*The number of field trip days is dependent on the major selected.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Describe and analyse aspects of the relationships between place, knowledge, meaning making and experience
**2.**
Demonstrate understanding of curriculum considerations and educational philosophies underpinning outdoor and sustainability education, including the ability to critically examine aims and purposes of outdoor experiences
**3.**
Demonstrate safe living and travel skills on a variety of extended journeys in outdoor environments
**4.**
Critically reflect on, and evaluate theoretical perspectives of programme design, curriculum and pedagogical approaches in the context of one’s own learning and teaching practice
**5.**
Articulate and evaluate philosophies, trends and issues that inform outdoor and sustainability education
**6.**
Discuss and evaluate the ideas and sociocultural beliefs that have shaped the way people perceive and interact within a more-than-human world
**7.**
Design, implement and evaluate safe and educationally coherent outdoor and sustainability education programmes in consultation with external agencies
**8.**
Apply suitable safety planning and risk management specific to outdoor and sustainability education

WBL placements will generally be arranged by the Office of the Provost Placements and WBL Team at La Trobe University. The number of hours undertaken can vary considerably, depending on the requirements of the course. Students may incur additional costs and it may be necessary to travel or relocate to complete a specific placement. Please refer to the individual subject entries for more information.

### Career outcomes
Graduates will have the skills and knowledge for a range of careers, including roles in education, land and resource management, adventure tourism, and environmental conservation.

Potential job titles include:

  * Outdoor adventure guide
  * Instruct and guide individuals and groups in outdoor adventure activities such as mountaineering, trekking and white-water rafting.

  * Park ranger
  * Play a vital role in maintaining and protecting national and state parks, historic sites, nature reserves and conservation areas.

  * Natural resource manager
  * Find practical solutions to manage and rehabilitate our natural environment and help manage our natural resources in a sustainable way.

  * Community environmental educator
  * Help increase community understanding of sustainability and issues affecting our natural environment.

Want to bring the outdoors to the classroom? Graduates of this degree could be eligible to apply for entry into the Master of Teaching (Secondary) to become a fully registered teacher.

Subject: WOMINJEKA LA TROBE: INDIGENOUS CULTURAL LITERACY FOR HIGHER EDUCATION (ABS0WOM)
Credit Points: 0
Overview: Wominjeka La Trobe is a short one-hour compulsory online subject that introduces all La Trobe students to Indigenous Australian history, culture and customs. A rich and relevant cultural heritage education is important for everyone. This topic highlights the link between Indigenous knowledge and values and broader cultural literacy. It asks you to think critically about your own attitudes, values and beliefs.
Learning Outcomes:
- Reflect critically on your own cultural attitudes, values and beliefs
- Demonstrate knowledge of and respect for Indigenous Australian perspectives and experiences, history, culture and customs, including an appreciation of the local people and country
- Recall important details regarding the history, culture, customs and people of Indigenous Australia
- Understand the need for and value of appropriate and effective intercultural communication

Subject: FRAMING CULTURAL IDENTITIES (EDU1004)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This is a cornerstone subject in which first year education students develop core knowledge, skills and dispositions required to be equity-oriented teachers. The subject embeds intercultural understanding and the development and deepening of students’ understanding of the diversity of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander languages, societies, cultures, world views, histories and contemporary experiences. Students examine their own cultural identities, representations of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities and become critically reflective of the historical circumstances that have impacted on people in Australia. Students will investigate policies, programs and practices of direct concern to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people.
Learning Outcomes:
- Illustrate how media representations and personal histories and experiences shape and influence perceptions of and assumptions about Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples and their community life, and how this impacts on Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander students' attitudes towards school and education.
- Critically reflect on ethics, beliefs, and emerging teaching philosophies relating to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander students' culture and education to understand how to address the impact of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander culture on students.
- Examine significant events impacting on Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people, as well as current rights-based approaches, educational policy and practices related to Indigenous education.
- Demonstrate knowledge of and respect for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander cultures and histories, along with understanding of the impact of culture, cultural identity and linguistic background on the education of students from Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander backgrounds.
- By exploring theories of cultural identity, learn how Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islanders' cultural identity and linguistic background play a role in shaping their understanding of education and how to productively engage in challenging conversations around equity and diversity for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander education.

Subject: UNDERSTANDING ABORIGINAL AND TORRES STRAIT ISLANDER EDUCATION (EDU2005)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Pre-service teachers will develop an understanding of theories, strategies and pedagogical approaches that align with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander knowledges and perspectives. Pre-service teachers will learn about the impact of culture, cultural identity and linguistic backgrounds on the education of students from Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander backgrounds.
Learning Outcomes:
- Prepare and deliver a presentation to peers that uses culturally inclusive practices in relation to curriculum, teaching strategies and pedagogical approaches that align with local Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspectives and knowledges.

- Critically reflect on what it means to be culturally competent, demonstrating your understanding of implementing Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspectives, knowledges and identity for all students, including students with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander backgrounds.
- Design a lesson demonstrating ability to provide learning opportunities that are culturally appropriate, culturally safe and responsive to all students’ needs.

Subject: READING AUSTRALIAN LANDSCAPES (OED1RAL)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Overview: You are introduced to the processes that shape Australian landscapes. Specific Victorian bioregions are explored to understand influences and interrelationships of climate, ecology, land forming processes and management practices of natural systems. Through practical work, field work and assignments, you will learn to use a range of techniques that will enable them to "read" landscapes from an ecological and an outdoor environmental educator’s perspective.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop a range of skills that will enable you to read landscapes that draws on lecture presentations, field-based activities and published literature.
- Understand and describe the landforms, soils, water properties, biota and cultural history of a selected field site visited in this subject.
- Describe the fundamental interrelationships of geology, landforms, soils, water, climate, biota and human influences on the natural environment within specific Australian bioregions.
- Demonstrate an understanding of the formational processes that shape Australian landscapes, and the influence of past and present land management practices.

Subject: WAYS OF KNOWING NATURE (OED1WKN)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you are introduced to ideas and cultural beliefs that shape the way Australians perceive and interact with nature. You will examine and explore connections between ways of experiencing nature and knowledge of places, with an emphasis on implications for outdoor environmental education. You will critically examine human-nature relationships through enquiry, academic writing, problem-solving, and dialogue. The learning experiences, including practical field trips, provide opportunities for the development of naturalist skills and reflections on theory.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate naturalist observation skills through the practice of nature journaling.
- Develop skills required for academic study, including written communication; critical thinking and enquiry; research; problem-solving; reading; and, referencing.
- Analyse how particular worldviews, influence human nature relationships.
- Describe and evaluate theoretical and practical understandings of the ways in which knowledge is developed through outdoor environmental education.

Subject: LEADING GROUPS IN OUTDOOR ENVIRONMENTS (OED2OLA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, students investigate, evaluate, and apply the theory and practice of small group leadership in the outdoors in relation to outdoor environmental education. Students examine and critique a range of relevant place based, adventure, and education leadership frameworks, alongside components of professional experience that include reflective practice, communication, facilitation, judgement and decision making. Students will analyse and apply these concepts in a range of learning experiences, including practical field trips in outdoor environments.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply judgement to enable ethical decision-making regarding group safety and facilitation that enhances learning in outdoor environmental education contexts.
- Critique and apply theories, models and principles of leadership in the context of peer led field experiences.
- Evaluate outdoor leadership, teamwork, group facilitation and instructional frameworks in relation to outdoor environmental education.

===== SOURCE: https://handbook.latrobe.edu.au/subjects/2026/OED2OLB =====
Subject: SAFETY IN OUTDOOR ENVIRONMENTS (OED2OLB)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, students develop competence and gain experience in outdoor leadership and safety within an outdoor environmental education context. Learning experiences, including practical field trips, emphasise knowledge of safety/risk management including the strict aversion to fatalities approach, sound judgement, trip planning, navigation, safety devices, wilderness first aid, mental health and legal liability.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe and plan for strategies that support students' well-being and safety working within schools and/or the outdoor profession and legislative requirements.
- Identify, analyse and plan for safety planning, management and leadership including the strict aversion to fatalities approach in a range of outdoor environmental education contexts.
- Demonstrate knowledge, understanding and skills in wilderness first aid and navigation for outdoor environmental education contexts.

Credit Points: 15
Overview: You will plan and undertake a supervised outdoor environmental teaching practice, and a negotiated programme of field experience to extend your outdoor environmental education preparation. You will undertake an agreed applied learning experience, which may include advanced skill development, work integrated learning and/or peer trip leading.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate development of an experience profile, informed self-appraisal, and critical judgement relevant to student career goals
- Develop knowledge of programme implementation for effective outdoor environmental education experiences
- Understand, plan, and practice safe and impactful pedagogies in introductory outdoor environmental education experiences
- Demonstrate depth of understanding in relation to managing safety and serious incidents in outdoor environments
