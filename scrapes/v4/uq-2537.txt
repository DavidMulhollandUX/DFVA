## Career Opportunities & Employment Outcomes
Graduates of the Bachelor of Arts find employment in a wide range of fields, with future directions dependent on the chosen area of study. Specialised areas of study can lead to careers in Psychology or Journalism, while study in History, Political Science, and Criminology, can provide the basis for careers in government, business, media, and humanitarian fields.
Bachelor of Music graduates may gain employment in areas such as performance, accompanying, composing, arranging, lecturing, music editing, arts or education administration, librarianship, research, music programming, media work (journalist, critic, author), music education (instrumental school or studio).

Subject: Introduction to Music Technology (MUSC1010)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Digital sound recording and editing, software-based music creation and performance.
Learning Outcomes:
- Create and edit original audio and MIDI material using a digital audio workstation and/or mobile applications in individual and collaborative contexts.
- Understand foundational music production technologies and their applications in sound recording, MIDI sequencing, editing, arrangement and other creative music making scenarios.
- Investigate and evaluate the potentialities of music recording technologies for music making practices in mobile and home studio contexts in individual and collaborative contexts.
- Retain technical and practical information that will allow the solving of technical problems.
Assessment Tasks:
- Creative Production Projects Identity Verified In-person: 36%% (Hurdle: False)
- In-Class Quiz Identity Verified In-person: 14%% (Hurdle: False)
- Major Creative Project & Presentation In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Western Art Music 1700-1900: Approaches and Concepts (MUSC1050)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Exploration of Western art music from the late Baroque to Romantic eras (c. 1700-1900), including knowledge of its major styles, through representative works, and understanding of its wider contexts. Foundational development of research and communication skills for music-specific studies.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate an overall grasp of major developments in Western art music of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, including changes in musical styles and social contexts;
- Demonstrate critical insight into specific case studies of music in terms of stylistic and aesthetic factors and social contexts;
- Work effectively with the materials of music (scores, sound recordings etc.) in order to make informed critical judgements;
- Apply bibliographic and research skills to specific problems or questions related to course content; and
- Communicate effectively in appropriate ways on various topics, questions, issue in relation to the course content.
Assessment Tasks:
- Music Research Foundations Assignment: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Video Essay Identity Verified Online: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Interactive Oral Assessment Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=MUSC1150 =====
Subject: Introduction to Guitar (MUSC1150)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The guitar is a portable and versatile instrument that can be used in a wide variety of musical genres and for a diverse repertoire, from classical to folk to contemporary popular music. This course provides an opportunity for practical study of this guitar, enabling the development of basic skills, knowledge and competencies to prepare you for future use of the instrument in diverse performance settings. The course covers the fundamental technical elements of guitar playing and provides students with an experience of the guitar tradition across a variety of styles. Students are required to have their own guitar in order to complete this course. Students must perform on acoustic guitars only, preferably nylon-string classical style guitars. Please contact the tutor/course co-ordinator for recommended instruments. The School of Music does not have guitars available for hire. Please note that this course offering may be cancelled if fewer than 10 students enrol.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate basic fingerstyle guitar technique and performance skills
- Understand foundational musicianship through note reading and rhythmic skills, and perform with appropriate expression and style
- Demonstrate a capacity for reflection and problem solving in your musical practice
- Demonstrate an ability to work in small ensemble settings to create a cohesive performance
Assessment Tasks:
- Guitar Performance Checkpoints Identity Verified In-person: 20% (10% per checkpoint)% (Hurdle: False)
- Practical Assessment on Guitar with Practice Journal Identity Verified: 50% (30% Performance / 20% Journal)% (Hurdle: False)
- Duet Performance and Evaluation Identity Verified Team or group-based: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Practical Studies 2 (MUSC1260)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Development of classical instrumental, vocal, or compositional technique through individual instruction, ensemble participation and practical application. For instrumentalists and vocalists, this includes the refinement of technical skills, historical performance practices, and interpretation of canonical works across various stylistic periods. Vocal studies further incorporate language and diction training appropriate to the classical repertoire. For composers, skills in notation, compositional approaches, responding to stimulus and orchestration are developed, along with understanding of a range of contemporary styles and genres, focusing on developing skills appropriate for your own practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate a foundational level of artistry and technical fluency as a solo performer
- Identify your progress at a foundational level towards being a professional musician.
- Demonstrate a foundational level of artistry as an ensemble performer and your awareness of professional expectations surrounding ensemble performance in varied contexts at a foundational level
Assessment Tasks:

Workload: Contact 4 Hours/ Week

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=MUSC1300 =====
Subject: Introduction to Music Psychology (MUSC1300)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Music psychology involves the scientific study of how people think, feel and behave in relation to music. How and why people engage with music will be explored from different fundamental psychological perspectives including perception, emotion, thought, development, learning, and social processes. Prominent themes examined through the course include music performance, music learning, and music in everyday life. You will be active in developing and co-creating knowledge through participatory experiences in music psychology research and research process.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate an understanding of the psychology of music field: scope, theory, key concepts and applications.
- Reflect critically on, and discuss psychology of music research as it relates to the various ways in which humans engage with music.
- Demonstrate an understanding of the scientific approach to research in the psychology of music: an ability to synthesise and critically discuss relevant research; conduct, report and discuss the findings of research.
- Demonstrate an ability to participate and engage in group activities, and critically reflect on learning and engagement through these activities
Assessment Tasks:
- Tutorial-Based Learning Reflection In-person: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Quizzes In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Written Assignment: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Individual Research Report of Class Project: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Musicianship 1 (MUSC1600)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Study of basic techniques of Western tonal music & integrated writing, analysis, keyboard & aural skills components. Students with advanced knowledge of music theory are encouraged to complete the Music Theory Test administered by the UQ School of Music. Successful completion may result in an exemption from MUSC1600. For more information or to arrange the test, please contact the School of Music.
Learning Outcomes:
- Aural and Practical Skills Musicianship - Demonstrate fluency and command of a set repertoire for this course.
- Aural and Practical Skills Musicianship - Analyse and communicate the pitch and rhythmic structures of selected repertoire
- Aural and Practical Skills Musicianship - Sing and notate familiar and unfamiliar musical material
- Aural and Practical Skills Musicianship - Demonstrate accuracy, confidence and fluency in all tasks
- Aural and Practical Skills Musicianship - Demonstrate the ability to work creatively on all tasks
- Aural and Practical Skills Musicianship - Engage critically and reflectively with methodologies used for aural studies
- Harmony and Keyboard Musicianship - Understand the principles of basic diatonic harmony and counterpoint.
- Harmony and Keyboard Musicianship - Recognise in written form and play at the keyboard any type of interval or triad.
- Harmony and Keyboard Musicianship - Develop skills in part writing at the keyboard and in written form.
- Harmony and Keyboard Musicianship – Analyse short chordal progressions using Roman Numerals or Figured Bass notation.
- Harmony and Keyboard Musicianship - Complete a short four-part (SATB) piece utilising the harmonic resources presented in class.
Assessment Tasks:
- Aural Practical Tasks Identity Verified Team or group-based: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Written & Keyboard Assignment Identity Verified: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Aural Dictation Test Identity Verified In-person: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
