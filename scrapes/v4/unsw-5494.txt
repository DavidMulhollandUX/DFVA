## Description
The Graduate Diploma develops advanced capability in artificial intelligence fluency, strategic analysis, organisational change, and governance in technology-enabled contexts. The program examines how artificial intelligence initiatives are identified, prioritised, implemented, and overseen within complex organisations.

Students build structured understanding of artificial intelligence capabilities, responsible practice, and computational or conceptual foundations, alongside formal risk management and governance frameworks. The curriculum integrates ethical reasoning, regulatory awareness, systems thinking, and change management to prepare professionals to guide artificial intelligence adoption responsibly and effectively.

Graduates are equipped with the analytical, organisational, and governance capabilities required to assess, implement, and sustain artificial intelligence initiatives in environments characterised by uncertainty, accountability, and ongoing transformation.

5. Develop and be committed to responsible business thinking and approaches, which are underpinned by ethical professional practice and sustainability considerations.

## Progression & Academic Rules
Students who successfully complete the Graduate Diploma may articulate into the Master of AI Leadership and Strategy with full credit for courses completed.

Students who wish to exit the Graduate Diploma early may nominate to transfer to and graduate from the Graduate Certificate in AI Strategy provided the requirements for the program have been met.

## Admission Requirements
**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
- A Bachelor degree in any discipline plus a minimum of 2 years’ relevant work experience*; OR

- An Honours degree or higher-level qualification in any discipline; OR

- A Bachelor degree or higher-level qualification in a cognate discipline**

*Relevant work experience includes:

- Being responsible for tasks within a business or organisational context that may involve people management, organisational development, or business process improvement

- Managing a small team, leading workplace change initiatives, overseeing a relevant project

- Holding supervisory HR, marketing, finance or administrative responsibilities within a commercial or public sector setting

**Cognate disciplines include:

- Business, Management, or Commerce

- Information Technology or Computer Science

- Engineering

- Data Science, Analytics, or Statistics

- Public Policy and Governance

- Health Administration

Applicants who do not meet the above entry requirements may be eligible for admission to the Graduate Certificate in AI Strategy. Students who successfully complete the Graduate Certificate may articulate into the Graduate Diploma in AI Leadership and Strategy or Master of AI Leadership and Strategy with full credit for courses completed.

**Limitations on Recognition of Prior Learning**:
Students may be eligible to receive up to 24 UOC (equivalent to four courses) as Recognition of Prior Learning, subject to approval and provided the eligible courses were completed within the last ten years.

Subject: Responsible AI for Business and Society (ZZBU0032)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly embedded in business operations and societal decision-making. While AI enables innovation and efficiency, its use introduces ethical challenges, organisational responsibilities, and broader societal implications. AI-enabled decisions influence fairness, accountability, transparency, employment, and public trust. This course examines the ethical, social, and organisational challenges created by AI-enabled decision-making in business and society. Students explore responsible AI principles and evaluate how organisations apply these principles in practice. Through real-world cases, students analyse stakeholder impacts, assess value-based trade-offs, and examine how AI reshapes accountability and organisational responsibility. The course emphasises ethical reasoning, societal impact, stakeholder analysis, and structured judgement as foundations for responsible organisational decision-making. This course aims to develop students’ ability to evaluate AI-enabled practices through ethical, organisational, and societal lenses; build structured judgement in assessing trade-offs and unintended consequences; strengthen organisational responsibility analysis; and enhance professional communication of responsible AI recommendations.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain how AI-enabled decision-making influences business processes, organisational accountability, and societal outcomes.
- Apply ethical reasoning, stakeholder analysis, and value-based trade-off evaluation to assess AI use in real organisational contexts.
- Identify organisational vulnerabilities and unintended consequences associated with AI adoption.
- Develop and communicate structured, ethically grounded recommendations to support responsible and sustainable AI use in business and society.

Subject: Leading AI-Enabled Change (ZZBU0035)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course examines organisational change in response to artificial intelligence adoption and transformation. As artificial intelligence reshapes workflows, decision processes, governance arrangements, and workforce capabilities, leaders must design and sustain change that is strategically aligned, ethically responsible, and operationally viable. Yet, some estimates suggest that up to 75% of all organisational change fail to deliver the results that were anticipated. The course introduces you to various established change management theories and the tools and techniques that are associated with effective change management. Students will have the opportunity to analyse how artificial intelligence initiatives affect organisational structures, incentives, risk management systems, and stakeholder relationships and apply change management frameworks and insights to the development of a change management plan that can be applied for artificial intelligence-enabled transformation. The course further explores leaders’ ability to adapt change processes over time and investigates various leadership styles (transformative, adoptive, and ethical leadership) for change management. Emphasis is placed on i) diagnosing and mobilising organisational readiness for artificial intelligence adoption, ii) sequencing and implementing change interventions while building commitment and managing resistance, and iii) sustaining transformation in environments characterised by technological uncertainty and institutional scrutiny. The focus is on practical, theory-informed leadership of artificial intelligence-enabled change in complex organisational environments.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply your understanding of leadership styles for managing resistance, sustaining change, and maintaining accountability in artificial intelligence-enabled environments.
- Explain key theories and frameworks of organisational change and systems thinking as they apply to artificial intelligence-enabled transformation.
- Design a structured artificial intelligence change intervention that integrates strategic alignment, governance considerations, and stakeholder engagement.
- Analyse organisational readiness, stakeholder dynamics, and system interdependencies in the context of artificial intelligence adoption.

Subject: Managing with Digital Technology   (ZZBU6101)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Managing with Digital Technology aims to increase technical literacy of managers and leaders. The main objective of this course is to give managers a high-level overview of the fundamental concepts, main topics and problems in contemporary information technology and how they relate to business settings, and thus equip them to make better decisions regarding applications of technology, even if they are not technologists themselves. This course takes a top-down approach and emphasises high-level mastery of important concepts in information technology. Students who successfully complete this course will feel confident in any technology organisation and will have an appreciation for the problems in the field, how they affect or constrain business decisions and how it all works. The course prepares students for product-management roles. Similarly, more and more industries, especially traditional service industries, are creating their own products to deliver more capabilities to their customers and require new capabilities from their managers. A successful product manager needs to master the business side of developing a product as well as be able to interact with a wide range of technical and non-technical stakeholders - from customers through to product engineers and user experience specialists. Managing with Digital Technology equips founders to succeed in technology endeavours. Successful start-up founders will come across technology from day zero. Virtually every business idea today involves technology - often entire stacks of various technologies. Founders with a non-technical background must rely entirely on their technical co-founders, if they are lucky, or on the word of developers and other technologists they hire, to help them realise their dream. They are often faced with difficult choices and this course aims to equip them with the knowledge and confidence to make informed choices not just in business but in technology as well.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate emerging business technologies used to deliver user experiences in a competitive marketplace
- Devise complex and compelling arguments to key stakeholders that ensure potential future funding of technology projects
- Analyse the key technology-related drivers of organisational decision making and the value of technology to deliver competitive solutions
- Adapt customer insights to develop innovative technology products that can be used to expose new market niches
- Critically review the role of the customer and their user needs as the driver of technological advancement and innovation of products and services
- Identify risks associated with new technology deployment and adapt mitigation strategies

Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course will provide students with a foundational understanding of the Australian legal system and the ethical frameworks within which businesses operate. Students will examine the nature of the Australian legal system, key institutions and principles, the evolution of that system over time and its place in the broader international context. The course will be delivered online through asynchronous modules, online forums and synchronous online classes.

- Interpret legislation with reference to the rules of statutory interpretation and find and understand caselaw (PLO 2, 3, 4)
- Evaluate the ethical frameworks for the conduct of business (PLO 3, 4, 5)
- Articulate legal and ethical concepts clearly to a broad audience (PLO 4, 5)
- Analyse the evolution and key components of law of the Australian legal system (PLO 1, 6)
- Examine the sources of law regulating business (PLO 1, 2, 5, 6)
