## Graduate Certificate of Green Chemistry and Sustainable Technologies
##### S4005
##### 24 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
The Graduate Certificate of Green Chemistry and Sustainable Technologies provides expert training in green technologies coupled with sustainability to support the transformation of various industries. The principles of green technologies will be coupled with an understanding of sustainability issues to advance the reduction or elimination of hazardous substances involved in the design, manufacture and or application of diverse chemical products. The environmental, economic and social benefits are examined. You will choose aspects of green technologies, from innovation to design, and focus on applications in different contexts. The course can act as a pathway to the Master of Green Chemistry and Sustainable Technologies (S6006).

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Reflect and demonstrate ethical decision making, pertinent to the chemical industry and its role in supplying goods and services for society.
**2.**
Analyse, evaluate and integrate knowledge of green chemistry in alignment with sustainability principles realising benefits for society, the economy and the environment.
**3.**
Apply the principles of green chemistry utilising technical and communication skills to analyse and design solutions that avoid environmental problems and enhance sustainability.
**4.**
Gain specialised knowledge and skills relevant to solutions, materials and processes that will assist the chemical industry and manufacturing to become more sustainable.

### Progression to further studies
Successful completion of this course will provide a pathway to the Master of Green Chemistry and Sustainable Technologies (S6006). You need to achieve a 50% average in the 24 credit points of studies within this Graduate Certificate to progress to the Master of Green Chemistry and Sustainable Technologies (S6006).

### AHEGS
The Graduate Certificate of Green Chemistry and Sustainable Technologies is a postgraduate award. The course is taught in English and takes half a year of full-time study or part-time equivalent to complete. Graduates will demonstrate advanced and integrated knowledge of green chemistry. Knowledge and skills to critically evaluate green chemistry and sustainability principles applying to the chemical industry, manufacturing and impact on society will be gained. The Graduate Certificate is a Level 8 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification.  Most students admitted into the Graduate Certificate of Applied Genomics will have an Australian bachelor degree with at least first year Chemistry studies.  

The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit equips you with an understanding of tools for environmental analysis and decision-making around development projects, production processes, and governmental policies. - critically analyse the EIA, LCA, and SEA literature in the context of environmental decision making.
- recognise, apply, and critique the process of Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) for development projects;
- analyse and showcase how Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) can inform sustainable production processes;
- evaluate the influence of Strategic Environmental Assessments (SEA) in policy development;

You will obtain practical skills in 3D printing of polymers, computer-aided design and processing of polymers for additive manufacturing.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate practical skills in 3D printing of polymers.
- Recognise and distinguish between the different classes of polymeric materials used for Additive Manufacturing.
- Demonstrate in-depth understanding of additive manufacturing technologies.
- Select the adequate Additive Manufacturing process based on the functional requirements of the product.
- Discuss in detail the factors that affect geometric properties such as minimum wall thickness, minimum feature size and/or minimum space between designed surface of 3D printed part.
- Choose appropriate strategy for the orientation of a 3D printed part on the build platform and the generation of the support structure in order to minimise postprocessing activities.
Assessment Tasks:
- Assignments: 50% (Hurdle: Threshold)
- Final assessment: 50% (Hurdle: Threshold)
Workload: The minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of 3-6 hours of scheduled learning activities and 6-9 hours of independent study per week. Scheduled activities may include a combination of teacher-directed learning, peer-directed learning and online engagement. Independent study may include associated readings, assessment and preparation for scheduled activities.
