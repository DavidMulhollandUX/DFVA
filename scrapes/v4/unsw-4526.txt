## Description
The Bachelor of Fine Arts (Honours) is a 48 UOC (one year equivalent) advanced undergraduate degree for high achieving students. The program is designed for those who have completed an undergraduate degree in Fine Arts (or cognate area), and wish to extend their critical and creative skills in contemporary art contexts. The BFA (Hons) enables students to advance their knowledge and understanding of artistic practice by developing a significant practice-led research project. Supported by coursework in art theory and professional practice, the program supports students to gain an in-depth understanding of the relationship between theory and practice in contemporary art contexts. By doing so, this program can provide pathways to higher degree research programs such as an MFA or PhD, as well as a diverse array of professional and career outcomes in the arts and cultural sectors.

On successful completion of this program, students will be able to:

2. Contribute to contemporary art and culture through rigorous and sustained projects that demonstrate an ethical responsiveness to diverse local and global perspectives.

1. Critically evaluate contemporary art practice within complex social, political, cultural, historical, and environmental contexts.
5. Work independently and collaboratively to develop and communicate advanced artistic ideas in creative, scholarly, and professional contexts.
3. Conduct rigorous and sustained independent research to develop advanced knowledge in a contemporary art context.

Subject: Art and Design in Practice (ADAD4020)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The course enhances your Honours research by helping you to identify key professional practice skills, attributes, and aspirations aligned with your practice, research, and professional interests. The course brings students together from across Art & Design Honours programs to explore and share a multitude of research and career trajectories. By asking you to pro-actively identify pathways, precedents, and opportunities that align with your current project and future interests, the course encourages you to consider how you can build on your research for contexts beyond the frameworks of the Honours year. Whether you are interested in developing an independent practice, working collaboratively, engaging with industry, seeking employment, and/or postgraduate study, this course is designed to enhance your repertoire of skills for a successful transition post-Honours. The course also actively contributes to your Honours project by facilitating the integration of professional practice skills in your research and seeking critical feedback through an open studio event showcasing your works in progress.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply professional practice skills and attributes in a peer-to-peer environment to develop and realise a showcase of works in progress.
- Identify, analyse, and evaluate professional pathways and opportunities aligned with your research interests and career aspirations.
- Critically examine exemplars in your field to develop a professional practice plan.

Subject: Fine Arts Honours Research Project 1 (DART4101)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: Fine Arts Honours Research Project 1 is a 12 UOC course designed to support you in initiating and establishing your Honours research project. In this course, you will develop and plan a practice-led research project that is directly related to the materials, processes, and ideas emerging from your artistic practice. This course is taught in a studio context, emphasising contemporary studio practice as a key mode of investigation and discovery. The studio also operates as a collegiate and peer-oriented environment, fostering a supportive and rigorous culture of creative practice, critical discussion, and exchange.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop a practice-led research methodology that demonstrates awareness of diverse perspectives and integrates ethical research principles
- Create new artworks that respond to a practice-led research framework through material, processual, and conceptual explorations
- Formulate a practice-led research question relevant to contemporary art practice
- Plan and design a practice-led research project relevant to contemporary art contexts

Subject: Fine Arts Honours Research Project 2 (DART4102)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: Fine Arts Honours Research Project 2 is a 12 UOC core course in the Fine Arts Honours program. Taken across two terms for a total of 24 UOC, the course is designed to support you in developing and completing your Honours research project. In this course, you will continue to build on your practice-led research project as established in DART4101. Formative tasks in T2 are designed to support you to resolve your research outcomes through a body of creative work and a thesis that are submitted in T3. This course is taught in a studio context, emphasising contemporary studio practice as a key mode of investigation and discovery. The studio also operates as a collegiate and peer-oriented environment, fostering a supportive and rigorous culture of creative practice, critical discussion, and exchange.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply a practice-led research methodology that demonstrates awareness of diverse perspectives and integrates ethical research principles
- Analyse and evaluate practice-led research outcomes in relation to relevant artistic, scholarly, and professional discourses
- Create and resolve a self-directed body of work that synthesises material, processual, and conceptual concerns relevant to contemporary art practice
- Undertake sustained practice-led research to critically investigate questions relevant to contemporary art practice

Subject: Contemporary Art Research Methods (DART4300)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course introduces you to inquiry, analysis, and research skills relevant for contemporary art research at Honours level. The course focuses on the foundational research skills of: formulating a clear topic, situating your research in a field of thinkers and practitioners (communities of practice), building literature and practice reviews, and understanding which methods are relevant for your research. The course fosters a community of student and academic researchers willing to experiment with ideas and learn from each other to develop innovative projects that advance contemporary art practice and thinking in Australia and beyond. Through seminars, small group discussions, and one-on-one feedback, the course supports you to create a toolkit for planning and undertaking research that contributes ideas and experimental practices to the art community. It encourages you to understand the foundations of your practice, whether in art making, writing, or curating, and to learn how to hone your research skills for various professional contexts after graduating.
Learning Outcomes:
- Adapt and apply a variety of research methods in contemporary art practice, history, and theory
- Evaluate and explain research methods in contemporary art discourse and practice
- Develop and plan a research project that engages with contemporary art contexts by integrating the principles of research methods
