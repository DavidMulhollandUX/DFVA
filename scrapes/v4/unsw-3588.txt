Subject: Foundations of Actuarial Models (ACTL2102)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course introduces the stochastic models used by actuaries to model both liabilities and assets and illustrates their applications in actuarial work. Topics covered include discrete-time and continuous-time Markov chains and their applications to experience rating; Markov process models and applications to actuarial modelling practice including in areas such as insurance, financial risk, and social policy; methods for simulation of a stochastic process; estimation of Markov chains. Students will be expected to implement models using the R software in a numerical computer package.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate Markov processes in financial and insurance modeling.
- Describe and utilize Markov Processes in insurance and financial models.
- Apply Markov Chains to frequency-based NCD schemes using data.
- Produce professionally structured reports based on actuarial modelling.
- Present complex actuarial models effectively in written form.
- Implement Monte Carlo simulations for stochastic processes.
- Identify features and properties of Markov Chains.
- Use Monte Carlo simulations in simple actuarial problems.
- Explain terminologies and classifications of stochastic processes.
- Define key principles of actuarial modeling.
- Discuss actuarial models with an understanding of their ethical implications.
- Estimate transition probability and matrix of discrete-time and continuous-time Markov chains.

Subject: Modelling and Prediction of Life and Health Related Risks (ACTL3141)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course equips students with the technical skills needed to estimate survival models, emphasising their applications in insurance, finance, demography, and epidemiology. These models help understand and predict risks related to events such as mortality, health changes, and credit defaults, allowing for better risk management. Key topics include: survival models; estimation of lifetime distributions; maximum likelihood estimation of transition intensities in multi-state models; graduation and smoothing of crude death rates and transition intensity estimates; and models for projecting mortality trends. The course will also focus on implementing these concepts using numerical computer packages. Additionally, it will address ethical considerations and issues related to discrimination and heterogeneity in mortality and health outcomes.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the main methods of projecting/forecasting mortality rates
- Describe how to estimate transition intensities depending on age, exactly or using the census approximation
- Identify maximum likelihood estimators for the transition intensities of a multiple state model.
- Describe estimation procedures for lifetime distributions.
- Describe how to test crude estimates for consistency with a standard table or a set of graduated estimates, and the process of graduation.
- Describe the concepts of survival models.
- Describe the Binomial and Poisson models of mortality.
- Discuss the ethical dimensions and implications of the modelling introduced in the course.

Subject: Actuarial Mathematics for Insurance and Superannuation (ACTL3151)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Long-term contingent cashflow valuation is fundamental for actuarial work, especially for life-, disability- and health insurance, pensions and superannuation. They are also increasingly applied in broader areas in social protection and policy. This course introduces models and techniques from actuarial mathematics combining the present value principles with stochastic modelling, in order to derive the value of contingent cashflows for pricing, reserving, projections and profit testing purposes. The course also covers contemporary management issues in life, health and superannuation products such as longevity, and premium rating. Spreadsheets will be used to perform computations.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe life insurance and life annuity products available in the market.
- Assess the risks associated with cash flows from contingent events.
- Assess the reasonableness of premium and policy value calculations for financial contingent products.
- Integrate and apply these technical skills to practical valuation problems in the life insurance and annuity markets.
- Apply contingent valuation concepts to practical scenarios.
- Describe fundamental techniques for valuing cash flows involving contingent events such as death and survival.
- Describe the basic valuation and funding principles of superannuation benefits.

- Describe stochastic investment models in actuarial contexts and their use in asset-liability management.
- Explain key asset-pricing models and their implications for actuarial practice.
- Utilise mean-variance criteria to optimise asset allocation for long-term investors such as insurers and superannuation funds.

This course will be a fully problem-based learning experience.
