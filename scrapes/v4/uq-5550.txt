Subject: Conservation & Wildlife Biology (CONS6009)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides a core baseline in conservation science and conservation biology in the context of wildlife conservation, discussing human wildlife-conflict, the processes for identifying threatened species, spatial conservation prioritisation and genetic considerations in biodiversity and wildlife management, providing hands-on experience in designing and undertaking applied conservation research using novel field techniques and approaches.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify conservation issues and the key factors involved
- Gain perspectives on, both the successes and failures, of national and international conservation initiatives with a focus on frontline conservation regions in Australia and Africa
- Produce short and concise summaries, both verbally and in written form, of conservation topics and in depth reviews of the scientific literature relating to particular conservation initiatives
- Advance teamwork and group work skills, as well as independent learning skills.
- Critically reflect on your development as a professional as a result of your course participation
- Advance understanding of applied and novel tools in conservation
Assessment Tasks:
- Assessment 1a - Mini-Project, RiPPLE submission Team or group-based Online: 35%% (Hurdle: False)
- Assessment 1b - Mini-project, Peer review Online: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: False)
- Assessment 2 - Conservation genomics in class quizzes (during prac time) In-person: 35%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Practical 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: Sampling Design & Analysis in Conservation Science (CONS7008)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The modern conservation professional possesses the ability to collect/interpret data, and to understand statistical analysis as presented in research reports. As part of this course you will develop highly important skills in statistical analysis using the R program. The underlying theory and philosophy is explained without recourse to mathematical symbolism. Illustrations draw from a wide variety of studies in conservation biology. These skills will be reinforced and utilized throughout many of the remaining courses in the Masters of Conservation Biology program
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the fundamentals of experimental design and statistics
- Become proficient at coding in the R programming language
- Pose scientific questions that are clear, feasible and lead to important new knowledge or solutions to conservation problems
- Apply experimental design and data analysis principles to real-world conservation problems
- Work effectively in teams to make informed team decisions and demonstrate interpersonal, leadership and communication skills
- Statistically analyse real-world data to directly address questions or hypotheses
- Clearly and effectively communicate results of statistical analyses to a range of audiences
- Interpret results obtained by other scientists, published in reports or publications, recognising the limitations of the methods they have applied
Assessment Tasks:
- Written project proposal: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- In-semester exam Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Statistical analysis using R (10 weekly quizzes worth 1 mark each) Online: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Final RMarkdown report (Statistical analysis of frog data) Hurdle: 35%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Workshop 1 Hour/ Week IT Computing 3 Hours/ Week

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=ENVM7505 =====
Subject: Conservation Policy (ENVM7505)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Overview of policy development process as it is applied to conservation & environmental management. Conservation policy in both international & national context. (Minimum enrolment required 8)
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and explain the main conservation policy instruments and frameworks at international and national levels, and how they interact.
- Articulate the policy development process and identify constraints on development and implementation of conservation policy
- For a given conservation problem, identify alternative policy responses, and analyze their respective strengths and weaknesses.
- Prepare credible, concise and evidence-informed policy positions/briefs on conservation issues, considering its socio-political context and other relevant issues.
- Analyse the effectiveness of a policy, and identify and articulate ways to improve policy effectiveness within a set of real-world constraints.
- Place common policy debates held in the public arena within their policy frameworks and identify motivations of stakeholders and interest groups in those debates.
- Anticipate and critically appraise likely impacts, both intended and unintended, positive and negative, of proposed policy approaches in different contexts
- Argue persuasively for and justify specific formulations of international policy statements, and connect current events together to improve appreciation of challenges to international policy effectiveness
Assessment Tasks:
- Policy review plan: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Full policy review: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
- Policy briefing Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week

Subject: Strategies for Sustainable Systems Transition (TIMS7328)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: What will business look like in a climate-constrained future? This course takes a big-picture view of how organisations navigate the complex, interconnected challenges of sustainability and climate change. Students will explore the strategic, technological, and systemic responses businesses can adopt to drive sustainable transformation. Emphasising foresight, innovation, and systems thinking, students will examine how emerging trends¿from climate policy to disruptive technologies¿shape long-term organisational planning and sustainability leadership. By envisioning alternative futures, students will develop the critical and collaborative capabilities needed to lead change in a rapidly evolving global landscape.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse the complex and interrelated sustainability challenges facing organisations.
- Evaluate climate policy developments and their strategic implications for business.
- Collaboratively design innovative strategic responses to sustainability challenges.
- Propose strategies for organisational adaptation and transformation.
- Reflect on personal learning, team dynamics, and leadership capacity to influence sustainability outcomes in organisational contexts.
Assessment Tasks:
- Climate Change Simulation - Reflection: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Video Team Project - Scenario Planning Team or group-based: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Reflection on Scenario: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Viva Voce Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Seminar 3 Hours/ Week
