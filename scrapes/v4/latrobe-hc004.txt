## Graduate Certificate in Child Protection - Melbourne (Bundoora)
##### HC004
##### 60 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
La Trobe's Graduate Certificate in Child Protection gives you the opportunity to gain a qualification that meets the State Government's Child Protection Capability Framework, enabling graduates to attain the qualification to enter the child protection workforce. This course will recognise your professional experience and enable you to gain a formal qualification in this important field.

The course has been developed with a focus on providing students with an introduction to the key theories, knowledge and skills needed to work effectively within the statutory child protection system. Case practice examples from the sector will be utilised to assist students to grapple with the complexities experienced in practice.

You'll learn:

  * Human rights, law and ethics in social work
Explore the legal context of Australian social work practices and the ethical principles that guide them, particularly as this applies to the child and family sector.
  * Social work with children and families
With a focus on children and families, discover the theoretical frameworks used to assess and build relationships with clients, including casework theory and preparing an intervention plan.
  * Fields of Social Work Practice - child and family welfare and family violence
With a focus on child and family welfare and family violence, you will be introduced to the key debates, policies, programs, service delivery and skills required to undertake beginning level practice in both fields.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Actively use critical reflection to systematically consider professional knowledge, use of self, skills, values, and assumptions in relation to child protection practice.
**2.**
Apply skills and theory to child protection practice specifically in relation to undertaking complex risk assessments and developing  comprehensive interventions to address family and children’s needs.
**3.**
Demonstrate high level communication skills to meaningfully engage and work effectively with children and families in the child protection system.
**4.**
Integrate theory, skills, and values, to undertake ethical decision making for effective child protection practice.
**5.**
Articulate how past and current child protection practices have impacted First Nations’ families and recognise the importance of including Indigenous ‘ways of knowing, being and doing’ in practice.

Subject: SOCIAL WORK WITH INDIVIDUALS, FAMILIES & GROUPS: APPLICATIONS A (SWP4APA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will develop appropriate levels of skill development to apply theoretical frameworks that inform practice with individuals, families and groups. You will be engaged with enquiries to clarify: the nature of direct social work practice, how social workers go about their practice in different fields, how to identify and organise case data, and the use of formal casework theory to prepare an assessment and, in conjunction with SWP4APB, how to prepare a basic intervention plan.
Learning Outcomes:
- Communicate complexity of knowledge re how organisational, legal, protective, and ethical considerations influence direct social work practice, including how social work practice may be named differently in different contexts.
- Consolidate and synthesise knowledge in preparing a plan for a specific social work service occasion
- Analyse and compare the basic theoretical concepts underpinning direct social work practice including discourses such as Western and First Nations worldviews and green social work; explanatory theories such as critical theory, psychodynamic and systems; practice theories such using a strengths focus or task focus.
- Identify, plan and suggest implementation of case data for a given social work case
- Synthesise knowledge of the essential elements of a given presenting situation and comprehensively connect to current practice knowledge and theory

Subject: SOCIAL WORK WITH INDIVIDUALS, FAMILIES & GROUPS: APPLICATIONS B (SWP4APB)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, the focus is on developing appropriate levels of interpersonal helping skills which relate to theoretical material covered in SWP4APA. You are expected to play an active role in group-based learning and develop an awareness of group dynamics and group process. To facilitate interpersonal helping skill development, you will participate in role play exercises. These exercises help you learn to: (i) establish the context of practice, (ii) develop an effective working alliance, (iii) identify the client's perspective, (iv) develop a sense of the client's strengths and issues, and (v) set a direction for future work. Central to the subject is the development of critical awareness of self, including your style as a worker.
Learning Outcomes:
- Consolidate and synthesise knowledge of how social work practice is contextualised in an agency setting and be able to communicate this knowledge to a variety of audiences.
- Critically reflect upon your own practice including how you have applied basic theoretical concepts to your practice.
- Analyse, articulate, apply and reflect upon basic theoretical concepts about group processes to a variety of group situations from a social work perspective.
- Plan and implement a basic interview or occasion of service with individuals, families and groups.

Subject: FIELDS OF SOCIAL WORK PRACTICE A (SWP4FPA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will be introduced to the theory and knowledge base of two fields of social work practice, child & family welfare, and family violence. Your introduction to social work practice in these fields will recognise the importance of respect for and enabling human rights and taking a person-centred approach. You will be introduced to the key debates, policies, programs, service delivery and skills required to undertake beginning level practice in both fields.
Learning Outcomes:
- Synthesize and apply research outcomes and relevant psycho-social knowledge and skills to case management
- Analyse the conceptualisation of child protection and family violence practice over time including the values that underpin these conceptualisations.
- Analyse and evaluate the construction of child protection and family violence as social problems to apply practice frameworks and interventions
- Critique the success of policy developments in child protection and family violence and the drivers that have led to policy shifts.

Subject: LAW, HUMAN RIGHTS,  AND ETHICS IN SOCIAL WORK PRACTICE (SWP4LHE)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will expand your knowledge base in the legal context of Australian social work practice and how human rights and ethical principles guide social work practice. Professional competency will be developed by applying ethical decision-making models to case studies by observing real courts or tribunals. Case studies will address issues of diversity and complexity in our society and guidance will be given on how to make decisions that conform to ethical, legal and professional requirements while respecting clients and their rights.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate case materials by the application of an ethical decision-making framework.
- Critically analyse conflicts between competing legal, ethical and human rights principles and their impact on social workers and their practice.
- Analyse the impact of the law on social work practice and the role and responsibilities of social work practitioners using a conceptual framework.
- Critically analyse how human rights are addressed in social work practice.
