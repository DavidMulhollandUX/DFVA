## Description
The Doctor of Education program serves the needs of top-level education professionals who wish to consolidate their theoretical understandings and to apply these in educational practice.

The aim of the Doctor of Education program is to serve the needs of top-level education professionals who wish to consolidate, refine and expand their theoretical understandings and to apply these in educational practice. The orientation of the program is therefore towards the improvement of professional practice by the application of research findings to the problems, issues and development of teaching, learning, and educational management and politics.

Intake for this program happens biennially, in odd-numbered years.

See UNSW Higher Degree Research Learning Outcomes.

## Program Structure & Degree Requirements
Students must complete 144 Units of Credit (UOC) structured across the following academic components:

### Course component (48 UOC)
Students must take 48 UOC of the following courses.

- **EDST5036**: Education Project 1 [6 UOC]
- **EDST5039**: Education Seminar 2 [6 UOC]
- **EDST5034**: Current Issues in Education 2 [6 UOC]
- **EDST5033**: Research Methods 2 [6 UOC]
- **EDST5037**: Education Project 2 [6 UOC]
- **EDST5035**: Current Issues in Education 1 [6 UOC]
- **EDST5038**: Education Seminar 1 [6 UOC]
- **EDST5031**: Research Methods 1 [6 UOC]

### Thesis component (144 UOC)
The Thesis Component consists of the following courses. Students will typically be enrolled in 12 UOC of thesis each term.

- **EDST5911**: Thesis 2 [12 UOC]

## Admission Requirements
**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
- A candidate for the degree shall have been awarded a first or upper-second class (Distinction) Honours degree in education OR a Masters (Coursework) degree in education with a distinction average and a significant research component, consisting of methodology training and a stand-alone research project, or a qualification considered equivalent from another university or tertiary institution at a level acceptable to the Committee; and

- Have completed at least three years' professional experience in a branch of education, or in some other area that is judged by the Committee to be appropriate; and

- In exceptional cases an applicant who submits evidence of such other academic and professional qualifications as may be approved by the Committee may be permitted to enrol for the degree.

For further information, see the Admission to Higher Degree Research Programs Procedure.

Subject: Current Issues in Education 2 (EDST5034)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course is part of the Education Professional Doctorate program. The purpose of this course is to provide you with an overview of questions of researcher positionality and the development of a background context in which your research is situated. The course will be conducted seminar style. It is designed to help you articulate your positionality in relation to your research topic and methodology; and to successfully locate academic literature related to questions of researcher standpoint, ethics, and reflexivity. The course also supports the development of the background to the study, in which you will identify and evaluate literature relevant to the issues in education you address in your developing thesis. Note: This course is available only to EdPrDoc students.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically engage with theory, research, and practice to identify current issues in education.
- Use a systematic process to locate and evaluate academic literature related to developing a background to the research topic in education.
- Articulate a positionality statement and provide an argument for issues shaping your stance as a researcher.

Subject: Education Project 2 (EDST5037)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The purpose of this course is to provide you with an opportunity to learn how to prepare a research proposal in education. This course reviews the steps in the research process and considers the elements, structure and organisation, and writing style of research proposals in education. It will enable you to plan educational research and outline the steps using appropriate structure and organisation and writing style for a research proposal in education. It will assist you with preparing your proposal for confirmation as well as your oral presentation. During this Term, you will work primarily with your supervisors to prepare for your confirmation in Term 3. Confirmation consists of presentation of your research proposal to your confirmation panel together with an oral presentation on your project. Note: This course is only available to EdPrDoc students.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and explain the elements, structure, organisation, and writing style of a research proposal in education.
- Recognise and address the practical considerations in a research proposal for a research project in education.
- Identify and explain the steps in the research process.

Subject: Education Seminar 1 (EDST5038)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The purpose of this course is to introduce you to ethical matters and dilemmas that you encounter in conducting research. The course will cover aspects of project planning including feasibility concerns around access, participants and time, along with examining sources of tension in the ethical debate, ethical problems and issues confronting the researcher and the national framework and guidelines for research integrity. The course will enable you to plan a research project and identify ethical risks and apply ethical principles to a research project, and to your own professional conduct. Note: This course is only available to EdPrDoc students.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain and apply practical strategies to manage the fieldwork process in educational research.
- Analyse and interpret data using appropriate qualitative and/or quantitative methods in educational research.
- Apply ethical principles to the collection and analysis of data in educational research.
- Apply strategies to maintain data integrity before, during, and after data collection in educational research.
