## Description
The Master of Industrial Research is a research degree with a coursework component taken over one year full-time or equivalent. The research component will lead to a thesis which embodies the result of an original investigation, design or engineering development. A program of advanced study comprising 3 courses (equivalent to 18 UOC) makes up the remainder of the program.

Program Structure

Streams in this program require students to complete 1 research methods course (GSOE9400), 1 course on the essentials of industrial research (GSOE9401), and 1 Advanced Disciplinary Knowledge course. The Advanced Disciplinary Knowledge course may be replaced by 1 Disciplinary Knowledge Course, with this possibility subject to the approval of the Program Authority.

This coursework component of the degree must be completed during the first 4 terms (full-time equivalent) of a student's enrolment in the program.

See UNSW Higher Degree Research Learning Outcomes.

## Program Learning Outcomes (CILOs)
On successful completion of this program, students will be able to:

1. Students will demonstrate substantial advanced knowledge in a discipline area with an awareness of existing knowledge and developments in the field.
2. Students will be able to analyse complex information objectively and apply methods to solve complex engineering problems relevant to industry in a professional, ethically aware manner.
3. Students will be able to carry out research with autonomy, integrity and originality that makes a significant contribution to knowledge in their field.
4. Students will be able to disseminate research results and their implications within the industry and in professional and public domains.

## Career Opportunities & Employment Areas
Leadership roles within the rapidly evolving tech, processing and manufacturing industries. Graduates should also be well placed for careers as laboratory managers and as part of research teams.

Subject: Essentials of Industrial Research  (GSOE9401)
Credit Points: 0
Overview: The focus of this project-based course is to give the students an appreciation for the multidisciplinary methods and activities involved in modern industrial practices around product innovation and entrepreneurship. The students will learn the structured methods involved in exploring and scoping innovations, product design and development process, design for environment, protecting innovation, managing projects and projects risks, project development economics, business plan basics, and lean startup method, when possible aided by industrial examples and case studies. Guest seminars by industrial and topical experts will provide further practical perspectives. To gain proficiency in the subject area and learn in a practical setting, students will apply theory to practice through a team-based product innovation project, which is proposed and selected by themselves. Essentially, students will be able to implement the understanding and skills acquired in the course in industrial and entrepreneurial setups.
Learning Outcomes:
- Implement innovation and entrepreneurial principles in practical scenarios.
- Plan, manage and perform product innovation tasks, including opportunity identification, product planning, the front end of the product design, and business plan development.
- Develop team working and technical communication skills.
- Identify, explain, and reflect on the critical aspects of innovation and entrepreneurship principles.
- Apply multidisciplinary methods of industrial research, development, and commercialisation in an innovation project.
