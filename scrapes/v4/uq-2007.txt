## Career Opportunities & Employment Outcomes
The UQ Agribusiness program is designed for students who aspire to be the business leaders and managers of the future. This program will equip you with the necessary knowledge, skills and self-confidence to assist in effective and successful management, within constantly changing and highly competitive environments. Our graduates establish careers in the private and public sector, both within Australia and internationally.

The Bachelor of Agribusiness will prepare you for a business career in the growing number of food and fibre industries. Subject: Accounting for Decision Making (ACCT1101)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Introduction to the accounting environment, analysis and interpretation of financial statements and other business information for the purpose of decision making.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and explain accounting terms and concepts that are relevant to business decision making.
- Record and interpret business transactions and financial statements.
- Apply relevant and appropriate accounting knowledge and techniques in business problem-solving.
- Make and justify effective business operating, financing and investing decisions informed by relevant accounting information.
- Demonstrate management and cost accounting techniques, including the use of budgets, to support business decisions.
Assessment Tasks:
- Inspera Case Study Part 1 Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Inspera Case Study Part 2 Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- End-of-Semester Examination Identity Verified In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=AGRC1012 =====
Subject: Food & Fibre Case Studies I (AGRC1012)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: At an introductory level, the course examines strategic issues set in the context of food and fibre industries. Students carry out investigations framed around contemporary issues faced by agribusiness managers in firms & organisations providing inputs and services (finance, farm merchandise, technical services, plant & equipment & human resources) to agricultural, pastoral and horticultural producers. Each case study involves interviews with managers and other stakeholders. This is the first level of three courses that constitute the educational spine of the Agribusiness degree program.
Learning Outcomes:
- Classify the types and operations of firms in the agribusiness supply chain
- Analyse aspects of the management of an individual agribusiness by using a Business Model Canvas
- Evaluate elements of the business environment that influence the management and profitability of these firms
- Demonstrate the preliminary steps of the entrepreneurship process involved in starting a business
- Communicate at an appropriate professional standard, both orally and in written form.
- Interact effectively as a group toward a common outcome.
Assessment Tasks:
- Business Model Canvas 1 (Individual): 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Business Model Canvas 2 - Final Report (Individual): 45%% (Hurdle: False)
- Group Presentation (Existing Agribusiness) Identity Verified Team or group-based In-person: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Group Presentation (Agropreneur) Identity Verified Team or group-based In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Workshop 1 Hour/ Week Fieldwork 10 Hours/ Ad Hoc 2 Fieldtrips for ~ 5 hrs each - subject to change dependant on weather, host organisation's availability etc

Subject: Agribusiness Planning & Management (AGRC2023)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The foundation of this course builds on the basic management functions applicable to most enterprises, with a specific focus on planning. After the foundation phase the focus shifts to applying those concepts to the organisations in the agri sector, including farming enterprises. The course aims to give the students as much exposure to industry as possible through guest lectures and numerous case studies, with a focus on class group presentations.
Learning Outcomes:
- Illustrate the underlying theoretical concepts and principles behind agribusiness planning and management
- Apply the concepts and principles to analyse the environment in which agribusinesses operate in case studies
- Propose strategies to overcome such challenges
- Create a comprehensive business plan for an agribusiness that integrates financial planning, marketing strategies, production processes, and human resource management
- Demonstrate their understanding of how these four critical elements in agribusiness are interconnected and contribute to the overall success of the business
- Interact effectively as a group toward a common outcome.
Assessment Tasks:
- Exam - In-Semester During Class- Case Study Identity Verified In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
- Business Plan Presentation (Group) Identity Verified Team or group-based: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Business Plan Report Submission (Group) Team or group-based: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Applied Class 1 Hour/ Week

Subject: Agricultural Economics (AGRC2030)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Economic concepts are introduced and applied: to analyse agricultural production and resource use decisions; to develop tools to aid farm management decisions; to understand the economics of agricultural markets and how they work; and to assess agricultural and resource policies.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate an understanding of basic economic principles and their application to agriculture
- Understand and appreciate the relationship between biophysical and socio-economic information in the analysis of resource allocation within the agricultural sector
- Use budgeting and economic analysis tools for farm and risk management and decision making
- Understand the economics of agricultural markets and the use of economic concepts in the analysis of these markets
- Apply economic analysis to the critical analysis of agricultural and resource policies

Subject: Introduction to Human Resource Management (MGTS2604)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides an introduction and overview of the role and functioning of various HRM activities in organizations for students to gain an appreciation of the breadth and scope of HRM. Topics include: HRM planning and job analysis, recruitment and selection, performance management, training and development, reward management, diversity management and inclusion, occupational health and safety, HR evaluation, strategic HRM and international HRM. - Critically evaluate the appropriateness of approaches to HRM functions in local and international contexts.
- Demonstrate practical skills in performing HRM functions to individual, team and organisational practices.
- Work as individuals to apply HRM concepts and theories to the analysis of individual's career management, team and organizational HRM practices.
- Work as individuals to communicate the analysis and recommendations relating to an individual's career management, team and organization's HRM practices.
Assessment Tasks:

Subject: Commodities, Futures &; Options (MKTG3961)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: An introduction to the process of price risk management associated with the trading of agricultural commodities. Provides a planning framework within which price risk can be managed as well as an introduction to concepts of forward selling, futures and options trading. The project involves a simulated trading exercise.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically analyse and identify the key factors contributing to price fluctuations in agricultural commodities, incorporating both macroeconomic and microeconomic perspectives.
- Demonstrate proficiency in employing essential risk management instruments available to agribusinesses, including pools, forward contracts, hectare contracts, multi-grade contracts, futures, and options, for mitigating price risk.
- Develop, apply, and execute trading and hedging strategies using commodity derivatives to effectively manage and mitigate price risk associated with agricultural production in live market environments.
- Effectively articulate and justify trading and hedging decisions and strategies in agribusiness, showcasing the ability to communicate complex concepts and outcomes clearly to both specialist and non-specialist audiences.
- Integrate and apply knowledge of price risk analysis, risk management instruments, and trading strategies to develop comprehensive price risk management solutions for agricultural commodities.
Assessment Tasks:
- Trading Simulation Presentation (Group) Identity Verified Team or group-based In-person: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Hedging Simulation Report (Group) Team or group-based: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Final Examination Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Applied Class 1 Hour/ Week
