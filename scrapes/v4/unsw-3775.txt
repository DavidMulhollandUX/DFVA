## Description
This program is only available combined with the Civil Engineering specialisation within the Bachelor of Engineering (Honours) (3707) program.

The Bachelor of Surveying is designed to equip you for a career as a professional Surveyor. We provide you with a solid background that will prepare you for learning knowledge and skills in this discipline. You will get the chance to apply yourself to surveying knowledge, application and professional practice.

## Career Opportunities & Employment Areas
Engineer working in the mining industry, construction surveying firms, architecture firms, land management and planning, hydrographic surveying, aerial imaging and cartography.

- Apply various statistical models and methods for drawing conclusions and making decisions under uncertainty in engineering contexts.

Subject: Sustainability in Construction (CVEN4104)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course will provide fundamental knowledge and hands-on experience for practising sustainability in construction. The aim is teaching the environmental, social and economic impacts of buildings and built environment and demonstrating how sustainable construction design can avoid global warming and resource depletion issues. The contents of this course are designed to provide hands-on experience in analytical thinking and decision making with regards to the application of sustainability in construction projects. This course explains history and drivers of sustainability in construction and review sustainability policies, programs, and incentives in Australia. Principles of life cycle assessment (LCA) and life cycle costs (LCC) will be explained, and hands-on experience of evaluating LCA and LCC will be offered in the workshop classes and group assignment. This course also provides an overview of the current green rating systems for buildings and built environment in Australia. Additionally, examples of sustainable construction design, materials and energy resources, as well as case studies of best sustainable practices and future trends of sustainable construction will be demonstrated. A number of alternative strategies available in design, procurement, construction, operation and end-of-life phases to reduce the overall environmental impact of a construction project are discussed. Students are expected to learn how the available strategies, standards and guidelines can be applied to analyse and improve sustainability in practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Summarise the history and drivers of sustainability in construction, as well as sustainability policies, programs, and incentives in Australia
- Demonstrate hands-on skills in evaluating life cycle assessment and life cycle cost assessment in a construction-related activity
- Distinguish sustainable construction design, materials and energy resources used in case studies of best sustainable practices and future trends in sustainable construction
- Explain how green rating schemes work in Australia

Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course will be delivered in two parts. Part one is focused on ground energy and ground source heat pump systems, common design parameters and approaches and available analytical solutions to understand the concept of geothermal systems. - Explain the concept and applications of ground energy systems. Design and evaluate of these systems via hand calculations and computational modelling.
- Explain the basic principles of critical state soil mechanics and soil plasticity, and use elastic-plastic constitutive models to simulate soil stress-strain behaviour.

Subject: Groundwater Resource Investigation (CVEN4503)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Groundwater is one of Australia's most critical natural resources, yet the nation faces a persistent shortage of engineers with the skills to understand and manage it sustainably. This course equips you with theoretical, numerical and field skills to address this challenge. You will learn how to measure groundwater resources, understand groundwater recharge, its interaction with rivers and what drives movement of groundwater. You will also look at the impact of extraction on our groundwater resources. A highlight of the course is a 5-day field trip to UNSW's Wellington Field Research Station. During this trip, you'll have a chance to learn from experts in groundwater and geophysics and utilise state-of-the-art equipment. In teams, you will conduct a variety of groundwater, geochemical and geophysical surveys on site. This site features a network of boreholes through a landscape of clay and river deposits into weathered basalt overlying tight basement rocks and limestone formations, a geological setting that presents real questions about groundwater as a resource, its movement, interaction with streams and recharge. You will collect your own data, interpret it and build a synthesised site analysis interpretation with your team. Through this course, you will develop skills directly relevant to professional practice, including conducting field investigations and groundwater assessments, developing and interpreting conceptual and analytical groundwater models, analysing geological, hydrogeological and geophysical data and preparing high quality technical reports.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop and use conceptual and analytical models to assess groundwater flow and the potential impacts of groundwater use.
- Work effectively in teams during field and analytical activities, demonstrating professional collaboration, safe fieldwork conduct, responsibility, and shared problem-solving.
- Communicate technical findings clearly and professionally supported by appropriate data, analysis, and engineering reasoning.
- Interpret field and experimental data to understand groundwater behaviour and aquifer properties.

Overview: In this course, you will experience first-hand one of the major things that engineers do: designing and building creative solutions to problems. - Apply foundational technical theory and skills to a design project.
- Demonstrate a systematic approach to design in response to a specified set of project requirements.
