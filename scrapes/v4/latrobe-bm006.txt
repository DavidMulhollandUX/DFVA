## Global Master of Business Administration - Melbourne (Bundoora)
##### BM006
##### 240 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
Wherever you are in your career – and wherever you want to go next – La Trobe’s Global Master of Business Administration (GMBA) can help you reach your goals.

Specifically designed for early-career and emerging professionals from diverse international backgrounds, you’ll accelerate your professional development and broaden your career opportunities – both domestically and internationally – when you build global business acumen, get hands-on experience, and grow your professional network.

To help launch your career, you’ll cultivate a deep understanding of best-practice management and strategic business knowledge. With globally orientated subjects, you’ll focus on cross-cultural awareness, organisational effectiveness, sustainability, and emerging technologies in business such as artificial intelligence (AI). You’ll further enhance your expertise with options to specialise in the business field you’re interested in.

Learn in dynamic and collaborative workshops, enriched by exposure to industry-informed, real-world learning activities strategically designed to get you career-ready. Aligned to current – and future – industry demands, you’ll gain invaluable understanding of contemporary and emerging businesses through interaction with academics, peers and industry experts, as well as hands-on, practical experience through an industry placement.

You'll learn:

  * Global strategic thinking, global marketing and consumer behaviour
  *   * Develop strategies that can be adapted to diverse cultural, economic, and political environments. Adapt marketing strategies to diverse cultural, demographic, and economic conditions.

  * Financial management
  *   * Acquire financial skills, including financial analysis, introductory accounting, and investment strategies.

  * Supply chain and operations management
  *   * Gain knowledge of global supply chain management, logistics, and operations in a global context.

  * Ethics, sustainability, and corporate social responsibility (CSR)
  *   * Understand the importance of ethical practices in a global business environment and analyse sustainable business practices and their implementation internationally.

  * Leadership and organisational behaviour
  *   * Understand organisational behaviour in a global context, including team dynamics, conflict resolution, and motivation strategies.

  * Digital transformation, business analytics and technology
  *   * Explore the role and impact of emerging technologies and automation in modern business management, and learn how to use data analytics, business intelligence and AI tools to inform decisions.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Evaluate and apply leadership, ethical reasoning, and responsible management practices in diverse organisational and cross-cultural settings that consider the well-being of stakeholders and the broader community
**2.**
Apply advanced qualitative and quantitative analytical methods to diagnose complex business problems and support evidence-based decisions
**3.**
Evaluate and apply emerging business models and technologies to enhance organisational adaptability and competitiveness
**4.**
Communicate complex business ideas clearly and persuasively to diverse stakeholders.
**5.**
Manage financial and non-financial resources to optimise organisational performance and ensure sustainable outcomes
**6.**
Design and implement innovative strategies for complex global business environments using multidisciplinary knowledge

### Course features
### Work based learning (placement) requirements
During this course, you will have the opportunity to participate in a Work Based Learning (WBL) placement experience, designed to allow you to extend your formal academic learning beyond the classroom. The WBL experience will provide the opportunity to bring your learning from the university into a work environment and test out your knowledge in a professional real-world environment. Placements are expected to be self-sourced by students and be related to their studies. Subject to approval from the University, placements can be undertaken within an organisation, remotely, or as a virtual experience.

Industry informed curriculum, Industry-informed projects, Field trips

Subject: SUSTAINABILITY AND ESG REPORTING (ACC5008)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject provides a comprehensive introduction to sustainability reporting within the context of contemporary business practices. You will explore the principles of 'Triple Bottom Line Accounting' and develop expertise in both external and internal reporting aspects of corporate social and environmental sustainability. The subject examines how sustainability information can be integrated into organisational decision-making processes and explores the implications for assurance functions in modern accounting practice. Sustainability Reporting addresses the growing demand for accounting professionals with expertise in Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) frameworks, climate-related financial disclosures, and sustainability metrics. Through a combination of theoretical foundations and practical applications, you will develop the skills needed to navigate the rapidly evolving landscape of sustainability reporting.
Learning Outcomes:
- Collaborate effectively in teams to analyse organisational sustainability reporting practices
- Communicate sustainability reporting findings clearly and persuasively to professional and academic audiences using appropriate formats and conventions
- Critically evaluate the value of sustainability reporting, accountability, and reporting in contemporary business contexts
- Apply relevant theoretical concepts to analyse complex sustainability reporting issues with consideration of ethical, social, and environmental consequences

Subject: ECONOMICS FOR GLOBAL LEADERS (BUA4003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Economics impacts on all aspects of business and society. Key individual, organisational and government decisions around the allocation of scarce resources are at the core of economics, and an understanding of economic concepts, frameworks and language can greatly enhance a leader’s capacity to make sound decisions and influence the decisions of those around them. This subject develops students’ knowledge of economic concepts and provides the opportunity to apply this knowledge to real world situations. Students will apply economic concepts and frameworks to current issues of concern for organisations and society. Students will learn how to evaluate and apply critical aspects of economics such as the main market forces of supply and demand; the choices producers make in relation to their costs; revenues and profit; competitive and monopoly market structures and how these affect market prices; the use of game theory to make more strategic economic decisions; measuring a nation's income and the cost of living; the drivers of economic growth and the causes of unemployment.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply economic frameworks to business and social problems for policy and decision-making.
- Construct and present logical and persuasive economic arguments and communicate concepts professionally.
- Research and apply economic concepts to predict how changes in economic conditions may impact individuals, businesses and industries within market structures.
- Critically analyse economic data to explain relationships between economic variables and their impacts on policy, business and individuals.

Subject: INTERNATIONAL FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT (FIN5IFM)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Financial globalisation, a heightened geopolitical environment, economic uncertainties, and increased ESG focus present unique challenges and opportunities for investors and corporations worldwide. International Financial Management develops your conceptual and applied knowledge and skills to manage the critical value drivers of firms operating globally. - Critically evaluate contemporary global financial systems and current issues in international finance that affect the global flow of funds and financial management functions of global firms.

Subject: INTERNATIONAL TRANSPORT AND LOGISTICS (MGT5004)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: When cargo flows and supply chains extend beyond national borders, the complexity of international transport and logistics activities increases. This subject focuses on the global transport of goods, which heavily relies on ships, aircraft, and trains. As a result, more stakeholders become involved, additional documentation is generated and transferred, financial payment risks intensify, and disruptions and uncertainties can impact supply chains. In this subject, you will acquire essential knowledge and develop job-ready skills necessary for roles such as international transport and logistics coordinators and freight forwarders. This includes gaining familiarity with trade and customs policies, understanding documentation requirements for efficient cargo flows and physical distribution, developing the ability to design international transport and logistics plans for various business scenarios, creating recovery plans to address changes and disruptions in international transport and logistics, and effectively communicating information and potential changes to stakeholders. Additionally, the subject explores sustainability concerns, data analytics, and emerging technologies related to digitalisation.
Learning Outcomes:
- Design international transport and logistics plans for efficient, profitable, sustainable and resilient supply chains.
- Exercise professional judgement and evaluate international logistics management practices for a collaborative supply chain.
- Critically analyse the impact of international transport and logistics on a company's performance and collaborations within global supply chains.
- Critically analyse the implications of trade policies, international conventions, sustainability commitments, and digitalisation on global transport, logistics, and supply chains.
