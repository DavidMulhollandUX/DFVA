## Bachelor of Business
##### B2000
##### 144 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
The Bachelor of Business will enable you to delve into the dynamic world of business. You will have the opportunity to understand what makes organisations, brands and markets function effectively and sustainably. Whether you are interested in working for a large corporation, or a not-for-profit organisation, managing a business, or becoming an entrepreneur, the Bachelor of Business will give you the knowledge and skills you need to thrive in a competitive business landscape. The course provides choice and flexibility by allowing you to develop broad business knowledge as well as specialised expertise in one or two chosen areas of business practice. It also offers you the opportunity to study overseas or undertake an internship, as well as a range of other experiences which enable you to maximise your employability at graduation. 

You will be well equipped to seek employment in both private and public-sector enterprises.

### Structure
This is a comprehensive course, structured in four parts: 

Part A. Core studies

These studies will provide you with a broad foundation for your study of business and expose you to several business disciplines. It will contribute breadth to your knowledge of business and develop your understanding of multi-disciplinary decision making in organisations. It will also give you the opportunity to learn more about each discipline before finalising your choice of major. 

Part B. Major studies

Your major studies will provide you with a focused program of study that will develop your expertise. You will develop, apply and communicate an advanced level of understanding of the concepts and theoretical frameworks that constitute the knowledge base of your major area of study.

Part C. Capstone studies

This is designed to consolidate the knowledge and skills acquired throughout the study of your major.

Part D. Elective studies

This will enable you to further develop knowledge of your chosen major, or business more broadly, or study a second business major. Alternatively you can select units from across the University in which you are eligible to enrol.

Masters Accelerated Pathway

The Bachelor of Business offers an Accelerated Pathway option into the Master of Business, Master of Global Business, Master of Management, Master of Applied Marketing and Master of Regulation and Compliance. This Accelerated Pathway allows you to complete a master’s degree with one additional year of full-time study.

If you have completed at least 96 credit points and have achieved a high credit (65%) average, you can apply to the Course Director for the master’s accelerated pathway. If admitted to the accelerated pathway, you will use four units (24 credit points) of your elective studies (Part D) to complete master-level units from one of the destination courses.

Note: You still need to meet the entry requirement of having an overall 65% weighted average mark at the end of your degree to enter the master’s program.

### Requirements
You must complete units as specified in Parts A, B, C and D (144 credit points): including at least 96 credit points of study in Parts A, B, and C and up to 48 credit points of electives in Part D.

In choosing your units, you must ensure that you complete no more than 10 units (60 credit points) at level 1 and at least six units (36 credit points) at level 3, including four units (24 credit points) at level 3 offered by the Faculty of Business and Economics.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
identify, gather and critically evaluate sources of knowledge and business information to support evidence-based decisions
**2.**
exhibit effective collaboration in teams to address business challenges
**3.**
integrate global contexts in business analysis and decision-making.
**4.**
demonstrate relevant knowledge of a range of business activities and their interrelationships as well as technical skills in at least one business discipline.
**5.**
demonstrate an inclusive mindset that embeds equity, diversity and inclusion in business practices by incorporating differing perspectives, cultures, identities, skills, and abilities.
**6.**
embed ethical considerations into decision-making processes and propose solutions that result in sustainable outcomes for stakeholders and the environment.
**7.**
communicate clearly, effectively and professionally in a business context with the ability to adapt to diverse contexts and audiences.
**8.**
develop the ability to critically analyse business problems and provide ethical, discipline-based solutions by applying business knowledge and technical skills.

### Progression to further studies
Successful completion of this course may provide a pathway to the one year honours program Bachelor of Commerce (Honours) (B3701).  To be eligible to apply for entry into the Bachelor of Commerce (Honours) (B3701), you must obtain a distinction grade average (70%) or above in 24 credit points of studies in relevant discipline units at Level 3. In addition, some majors require particular Level 3 units to be taken for admission to honours. The details are provided with the requirements for each major to which this applies.

### AHEGS
The Bachelor of Business is an undergraduate degree. The course is taught in English and normally takes three years of full-time study or six years of part-time study or equivalent. The course is designed to provide graduates with high-level skills in a range of key business disciplines. It blends a conceptual theoretical framework with practical applications and covers basic discipline material through to more specialised knowledge in the selected discipline. The Bachelor of Business is a level 7 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

## ACF1100 - Introduction to financial accounting
Faculty: Department of Accounting | School: Faculty of Business and Economics | Credit points: 6

### Overview
This unit introduces you to fundamental accounting concepts and rules to provide a foundation for further study within the accounting discipline. Emphasis is placed on developing meaningful understanding of both accounting terminology and technical rules for recording, guided by the Conceptual Framework and selected Accounting Standards. Applying this knowledge along with critical thinking skills will facilitate evidence-based decision-making using accounting data, and the preparation and interpretation of financial statements.

### Learning Outcomes
1. FIRST SEMESTER: apply principles of selected Accounting Standards and prepare financial statements for business entities including service and retail organisations SECOND SEMESTER: collaborate effectively in team-based accounting tasks to contribute disciplinary knowledge and support collective problem solving
2. FIRST SEMESTER: apply the principles of double-entry and accrual accounting SECOND SEMESTER: apply the Conceptual Framework and selected Accounting Standards to record transactions and prepare basic financial statements
3. FIRST SEMESTER: identify and analyse measurement systems and their relationship with the accounting Conceptual Framework SECOND SEMESTER: explain fundamental accounting concepts and rules by justifying how and why economic transactions are recorded in accounting systems
4. FIRST SEMESTER: describe and apply definitions and recognition criteria for assets, liabilities, incomes and expenses as specified in the Conceptual Framework SECOND SEMESTER: interpret accounting data to derive and communicate meaningful and relevant business insights
5. FIRST SEMESTER: apply critical thinking, problem solving, and communication skills within corporate financial reporting topics. SECOND SEMESTER: analyse accounting data to identify issues and support reasoned conclusions in business contexts.

## BTF1010 - Business law
Faculty: Department of Business Law and Taxation | School: Faculty of Business and Economics | Credit points: 6

### Overview
As the law affects all kinds of business activities, learning about the law as a non-lawyer is vital for everyone working in business. There are serious legal consequences for failing to comply with the law, which in turn can impact upon business profitability and value. Understanding the law will enable you to understand business risks and to seize upon business opportunities, while also recognising when professional legal advice might be required. Business Law focuses on learning and applying essential legal rules to real-world business situations in Australia, including the Australian legal system, the law of contract, and the law of torts (focusing on negligence as it applies to professionals). Privacy and data protection laws and the law of business organisations, including the law of agency and an introduction to company law, will also be covered.

### Learning Outcomes
1. FIRST SEMESTER: explain the nature of the Australian legal system and its role in regulating business activity SECOND SEMESTER: discuss foundational aspects and institutional arrangements of the Australian legal system and its role in regulating business activity
2. FIRST SEMESTER: assess common legal scenarios arising in business and identify relevant legal principles to predict possible legal risks and outcomes SECOND SEMESTER: show legal obligations and ethical considerations in the context of business decision-making and conduct
3. FIRST SEMESTER: understand key legal principles from contract law, consumer protection law, tort law, privacy and data protection law, and the law of business organisations, and the impact these principles have on business conduct SECOND SEMESTER: apply key areas of law, including contract law and tort law, to common business problems
4. FIRST SEMESTER: demonstrate an understanding of legal reasoning and apply this skill set to solve legal problems and inform business decision-making and conduct SECOND SEMESTER: employ legal reasoning to communicate professionally on legal issues.
5. FIRST SEMESTER: recall business ethical obligations arising under the law relevant to business activity and conduct.

## ECF1100 - Microeconomics
Faculty: Department of Economics | School: Faculty of Business and Economics | Credit points: 6

### Overview
This unit provides you with an introduction to microeconomics. You will be given the tools to understand basic economic ideas and to apply their learning to analyse economic applications in everyday life. It begins with an introduction to supply and demand and the basic forces that determine an equilibrium in a market economy. Next, it introduces a framework for learning about consumer behaviour and analysing consumer decisions. We then turn our attention to firms and their decisions about optimal production, and the impact of different market structures on firms’ behaviour. The final section of the unit provides an introduction to some of the more advanced topics that can be analysed using microeconomic theory, including consumer choice, and game theory.

### Learning Outcomes
1. evaluate the 'economic way of thinking' about a broad range of individual, business and government choices and decisions
2. apply economic principles to evaluate economic institutions, economic decisions and performance
3. recognise regulatory and political environments and how they impact on business.

## ETF1100 - Business statistics
Faculty: Department of Econometrics and Business Statistics | School: Faculty of Business and Economics | Credit points: 6

### Overview
This unit introduces you to the basic foundations of Business Analytics and Statistics. You will learn how to work with data, in conjunction with basic statistics and spreadsheet tools, to solve simple business problems. The unit introduces the appropriate methods to visualise and summarise data, and statistical tools to quantify uncertainty that may arise from data analysis. You will learn how to assess relationships between business variables, and learn how to build basic regression models to capture such relationships in a business context. Spreadsheet optimisation tools that assist in solving business optimisation problems will also be covered. The skills learnt from this unit assist you in developing critical thinking and analytical skills around data and decision making in business.

### Learning Outcomes
1. analyse probability in a given business context to support decision making
2. build the simple and multiple regression model in Excel and communicate the results in relevant business contexts, including in a team setting
3. classify the different data types and construct appropriate data visualisation tools for effective data presentation
4. solve basic business optimisation problems using Excel.
5. analyse relationships between business data using appropriate visualisation and statistical tools
6. apply the concept of random sampling to evaluate uncertainty in statistics and formulate hypothesis testing problems using business data for decision-making
