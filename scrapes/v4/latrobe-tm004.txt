## Master of Quantum Information Technology - Melbourne (Bundoora)
##### TM004
##### 195 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
Build the scientific, technical and entrepreneurial capabilities to work in the emerging quantum information industries revolutionising our world with La Trobe’s unique and cross-disciplinary Master of Quantum Information Technology. 

Maximise your background in engineering, IT or science by focusing your future career through directed learning and mastering key areas in quantum information technology. Hone your skills with 24/7 access to purpose-built, high-tech labs. Develop a quantum technology start-up concept and pitch by investigating platforms using a technology or business development lens. Tap into industry expertise through our Engineering and IT Showcase and long-standing partnerships with industry leaders in technology, from organisations as big as Microsoft to start-ups like Quantum Brilliance.

By completing La Trobe's Master of Quantum Information Technology, you will:

  * explore the core principles and underlying technologies of quantum computing, including programming and algorithm design
  * study key application areas such as cybersecurity, artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning and cryptography
  * build transferable employability skills in entrepreneurship, business development and project management
  * engage with industry leaders from local and global start-ups and grow your professional networks.

Subject: CYBER SECURITY PRINCIPLES (CSE4004)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In the Internet era, industries and organisations need to be aware of, and be prepared to defend against threats and attacks. Stakeholders should be familiar with the basic principles and best practices of cyber security to better protect their businesses. In this subject, the principles, the state of the art, and strategies for the future of cyber security is explored thoroughly. - Formulate approaches for incident analysis, risk management in cyber space and incident response
- Analyse various software design practices and principles of web security

Subject: MATHEMATICS FOR DATA SCIENCE (MAT4MDS)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Important mathematical ideas which underpin the theory and techniques of data science are introduced and consolidated in this subject. Matrices are used to store and work with quantitative information, and the methods of calculus are used to find extreme values and accumulation. The Gamma and Beta functions are introduced, as are eigenvalues, eigenvectors and the rank of a matrix. Emphasis is placed on the relevance of the mathematics to data science applications (such as least squares estimators and calculation of variance in data), and on the development of clear communication in explaining technical ideas. This is a foundational subject for the Master of Data Science.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply mathematical skills to analysis of data science literature.
- Perform mathematical calculations relevant to data science fluently and accurately.
- Creatively apply mathematical techniques to unfamiliar problems.
- Present mathematical thinking in written form in a meaningful and succinct way using both words and mathematical notation.

Subject: ESSENTIALS OF QUANTUM INFORMATION SCIENCE (PHY5001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject introduces quantum information science and technology, with a focus on quantum computing, for those who need to understand how it will transform science and industry. Students will learn about underlying physical principles that make quantum computers possible and exponentially faster than digital supercomputers for certain calculations. This knowledge will be applied to consider the implications of this new frontier of computing for cybersecurity and secure communication, information processing, and numerical simulation. No prior knowledge of quantum physics is required. Abstract quantum effects, including superposition and entanglement, will be explored through practical activities that help students understand tangible applications, including encoding quantum information, quantum logic operations and quantum cryptography. Students will then learn to code simple algorithms and execute them on real quantum computers and in simulation environments. The application of key quantum algorithms to real-world problems will be investigated, along with their potential performance advantage over classical algorithms.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate key technology requirements for quantum computers to be able to function properly.
- Assess quantum algorithms and quantum protocols for application to information processing and communication.
- Describe the differences between quantum and classical computation and differentiate their computational capabilities.
- Critique the potential for quantum computing to disrupt modern computation, communication and cryptography.

Subject: QUANTUM INFORMATION THEORY FOR COMPUTER SCIENTISTS AND ENGINEERS (PHY5004)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Recent advances in quantum technology have not only opened up a whole new world of applications, but also changed the way that we understand quantum theory. In this subject, students will learn about quantum theory as a framework for information processing, instead of the traditional approach to learning quantum theory as a theory of microscopic matter. Emphasis will be given to relevant concepts of measurement, probability, correlation and transformations, rather than waves and particles.
Learning Outcomes:
- Investigate how features of quantum systems are related to concepts of information theory.
- Critically review differences between classical and quantum information
- Critically analyse different aspects of quantum computation, communication and cryptography using the framework of quantum theory.
- Elucidate basic problems of quantum information theory.
