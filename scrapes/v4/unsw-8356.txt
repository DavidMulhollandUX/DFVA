## Description
The AGSM MBA (Executive) degree is designed for experienced professionals. The degree will equip you with the knowledge, skills and connections that you need to accelerate your career progression and take on new challenges in your current industry or in a new industry or sector. The core of the program helps ensure that you have the necessary contemporary business knowledge and skills required in our rapidly changing environment. In addition, you can choose from a wide range of elective courses to ensure that the program is tailored to your specific needs and interests. This includes the opportunity to participate in AGSM's international offerings.

Courses are delivered in a range of modes to ensure that you can fit study around your personal, work and other commitments. In these courses you will be working with skilled facilitators and studying with experienced colleagues from diverse backgrounds.

The most distinctive feature of the AGSM MBA (Executive) Program is the Executive Year, with residential courses delivered over three consecutive terms. You will participate with the same cohort in a transformational learning experience that will help you develop the leadership skills and strategic insights that contemporary executives need in an accelerating world.

You will be able to apply the concepts and tools encountered in the program to your professional life immediately, while preparing for great things in the future – accelerating your current career, following a new path or building a new enterprise.

The program is designed to be undertaken part-time in combination with full-time employment.

## Admission Requirements
**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
To be eligible to be admitted to the MBA (Executive) Program, students must meet the following minimum entry criteria:

- A Bachelor’s degree (or equivalent) in any discipline plus at least 5 years' relevant professional experience

OR

- For students without a previous degree, at least 5 years' relevant professional experience plus a minimum of 5 years' managerial experience with accountability for outcomes, projects and people. Your CV will need to effectively demonstrate this.

Subject: Accounting and Financial Management (AGSM6210)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: NOTE: This course was previously identified as MNGT6210. The aim of this course is to show how the effective use of financial information can improve organisational decision-making. The course provides a broad introduction to how accounting contributes to an organisation. You will examine how accounting can help managers measure and analyse their organisation's economic performance, to improve resource allocation, and build accountability for performance through effective governance. The users of accounting information are typically classified as either external (such as investors or lenders) or internal (such as operational managers). Different users have different information needs. This course deals with the needs of these two main user groups and how senior managers can more effectively use accounting data to connect with them. The course explores accounting techniques and terminology, to equip you with sufficient conceptual and practical skills to make you confident in your ability to understand and communicate complex financial strategies. You will also examine the design and operation of accounting systems. As a result, the course integrates ideas and concepts from accounting with a range of other disciplines and reveals how the effective use of financial information provided by such systems can improve organisational decision-making. You will also examine how firms use non-financial information such as Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) to communicate their performance and strategies. It is assumed that participants have little prior knowledge of accounting and financial management.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the objectives, concepts and relationships that underpin both internal and external financial reports
- Apply analytical tools, techniques and frameworks to evaluate and critically examine the contents of both internal and external financial reports
- Communicate financial information to interested stakeholders in an ethical and professional manner
- Research the financial performance and position of reporting entities

Subject: Data Analytics and Decision-making (AGSM6234)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course is designed to provide a broad strategic perspective on issues and challenges related to incorporating data analytics and AI into business decision-making for strategic gain and meaningful change. Due to the complex and multi-faceted nature of the world of analytics, leaders and managers need some basic and abstract familiarity with the technical building blocks of analytics. This enables them to speak the language of analytics with fluency. Also, since emerging tools and objects in the world of AI and analytics are underpinned by these technical building blocks, this familiarity enables leaders and managers to separate hype from reality with confidence and make better decisions when integrating analytics/AI into their organisations. We deliver this course in three phases to provide an integrated strategic–technical–execution view of data analytics. In Phase 1, we will explore the concept of data strategy and its connection to business strategy. In Phase 2, we aim to help you understand basic analytics and statistical concepts, building technical confidence to distinguish between hype and reality in analytics and AI, while learning to speak the language of analytics fluently. In Phase 3, we will address the challenges of incorporating analytics and AI into organisations and explore their potential to create business value and drive innovative changes in business processes.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop basic data literacy and explain how data analytics can support decision-making in business contexts.
- Explain the importance of ethics and governance in order to apply relevant frameworks in data-driven organisations, and evaluate the social and ethical implications of data collection and analysis.
- Select and appropriately apply various data-analytics techniques to identify business problems, trends and patterns used to inform business decision-making.
- Develop and integrate data-driven recommendations and solutions to enhance and support business strategies and decision-making through well-reasoned analysis.
- Use visual and digital mediums to communicate clear analytical insights and develop convincing narratives from complex datasets.

Subject: Managing People and Organisations (AGSM6275)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The organisations we work in are rich and complex entities in which many interactions, relationships and processes are played out every day. The complexity of these elements often poses challenges in understanding and management. Managing People and Organisations (MPO) is focused on managers who manage people – some you manage formally, such as your subordinates, and others informally, such as your peers and bosses. Central to the course is the characterisation and discussion of everyday functions and roles in which managers take part. These include sense-making, decision-making, leading, motivating, planning, organising, mentoring, monitoring and influencing. We also address the context in which managers function and examine the effect of current issues they face. These include an increased emphasis on entrepreneurship; managing with social responsibility and the role of ethics; leading diverse, equitable and inclusive teams, and managing in a global environment. Each Unit in this course covers a topic that contributes to your undertaking these managerial roles more effectively so that you can become a better manager of yourself, others, teams and organisations. In this course, we will ask you to test your understanding of the concepts that we introduce by examining your working life. This could involve analysing your actions, the actions of others, processes that occur within your organisation and key organisational elements. You can use these activities to evaluate the concepts by asking yourself: how useful is this idea to me? how can I modify this idea in light of my own experiences? how can I apply this idea to my own organisation? MPO will introduce you to some of the key concepts, issues and debates in management, offering a glimpse into various topics that could individually constitute full courses. It is intended to serve as a foundation for your understanding of management and managing others, marking the beginning of your learning journey rather than its conclusion.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate the role of the environment within and outside of the organisation in managing and leading others, teams and the organisation.
- Appraise potential biases in decision-making to make defendable decisions based on critical evidence.
- Apply the principles of ethics and responsible management and discuss the role they play in driving sustainable outcomes for organisations.
- Explore different frameworks and models that inform the role of a manager inside an organisation.
- Apply course frameworks to critically reflect on your practical experience in prior or existing professional or managerial roles.
- Analyse and discuss how teams work in organisations to enable you to design, manage and lead effective teams.

It provides you with a deeper understanding of the economic factors that drive financial performance. Subject: Social Impact Investment (COMM5703)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course is managed by the Centre for Social Impact. The landscape of social finance and impact investment has undergone a transformation – driven by global economic realignment, the privatisation of community and government services, shifting patterns of wealth distribution and intergenerational wealth transfer, and the rising expectations of corporate citizenship and philanthropy. These forces have accelerated the growth of social impact investment: a field in which commercial enterprise, blended finance, and philanthropic capital are increasingly deployed together to address complex social and environmental challenges. COMM5703 Social Impact Investment develops critical understanding of the history, core concepts, and current issues shaping philanthropy, fundraising, and social impact investment – both in Australia and globally. Students will examine the full capital spectrum, from philanthropic giving to impact bonds and ESG-aligned investment vehicles, exploring how these instruments are structured, deployed, and evaluated across diverse economic regulatory, and cultural contexts. Ethical and philosophical perspectives, impact measurement, and the legal dimensions of social investment programs are also examined, equipping students to engage as informed and strategic practitioners in this field.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and apply key instruments of social impact investment
- Identify and critically evaluate the roles and interrelationships of diverse players in the social impact investment ecosystem
- Explain the trends, drivers and challenges shaping the dynamics of the social impact investment market in Australia and globally
- Discuss the drivers of the emergence of social impact investment and the role it plays within the capital spectrum

Subject: Corporate Responsibility and Accountability (COMM5709)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Corporate Responsibility and Accountability is a core course for the Global Sustainability and Social Impact specialisation in the Master of Commerce, and an elective for those undertaking the Graduate Certificate in Social Impact, Graduate Diploma in Social Impact or MBA Programs. It examines how business manages its social, environmental and economic impact on society. The course first reviews the historical evolution and development of the concept of Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) and Corporate Citizenship (CC) and why it is now a key part of managing the business enterprise. Second, the course examines what acting responsibly means for corporations in terms of the market, community, environment, workplace and government. Issues covered include business and human rights, business-community partnerships, corporate philanthropy, the human resource management implications of CSR, business and the environment and businesses' relationship with government. Third, the course examines how business accounts for its behaviour and impact in society through sustainability reporting and the role of external assurance and outlines debates on the appropriate role of government policy in either regulating and/or enabling responsible corporate behaviour.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate and apply ethical and accountability issues and frameworks in business
- Describe the key drivers behind its adoption by business
- Analyse and understand a corporation's impact in the social, workplace, market, environment and political spheres;
- Apply Corporate Responsibility theories and frameworks to critically evaluate corporate responsibility performance;
- Describe and critically evaluate the concepts of Corporate Responsibility and Accountability, and future trends in the role of the corporation in society

It focuses on the governance of change across interconnected systems, where decisions in one domain can create significant implications for markets, organisations, communities, and the environment. A central question is: how can systemic transitions be governed effectively and equitably, while balancing competing interests, risks, and opportunities? This course adopts a systems-level perspective that investigates decisions of justice, fairness, and inclusion in navigating structural changes such as decarbonisation, digitalisation, artificial intelligence, and automation. Students will critically examine how businesses, investors, governments, and civil society can work together to reimagine economic futures that are both environmentally sustainable and socially inclusive . Through interdisciplinary theories and real-world case studies, students will explore Just Transitions across five key conceptual domains: System Thinking – A system value domain sits at the core of this course, recognising that just transition occur within interconnected systems shaped by power, institutions, stakeholder relationships and competing ideas of justice. Civic Wealth Creation – Examining how community-driven entrepreneurship, participation, and coalition-building generate economic, social, and communal value. Place-Based Development and Investment – Investigating financial models that acquire, absorb, and deploy capital equitably across different communities and geographies. Indigenous Knowledge and Systems – Exploring the Indigenous business sector and the role of cultural values in fostering sustainable, community-led development. Ethical Infrastructure and Intergenerational Equity – Engaging with long-term governance frameworks that consider who benefits from change, who bears the costs, and how future generations are impacted. Designed for future leaders, strategists, and change agents, this course equips students with the critical tools to design, assess, and lead transitions that align business innovation with societal and environmental resilience.
Learning Outcomes:

Subject: Executive Strategy: Growth and Innovation (MBAE7502)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The capstone year of the MBA (Executive) Program focuses on themes of growth, innovation, disruption and transformation. As part of the capstone year, this course will help you develop strategic thinking and analysis skills that you will apply in formulating effective growth strategies for your organisation. You will do this by applying tools and frameworks from the field of strategic management and using aspects of human-centred design, including empathy, ideation and prototyping to identify, evaluate and plan the implementation of value-creating growth options. Primary paths for organisational growth include expansion into new products/services, and expansion into new markets (i.e. geographic locations and customer segments). For each of these two expansion paths there are several alternative vehicles for growth, including organic growth, mergers and acquisitions, strategic partnerships or alliances. Many organisations employ all of these vehicles in their growth strategies. In the course, we will discuss each of these paths and vehicles for growing organisations and identify and evaluate growth options in a variety of sectors and contexts. We will also apply a resource and capability perspective as the platform for identifying growth and innovation options ranging from close adjacencies, further adjacencies, and (currently) unrelated businesses. However, identifying attractive growth options does not automatically translate into success. Managers also need to be able to successfully implement their growth strategy. An integrated growth strategy therefore includes a plan for obtaining the capabilities needed to succeed in each growth and innovation option, and the timing and phasing of each step of expanding the organisation. An integrated growth strategy also requires identifying and analysing the risks that may prevent the capture of expected economic benefits. We will discuss how to limit risk by identifying growth and innovation options that leverage the existing strengths of the organisation, evaluating whether an organisation is the natural or best owner of a given business, and avoiding paying too much for an acquisition.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the role of strategic innovation in growth strategies.
- Be able to communicate (verbally and in writing) a coherent and compelling growth strategy to a senior management audience.
- Distinguish between different growth paths via expanding organisational boundaries: vertical, horizontal and geographic (including international expansion).
- Combine critical, creative and practical thinking to generate, evaluate and plan the implementation of organisational growth options into an integrated growth strategy
- Identify the risks of different growth options and propose ways to mitigate and manage these risks.
- Understand how to identify, analyse and address key ethical, cultural and sustainability (profit, people, planet) risks and opportunities as part of organisational growth strategies.
- Understand the motivations for business growth and how to assess potential value creation from growth initiatives.
- Recommend appropriate strategies for addressing resource and capability gaps to enable growth.
- Collaborate with your peers in a team to develop an integrated growth strategy for an organisation, and reflect on your and your team members’ contributions to the team’s processes and achievement.
- Use aspects of human centred design as part of a growth strategy formulation and implementation process.
- Analyse, select and justify the choice of organic growth, strategic partnerships, or mergers and acquisitions.

Credit Points: 6
Overview: The capstone year of the MBA (Executive) Program focuses on themes of growth, innovation, disruption and transformation. This course focuses on disruption and transformation of industries and organisations. You will learn to accept and embrace uncertainty. Industry disruption is challenging many incumbent businesses while simultaneously creating opportunities. In disruptive environments, many organisations find they need to transform their business model and their organisation. Transformation means dealing with the challenges of change and uncertainty, sensing the situation, seizing the opportunities, and transforming the organisation. This requires critical thinking to deal with the frequent paradoxical choices in today’s business environment where transformation is increasingly an ongoing activity rather than a once-in-a-career event. You will build important skills and mindsets to understand disruption, lead organisational business model shifts, and direct organisational transformation projects with confidence. This course is designed to build upon and extend your learning in MBAE7502 Growth and Innovation , as the strategies and tools of growth and innovation also apply in developing new business models. Business model shifts and organisational transformations are complex, and this course explores many distinct types of business models as well as approaches to increase the success probability of organisational transformations. In a world besotted with disruption, exponential technology change, globalisation, and the digitalisation of the economy, the concepts addressed in this course are of paramount importance to any leader looking to run a successful organisation into the future.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and assess ethical, cultural and sustainability (profit, people, planet) risks in organisational transformation activity.
- Analyse and devise a well-justified transformation strategy by selecting an ‘appropriate’ strategy suited to the organisation’s evolving context and applying organisational change processes and supporting frameworks.
- Collaborate with your peers in a team to effectively develop new organisational business models and organisational transformation plans, and reflect on your and your team members’ contributions to the team’s processes and achievement.

- Combine critical, creative and scenario thinking to assess the situation and generate, evaluate and plan the implementation of new organisational business models and organisational transformations.
- Identify, evaluate, and mitigate the risks associated with a transformational change by applying assumptions testing and experimenting to business model changes and organisational transformations.
