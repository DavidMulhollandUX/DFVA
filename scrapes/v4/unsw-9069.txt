## Description
This degree provides graduates with accreditation as an Exercise Physiologist (AEP) with the national governing body, Exercise and Sports Science Australia (ESSA).  AEPs are allied health professionals who provide safe and effective exercise interventions for preventative health care, as well as for people with acute, sub-acute or chronic medical conditions, injuries, or disabilities. Pathology domains covered by the services of AEPs include cardiovascular, metabolic, neurological, musculoskeletal, cancers, kidney, respiratory/pulmonary and mental health, and any other conditions for which there is evidence that exercise can improve the client’s clinical status.

Accredited exercise physiologists may find employment in a variety of settings including public and private clinics and hospitals, sports medicine clinics, corporate health, work cover and in the general exercise and fitness industry.

This program is only available as part of the integrated Bachelor of Applied Exercise Science/ Master of Clinical Exercise Physiology double-degree (3897) and is not offered as a separate standalone program for direct entry.

## Program Learning Outcomes (CILOs)
On successful completion of this program, students will be able to:

2. Recommend, conduct and interpret assessments and deliver safe and effective exercise interventions appropriate to a variety of client disease and injury states that demonstrate best practice and sound clinical judgement
4. Apply the national, state, and compensable scheme frameworks as they relate to AEPs to the health care, aged care, and disability sectors
3. Demonstrate competency to recognise, monitor, interpret, report and take appropriate action regarding adverse signs and symptoms that may arise during exercise and or recovery
1. Explain the pathophysiological basis of conditions, treatments and interventions relevant to practice

Subject: Clinical Practice for Chronic Conditions (EXPT3174)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this course you will learn how to individualise exercise assessment and prescription for clients with a chronic disease. You will learn about the pathophysiology, medication interactions, and specific considerations for testing and training populations, particularly those with cardiometabolic conditions such as diabetes, cancer, and other chronic health conditions in which an emerging and niche evidence base arises.
Learning Outcomes:
- Formulate appropriate assessments and interventions for individuals with chronic disease, taking into consideration client goals, health status , function and self-management strategies
- Evaluate the effect of commonly prescribed medications, and diagnostic and medical procedures on both resting and exercise-related physiological responses in individuals with chronic disease(s)
- Formulate appropriate screening processes to evaluate and stratify risk for participation in assessments and interventions
- Communicate the expected outcomes of evidence-based clinical decision making using an effective, ethical, person-centred approach.
- Explain the relevance of client risk factors, and the importance of adverse signs and symptoms relevant for exercise assessments and interventions, in individuals with chronic disease

Subject: Exercise Oncology (EXPT4100)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this course, you will gain an in-depth understanding of the pathophysiology and burden of a variety of cancers and be introduced to common treatments. You will learn about the effect that treatments, medications and their interactions have on capacity for exercise and physical activity. You will learn to safely prescribe, monitor, and evaluate exercise programs for people undergoing treatment for cancer and cancer survivors’.
Learning Outcomes:
- Formulate appropriate screening processes to evaluate and stratify risk for participation in assessments and interventions. Take appropriate action regarding changing risk factors and adverse signs and symptoms that may arise before, during and after assessments and interventions.
- Design safe and effective exercise-based interventions for individuals with cancer and cancer survivors, based on an integrated knowledge of cancer pathophysiology, and other determinants of health and function
- Evaluate the effect of diagnostic procedures, commonly prescribed medications and treatments (including medical, surgical, radiotherapy and other interventions) on resting and exercise-related physiological responses in individuals with, and recovering from, cancer
- Formulate strategies to empower clients to undertake safe and effective self-management of exercise during and following discharge from exercise interventions

Subject: Cardiorespiratory Rehabilitation (EXPT4136)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course is specifically designed for students enrolled in the Applied Exercise Science/Clinical Exercise Physiology program and integrates knowledge of cardiorespiratory and cardiovascular anatomy, physiology and pathophysiology, and other determinants of health to inform safe and effective exercise testing, assessment and delivery of exercise-based interventions for people with cardiac, vascular, respiratory and metabolic conditions. You will develop competency in evaluating physiological responses and adaptations to acute and chronic exercise. You will also develop skills in appropriate planning, delivery and monitoring of safe and effective exercise-based interventions for these cohorts.
Learning Outcomes:
- Choose and use relevant technology and equipment efficiently, effectively, and safely
- Apply behavioural change principles to support adherence to exercise interventions and to prevent or minimise impairments, limitations, adverse events, and activity restrictions
- Describe and evaluate physiological responses to acute and chronic exercise in people with cardiac, vascular, respiratory, and metabolic conditions
- Conduct appropriate screening procedures to stratify risks for participation in clinical exercise testing and exercise-based interventions
- Describe the effects of commonly prescribed medications on acute and chronic exercise responses that must be considered when prescribing and delivering exercise interventions for people with cardiac, vascular, respiratory and metabolic conditions
- Apply clinical reasoning and evidence-based practice to design and monitor the delivery of exercise interventions and select appropriate patient-centred and safe outcome measures for people with cardiac, vascular, respiratory and metabolic conditions
- Communicate effectively and respectfully with patients and support person-centred care through shared decision-making, and maintain accurate, timely, and appropriate documentation and records of services
- Distinguish when client needs are outside of scope or competence and take appropriate, timely actions to refer onwards to other health professionals

Learning Outcomes:
- Conduct safe, culturally sensitive, respectful, and person-centred care underpinned by a biopsychosocial framework
- Apply principles of rehabilitation and biomechanics to inform exercise prescription for a range of musculoskeletal conditions in a multidisciplinary setting including risk stratification to inform relevant referal pathways
- Synthesise and critically appraise relevant scientific peer-reviewed literature to apply to clinical practice
- Discuss pain as a biopsychosocial experience to inform management of acute, subacute, chronic and complex musculoskeletal conditions
- Develop and implement interventions that address contributors to pain and disability in musculoskeletal and broader health conditions based on evidence and clinical reasoning
- Explain the role of exercise, education, and emerging approaches to pain management across the lifespan in various populations, for musculoskeletal, neurological, and other medical conditions

Subject: Rehabilitation for Neurological and Neurodegenerative Diseases  (EXPT4153)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course is specifically designed for students enrolled in the Applied Exercise Science/ Clinical Exercise Physiology program and examines the pathophysiology, clinical presentation, impairments, activity limitations and participation restrictions arising from disease and trauma to the nervous system. The course consists of two modules: acute neurological disorders (including stroke, acquired brain injury and spinal cord injury) and neurodegenerative diseases (including Parkinson’s Disease, multiple sclerosis and motor neuron disease). You will develop competency in appropriate assessment of motor impairments and secondary adaptation and develop skills in appropriate planning and delivery of safe and effective exercise and activity-based interventions. The course includes a series of simulated case scenarios, online learning modules and practical hands-on workshops that focus on applying theoretical knowledge to sound clinical reasoning and practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Implement effective strategies to reduce or minimise secondary impairments and prevent decline in health status
- Prescribe safe and effective exercise programs to promote reablement and maximise independence
- Analyse the role of exercise for the management of neurological and neurodegenerative disorders
- Evaluate, select and implement appropriate outcome measures relevant to treatment and client goals
- Apply principles of evidence-based practice with sound clinical reasoning and knowledge of emerging rehabilitation approaches in neurological rehabilitation
- Demonstrate understanding of informed consent and communicate effectively and respectfully with clients with cognitive and sensory impairments and other health care professionals and stakeholders

Subject: Health Research Project (HLTH5003)
Credit Points: 9
Overview: In this course, you will contribute to an in-depth study of a relevant health science issue. You will apply the research skills you have acquired throughout your program to: identify a specific issue that requires investigation, formulate a research question, ethically and systematically collect relevant data, analyse, interpret and discuss your findings, draw conclusions, articulate the implications of your results, and disseminate the findings to the target audience. The research project may be a negotiated student-led proposal or be a proposal offered by an academic supervisor. Projects may be in a cross-disciplinary partnership with another School or Faculty at UNSW, or with an external stakeholder. You will be expected to carry out the research project independently, with the support of academic supervisor(s). With your academic supervisor(s), you will develop a ‘contract’ which outlines the roles and expectations for each, including an agreed meeting schedule, communication methods, milestones and timeline. This activity is a hurdle task and your contract with your supervisor must be submitted at the beginning of the first of the two research terms. You will not receive feedback on this task To fulfil the research project requirements of your degree, you must enrol in this course over two terms (2 x 9UOC) to complete an 18UOC research project. The assessment tasks for your research project are spread over two terms (Year 4 Term 3 and Year 5 Term 1). You will receive an ‘EC’ (enrolment continuing) on your academic transcript at the end of your first term of HLTH5003. Once all assessment tasks have been completed and graded at the end of your second term, a numeric grade will be applied your academic transcript for your second term of HLTH5003. Further details on the UNSW grade definitions can be found here: https://www.student.unsw.edu.au/grade
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate effective oral and written communication skills that enables information to be disseminated clearly and concisely and be of a standard acceptable to a scientific journal and conference.
- Demonstrate professional skills in planning, time management, teamwork, ethical practice and reflective practice.
- Manage the collection, analysis and interpretation of data throughout the research process together with a supervisor(s) and within ethical standards.
- Demonstrate an understanding of the research process, including formulating a research question and proposing appropriate methods to investigate the question.
