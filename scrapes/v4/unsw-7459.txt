## Description
This Graduate Certificate is designed for business professionals who are seeking to develop the knowledge, skills and attributes to become more sustainable leaders and to make more sustainable decisions in their work. It will help them to understand the sustainability topics that are dramatically reshaping the way we do business, including the environmental, social and economic challenges we face, the opportunities we have to solve them, and how we can position our organisations to succeed.

It will help students to become more valuable leaders for their organisation and society. They will complete this program with a more sustainable mindset and a sustainability toolkit that they can apply immediately in their organisation and in their life.

Subject: Decarbonisation and Transition to Clean Energy (AGSM9162)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course will help you understand the core concepts behind measuring and abating carbon emissions in order to achieve the science-based targets and Net Zero emissions by 2050. Clean energy will need to replace fossil fuels before 2050. McKinsey (2022) estimates this will cost an additional $3.5 trillion a year - the largest reallocation of capital in history - and will affect every country and every sector of the economy. The enormity and complexity of this challenge is creating strong and rapidly growing demand for leaders in every industry who understand the principles of decarbonisation and how an organisation can develop a realistic pathway to achieve Net Zero emissions. Regardless of the industry in which you are working, or your role in that industry, this course will help you to become a more valuable leader for your organisation and for society. This course incorporates a Net Positive approach to decarbonisation, identifying the unprecedented opportunities for organisations and professionals to create value in a just transition to a decarbonised economy. The scale of these opportunities is evident in New South Wales, where the formal announcement of a Renewable Energy Zone in the Hunter Valley and Central Coast Region in 2022 attracted more than $100 billion worth of proposed projects.
Learning Outcomes:
- Discuss fundamental principles of Climate Science and Carbon Literacy
- Identify the global environmental, social and economic challenges of a just transition to clean energy
- Review industry decarbonisation pathways and understand the impact of different decarbonisation levers
- Critically evaluate case studies of global organisations that are leading the way in decarbonisation and a just transition to clean energy
- Learn how to harness the value creation opportunities in decarbonisation
- Identify the role of technology and innovation in decarbonisation and the transition to clean energy
- Analyse climate risk in different industries and understand how organisations can develop appropriate resilience and adaptation strategies

Subject: Sustainability Governance, Regulation and Reporting (AGSM9163)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course is presented in two parts: Sustainability Governance and Regulation, and Sustainability Reporting. Leaders and managers must understand their obligations to the long-term financial health of the organisations they lead and therefore must also understand their requirements to manage for sustainable outcomes and to report performance. In order to achieve this, leaders and managers must understand key global and national sustainability policies, standards and frameworks, and ensure that the management of material sustainability issues is well integrated into their governance structures, strategies and business models, and risk-management practices. This is critical to ensuring that managers can set, operationalise and implement their organisations’ sustainability commitments, demonstrate and publicly report sustainability performance, and avoid greenwashing and the risk of failing to deliver on their commitments.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop awareness of the organisational changes to underlying systems, processes and behaviours required to implement 'investor grade' integrated reporting and embed integrated thinking across the organisation.
- Critically evaluate case studies of global organisations that have struggled to meet their obligations to regulators and financial markets and of organisations demonstrating leading practice in these areas.
- Develop awareness of integrated reporting and requirements for the preparation of integrated reports.
- Critically evaluate examples of integrated reports, case situations and the related academic research.
- Develop awareness of existing and potential standards, frameworks, and policy instruments related to sustainability in Australia and around the world, and understand how these may impact your organisation, and changes that may be required in your organisation to address current and potential regulatory obligations.
- Describe the drivers and the direction of change in sustainability policy, governance, regulation and business reporting in key critical areas such as Climate Change, Natural Capital, Modern Slavery, and Indigenous communities.

Subject: Sustainable Value Chains and Business Models (AGSM9164)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: One of the most important ways for organisations to make progress towards their sustainability goals is to analyse their value chain and business model and how they positively impact people, profit and planet. Value-chain analysis includes mapping and managing carbon emissions, and it must also include an analysis based on other United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (for example, Reduced Inequalities and Decent Work and Economic Growth). This course will bring together many topics of business sustainability – such as responsible sourcing, green supply chains, designing for circularity, and sustainable consumption – to encourage a systemic and integrated approach of looking at a business value chain. Through this, you will learn how a business can create value in a sustainable way – whether through business model innovation, technology and design, or ecosystem partnerships. There are many options for building sustainable business models, including circular business models, shared value models and cooperative ownership models. This course will provide a framework for thinking about sustainable business models and provide examples and structures for some common approaches.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand approaches for developing sustainable business models and be able to apply these to real-world challenges
- Review the supply chain of an organisation and be able to redesign the value chain to improve resilience and sustainability outcomes
- Define the concept of a sustainable business model and explain the role of sustainable business models in creating a sustainable future
- Design a sustainable business model for an organisation and be able to evaluate its feasibility and impact
- Develop an understanding of a range of possible sustainable business models (including circular models, shared value models, nature positive models and sharing models) and be able to understand the potential application of each type of model.
- Examine case studies of sustainable value chains and be able to evaluate their effectiveness in addressing key sustainability challenges
- Identify key strategies for addressing sustainability in the value chain, including modern slavery, emission reduction, abatement, sustainable sourcing and business model changes
