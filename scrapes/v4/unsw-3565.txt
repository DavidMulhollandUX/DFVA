## Description
This program is available only to students in 3554 through an Internal Program Transfer. For more details, please contact the UNSW Business School Co-op Office.

The Bachelor of Commerce (Co-op) (Honours) is a highly valued and unique business qualification. It provides high-achieving students with a strong, 4-year academic program, integrated with:

(i) Fully assessable industry placement experiences that enable students to work with some of Australia's leading employers.
(ii) Research training via advanced disciplinary and methodology courses and a thesis.

The BCom (Co-op) (Honours) combines the requirements of the degree with 18 months of coordinated industry placement at three different sponsoring organisations. The industry placement extends outside university terms.

A scholarship is payable from funds donated by the sponsoring organisations, and entry to the program is limited to students awarded a scholarship through the BCom (Co-op) (Honours) selection procedures administered by UNSW Co-op Program Office. For baseline requirements to the UNSW Co-op program visit the future students page.

Program benefits:

- Highly regarded by industry
- Provides students with the fundamentals of business and the option to explore their interests
- Prepares students academically, technically and professionally
- Equips students with the skills and knowledge to pursue a variety of career options
- Allows students to qualify for professional accreditation in a range of areas

Subject: Retirement Saving and Spending Over the Lifecycle (ACTL3192)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Helping individuals make better financial decisions and improve their financial well-being over the lifecycle represents a grand challenge for government, business, and not-for-profit organisations. This synthesis course provides opportunities for students to work in diverse teams to solve real-world problems related to this grand challenge through applying, integrating and synthesising their knowledge and skills from multi-disciplinary perspectives. This course will be a fully problem-based learning experience. Throughout the course, students will apply their multi-disciplinary knowledge, critical thinking, problem-solving and teamwork skills to solve real industry problems. Students will have opportunities to engage with and receive feedback from industry partners. Students intending to enrol in ECON3117 should instead enrol in this course.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically reflect on your professional skills and behaviours, including identified gaps, and how you will adapt to a changing business environment.
- Access, manage and use the appropriate digital resources and tools to deliver your recommendations or conclusions.
- Examine the influences of the global business environment on key ethical or sustainability considerations in relation to the contemporary business problem.
- Identify, apply and adapt business skills and knowledge acquired from across your program to critically analyse a contemporary business challenge.

- Draw on evidence to frame and apply logical reasoning, and distinguish relevant information from irrelevant information when providing original solutions to real life problem-solving for organisations.
- Explain basic statistical methods and know when to apply appropriate methods in practical scenarios
- Recognise various ethical frameworks that can apply to a situation and apply these frameworks to resolve a simple ethical dilemma.

Subject: Value Creation (COMM1180)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: COMM1180 explores how organisations create value through the decisions they make. From choosing which customers to target, to evaluating investments, setting prices, and measuring performance, each decision shapes the outcomes delivered to stakeholders. In this course, students engage with real business problems using a problem-based learning (PBL) approach that integrates finance, accounting, and marketing. Each week, students analyse decisions from different perspectives, considering opportunities, trade-offs, risk, and their impact on outcomes. As the term progresses, students develop core skills in evaluating investments, understanding customers, designing value propositions, setting prices, and assessing performance using both financial and non‑financial measures. By the end of the course, students will be able to analyse business situations, evaluate alternatives, and make informed decisions that influence how value is created and sustained.
Learning Outcomes:
- Collaborate effectively in small groups to address business problems, incorporating diverse perspectives and providing constructive feedback.
- Apply methods to value financial assets and evaluate the value created or destroyed by financial decisions.
- Apply critical thinking and ethical decision making, to define and measure value from different perspectives to know when value is (and is not) created.
- Analyse how organisations create and capture stakeholder value through pricing and production volume decisions, plus performance measurement techniques (including potential dysfunctional effects).
- Compare basic strategies to create value for and from customers, and a recognition that such value can be financial and non-financial.

Subject: Introduction to the Australian Legal System (GENL2021)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Different legal systems have evolved in different parts of the world. Australia is a common law country – its legal system is based on the English common law system. This course offers students a comprehensive introduction to the Australian legal system, a common law system, and contrasts it with the civil law system. The course will examine the historical origins of the Australian legal system, some of the principles underpinning it's legal system, including the importance of the concept of the rule of law. It also covers the operation of the principal institutions of the legal system, the legislative and executive arms of government and the judiciary and courts. This will include the jurisdiction of Australian courts, the process by which cases are decided and the doctrine of precedent, consideration of alternative methods of dispute resolution and an examination of the role of the legal profession. There will also be an overview of the main branches of law. First Nations laws will be explored and there will be a critical analysis of the circumstances of the adoption of the common law in Australia and the effect on First Nations people. There will also be discussion of the recognition of customary law and native title. No prior knowledge of the Australian legal system is required. A major focus of the course is the social relevance of law in our daily lives and in today’s society.
Learning Outcomes:
- Deliver accurate and reflective oral contributions that demonstrate effective communication skills and knowledge of course concepts.
- Produce clear and persuasive written work that articulates legal concepts and analysis effectively.
- Evaluate legal and key practical problems in the area of the Australian legal system.
- Analyse the Australian legal system in practice from a range of perspectives.
