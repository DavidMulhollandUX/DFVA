## Master of Interpreting and Translation Studies
##### A6007
##### 96 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
The Master of Interpreting and Translation Studies is aimed at students with advanced bilingual proficiency and seeks to develop your skills in translation and interpreting from/to English and another language, and your awareness of practical and theoretical approaches to translation/interpreting practice and studies. The course offers two specialisations, focusing on:

  * Translation (Chinese, French, German, Indonesian, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Russian, Spanish)

  * Interpreting and translation (Chinese, French, German, Indonesian, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Russian, Spanish).

Note: Languages on offer in any one year may vary depending on student enrolments.

You will gain a foundation in theories of translation and interpreting and receive training in research in the field. Upon completion of this course, you will be able to conceptualise translation/interpreting studies as an academic discipline in its historical, cross-disciplinary and intercultural context.

Note: Online delivery is available to domestic students in the translation-only specialisation

### Structure
The course is structured in five parts: Part A. Foundation studies, Part B. Core studies, Part C. Applied studies, Part D. Professional practice studies, and Part E. Elective studies.

Part A. Foundation studies

The focus of these studies is the theory and practice of translation and interpreting, familiarising you with current research models and methodologies in translation studies.

Part B. Core studies

These studies draw on best practices within interpreting and translation fields, providing intensive language-specific practice in written and oral transfer skills at a level required for certification by the National Accreditation Authority for Translators and Interpreters (NAATI). In addition to the relevant theoretical frameworks, the core units will also provide you with the discourse analysis, ethical and professional frameworks that are essential for professional interpreting and translation practice.

Part C. Applied studies

The focus of these studies is professional or scholarly work that can contribute to a portfolio of professional development or a research pathway. Students in the interpreting and translation specialisation will also develop advanced skills in specialised fields of interpreting.

Part D. Professional practice studies

Designed in association with industry experts, select from this suite of professional enhancement offerings to build essential competencies for your career pathway.

Part E. Elective studies

Select from a range of options designed to enhance specific knowledge and skills in interpreting and translation and related fields, including a suite of professional enhancement offerings to build essential competencies for your career pathway. 

Master's entry points

Depending on prior qualifications you may receive entry level credit (a form of block credit) which determines your point of entry to the course. 

Translation (Clayton)

  * If you are admitted at entry level 1 you complete 96 credit points, comprising Part. A, Part. B, Part. C, Part. D and Part E.
  * If you are admitted at entry level 2 you complete 84 credit points, comprising Part. A, Part. B, Part. C, Part D (6 credit points) and Part E (24 credit points).

Interpreting and translation (Clayton)

  * If you are admitted at entry level 1 you complete 96 credit points, comprising Part. A, Part. B, Part. C, Part. D and Part E.
  * If you are admitted at entry level 2 you complete 84 credit points, comprising Part. A, Part. B and Part. C and Part D (6 credit points).

Interpreting and translation (SEU)

  * If you are at Southeast University, China you are admitted at entry level 1 and complete 96 credit points, comprising of Part A (12 credit points) and Part B (36 credit points). You will receive block credit for master's-level study at Southeast University (48 credit points) towards the Master of Interpreting and Translation Studies.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
critically analyse, evaluate, and engage with key texts and cultural products pertaining to the theoretical field of interpreting and translation studies.
**2.**
apply and communicate knowledge of global best practice in interpretation and translation in diverse and collaborative professional contexts
**3.**
translate and interpret spoken and written language in English and another language at an advanced level, applying ethical practice commensurate with professional certification

### Progression to further studies
Successful completion of this course may provide a pathway to a graduate research degree.

### AHEGS
The Master of Interpreting and Translation Studies is a postgraduate course. It is taught in English and normally takes two years of full-time study or four years of part-time study. Graduates will be able to demonstrate advanced skills in translation and interpreting from/to English and another language and awareness of practical and theoretical approaches to translating/interpreting practice suitable for accreditation. In addition to advanced analytical and communication skills they will demonstrate a thorough understanding of research methodology and ethics. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Graduates will have achieved intensive language-specific practice in written and oral transfer skills to standards set by the National Accreditation Authority for Translators and Interpreters (NAATI).
