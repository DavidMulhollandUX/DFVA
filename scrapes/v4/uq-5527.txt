## Career Opportunities & Employment Outcomes
Graduate Certificate in Engineering Science graduates have acquired experience that enables them to cross disciplinary boundaries, and are equipped to take on advanced engineering work. They will have a combination of business, problem solving and interpersonal skills; as well as technical engineering experience that will enable them to understand markets and develop relationships of trust with customers, suppliers, business partners, and to work within teams.

Subject: BIOE6100 - Fundamentals of Neuroengineering
### BIOE6100 - Fundamentals of Neuroengineering
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate how neurons generate and propagate electrical signals and the basic properties of signal transmission in neural circuits
- Critique and select relevant frameworks for analysing neural data
- Implement signal processing algorithms for practical problems involving neuroscience
- Demonstrate the need of engineering frameworks for large scale neural modelling
- Critically analyse information independently acquired from diverse sources (e.g., research articles, books, patents, company product data sheets) in providing solutions to real-world neuroscience problems.
- Effectively communicate (using oral and written communication and appropriate figures and visualizations) the real-world implementation of analytical frameworks in neuroscience
- Orally present and report a research project on neural signal processing
Assessment Tasks:
- Problem Sets In-person: 40%%
- Project proposal oral assessment Identity Verified In-person: 15%%
- Project Presentation Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 35%%
- Project Final Report: 10%%
Workload: Problem Based Learning 4.00 Hours/ Week 2 x 2 hour problem-based learning sessions

Subject: BIOE6403 - Biomedical Instrumentation
### BIOE6403 - Biomedical Instrumentation
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the origins of biopotentials and physiological parameters for the purposes of diagnostic and therapeutic device design.
- Design and construct conditioning circuits for medical devices, particularly concerning human cardiopulmonary measurements.
- Apply appropriate means of mathematical analysis to biosignals for research and diagnostic purposes.
- Identify sources of noise and signals artifacts in instrumentation systems and execute corrective techniques in both hardware and software.
- Appraise micro and macro shock risks in electromedical equipment and be able to design equipment compliant with relevant international electromedical safety standards.
- Identify appropriate sensor and actuation solutions for a given measurement task, design an electronic front-end and signal processing chain.
Assessment Tasks:
- Initial Quiz: 5%%
- Instrumentation amplifier & filter Identity Verified: 15%%
- Digitisation and feature detection Identity Verified: 25%%
- Final Examination Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 55%%
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Practical 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: ELEC3004 - Signals, Systems and Control
### ELEC3004 - Signals, Systems and Control
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate the role of the Fourier, Laplace and z-transforms, poles and zeros in the analysis of continuous-time and discrete-time signals and systems.
- Evaluate Laplace and z-transforms and their inverses and be able to use them to solve differential and difference equations
- Explain the Nyquist sampling theorem and its implications in signal and image processing systems
- Analyse the concepts of amplitude distortion, phase distortion and group delay in filters
- Design continuous-time and discrete-time filters
- Explain the computational benefits of the Laplace and Fourier transforms in system characterisation.
- Outline the architectural features necessary for digital signal processors and obtain hands-on experience with DSP programming.
- Apply and explain the basics of linear control, stability and controller design
- Analyse system stability via root loci and bode plots
- Design analogue and digital controllers and compensators
Assessment Tasks:
- Problem Set 1: 20%%
- Problem Set 2: 20%%
- Problem Set 3: 20%%
- Final Exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 40%%
Workload: Lecture 4 Hours/ Week Practical 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: ELEC3310 - Electrical Energy Conversion and Utilisation
### ELEC3310 - Electrical Energy Conversion and Utilisation
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain electric and magnetic circuits related to electrical machines.
- Apply various tests for parameter calculations of models of electrical machines.
- Develop equivalent circuits of electric machines for given conditions and analyse them.
- Distinguish different modes of bulk electrical energy transmission and their inherent limitations.
- Comprehend the distribution and utilization side of electrical energy and appropriate load management strategies.
- Develop an overall understanding of how electrical energy conversion, transmission and utilisation work in power systems.
Assessment Tasks:
- Practical assessment In-person: 30%%
- In-semester class test Identity Verified In-person: 30%%
- Final examination Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 40%%
Workload: Lecture 3 Hours/ Week Applied Class 2 Hours/ Week Practical 3 Hours/ Week
