### Overview
The Master of Design is a dynamic and forward-thinking program tailored for the rapidly evolving field of design. This course equips you with the skills and knowledge required to excel and lead in various design disciplines. You will delve into advanced principles of contemporary design, refine your design practice, and graduate with an industry-relevant qualification that positions you at the forefront of the profession.

At Monash University, we cultivate designers who are independent, inquisitive, and socially responsible. Our graduates emerge as vital contributors and potential leaders in sustainable design, addressing issues ranging from climate action to social and cultural equity. The Master of Design offers three specialisations:

  * Interaction Design

  * Multimedia Design

  * Design Research

These specialisations prepare graduates to play crucial roles in various domains of design, fostering innovative solutions and leadership. You can personalise your learning experience by selecting a specific design specialisation or opting for the Design Research specialisation. This specialisation allows you to concentrate on a design research project within any field of design, such as industrial, spatial, collaborative, communication, and motion design.

The program is structured around four core design studio units, spread across four semesters. These studios include both defined and self-directed design projects that span design systems, services, processes, environments, and artefacts. Through these projects, you will demonstrate high-level planning, resource management, implementation, and evaluation, leading to a thorough understanding of professional design processes.

In addition to the core studios and research units, you will complete three elective units that enhance your skills and knowledge in relevant disciplines. These electives are designed to complement and extend your core studies, providing a well-rounded education in design.

The Master of Design offers an enriching educational journey that prepares you to become a leading design professional. With a strong focus on advanced design principles, research, and industry relevance, this course empowers you to make a meaningful impact in the world of design.

### Structure
The course is structured in four parts: Part A Foundation studies (24 credit points), Part B. Studio practices (42 credit points), Part C. Critical contexts (12 credit points) and Part D. Discipline electives (18 credit points).

Part A. Foundation studies
These studies provide you with the conceptual thinking and technical skill set required for advanced postgraduate study in this area.

Part B. Studio practices and Part C. Critical Contexts
In your studio practice units you will focus on the application of advanced design problem solving skills at a professional level. You will consolidate skills and practice of design research methodologies and may extend your research trajectory to further study.

Your critical context studies will support your research and practice and formalise your research skills.

You will focus on the application of conceptual thinking and technical skills to advanced design problem solving. You will analyse and create a project outcome based on research, critique, and the application of design processes appropriate to your specialisation.

In the final semester you will pursue a major design project or participate in a leading industry project. You will bring together your advanced technical ability, conceptual thinking, entrepreneurial studies and design management in practice.

Part D. Discipline electives
These elective studies provide you with the opportunity to deepen and broaden your knowledge and practice through the selection of electives and allow you to build capabilities in your chosen area.

Master's entry points

Depending on prior qualifications you may receive entry level credit (a form of block credit) which determines your point of entry to the course:

  * If you are admitted at entry level 1 you complete 96 credit points, comprising Part A, B, C and D.
  * If you are admitted at entry level 2 you complete 72 credit points, comprising Part B, C and D
  * If you are admitted at entry level 3 you complete 48 credit points, comprising Part B (30 credit points, comprising Studio 3 and Studio 4), Part C (6 credit points, comprising TAD5500) and Part D (12 credit points).

Note: If you are eligible for credit for prior studies you may elect not to receive the credit and complete one of the higher credit-point options. 

A zero-credit-point unit in art, design and architecture occupational health and safety (OHS1000) will also be undertaken. This unit is required of all students in the Master of Design and must be completed even if credit is obtained for Parts A or B.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Work collaboratively to assess the needs, deliverables, and constraints of complex design projects, while demonstrating an understanding of the social, cultural, and ethical impact of design on diverse stakeholders;
**2.**
Integrate advanced research methodologies to analyse, synthesise, and evaluate diverse and innovative solutions for contemporary and future design challenges;
**3.**
Apply advanced design principles and skills to produce and implement innovative ideas, leveraging emerging technologies and concepts to solve complex design challenges;
**4.**
Engage critically and reflectively with advanced design theories, concepts, and methods, demonstrating a deep understanding of their application in contemporary practice;
**5.**
Identify, evaluate, and apply appropriate occupational health and safety measures appropriate to the area of study.
**6.**
Critically analyse and contextualise design practices within cultural (including Indigenous and First Nations knowledges), environmental, ethical, technological, and professional contexts, recognising their societal and environmental impacts, and fostering a commitment to sustainable and ethical design solutions;
**7.**
Demonstrate the capacity to navigate change by committing to continuous professional development and critically integrating emerging trends, tools, and technologies shaping the field of design;

### Progression to further studies
Successful completion of the Master of Design (F6002) may provide a pathway to further postgraduate study.

### AHEGS
The Master of Design is a postgraduate degree. The course is taught in English and normally takes two full-time years, or equivalent, to complete. Graduates have demonstrated skills and competencies in a range of design processes including design research methodologies, design fabrication, entrepreneurship and design management. The Master of Design is a Level 9 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification.  The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Design thinking approaches (DGN4500)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit will introduce you to the key skills and practices associated with Design Thinking, and offer opportunities to explore how basic creativity techniques can be applied in different contexts. It has been developed to support non-cognate students in Masters courses by introducing Design Thinking and its use across a wide range of problems and situations. These skills and strategies include various conceptual and communication approaches, ways to investigate and reconsider the contexts in which we live and work, and opportunities for innovation. Design Thinking combines familiar methods from the sciences and humanities - such as analysis and synthesis - but applies them in ways that are specific to a design approach. It encourages more flexible thinking and fosters creative engagement with the complex challenges of contemporary life.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the core principles, methods, and mindsets of Design Thinking, and explain their relevance across disciplines and contexts;
- Collaborate with peers to generate, test and refine design proposals that are responsive to cultural, social and ethical needs of diverse users and stakeholders.;
- Apply Design Thinking strategies such as problem framing, ideation, prototyping and iteration to explore creative responses to complex, ill-defined problems;
- Analyse real-world project challenges using appropriate investigative approaches, including the integration of conceptual, social and technological considerations;
- Demonstrate strategies for independently evaluating and adapting Design Thinking practices for future professional learning and interdisciplinary application.
- Reflect on the role of Design Thinking in promoting sustainable, ethical and socially responsible innovation in both personal and professional contexts;
