## Description
Orientation and Mobility (O&M) is the practice of enabling people with low vision or who are blind to know where they are, where they want to go and how to travel there in a safe manner. O&M specialists work with people of all ages, tailoring interventions to suit the needs of the individual client, including people with additional complexities, such as health conditions. The degree will provide graduates with specialised knowledge, critical and analytical skills to support life-long learning. It will give practical experience to enable them to apply for industry certification and work as an O&M specialist, either as a self-employed practitioner or at an organisation that provides orientation and mobility services to clients with low vision or blindness.

## Program Learning Outcomes (CILOs)
On successful completion of this program, students will be able to:

4. Develop, implement and critically appraise Orientation and Mobility interventions using appropriate concepts, theories and evidence.
1. Understand and critically analyse theories of Orientation and Mobility practice using primary and secondary resources and evidence
2. Design, prepare and teach Orientation and Mobility skills and techniques
5. Develop and critically evaluate new ideas, challenge current thinking and practices and create solutions to evolve the profession
6. Deliver client focused services with an ethical and culturally aware lens, and understand the wider personal ecosystem that impacts delivering Orientation and Mobility services
3. Develop communication and collaboration skills to work with clients and other disability and health professionals

Subject: The Visual System, Impairments and Implications (VISN5511)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course provides you with a broad understanding of the visual system, its disorders, and how vision loss affects visually guided behaviour and daily activities. Its scope includes a review of the visual pathways, visual function and its measurement, as well as diseases and disorders of the visual system that lead to low vision and blindness. The course also includes an introduction to educational strategies and training to improve orientation and mobility in people with vision loss. This course will be delivered online.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and appraise methods used to assess vision and to quantify visual loss
- Evaluate how diseases/disorders that affect the eye and brain cause functional vision loss
- Identify anatomical structures of the visual system and articulate how they are organised and function
- Recognise and articulate the psychosocial implications of low vision and blindness

Subject: Sensory Processes and Movement (VISN5512)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course provides you with an understanding of the brain, the sensory and motor systems and their integration in behavioural interactions with the environment. This course will cover topics such as the anatomy and organisation of the brain, the sensory systems such as vision, hearing and touch, motor processes such as body awareness, proprioception, and kinesthetics. This course will be delivered online and in-person, comprising of a series of recorded lectures, tutorials and practicals. Key concepts will be reinforced through reading and online multimedia exercises.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and explain anatomical and functional aspects of vision, hearing and motor control and its application to Orientation and Mobility
- Evaluate the implications of common health conditions on the integration of sensory information
- Recognise and articulate the processes of spatial cognition and their functional implications to Orientation and Mobility
- Explain and judge the efficacy of clinical and practical methods that assess sensory and motor deficits
- Identify and demonstrate the initial blind mobility skill set and visual strategies and analyse and demonstrate the mechanics of teaching them.

Subject: Disability and Inclusion in Vision Rehabilitation (VISN5513)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course provides you with foundational knowledge for the practice of orientation and mobility and other forms of vision re/habilitation in Australian and international contexts. This course will be delivered online and includes a review of key disability philosophies, practice guidelines and inclusive practices for diverse clients of vision rehabilitation/habilitation services.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and interpret legislations and guidelines that impact vision re/habilitation and Orientation and Mobility practice in Australia
- Explain the history of the Orientation and Mobility profession and current disability philosophies and paradigms which impact vision re/habilitation services
- Examine and critically evaluate the scope and service models of Orientation and Mobility practice and other vision re/habilitation services.
- Justify and apply appropriate visual and non-visual methods of accessing information for people who are blind or vision impaired
- Appraise and value the diversity of people who are blind or have low vision by identifying inclusive practices.

Subject: Orientation and Mobility Techniques (VISN5521)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course provides you with opportunities to learn, demonstrate and apply techniques, strategies, and teaching while critically analysing them as tools for intervention design in orientation and mobility. The face-to-face practical component will involve supervised partner work, allowing embodying simulation of travel for people who are blind or have low vision. This also allows you to refine your instructional skills.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and demonstrate the blind mobility skill set and visual strategies by analysing the mechanics of teaching them
- Critically analyse travel environments and apply appropriate techniques and strategies for those environments
- Critically appraise practical sessions and devise ways to improve their outcomes
- Apply visual and non-visual orientation strategies in practice

Subject: Vision Rehabilitation  (VISN5522)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course provides you with a review of contemporary learning theories, vision enablers, and the opportunity to critically analyse emerging technology in the field of vision rehabilitation/habilitation. Further, this course will detail optical and non-optical aids, especially those that improve orientation and mobility in people with vision loss. Practicals will support learning with hands-on use of low vision aids prescribed to individuals with vision loss.

- Identify vision re/habilitation enablers (optical/non-optical aids) and evaluate their application for intervention
- Explain, analyse and apply instructional Orientation and Mobility strategies to optimise client outcomes
- Critique emerging technologies for people who are blind or vision impaired
- Articulate and evaluate contemporary learning theories and teaching methods to design orientation and mobility interventions

Subject: Development and Ageing: Implications for Orientation and Mobility  (VISN5531)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course provides you with a broad understanding of human development and ageing with respect to vision impairment and blindness. This course will be delivered online, and its scope includes an in-depth review of child development, the effects of low vision and blindness on child development and the increased prevalence of vision impairment and co-morbidities with ageing.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and review general health conditions that need to be considered in the design of interventions in children and adults
- Assess the impact of vision impairment and blindness on child development
- Identify and evaluate how significant points of transition in the lifespan affect Orientation and Mobility interventions
- Evaluate the impact of other sensory impairments and co-morbidities on the development of children who are visually impaired or blind
- Explain and appraise the impact of the increased prevalence of vision impairment and co-morbidities with ageing on Orientation and Mobility intervention
