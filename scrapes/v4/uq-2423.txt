### Program Structure (Courses & Requirements)

  * [ BIOM6070 Biomedical Science Research Preparative Skills ](https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=BIOM6070)
  * [ BIOM6080 Biomedical Science Research Techniques ](https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=BIOM6080)
  * [ BIOM6100 Biomedical Science Research Project ](https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=BIOM6100)

Subject: Biomedical Science Research Preparative Skills (BIOM6070)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course enables students to elevate their existing scientific communication skillsets, directly supporting the presentation of original research both in written and oral presentations. Students will be trained in critical, creative and practical thinking, communicating with expert and lay audiences, project management and data analysis.
Learning Outcomes:
- To understand and apply various software programs for analysis of biomedical literature and data
- To create a scientific question and design experiments for testing a hypothesis
- To critically evaluate scientific data and previously published work
- To communicate research findings through written, oral, poster formats to diverse audiences
- To work effectively in a research team, identify the characteristics of effective teamwork and critically evaluate personal and peer contributions to team processes
- To evaluate ethical issues in biomedical research and apply culturally inclusive, responsible, and ethical conduct in their research practice
Assessment Tasks:
- Workshop diary In-person: 25% (5 x 5%)% (Hurdle: False)
- Experimental design and research practices test Identity Verified In-person: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Poster Planning and Production - PDF Identity Verified In-person: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Poster Oral Presentation Assessment Identity Verified In-person: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Seminar Reflection Report: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Workshop 12 Hours/ Week 12 hours per week in Weeks 1-5

Subject: Biomedical Science Research Techniques (BIOM6080)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Biomedical Science Research Techniques is a preparative course for Biomedical Science Honours students and forms 2 units of the 16-unit program. An in-depth understanding of biomedical research requires knowledge of the experimental techniques used to generate scientific data. Students will learn foundational skills in biostatistics, hypothesis testing, experimental design, and research integrity. A focus on selected research techniques will round out student¿s preparation and include in depth background knowledge of the chosen techniques, hands-on demonstrations of critical technologies and analysis of captured datasets. Students will be assessed on their understanding of a range of research techniques and biostatistics.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate and analyse the theory behind different biomedical techniques.
- Develop skills in qualitative and quantitative data analysis and presentation.
- Evaluate and incorporate biostatistics into experimental design and data analysis.
- Recognise and apply good laboratory practices consistent with high standards of research integrity.
- Work and learn collaboratively, leveraging the diverse abilities, backgrounds, and perspectives within the group.
Assessment Tasks:
- Quiz 1 Identity Verified In-person Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Quiz 2 Identity Verified In-person Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Quiz 3 Identity Verified In-person Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Quiz 4 Identity Verified In-person Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Week 5 Module Assignment: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 3 Hours/ Week Practical 6 Hours/ Week Learning activities are delivered intensively in weeks 1-5

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=BIOM6100 =====
Subject: Biomedical Science Research Project (BIOM6100)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: Honours students within the School of Biomedical Sciences will undertake their own original research project under the guidance of an academic supervisor based at the School or affiliated Institutes. Students will learn and be assessed on their scientific communication skills in both oral and written presentations of their research project.
Learning Outcomes:
- Read and critically analyse current scientific literature related to the area of the research project.
- Identify research questions, investigate appropriate methodology and plan experiments to address these questions.
- Perform practical and/or theoretical experimental work, under the guidance of an experienced supervisor.
- Record the planning and outcome of experiments accurately and at a level that is both appropriate and expected in modern day scientific research.
- Write a substantial report based on your scientific investigation in a format suitable for a biomedical science audience.
- Critically discuss scientific data in the context of existing literature, draw conclusions from such discussions and suggest further experiments.
- Present the outcomes of a scientific project and its significance to both expert and non-expert audiences, accurately and appropriately.
- Manage time in project planning.
Assessment Tasks:
- Research Proposal Hurdle: 10%% (Hurdle: True)
- Research Progress Meeting: Formative, but contributes to Supervisor's Report% (Hurdle: False)
- Research Report Hurdle: 55% Draft is formative but contributes to Supervisor's Report% (Hurdle: True)
- Research Outcomes Seminar Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 25%% (Hurdle: True)
- Supervisor's Report Identity Verified In-person: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: 40 hours per week in active weeks
