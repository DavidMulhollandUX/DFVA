## Description
Immersive technologies, simulation, and visualisation are converging to develop and drive many of the transformations in today’s workforce, as industries transition from historical production models towards more agile and adaptable work practices. They are being used to envision and portray unique cultural and artistic experiences; visually explore complex datasets; design creative experiences; design, develop and manufacture products; plan cities and model future scenarios; predict financial outcomes and make decisions; rehearse medical and defence procedures; undertake ground-breaking research; explore the evolving landscape of human-robotic interaction; and to problem solve, teach, learn and train.

This integrated, cross-domain Graduate Certificate in Simulation and Immersive Technologies will qualify you to take up a range of high value positions as future immersive technology professionals and designers capable of understanding how these practices are shaping industries. You will become part of a new generation reshaping the future of workforce creativity and productivity.

You will experience a unique, integrated and industry-focussed approach to simulation and immersive technologies that will augment your technical knowledge with analytical and philosophical understandings of these evolving practices.

Our human-centric, design-led approach will be underpinned by a creative and critical mindset focussing on human perception and experience, enabling you to analyse how, why, when, and where to utilise simulation, visualisation and immersive platforms such as virtual, augmented and mixed reality (VR, AR and XR) across different sectors.

The program is structured to cater to the demands of professionals in the workforce from diverse industry sectors, including but not limited to, media and creative arts, health, defence, business, education, and manufacturing. Students with an advanced level of knowledge and experience in a specific domain will benefit from a broadening human-centric conceptual foundation. The program content will enable you to personalise your learning and bring your industry experience and interests into your study.

The program can be studied full-time or part-time and can be completed fully online. There is also an option to undertake the program in blended mode which requires students to attend two intensive face-to-face meetups per year on campus or at our industry partner facilities. Students will also have full access to all campus facilities throughout each term. For elective courses, students will be able to select from a range of fully online courses or on-campus face-to-face courses with weekly classes.

## Program Learning Outcomes (CILOs)
On successful completion of this program, students will be able to:

1. Evaluate human performance, user and interaction requirements to inform the design of visualisation, simulation and immersive applications.
2. Critically analyse evolving technological processes and design strategies for creative and professional visualisation, simulation and immersive solutions.
3. Evaluate and develop an advanced understanding of emergent and converging immersive simulation and visualisation systems and practices.

## Progression & Academic Rules
Students will require a WAM of 65+ with no fails to articulate from the Graduate Certificate to Masters.

## Admission Requirements
**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
A bachelor's degree in any field.

Applicants who do not have a bachelor's degree may apply for admission into the Graduate Certificate based on a minimum 5 years industry experience. To be considered, applicants must provide a digital portfolio (PDF, website, etc.) that includes:

- A CV detailing a minimum of 5 years of work experience in their industry specialisation

- Evidence of professional work/achievement via a portfolio or similar

- A 2 page statement which explains: their specific strengths and achievements in their field; how they see simulation and immersive practices impacting their discipline; and their motivations for joining the program, and how they believe the program will enhance their career

The digital portfolio will be assessed on a case-by-case basis by Art & Design staff. Students in this category who complete the Graduate Certificate with a WAM of 65 may apply to articulate to the Master of Simulation and Immersive Technologies.

Subject: Designing and Experiencing Immersion (DDES9901)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Virtual experiences and immersive environments are an integrated aspect of contemporary work, study, and entertainment. In response to this evolving landscape, this course offers a rigorous inquiry into the principles and practices that shape meaningful virtual and immersive experiences. You will explore how, when, and why immersive technologies can be incorporated into design solutions, considering neuroscientific perspectives on perception, historical uses across the creative arts and sciences, and current industry examples. Emphasis is placed on immersion as a form of embodied cognition that connects mind and body. A central feature of the course is the chance to engage first-hand with a curated selection of immersive works through VR headsets. These direct encounters give you the opportunity to analyse, reflect on, and contextualise the principles explored in class. Building on this experience, you will then develop a proposal for an original immersive concept that demonstrates your understanding of immersion’s possibilities and applications. Note: It is a requirement for students who are unable to physically attend class on campus to have their own high-end VR headset (Quest 3 or equivalent) and VR capable PC to meet assessment requirements.
Learning Outcomes:
- Conceptualise and propose an original immersive experience that demonstrates understanding of foundational principles of immersion and embodied cognition
- Assess and justify why, how, when, and where different immersive design techniques and technologies should be applied
- Critically test, analyse, and evaluate immersive experiences through hands-on engagement, with a focus on how humans perceive and experience immersion

Subject: Human Dimensions in Immersive Environments (DDES9902)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: As digital and automated systems increasingly shape our daily interactions, it is essential to understand not only their potential but also their impact on human experience. This course focuses on how and why humans engage with immersive environments, examining the cognitive, behavioural, and ergonomic factors that influence performance and well-being. Through first-hand experiences with VR headsets and your own independent research, you will investigate how humans respond to stimuli within synthetic, virtual, mixed, and live immersive contexts. Particular attention will be given to ergonomics, comfort, safety, and usability. You will also explore complex aspects of cognition and perception, the evolving dynamics of human–machine interaction, and broader considerations such as accessibility and the impact of emerging technologies, including next-generation AI. By the end of this course, you will have both a theoretical and practical understanding of how to design effective interactions in immersive contexts, distinct from traditional screen-based paradigms. You will learn to apply immersive design principles to improve existing practices and to conceptualise and implement novel forms of interaction that leverage the unique affordances of immersive technologies. Note: It is a requirement for students who are unable to physically attend class on campus to have their own high-end VR headset (Quest 3 or equivalent) and VR capable PC to meet assessment requirements.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically assess the ethical, accessibility, and human-centred implications of immersive and interactive design
- Plan, prototype, test, and iterate an immersive design project that demonstrates effective application of ergonomic, safety, and cognitive principles
- Apply immersive design principles to analyse and select interaction strategies appropriate to specific real-world or industry contexts
- Evaluate the physiological, cognitive, and behavioural impacts of human interaction in immersive environments using established industry ergonomic and usability frameworks

Subject: Narrative and Sensemaking in Immersive Environments (DDES9903)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Story and narrative have always been central to how humans communicate and make meaning. With the rise of immersive and virtual systems, new modes of narrative design are emerging that extend beyond linear storytelling to create dynamic, embodied experiences. This course focuses on narrative design in immersive environments, building on the foundations of perceptual psychology (DDES9901) and human interaction and ergonomics (DDES9902). You will examine a spectrum of narrative modalities - from visual and cinematic to aural and performative - drawing on industry examples in simulation, gaming, 360 cinema, VR, and installation art. Through the lens of multimodal sensemaking and embodied cognition, you will explore how agency, interactivity, situational awareness, and navigation transform narrative into lived experience. Key concepts such as spatial navigation and embodied action will be demonstrated and critically analysed to understand how composition and multimodality shape meaning. Your learning will culminate in the design and prototyping of an immersive narrative experience, demonstrating your ability to integrate narrative principles with the psychological and human-centred foundations established in earlier courses. Note: It is a requirement for students who are unable to physically attend class on campus to have their own high-end VR headset (Quest 3 or equivalent) and VR capable PC to meet assessment requirements.
Learning Outcomes:
- Integrate multimodal approaches - including situation awareness, sensemaking, and embodied cognition - into the design of immersive narrative experiences
- Critically analyse the role and transformation of narrative modalities across traditional and immersive forms
- Design, implement, and refine immersive narrative experiences using contemporary industry standards, demonstrating the integration of narrative design principles with technical execution
- Apply principles of agency, interactivity, and navigation to conceptualise and prototype immersive narrative experiences

Subject: Models, Systems and Solution Design (DDES9904)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Models form the basis for how we represent the world and design solutions. While they are used differently across domains to analyse, test hypotheses, inform decisions and make predictions, model and systems thinking offers an important perspective to help us understand and shape our environment. Answering the question ‘what is a model?’, this course will give you a holistic perspective of models and systems examining how, why and when it is appropriate to model. You will consider how models can be used to help problem-solve and design solutions in environmental, humanitarian, and creative and creative contexts. Different modelling and systems paradigms will also be examined with a specific focus on their application in visualisation, simulation and immersive design.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate modelling paradigms and their fitness for purpose in visualisation, simulation and immersive design.
- Integrate social, technical, and human perspectives into modelling and simulation design using a systems approach.
- Conceptualise the use of modelling by examining techniques used in different industries.

Subject: Managing Immersive Projects (DDES9914)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: As immersive practices and technologies become embedded in our workplace environments, business leaders and managers are required to make critical decisions regarding their design, acquisition and operation. - Assess the relative benefits and values of immersive technologies and simulation environments for different levels of scalability

Subject: Information Architecture for Immersive Aesthetics (DDES9920)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course examines the principles of information architecture design as they apply to the context content and users of immersive visualisations and simulations. You will learn about systems for data classification and metadata labelling, search methodologies and user navigation, and how these may be translated into interactive systems for embodied environments. The course will help you understand how to move beyond simple technical schemas for data organisation, to consider how the physical and social spaces in which these data are encountered affect the formation of knowledge in users. In doing so, your will combine human-centred design considerations with artificial intelligence-assisted data management practices, and explore new methods for interpreting and visualising information for applications in immersive media.
Learning Outcomes:
- Design embodied user interface schema to allow for effective knowledge transfer in immersive environments.
- Design classification schema for complex and varied datasets for a range of simulative contexts.
- Analyse the information architectures of immersive and embodied 3D experiences.
