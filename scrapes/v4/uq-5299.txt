These performance-based programs provide students with extended application of pedagogical principles, skills and resources in a workshop situation. Students have the opportunity to select courses in instrumental pedagogy, aural studies and methodology, choral or orchestral conducting and classroom pedagogy. Graduates of the program demonstrate knowledge and application of the principles of music pedagogy - instrumental or aural - in educational and community settings.

Please note that, if CSPs are limited, places will be allocated on the basis of academic merit.

Subject: Practical Studies 9 (MUSC7250)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides high-level postgraduate instrumentalists the opportunity to focus on performance skills and to bridge the divide between undergraduate and professional assessment and engagement.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate a postgraduate/professional level of artistry and technical fluency as a solo performer
- Identify your progress at a postgraduate/professional level towards being a professional musician.
- Demonstrate a postgraduate/professional level of artistry as an ensemble performer and your awareness of professional expectations surrounding ensemble performance in varied contexts at a postgraduate/professional level
Assessment Tasks:
- Teacher's Report: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Cohort Collaboration - Director Report Hurdle Team or group-based In-person: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- Instrumental, Vocal or Composition Workshop Hurdle In-person: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- INSTRUMENTAL/VOICE STUDENTS ONLY - Performance Recital: 60%% (Hurdle: False)
- COMPOSITION STUDENTS ONLY - Composition Portfolio: 60%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Practical 1 Hour/ Week Workshop 1 Hour/ Week Contact 1 Hour/ Ad Hoc

Subject: Practical Studies 10 (MUSC7260)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides high-level postgraduate instrumentalists the opportunity to focus on performance skills and to bridge the divide between undergraduate and professional assessment and engagement. For instrumentalists and vocalists, this includes the refinement of technical skills, historical performance practices, and interpretation of canonical works across various stylistic periods. Vocal studies further incorporate language and diction training appropriate to the classical repertoire. For composers, skills in notation, compositional approaches, responding to stimulus and orchestration are developed, along with understanding of a range of contemporary styles and genres, focusing on developing skills appropriate for your own practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate a postgraduate/professional level of artistry and technical fluency as a solo performer
- Identify your progress at a postgraduate/professional level towards being a professional musician.
- Demonstrate a postgraduate/professional level of artistry as an ensemble performer and your awareness of professional expectations surrounding ensemble performance in varied contexts at a postgraduate/professional level
Assessment Tasks:
- Teacher's Report: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Cohort Collaboration Ensemble Hurdle: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- Instrumental, Vocal or Composition Workshop Hurdle: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- Recital or Portfolio: 60%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Workshop 1 Hour/ Week Seminar 1 Hour/ Week Contact 1 Hour/ Ad Hoc

Subject: Instrumental and Vocal Studio Pedagogy (MUSC7630)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: If you are an instrumental music teacher and you¿ve ever asked ¿how can I get my students to practice?¿ or ¿why does this teaching idea work well with one student but not the other?¿ or even ¿why are my students afraid of performing?¿, then this course is for you. This course explores the musical, pedagogical and social contexts of instrumental learning in practical and collaborative settings informed by scholarship in music, education and psychology.
Learning Outcomes:
- collaborate in the design of lesson and practice plans for students
- apply contemporary teaching and learning frameworks to instrumental teaching of your instrument in a practical setting
- demonstrate reflexivity in your instrumental teaching practice
Assessment Tasks:
- Lesson Sequence Planning Rationale submission and Viva Voce Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Teaching Videos, Oral Presentation (video) and Peer Feedback Identity Verified: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Viva Voce 30% In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Ensemble Studies 9 (MUSC7650)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Advanced study of Western classical music ensemble preparation and performance for postgraduate/professional instrumentalists.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate your understanding of effective collaborative approaches to the preparation of ensemble repertoire for performance at a postgraduate/professional level
- Identify and overcome challenges in the preparation of individual parts in a manner that supports being an effective ensemble performer at a postgraduate/professional level
- Demonstrate your awareness of professional expectations surrounding ensemble performance in varied contexts at a postgraduate/professional level
Assessment Tasks:
- Hurdle Task - Chamber Music ONLY - Chamber Music video excerpt Hurdle: hurdle requirement% (Hurdle: True)
- Performance Report/Director's Mid-Semester Report Team or group-based In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Performance Report/Director's Report Team or group-based In-person: 60%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Contact 6 Hours/ Week
