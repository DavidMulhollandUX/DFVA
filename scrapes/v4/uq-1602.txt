The Diploma in Languages is a way of value-adding to any bachelors degree. This program will enable you to benefit from the advantages that knowledge of another language can bring in a global economy, as well as earning you an additional academic qualification.
Students enrol in the Diploma in Languages concurrently with enrolment in another University of Queensland program, the "parent" program. Students will complete a program which is equivalent to two minors or one major in language(s) other than English. Students may be able to "overload" (enrol in five courses rather than four per semester) to complete both the "parent" program and the Diploma in approximately the time required to complete the "parent" program alone.

Subject: CHIN1100 - Spoken Chinese IA
### CHIN1100 - Spoken Chinese IA
Learning Outcomes:
- communicate in Mandarin in a range of simple everyday situations
- comprehend a simple dialogue or narrative in Mandarin
- read and write Mandarin sentences in the pinyin phonetic system correctly and consistently
- acquire vocabulary and grammar structures appropriate to the beginners level of Mandarin
- pronounce and distinguish Mandarin sounds and tones
- understand various aspects of Chinese culture as reflected in the language
- possess collaborative skills via team work and peer interaction in class
Assessment Tasks:
- Course Placement Hurdle In-person Online: %
- Aural Exam 1 Identity Verified In-person: 20%%
- Aural Exam 2 Identity Verified In-person: 30%%
- Final oral interview Identity Verified Team or group-based In-person: 50%%
Workload: Workshop 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: CHIN1200 - Written Chinese IA
### CHIN1200 - Written Chinese IA
Learning Outcomes:
- recognize, understand and write from memory individual characters from the prescribed list (about 250 characters this semester).
- reproduce characters with a functional standard in the following aspects: accuracy, neatness, balance, uniformity of size and spacing, cohesion of components, firmness and fluidity of strokes, correct direction and angle of strokes.
- identify "radicals" of traditional characters, their meanings and certain modifications.
- recognize individual characters as words and combinations as words, of phrase grouping and idiom, and of sentence structures - correct grammatical sequence.
- read and translate characters in prose and conversation passages with understanding of the whole statement including colloquial accuracy.
- understand the historical development of Chinese characters from the oracle bone scripts to modern fonts.
- write short passages of prose with appropriate choice of vocabulary and variety of expression.
- understand various aspects of Chinese culture as reflected in the language
Assessment Tasks:
- Course Placement Test Hurdle In-person Online: %
- In-semester exam Identity Verified In-person: 40%%
- Character practice booklet and exercise sheet (1) Online: 10%%
- Character practice booklet and exercise sheet (2) Online: 10%%
- Final exam Identity Verified In-person: 40%%
Workload: Workshop 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: CHIN2100 - Spoken Chinese IB
### CHIN2100 - Spoken Chinese IB
Learning Outcomes:
- improve general speaking skills in Chinese to HSK 2 level
- follow and comprehend familiar language in a spoken text
- gain better knowledge and understanding of Chinese Language up to the HSK 2 level and the attendant culture
- compose a short dialogue or narrative in colloquial language
Assessment Tasks:
- Diagnostic Quiz Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: %
- Aural Exam Identity Verified In-person: 30%%
- Drama Project Identity Verified Team or group-based In-person: 30%%
- Oral Interview In-person: 40%%
Workload: Workshop 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: CHIN3010 - Spoken Chinese IIB
### CHIN3010 - Spoken Chinese IIB
Learning Outcomes:
- At a high intermediate language level and in a culturally sensitive way discuss topics of interest through the use of social routines, exchange of information, ideas, opinions, experiences and plans.
- At a high intermediate language level and in a culturally sensitive way participate in social interaction related to solving a problem, making arrangements and decisions, and transacting for services.
- At a high intermediate language level and in a culturally sensitive way obtain information by searching for specific details in a spoken text, or by listening to or reading the text as a whole.
- Understand the sociocultural aspects of the language they have learnt.
- Learn to build up the attribute of persistence in doing something challenging like learning the target language.
- Demonstrate respect for cultural and social diversity and for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples and perspectives.
- Demonstrate knowledge and respect for ethics and ethical standards in completing assessment tasks.
- Lead self and others in group activities.
- Be confident dealing with ambiguity and uncertainty.
- Present persuasive oral and written arguments.
- Be empathetic of other people, cultures, and knowledge systems.
Assessment Tasks:
- Weekly Oral Task In-person Online: 25%%
- In-Semester exam Identity Verified In-person: 30%%
- Oral Presentation Identity Verified In-person Online: 10%%
- Final Oral Interview Identity Verified In-person: 35%%
Workload: Tutorial 3 Hours/ Week
