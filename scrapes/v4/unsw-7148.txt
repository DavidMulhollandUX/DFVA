## Description
Planning is an exciting, varied and progressive profession which plays a pivotal role in decisions shaping the future of cities and regions. Planners need to see the whole picture: we need to be good thinkers, good analysts and good communicators. We work with stakeholders across the spectrum of interests - from global developers to local communities - to foster feasible, equitable and sustainable outcomes. Sydney and NSW provide a fantastic laboratory to help understand the crucial role played by planners in addressing complex challenges. The Graduate Certificate in City Planning [GCCP] degree is designed for students with an undergraduate qualification in a related discipline seeking to move into a planning or planning-related field. It is also tailored for professionals working in other sectors, especially those whose work may intersect with the planning system or requires involvement with planning agencies. Subject to progress, students can articulate from the GCCP into the Graduate Diploma or Master of City Planning.

The Grad Cert in City Planning aims to produce high quality graduates who have acquired UNSW Graduate attributes and are capable, through their skills, knowledge and experience, of undertaking a wide range of tasks required of a qualified planning practitioner. As well as preparing for the profession, a planning degree provides lifelong analytical, collaborate and communication skills much in demand more widely.

## Career Opportunities & Employment Areas
The Graduate Certificate in City Planning [GCCP] degree provides an excellent platform to take up planning and urban policy-related opportunities across all tiers of government (federal, state, local), private sector consultancies, property development, and indeed the breadth of built environment professions where planning skills are required. The interdisciplinary skill sets developed through the diploma also enable graduates move into wider policy, research, analytical, creative and community roles both in Australia and overseas

## Admission Requirements
**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
A Bachelor degree with a credit average (equivalent to a UNSW WAM of 65).

Note: Graduates of UNSW City Planning (Honours), or equivalent programs accredited by the Planning Institute of Australia are ineligible for admission as they have already met the learning outcomes of this degree.

Alternative Entry Pathways

Applicants with other relevant qualifications not listed may apply for admission to this program as follows. This is in accordance with UNSW Policy and Procedure on Recognition Of Prior Learning.

- Applicants will need to demonstrate that they have achieved the same knowledge and skills (learning outcomes) through their formal or informal learning as the qualifications listed for entry for this program.

- This may be evidenced by qualification certificates, records of assessment activities relating to course or program learning outcomes, examples of experience from the workplace, community, or other context which demonstrate relevant skills, knowledge or competencies, membership (or eligibility for membership) of professional organisations which require attainment of certain qualifications, or testimonials of skills, knowledge or competencies from workplace supervisors.

- Any relevant documentation should be attached to the application and will be considered by the Academic Coordinator for the Program.

Applicants applying for either the Graduate Diploma or Master degrees who do not meet the standard admission requirements above may be able to enter the Graduate Certificate. This will be at the discretion of the Program Director or Academic Coordinator who will assess applications on a case-by-case basis.

Subject: Land & Environment Law (PLAN7140)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Land & Environment Law provides an overview of the legal system and environmental planning law, with particular reference to the Australian experience. There are three main areas of focus: planning law, planning administration and land/real property. Planning law considers historical, theoretical and administrative issues in law and jurisprudence, focusing on the evolution of NSW planning legislation since 1979. Planning administration explores the administrative context within which planning operates, particularly the role and responsibilities of key statutory bodies that shape the planning process. The property dimension covers fundamental issues of property rights and the interaction between common law and statutory principles and processes.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the role of land and environmental law in shaping the use, development and management of land.
- Critically evaluate the role and principles of environmental planning law as a defining framework for planning activity.
- Analyse the relationship between common law and statutory laws in shaping planning outcomes and achieving the protection of the environment.
- Demonstrate a working knowledge of the legislative aspects of the NSW planning system.
- Discuss the administrative context within which planning operates as a function of government.

Subject: Land Use Policy & Practice (PLAN7141)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Land Use Policy & Practice examines how broad planning objectives are realised through systems of land-use management and development control, with particular reference to the NSW planning system. You will develop the practical and creative skills required of a professional planner to undertake tasks involved in regulatory plan-making and development assessment.

- Interpret and evaluate development controls with precinct, local government or statewide application.
- Compare and evaluate statutory planning systems and legislation used to manage land use and development.
- Assess proposed developments against their alignment with government statutes, plans and policies.

Subject: City Equity & Wellbeing (PLAN7142)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this course, you will explore contemporary issues facing planners in an increasingly diverse and complex society. You will critically engage with the social, economic, and cultural shifts which are continually reshaping the neoliberal city and consider questions of spatial disadvantage, social exclusion, equity, spatial justice and and ‘rights to the city’. Change in the built environment often impacts disadvantaged groups disproportionately; for example, redevelopment and gentrification may be accompanied by quickly changing community profiles, rising housing costs and displacement. As planners, we must steward the future direction of our cities in ways that foster diversity and recognise the needs of all groups, including culturally diverse communities, children, the aged, women, people with disabilities, LGBTIQ+ people, indigenous peoples and homeless people. In this course, you will gain an understanding of how to proactively incorporate elements that foster well-being into decisions related to the built environment. You will explore the capacity of current planning frameworks to achieve more equitable outcomes, including through the preparation of community strategic plans, the use of social impact assessment and more creative inter-disciplinary and bottom-up approaches.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and synthesise priority issues relevant to preparation of social and health impact assessments.
- Reflect on your attitudes, beliefs, assumptions and value judgements in relation to how they influence and impact upon your planning approach.
- Analyse how equity and concerns regarding wellbeing are addressed in planning activity.
- Assess diversity and difference within communities in relation to the demands upon and outcomes of planning processes.
- Explain the role of planning in social wellbeing and health.

Subject: Urban Design (PLAN7143)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course will introduce you to urban design concepts and methodologies in the context of current planning practice. Starting with an appreciation of the fundamentals of design, we will explore topics such as site investigations, the definition of urban structure, enhancing the public realm, understanding building typologies, and controlling built form. You will develop an awareness of the scope of urban design projects, an appreciation for the physical structure of places, an understanding of shaping built form, familiarity with different building typologies, and insights into current urban design debates. You will also acquire essential skills such as site analysis methodology, establishing robust planning and design principles, mapping and spatial understanding, design review criteria and mechanisms of development control.
Learning Outcomes:
- Work collaboratively in addressing real-world planning and urban design problems
- Create diagrams that clearly and accurately convey urban design information
- Develop applied skills in the use of computer-based urban design packages
- Critically evaluate the urban design of our cities and how urban design influences our experience of a place
- Develop and communicate effective and creative urban design visions

Subject: City Building - Infrastructure Planning (PLAN7145)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: City Building - Infrastructure Planning explores the role of infrastructure considerations within strategic and statutory planning contexts. The pipeline of major transport infrastructure projects in Australian cities offer an excellent lens through which to consider the increasingly integrative role planners play in key city shaping activities which have social, economic and environmental impacts as well as mobility outcomes. In this course, you will explore the concept of city building as a framework for maximising the synergies between infrastructure expenditure and urban growth and change. In doing so you will learn about the key role that planners can play in linking these large financial (and political) commitments to considerations of city sustainability, productivity, efficiency and equity.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply knowledge of the policy and statutory frameworks of urban infrastructure provision to address planning issues.
- Analyse urban transport infrastructure using the range of approaches and data to evaluate the economic, equity, and sustainability consequences.
- Assess the role of government and the private sector in infrastructure provision and funding.
- Explain the conceptual frameworks and techniques for incorporating infrastructure considerations into urban planning decisions at both strategic and tactical scales.
- Explain the relationship between transport and sustainable development, and how transport infrastructure can contribute to sustainble development goals.

Subject: City Economics, Urban Development & Finance (PLAN7146)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: City Economics, Urban Development and Finance introduces the application of economic theory to the planning imperatives which drive and shape urban development. You will consider broad spatial-economic factors which shape our understanding of how cities ‘work’ including land use pressures, supply and demand, location theory, opportunity costs, marginalism and the effects of technological transformation. You will also examine the processes involved in land and property development in market economies and the role of government supervision through regulatory and approval processes. Through a range of case studies and drawing upon industry expertise, you will be introduced to economic feasibility models to consider development from the perspective of all stakeholders involved, building greater awareness of the risks, costs and key considerations.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the role of the public and private sector in the development process and their influence on urban property markets.
- Discuss the effect of infrastructure provision on the use, development, management and value of land.
- Identify the economic basis of urban social and environmental issues and the analytical tools to address them.
- Discuss economic concepts and models that help explain land values and the location of economic activity and that shape decision-making regarding the development of cities and regions.
- Apply elementary concepts in urban growth theory and the potential for government intervention to promote growth.

Subject: Strategic Spatial Planning (PLAN7148)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Strategic Spatial Planning explores the principal mechanisms whereby planners understand, create, and care for, places and address the core challenges and opportunities faced by urban environments and their communities – both existing and future. You will consider the key drivers of change within our cities, unpacking issues including housing supply and affordability, urban renewal, urban justice, infrastructure and climate change. You will also engage with the tensions that shape how decisions get made – or not – through the complex lens of urban governance and how planning – and planners – engaged with those we plan for. As planners we are interested in the inherently spatial dimensions of these urban processes, recognising that collective efforts to influence land use change and behaviours of urban stakeholders take place at different geographical scales, and impact on different groups in different ways. This course focuses on the metropolitan and regional scale, where the conditions requiring strategic planning are arguably most evident.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and evaluate the multiple drivers shaping the dynamics and parameters for long-term strategic planning at the sub-regional and metropolitan scale.
- Explain the different forms of urban governance arrangements and the centrality of those arrangements in facilitating effective, efficient and equitable strategic outcomes.
- Critically engage with academic contributions to spatial planning theory which have underpinned recent developments in strategic planning debates.
- Interpret strategic planning frameworks and metropolitan planning documents, distilling their key elements, strengths and weaknesses, and applicability to the Australian context.

Subject: Sustainable Development and the Urban Environment (SUSD0001)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Sustainable Development and the Urban Environment offers a comprehensive exploration of how cities can be shaped to be environmentally responsible, socially equitable, and economically resilient. The course examines contemporary planning, design, and governance strategies that support the transition toward more sustainable and adaptive urban systems. Particular emphasis is placed on improving air and water quality, restoring biodiversity, strengthening social cohesion, and enhancing overall urban liveability. Through concepts such as resource and energy flows, climate adaptation and resilience, and emerging circular urban models, you will engage with the dynamics that underpin sustainable urban development. The course draws on real-world case studies and practical approaches used in planning and design practice. By the end of the course, you will understand the challenges and opportunities inherent in advancing urban sustainability. You will be equipped with practical, transferable knowledge applicable to urban planning, design, public policy, community engagement, and broader sustainability initiatives.
Learning Outcomes:
- Collaborate in interdisciplinary teams to address challenges of sustainable urban development and produce holistic, forward-looking solutions
- Discuss and assess how environmental, social, and economic dimensions interact within urban systems and drive their development and performance
- Critically analyse the significance of the interdependence between urban form and function for achieving sustainable urban development
- Analyse the application of sustainability principles across the built and natural environments, considering their interactions, impacts, and long-term resilience outcomes
- Critically examine how sustainability is embedded in urban development decisions, growth trajectories, and strategies in order to transform cities over time
