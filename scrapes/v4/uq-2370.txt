Speech pathologists assess and treat disorders of communication in areas such as speech, voice, language and fluency. A speech pathologist works with everyone from young children who do not understand what their parents are telling them, through to injured adults who need to learn how to speak and swallow again or people who want to modify their accents or voices.
Study the Bachelor of Speech Pathology (Honours) at UQ and you will join one of the leading schools for health and rehabilitation sciences in Australia. You will have access to a six-bed mock ward and learn within a school with a long tradition of teaching, research and graduate excellence.
Graduate with a highly respected honours qualification, with advanced knowledge and skills that will set you apart in a competitive job market and provide a pathway to future research or learning. By graduating with a Bachelor of Speech Pathology (Honours) from UQ you'll be set for a rich and rewarding career as a specialist in communication and swallowing difficulties.

Subject: Professions, People and Healthcare (HLTH1000)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: HLTH1000 (Professions, People and Healthcare) has been designed for students with an interest in working in the Australian health and behavioural sciences sectors. The multidisciplinary approach to the course delivery will provide students an understanding of the range of roles within Australia's health system and how they contribute to the health and wellbeing of individuals and communities. Students will have an enhanced capacity to navigate the health landscape in increasingly complex and changing interprofessional collaborative workplaces.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify determinants of health, including impacts of social exclusion and marginalisation in Australia.
- Describe roles in health and health-related settings in Australia, and priorities to build a resilient, sustainable, distributed and skilled health workforce, including digital health capabilities, health workforce wellbeing and retention.
- Explain the structure and organisation of the Australian health care system, including regulation, infrastructure, governance, and funding.
- Describe and demonstrate effective collaboration and interprofessional communication in a health context, including emphasising person-centred practices and including people, family, carers and communities as equal participants.
- Recognise the different dimensions of cultural safety in health care practice and services, to advance Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples health outcomes.
Assessment Tasks:
- Pre-class quizzes: IRATs (Individual Readiness Assurance Tests) Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Collaborative Assessment Team or group-based In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Exam Identity Verified In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: The Sound Patterns of Language (LING1005)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Introduction to basic concepts in linguistics focussing on the study of sounds and sound systems. In this course students will learn how speech is produced, how to systematically describe, recognise, and transcribe speech sounds, and about the organisation of sound systems and their analysis, with various languages, including Australian English, used in exemplifications. No previous knowledge of the subject is assumed.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate a well-founded understanding of speech sounds and sound systems as part of the discipline of linguistics.
- Understand the scientific classification of speech sounds and sound systems, and apply that knowledge to the analysis of individual sounds and sound patterns.
- Understand how people use sounds for social purposes, how they change over time, and how children acquire them.
- Understand the diversity of First Nations languages at the sound level.
- Write and present clearly on the sounds of languages.
- Understand how differences between languages in sounds leads to power imbalances in people.
Assessment Tasks:
- Study tasks Online: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- In-semester Examination Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Final Examination Identity Verified In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Communication and Swallowing Across the Lifespan I (SPCH1101)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides an introduction to typical development of paediatric speech, language, voice, feeding/mealtime behaviours, hearing, social, literacy and play behaviours and includes an observational visit to a Kindergarten. Principles of observational assessment and reporting as well as theories of language and speech processing, learning, and cognition relating to child development are presented.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the components of communication and swallowing in typically developing children and adolescents (SPA PS Domains 2.5, and 3.1).
- Describe the development of functional skills, including play, communication and swallowing in children and adolescents (SPA PS Domain 2.5).
- Explain the principles of observational assessments used with children and adolescents in speech pathology practice (SPA PS Domains 1.1, 2.5, 3.1, and 3.2).
- Demonstrate holistic observation, recording, and reporting skills of the communication behaviours of typically developing children and adolescents (SPA PS Domains 1.1, 1.2, 1.4, 1.5, 2.5, 2.6, 3.1, 3.2 and 3.3).
- Describe key theories of language and speech processing, fluency, learning, and cognition in children and adolescents (SPA PS Domain 2.5).
- Explore, synthesise and apply new information relating to communication and swallowing in children (SPA PS Domains 2.5, 2.6 and 3.1).
- Use effective verbal and non-verbal communication and critical reflection skills across a variety of contexts in collaboration with peers (SPA PS Domains 1.1, 1.5, 2.1, 2.2 and 2.6).
Assessment Tasks:
- Child development educational resource Online: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
