## Description
This program is for students commencing from 2026.

The double degree program, Bachelor of Design/Bachelor of Education (Secondary) is a 4 year program (192 UOC) for intending secondary school teachers. The program is a nationally accredited pre-service qualification.

The program covers both theoretical and practical aspects of education, including 80 days supervised professional experience in at least two different secondary schools.

The Computational Design specialisation enables students to gain teaching accreditation in:

- Information Processes and Technology (first or second teaching area)
- Design and Technology (first or second teaching area)

The Industrial Design specialisation enables students to gain teaching accreditation in:

- Design and Technology (first teaching area only)
- Graphics and Multimedia Technology (second teaching area only)

The Integrated Design specialisation enables students to gain teaching accreditation in:

- Visual Arts (first teaching area only)
- Graphics and Multimedia Technology (optional second teaching area)
- Textiles and Fashion (optional second teaching area)

## Program Structure & Degree Requirements
Students must complete 192 Units of Credit (UOC) structured across the following academic components:

### NESA Accreditation Requirements (0 UOC)
#### Specialisation - Integrated Design (0 UOC)
A specialisation in Integrated Design enables you to pursue a teaching area in Visual Arts, with the option of pursuing a second teaching area in either Graphics and Multimedia Technology or Textiles and Fashion Design.

To meet NESA accreditation requirements for Visual Arts, you must choose one 2D (Graphics) AND one 3D/4D disciplinary studio stream (Object (3D), 3D Visualisation, Experience, Textiles, Fashion, or Interaction (4D)), for your Integrated Design Disciplinary Studios.

If you choose to pursue only Visual Arts as your teaching area, you must complete the following method courses: Visual Arts Method 1 and 2; Extension Visual Arts Method 1 and 2.

If you choose to pursue Visual Arts and Graphics and Multimedia Technology as your teaching areas, you must complete the following method courses: Visual Arts Method 1 and 2; Graphics and Multimedia Method 1 and 2.

If you choose to pursue Visual Arts and Textiles and Fashion Design as your teaching areas, you must take Textiles as your 3D/4D disciplinary studio, and you must complete the following method courses: Visual Arts Method 1 and 2; Textiles and Design Method 1 and 2.

#### Specialisation - Industrial Design (0 UOC)
A specialisation in Industrial Design enables you to pursue teaching areas in Design and Technology, and Graphics and Multimedia Technology. You must complete the following teaching method courses: Design and Technology Method 1 and 2; and Graphics and Multimedia Method 1 and 2.

#### Specialisation - Computational Design (0 UOC)
A specialisation in Computational Design enables you to pursue teaching areas in Information Processes and Technology, and Design and Technology. You must complete the following teaching method courses: Computing Technology Method 1 and 2; and Design and Technology Method 1 and 2.

Subject: Social Perspectives in Education (EDST1104)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Do schools reduce or reinforce inequalities based on social, cultural, economic, and gender differences? What makes schools effective? Is education a public good or a private commodity? And what is the role of teachers, given that most research shows that schools only play a small role in explaining differences in how children do in school? By the end of this course, you should be able to discuss these four key questions. To do so, the course uses equity and social justice as key themes, and current research in educational sociology as evidence. You should complete the course with a critical understanding of how education, teachers and teaching interact with society.
Learning Outcomes:
- Examine, discuss and evaluate education policies such as those relating to low-SES, race, and multiculturalism.
- Describe, discuss and analyse the relationships between educational outcomes of school students and the differences between schools and within schools.
- Identify and explain the relationships between family background factors such as SES, race and gender and the educational outcomes of school students.

Subject: Indigenous Perspectives in Education (EDST2009)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Note: The course code for this course was previously EDST1108. Teachers hold significant responsibilities to support, implement, respect, and learn from Indigenous perspectives in the classroom and in educational communities. In this course, you will learn how to enact this responsibility in classroom teaching, schooling, educational policy, and other professional practices. First, you will reflect on and locate yourself, your beliefs, and your perspectives as a future teacher in relation to land, language, culture, time, place, and relationships. You will then learn how this positionality impacts and informs your responsibility as a teacher, and helps you understand your students and their learning needs. Finally, you will engage with a range of Indigenous perspectives, research, theory, policy, and evidence to critically evaluate teaching resources and scenarios. This course will shape your professional practice in teaching in Australia or in other places that have their own histories of occupation, colonisation, or custodianship.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify, evaluate and justify curriculum and pedagogical practices in relation to Indigenous education
- Explain what the professional, political and personal responsibilities of an educator are in relation to Indigenous education
- Develop suitable skills and strategies to work effectively as a teacher with diverse Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islanders peoples, perspectives, and histories
- Respect and value Indigenous sovereignties and the ability to genuinely promote Reconciliation through education

Subject: Special Education: Inclusive Strategies (EDST4080)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course provides an overview of the field of special and inclusive education. It surveys the characteristics, training, and educational needs of students with a disability. The course emphasizes accommodating students with a disability in inclusive settings, including legal mandates and regulations; characteristics and educational needs of students with a disability; and evidence-based practices which can be used with these students in the general education classroom. By the end of the course, students will have a broad overview of the field and its key concepts, issues, and the relevant Department of Education policies. They will be conversant with the main categories of students with a disability and know some ways of accommodating for these students in the inclusive classroom.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate the ability to apply research-based decision making to select appropriate programming options for students with diverse needs.
- Develop strategies to implement NESA syllabus documents including Life Skills outcomes and content, and additional literacy and numeracy support.
- Understanding of differentiated instruction and layered curricula
- Demonstrate proficiency in the knowledge of the current issues and attitudes affecting policymaking, identification of needs, and educational provision for diverse students in NSW.

- Match research on the cognitive and affective characteristics of gifted students to practical teaching strategies and individual learning needs

Subject: Extension Visual Arts Method 1 (EDST6777)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This is a hybrid course. It is available to both undergraduate and postgraduate students. The course content, delivery and assessment will be identical for both groups of students. This course expands students' skills and knowledge to teach visual arts in secondary schools, with an emphasis on elective visual arts and preparation for the HSC. Students will think deeply about their philosophy of visual arts education while critically examining current high school visual arts curriculums and developing skills related to teaching visual arts lessons with innovations in ICT, composition, and formal and informal visual arts learning practices.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate the essential link between outcomes, assessment, teaching strategies and lesson planning
- Plan for and implement a range of literacy strategies to meet the needs of all students
- Investigate and discuss a variety of strategies to develop rapport with students, a positive classroom learning environment and approaches to managing student behaviour
- Demonstrate knowledge and understanding of the NSW Stage 5 and 6 Visual Arts Syllabi and various Department of Education policies, particularly those relating to ICT, Literacy, Aboriginal Education and Cultural Diversity
- Plan and implement coherent, goal oriented lessons and lesson sequences that are designed to engage all students and address learning outcomes
- Develop appropriate and engaging resources for the visual arts classroom that take into account students’ skills, interests and prior achievements and that respect the social, ethnic and religious backgrounds of students
- Demonstrate knowledge and understanding of learning outcomes and classroom practice related to teaching ICT
- Use the internet and web-based Learning Management Systems to deliver visual arts curriculum to students
- Analyse specific strategies for teaching Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander students, students with Special Education Needs, Non-English Speaking Background students, Students with Challenging Behaviours
- Discuss classroom strategies that recognise students’ different approaches to learning

Subject: Extension Visual Arts Method 2 (EDST6778)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This is a hybrid course. It is available to both undergraduate and postgraduate students. The course content, delivery and assessment will be identical for both groups of students. This course is an extension to learning some basic skills and concepts in visual arts education. Students undertaking this course will think deeply about some of the most critical issues facing visual arts educators. Content knowledge will be integrated with pedagogy and linked to professional issues unique to visual arts educators. This course is designed with a view to students eventually becoming excellent teachers who are able to continue to improve the quality of visual arts education in Australia.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically evaluate and implement a variety of forms of assessment of student learning in visual arts tasks appropriate for the HSC
- Advocate for visual arts education
- Understand the role of parents in a quality visual arts education and communicate effective learning goals and strategies with parents
- Reflect on pre-service teacher education in preparation for the final Professional Experience
- Understand the scope and limitations of the quality of visual arts education in Australia with a focus on National Reviews of School Visual Arts Education
- Develop and articulate a philosophy of education
- Understand the syllabus requirements for visual arts education in senior levels of high school
- Establish links with other members of the visual arts education community through professional associations

Subject: Computing Technology Method 1 (EDST6788)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This is a hybrid course. It is available to both undergraduate and postgraduate students. The course content, delivery and assessment will be identical for both groups of students. In this course, you will learn how to teach Technology and Computing Technology (7-10 Syllabus) in secondary contexts. You will use relevant syllabus documents to develop innovative and engaging lesson plans. You will learn and practise a range of effective teaching strategies designed to engage a diversity of learners in a safe and supportive classroom environment. You will learn how to use digital and other innovative resources to assess and improve discipline learning and develop language, literacy and numeracy skills across the curriculum. Through self and peer evaluation, you will also learn how to present yourself in a professional, supportive manner. This course will be offered in the Bachelor of Design/Education (4067), Master of Teaching (8926) and Graduate Certificate of Teaching (7327). It will be excluded from Bachelor of Science/Education (4076), Bachelor of Arts/Education (4053), Bachelor of Fine Arts/Education (4068), Bachelor of Commerce/Education (3462), Bachelor of Economics/Education (4058).
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify foundational aspects and structure of the NESA Technology and Computing Technology Syllabuses and the depth of subject knowledge required to implement the syllabuses.
- Use a range of strategies to plan and teach effective lessons to engage all students, address relevant syllabus outcomes and ensure a safe learning environment.
- Evaluate how student characteristics affect learning and evaluate implications for teaching students with different characteristics and from diverse backgrounds.
- Design and evaluate formative assessment strategies and use assessment information to improve learning.
- Select appropriate resources, including ICT, to engage students and expand learning opportunities.
- Practice the ethical and professional values expected of teachers.
