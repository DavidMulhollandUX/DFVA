Subject: Food Safety & Quality Management (FOOD3017)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Total quality management principles and their application in ensuring product quality and safety. International and national food regulatory systems and the development of, and scientific basis for food regulations.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply the principles of Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points (HACCP) to identify food safety hazards, determine critical control points and develop monitoring and corrective action strategies to ensure the safety and quality of food products.
- Use risk analysis approaches to identify, evaluate and manage biological, chemical, physical and allergenic hazards in the food supply chain.
- Interpret food regulations, guidelines and codes of practice to evaluate compliance and inform evidence-based decision making in food safety and quality management.
- Evaluate foodborne hazards and toxic substances including naturally occurring toxins, contaminants and additives by applying principles of toxicology, exposure assessment and health risk evaluation.
Assessment Tasks:
- Sequential Quiz (In-class, Online) Identity Verified In-person Online: 20% 20% (Each Quiz contributes 10% to the overall weighting of 20%)% (Hurdle: False)
- In-Semester Exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: True)
- End-of-Semester Examination Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Advanced Food Materials Science (FOOD7000)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Advanced knowledge and understanding of food materials systems; relation between property-process-structure-performance of foods; glassy and crystalline state of foods; phase transitions; gel structures; interfacial properties; nanostructures and nanotechnology for foods; micro- and nano-encapsulation technologies and stability and delivery of nutrients; sensory and rheology/tribology relationships; food product design based on materials properties; food material characterising techniques.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate the impact of dimensions and phase transition and states on food properties
- Use different characterization techniques to analyse the changes in relationship in micro, macro, and nano composition of food.
- Describe the influence of gels, foams, suspensions, colloids, enzymes, and other additive to food structure.
- Use rheology, tribology, and oral processing to analyse and evaluate possible enhancement in food texture via 3D printing and alternative techniques.
- Compare the properties, composition, structure, and texture of different types of food.
Assessment Tasks:
- In-semester Examination Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 35%% (Hurdle: True)
- Presentation (one page format) Identity Verified Team or group-based Online: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Examination Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: True)

Subject: Advanced Functional Foods (FOOD7025)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Functional foods are considered as those foods which are intended to be consumed as part of the normal diet but contain bioactive substances that offer the potential of enhanced health or reduced risk of disease. Nutraceuticals are concentrated forms of the food bioactives intended to be consumed as supplements in the form of tablets, capsules or liquid concentrates. Examples of functional foods include foods that contain specific fatty acids (e.g. omega 3) or dietary fibre (e.g. beta glucan), or biologically active substances such as phytochemicals or other antioxidants or probiotics with live beneficial cultures. Consumer awareness and interest in the relationship between diet and health has increased substantially and health is now a major driver for market positioning of foods. The course covers the issues and challenges in the development, evidence testing, marketing and changing regulations controlling functional foods and nutraceuticals. In this course students will develop an advanced knowledge of functional foods allowing them to form critical opinions of their efficacy and also about the currently increasing stringent health claim regulations within Australia and overseas.
Learning Outcomes:
- Comprehend the distinctions and roles of functional foods and nutraceuticals as options to support health.
- Comprehend and summarise the active ingredients in functional foods and nutraceuticals, emphasising their health benefits.
- Review legislation governing functional foods and nutraceuticals.
- Critically analyse and synthesize evidence on functional foods and nutraceuticals, independently/collaboratively relating findings to health claim.
- Interact & engage with others to effectively communicate scientific information visually.
Assessment Tasks:
- Sequential Online Quizzes (during Lecture) In-person Online: 30% Each quiz contributes 15% towards the 30% weighting.% (Hurdle: False)
- Poster Presentation Identity Verified Team or group-based In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Examination Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: True)

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=MICR7001 =====
Subject: Food Microbiology & Biotechnology (MICR7001)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Examines the positive (fermentation) and negative (spoilage) roles microbes play in foods and methods of preservation. Rapid detection methods and molecular (DNA) methods relevant to food microbiology including genomics (WGS), real-time PCR and culture-independent identification are investigated. It also explores spores, biofilms and problems and applications of bacteriophages, GM microbes and bioreactors. Practical components include organic acid preservation of yeast in apple juice, extension of shelf-life using carbon dioxide, decimal reduction of bacteria using heat, the isolation and identification of food bacteria using 16s rDNA PCR, DNA sequencing and bioinformatics.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate the roles of microorganisms in food spoilage, preservation and fermentation, and their impacts on food quality, safety, and shelf life.
- Appraise rapid detection, molecular and genomic methods including real-time PCR, DNA sequencing, and whole genome sequencing for identifying and tracking food-related microorganisms.
- Synthesise knowledge of microbial stress responses, spores, and biofilms to assess their significance for food processing and control strategies.
- Justify the use of bacteriophages, biocontrol agents and genetically modified microorganisms in food and biotechnological applications.
- Interpret experimental data from food microbiology investigations to draw evidence-based conclusions.
- Appraise emerging applications in food microbiology and biotechnology, including precision fermentation and alternative protein production
Assessment Tasks:
- In-semester exam Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Group poster presentation Team or group-based In-person: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Examination Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 45%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week Practical 3 Hours/ Week Exam 2 Hours/ One Off 2L1T3P2E

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=MICR7003 =====
Subject: Food Microbiology I (MICR7003)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Introduction to microbiology and food microbiology including microbial ecology of food; important foodborne pathogens (bacteria, viruses, fungi, seafood toxins, parasites, prions); food borne illness; indicator organisms; culture-based microbe detection; epidemiological and DNA fingerprinting methods used in outbreak investigations, antibiotic resistance in foodborne bacteria, foodborne pathogen pathogenesis mechanisms and emerging foodborne pathogens and evolution; practical culture-based microbiological examination of foods.
Learning Outcomes:
- Categorise the broad microbe groups in foods and general factors that influence their contamination and growth.
- Evaluate microbial test results for foods by calculating microbial loads per unit of food and judging compliance with relevant regulatory microbiological criteria.
- Categorise important foodborne disease-causing microorganisms based on their characteristics, epidemiological features, and diseases they cause.
- Illustrate relevant control methods for preventing food poisoning at different stages of food production and processing.
- Work individually or as part of a team to determine how lab techniques could be used to solve experimental problems involving the accurate isolation, enumeration, and confirmation of the identity of microorganisms from foods.
- Demonstrate the ability to apply public health outbreak investigation principles to a simulated foodborne illness event, including case definition, hypothesis generation, basic epidemiological analysis, and risk communication.
- Explain the mechanisms driving antimicrobial resistance, the key stages of foodborne pathogen pathogenesis in the host, and the processes underpinning pathogen evolution and emergence.
Assessment Tasks:
- In-semester exam Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Simulated public health response to a foodborne illness outbreak Team or group-based: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of semester exam Identity Verified: 45%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Practical 3 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week 2L3P2T
