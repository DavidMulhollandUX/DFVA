The Master of Theatre is an integrated coursework degree that offers three specialisations – Directing, Dramaturgy and Writing. The core of our work in Theatre is collaboration, which we aim to foster and develop through the course to enable experimentation, partnerships and relationships within the Theatre cohorts, as well as the Victorian College of the Arts (VCA) and artistic community more broadly.

The program offers in-depth research and practical skills in a studio-based learning environment, which include workshops, seminars, intensives, mentorships, professional placements and projects to develop the student's artistic voice and shared visions, preparing them for a career in the theatre industry.

Classes bring together students across specialisations and are taught by staff and guest lecturers with wide industry experience to instil, enhance and cultivate individual and collaborative creative skills and processes, enabling students to develop their artistic projects and research aptitude to their full potential.

Students will be exposed to a comprehensive array of voices, cultures and ways of making theatre, from the classic canon to contemporary Australian and international work, in a nurturing space that encourages experimentation and rigorous development and dramaturgical collaboration.

Through a focus on skills development, creative projects – including an independent capstone project – conceptual innovation, optional industry internships in professional theatre contexts, the Master of Theatre provides students with comprehensive practical experience and an acute level of critical thinking that enables each student to create their own portfolio of work to facilitate their career trajectory and/or pathway towards graduate research.

Intended learning outcomes

On completion of this course, graduates should be able to:

Apply collaborative processes within creative teams, performance contexts, and diverse theatre communities;
Demonstrate skills to formulate, develop, and apply diverse methodologies for creative practice;
Employ advanced critical, artistic, and conceptual skills to develop and engage theatre practice and scholarship;
Develop reflective practice through enhanced research capacity and knowledge of significant practitioners and theorists in the field;
Exhibit advanced leadership competency, with broad theatrical knowledge, sophisticated practice and ability to critically engage with industry and artistic communities;
Engage in innovative theatre practice to apply personal vision, autonomy, and creative experimentation;
Foster collaborative networks nationally and/or internationally.

work across disciplines, creating and organising a range of aesthetic material;

communicate verbally and orally;
collaborate and be flexible;
use a range of research tools and methodologies;

interpret and analyse with a capacity for critical thinking;

a capacity to engage where appropriate with issues in contemporary society;
the capacity to value and participate in projects which require team work;
an appreciation of the design, conduct and reporting of original research;
an ability to evaluate and synthesise the research and professional literature in the discipline;
a capacity to articulate knowledge and understanding in oral and written presentations;
an advanced understanding of the changing knowledge base in the specialist area;
well-developed problem-solving abilities in the discipline area characterised by flexibility of approach;
an understanding of the significance and value of knowledge to the wider community (including business and industry).

This subject focuses on close reading and analysis of existing plays drawn from a range of pre-dramatic, dramatic, postdramatic and other performance contexts, including Greek, Shakespearean, nineteenth and twentieth century realist and anti-realist movements, non-narrative forms, and more contemporary developments.

Drawing on international and Australian examples, both historical and contemporary, the subject will examine organising concepts such as dramatic structure, story, plot, dialogue, character, dramatic action, lived, observed and imagined experience, language and subtext, space and time, for their utility, particularity and applicability.

Theories of dramaturgy from Aristotle through Lessing to Brecht, Lehmann, Van Kerkhoven and New Dramaturgies, as well as other forms of performance writing (e.g. First Nations performance, choreographic, film script, digital media, live art) will all be considered. This subject offers students opportunities to examine writing for the theatre and better understand their context, ambition and how dramatic elements work together in a dynamic, interconnected and creative manner.

Intended learning outcomes

On completion of this subject, students should be able to:

demonstrate an ability to interpret and analyse performance in the context of professional practice, drawing on a wide range of cultures, performance practices and theatre theories;
analyse diverse texts for performance from a range of historical periods including the present;
articulate dramaturgical theory and practice in relation to performance;
demonstrate skills in the gathering, organisation and interpretation of research materials.
Generic skills
evaluate and synthesise data and other information;
access data and other information from a range of sources;
discourse on artistic practice in the context of wider aesthetic, social and historical domains;
apply relevant research processes to specific cultural practice;
communicate effectively in oral and written form;
demonstrate a capacity for critical thinking.

This subject examines collaborative dramaturgies through the experience of a range of artists practicing today, viewing and reviewing specific preparation activities, rehearsal techniques and collective authorship of a devised performance project or projects. The subject draws on critical, practical and theoretical understandings of dramaturgy to apply it as a tool in creating live performance.

Students will translate given stimuli into studio-scale works-in-progress performance through research, iterative feedback, design, documentation and practical investigation. Reflective practice techniques are used to develop robust dramaturgical analyses of collaborative working processes in a written assessment.

Intended learning outcomes

On completion of this subject, students should be able to:

devise and perform collaboratively authored material;
articulate an approach to collaborative dramaturgy in rehearsal room practice and processes;
apply dramaturgical decisions and implement changes within a creative process based on group discussions and staff feedback;
produce relevant documents and materials required by a collaborative devising project in a production process;
critically analyse the balance of individual effort within collaborative devising contexts.

This subject provides a direct opportunity for students to work in a setting that underpins and is relevant to their practice and/or research trajectory, or alternatively, to research and develop a practice-based project.

The structure of this subject will involve:

Meetings in person or virtually, with the industry partner and/or research project supervisor.
Occasional academic supervision before, during and after the placement/research report - to be determined in consultation with the subject coordinator, and participation in seminars as required.
An intensive placement within the performing arts industry, or equivalent time spent on the development of a practice-based project.
Documentation of the placement/research project through journal writing and archiving of relevant materials.
A presentation of the placement/research project to the cohort in a group forum after the completion of the project.
Final reflective/critical investigation or minor thesis.

Placement Option
The subject allows students to undertake a local, national or international placement, completing this across the teaching period. Placement details, its content and interface will align with the individual student’s aims in both their developing practice and the research direction of their capstone practical work and exegesis.

Students also undertake classes in exegesis writing, to analyse and reflect upon their placement experience by setting up critical research questions.

The placement may involve local theatre or community arts organisations, or international institutions, companies or festivals.

Research Project Option
Students who are unable to undertake a placement, or who choose not to, have the option of completing a research project that can be negotiated with their lecturer. This would involve writing a thesis, in addition to documenting the practical project or written task.

The educational and practice/research aims of the subject are the same for all students, but the type of experience, content and ultimate structure of the subject will be determined by the nature of the interface the student has planned through preparatory work.

Each student will have a supervisor to support them through planning, preparation, and the writing-up phase following the placement/project.

Intended learning outcomes

On completion of this subject, students should be able to:

demonstrate experience and developing research and practice skills in a relevant project setting;
identify and engage areas of specialisation in practice or research;
apply advanced skills of reflection and analysis of artistic practice;
identify and set up industry or project linkages relevant to their developing practice;
evaluate and analyse research methodologies and critical issues in relation to performance;
demonstrate imaginative and creative communication and presentation skills in the delivery of a research proposal.
Generic skills
use a range of research tools and methodologies;
work independently;
communicate effectively;
solve problems;
interpret and analyse;
develop the capacity for critical thinking;
organise and manage time;
understand the relationship with, and responsibility to, the cultural environment;
work with respect for intellectual integrity, intellectual property and for the ethics of research and scholarship.

This subject focuses closely on the development of a project idea that builds and demonstrates the creative vision that will underpin the student’s work for their final year and beyond.

The delivery incorporates lectures, visiting speakers, and collaborative practical workshops. The subject will introduce students to theoretical and practical concepts of project realisation, implementation methodologies, strategic planning, networking, budgeting, documentation, evaluation, report making, pitching and marketing. It will investigate the issues and demands of being an artist in the broader community. Students will explore their own ideas for a project that they will develop over the duration of the subject.

The emphasis of the subject will be the entrepreneurial aspect of the theatrical endeavour. The subject includes the presentation of a project pitch, a draft funding application, and a detailed written project management plan.

Intended learning outcomes

On completion of this subject, students should be able to:

integrate entrepreneurial skills into project creation;
apply collaborative and participatory practice in the formation of a creative vision for a project;
develop capacity for budgeting, strategic planning, documentation and evaluation methodologies in project creation;
apply research methodology appropriate to their practice and project;
present and pitch a creative project and management plan in written and oral form.
