## Graduate Diploma in Psychology
##### M5013
##### 60 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
This course offers graduates a major in psychology accredited by the Australian Psychology Accreditation Council (APAC), permitting them to undertake the further training needed to obtain professional qualifications.

This course enables you to gain an introduction to psychology as a behavioural science including training in research techniques and research design and analysis. As you progress through the course, you will cover topics such as developmental and biological psychology, psychopathology and clinical psychology, principles and processes of test development, test administration and test interpretation. The course also covers cognitive psychology, cross-cultural and Indigenous psychology, social psychology and personality. You will gain a strong foundation in professional ethics for psychologists, including advanced knowledge of the ethical, legal and professional responsibilities of practising psychologists.

### AQF level
Level 8 - Graduate Diploma
### Post-nominals
GradDipPsych

### Structure
The course consists of nine core units and one elective unit. You must complete both foundational units (PSY4111 and PSY4122) before proceeding to the intermediate units (PSY4131 and PSY4051). Once both intermediate units are complete, you are able to progress onto the advanced level units.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Apply knowledge of group processes and dynamics, and demonstrate the ability to lead and productively participate in group work.
**2.**
Demonstrate self-reflection and reflexivity in the application of psychological knowledge and skills.
**3.**
Critically evaluate a range of research paradigms and methods and undertake research to solve real world problems.
**4.**
Critically appraise and apply professional, ethical, and legal frameworks in the discipline of psychology.
**5.**
Recognise the diversity of populations and apply psychological knowledge and skills in a culturally responsive manner.
**6.**
Critically appraise and apply psychological knowledge to individual, societal and global challenges.
**7.**
Effectively communicate a broad and coherent body of psychological knowledge to diverse audiences in a variety of formats.

### Progression to further studies
Successful completion of this course may provide a pathway to one of the following Monash University APAC-accredited fourth year psychology programs:

  * Honours in Psychology
  * Graduate Diploma of Psychology Advanced (M5003)
  * Graduate Diploma of Professional Psychology (D5002)

Admission to fourth-year courses is determined by academic merit. Admission requirements can be found on the Monash Study website.

### AHEGS
The Graduate Diploma in Psychology is a postgraduate award. The course is taught in English and normally takes 1.7 years of part-time study to complete. Graduates have completed a major in psychology accredited by the Australian Psychology Accreditation Council (APAC), permitting them to undertake the further training needed to obtain professional qualifications. The Graduate Diploma in Psychology is a Level 8 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Perception and cognition (PSY4081)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit covers a variety of perceptive and cognitive processes. In the first half, you will study auditory and visual perception, as well as language comprehension. Subject: Developmental and biological psychology (PSY4131)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit provides an overview of the two fascinating areas of the human experience: our biology and our development. In Biological Psychology, you will explore the relationship between structures and functions of the brain and human behaviour. You will be introduced to neural mechanisms that underlie states of consciousness, mental disorders, addiction, typical and atypical learning and memory, and the regulation of emotional and motivated states.
