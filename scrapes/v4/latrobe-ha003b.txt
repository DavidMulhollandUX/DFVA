### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Explain the causes and possible interprofessional approaches to common oral health issues
**2.**
Review ethical practice in an interdisciplinary approach to oral health care
**3.**
Communicate responsibly about interprofessional preventive oral health approaches with a range of stakeholders
**4.**
Plan integrated oral health-promoting strategies
**5.**
Appraise research relevant to preventive and interdisciplinary oral health care

Subject: DENTAL SCIENCE A (DEN1DSA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will be introduced to the anatomical organisation of the body and the basics of cell structure, function, nutrition and metabolism. The fundamentals of the nervous and endocrine systems will be explored in the context of mechanisms of physiological control and homeostasis. This information will provide the foundation for the study of the major organ systems of the body, and an understanding of the oral systemic connections. Communication is important in engaging patients and communities in relation to oral health, so you will also learn to communicate in groups with peers about the body systems.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain how a given body system contributes to homeostasis under normal conditions.
- Explain how cellular activity contributes to the function of organs and the body as a whole.
- Relate the anatomical organisation of the human body to whole body function.
- Work effectively individually and with peers to apply professional and medical terminology into communicating oral-systemic connections.

Subject: DENTAL SCIENCE B (DEN1DSB)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will learn about the gross anatomy of structures predominantly in the region of the head and neck. The subject will include a histology component where you will learn details of normal histology of structures within the oral cavity The subject will also include embryology where you will learn about the development of teeth. Principles of anatomy will be introduced and applied to the anatomy of the head and neck relevant for Dentistry and Oral Health. The subject will also introduce you into other visceral cavities of the head and neck which will then provide you with relevant background for future clinical practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the histological features of different tooth components and discuss the processes involved in the formation and differentiation of dental tissues during embryogenesis
- Explain the structure and function of the head and neck including its role in housing visceral structures, special sense organs and the central nervous system using appropriate medical terminology, surface anatomy and observational skills
- Be able to identify anatomical structures and infer their relationship to function and communicate anatomical concepts using correct medical terminology in writing.
- Describe the anatomical basis of some common abnormalities of, or injuries to structures in the head and neck.

Subject: SCIENTIFIC AND CLINICAL KNOWLEDGE A (DEN1SKA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will study the anatomy of the head and face with a focus on anatomy of the teeth. You will study human tooth morphology, where morphology is the study of the form and structure, in this instance of teeth and their specific structural features. This subject will also introduce you to the core values of a health practitioner and awareness of the First Nations culture which set a foundation to the development of your professional identity.
Learning Outcomes:
- Articulate core concepts related to the foundational cultural awareness and cultural responsiveness for improving the health outcomes in the First Nations peoples
- Identify and discuss the anatomy of each tooth in the human dentitions and determine their functions
- Contextualise the characteristics of a professional oral health practitioner with reference to professional behaviour, ethical principles and communication
- Apply discipline-specific terminology and nomenclature to describe the functional anatomy of the orofacial region

Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will learn the determinants of health and oral health that operate at individual and community levels. You will explore how to analyse the risk factors and behaviours that influence an individual or group and how this can affect their oral health and access to care. You will develop skills in preventing oral diseases at individual and community levels. - Describe the determinants of health, risk factors and behaviours that influence individual and community oral health.
- Apply the principles of population health and health promotion and explain the role of health policy for collaboratively developing, implementing and evaluating evidence-based health promotion strategies.
- Demonstrate familiarity with Australian standards on safety and quality in health care and appraise the concept of person-centred care, including the identification of high-risk populations, and the provision of trauma-informed care.
- Discuss the access and provision of oral health care services in Australia and the environmental impact of providing health care.

Subject: SCIENTIFIC AND CLINICAL KNOWLEDGE 2A (DEN2004)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, students will build upon theoretical foundations, translating principles guiding periodontal condition management into clinical contexts for diagnosis, care planning, and preventive strategies. Additionally, they will relate the theoretical concepts underpinning radiographic techniques and the administration of local anaesthesia, to ensure safe and effective clinical practice. Furthermore, they will extend their insight into the theoretical principles of operative dentistry, in general, and minimal intervention dentistry specifically, as applied to both the paediatric and adult dentitions. This holistic approach will equip students with the knowledge needed to navigate diverse clinical presentations and deliver optimal person-centred care.
Learning Outcomes:
- Applying the theoretical principles of Minimal Intervention Dentistry to the provision of person-centred care.
- Critique the theoretical principles underpinning intra-oral radiographic techniques, panoramic imaging principles, and radiographic image interpretation for clinical application.
- Compare and contrast the management of periodontal conditions, encompassing diagnosis, treatment planning, and preventive interventions to uphold periodontal health.
- Relate concepts and principles of local anaesthetics intra and extra oral administration to patient safety and potential complications.

Subject: RESEARCH AND EVIDENCE IN PRACTICE (HLT1RAE)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject is an introduction to the use of research-based evidence in professional health care practice. Working in interprofessional teams and using a range of case scenarios, you will develop research skills in areas relevant to your field of practice. Through online activities and workshops, you will learn about the role of evidence-based practice in health. Areas of study include systematic approaches to acquiring evidence, critical appraisal of the literature, interpretation of research design, descriptive and inferential statistics and assessment of research outcomes. You will learn how an evidence-based approach in health informs clinical practice. You will develop research skills to determine the most appropriate intervention techniques for application in a given clinical population, while understanding the complex interaction between social, economic and environmental influences that contribute to sustainability thinking in health research.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically appraise some components of the validity of health research evidence
- Explain the relative merits and uses of the different types of evidence used in practice
- Communicate research outcomes appropriate to peers
- Develop an effective search strategy to address practice based research questions

Subject: INTEGRATING EVIDENCE INTO PRACTICE (HLT2IEP)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will learn to formulate answerable practice-related questions, search for evidence, evaluate the evidence, apply the evidence and reflect on your own performance. During the course of this learning, you also begin to develop an understanding of research processes and, thereby, develop 'research literacy' as an essential tool for health practice and for the utilisation of research evidence in practice. Relevant aspects of research methods are introduced, and you will acquire knowledge relating to the selection and use of measures and/or tests that are used in research and in practice in their discipline.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply appraised evidence to a practice-related problem or issue or situation
- Appraise the strength of evidence generated by a range of methodologies
- Assess your own performance in developing capacity to be an evidence-based practitioner.
- Ask, develop and refine relevant and answerable practice-related questions using a structured approach
- Acquire evidence relevant to practice by developing and implementing complex search strategies applicable to databases

Subject: INTRODUCTION TO CLINICAL PRACTICE A (ORH2002)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this clinical placement subject, you'll develop professional and technical skills under close supervision by clinical educators. You'll develop self-reflection skills and apply them in your clinical work
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify systemic and other health conditions from patients' medical histories and presentations impacting oral structures
- Apply the principles and practices of infection control, diagnostic investigation, and preventive care, in the clinical environment with respect to the provision of clinical care and non-complex management of oral diseases.
- Analyse the implications for treatment and oral health care which arise from the effects of therapeutic drugs, their impact on patient management and appropriate application to the clinical environment.
- Demonstrate continual improvement and clinical practice through self-reflection and appraisal.
