Subject: Genes, Cells & Evolution (BIOL1020)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Students taking this course will learn about the fundamentals of molecular cell biology and genetics by examining what cells are made of and how the genetic information to build cells and perform cellular processes are encoded, executed, and inherited. The course explores the connections between physical processes at the molecular level and the whole organism phenotype, and identifies how cellular, genetic, and evolutionary processes affect everyday life.
Learning Outcomes:
- Generic Skills - Identify and describe how cellular, genetic, and evolutionary processes impact everyday human life, including (but not limited to) effects on human health, agriculture, and biodiversity.
- Generic Skills - Be able to formulate testable hypotheses and evaluate those hypotheses against empirical results.
- Generic Skills - Demonstrate practical skills in different aspects in biology.
- Molecular & cellular biology - Compare and contrast the diversity in molecular components and cellular structures across the three domains of life and describe examples of fundamental similarities arising from the common origin of life.
- Molecular & cellular biology - Explain how cells are able to coordinate the basic molecular building blocks of life in order to divide, replicate, and survive.
- Genes to traits - Understand what genes are and how they link to traits, the physical nature of the gene and molecular processes underlying the “Central Dogma” of molecular biology.
- Genes to traits - Compare and contrast the gene regulatory mechanisms between bacteria and eukaryotes.
- Genes to traits - Describe sexual reproduction through meiosis and how it contributes to genetic variation.
- Genes to traits - Conceptual knowledge of viruses and synthetic biology
- Inheritance and Evolution - Explain and compare processes contributing to genetic variability, including but not limited to: mutation, recombination, transformation, gene flow, horizontal gene transfer, gene and genome duplication, genetic drift, and natural selection.
- Inheritance and Evolution - Understand the relationship between recombination and linkage.
- Inheritance and Evolution - How to detect if evolution is occurring by calculating allele frequencies.
Assessment Tasks:
- Practical preparation and participation In-person: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Mini Test 1 Identity Verified In-person: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Mini Test 2 Identity Verified In-person: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of semester exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 60%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week Practical 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: Genetics (BIOL2202)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The discipline of Genetics has pioneered the core concepts underlying many diverse fields of biology - ranging from medicine, biochemistry and microbiology to ecology, evolution and environmental sciences. BIOL2202 Genetics is therefore a recommended second level subject for all students pursuing a biology-focused major. Employing a strong experimental component, BIOL2202 integrates classical concepts of inheritance, development and variation with modern molecular advances arising from the post-genomic era.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and interpret nucleotide sequences with the aid of the genetic code, and understand the effects of mutation and DNA repair on gene structure and function.
- Discuss the molecular basis of genetic inheritance and the mechanisms that enable the transfer of genetic material.
- Explain how gene regulation influences cell differentiation, and clarify the role of epigenetics in modulating gene expression.
- Interpret genetic screens, genetic interactions, and molecular genetic analyses of simple and complex phenotypic traits.
- Clarify the central role of genetic variation in shaping evolution.
- Develop integrated knowledge of the the broad concepts of the discipline of Genetics in the context of humans and consider how advances in Genetics are perceived by society.
- Understand how to apply key genetic techniques in the laboratory, including manipulation of classic model systems.
- Independently research manuscripts published in the field of Genetics, and show your deep understanding of the topic by communicating it effectively to others.
Assessment Tasks:
- Genetics practical Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- In-Semester Exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 25%% (Hurdle: True)
- The Genetic Basis of Stuff and Things: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 45%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 3 Hours/ Week Practical 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: Genomics (BIOL3303)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: BIOL3303 is focused on teaching students contemporary genetics through the lens of genomics and associated technologies. The course content is framed around outstanding challenges in genetics and the genomics discoveries and approaches that can help solve them. The course lecturers will take an active learning approach to engage students in the theoretical and applied components of Genomics, using examples from genetic model and non-model organisms, including humans, fruit flies, plants, and vertebrates. Through a mixture of problem-based learning and practical activities, students will learn about the structural and functional aspects of genomes composition, function, regulation, variation and evolution. Students in the course will also learn about gene and genome editing methods and the ethical and legal frameworks underlying issues like precision genome engineering and personalised genomics.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the core principles of Genomics as a genetics discipline
- Identify and describe the technologies used to generate genomics data
- Describe the structure, function, and diversity of genomes
- Understand and apply key analytical approaches in genomics data analysis
- Explain the roles of various genomic elements in evolutionary processes
- Integrate practical, theoretical, and applied aspects of genome engineering
- Evaluate impacts of genomics knowledge on society and on individuals
- Anticipate the role of genomics in knowledge economies and future job markets
- Develop scientific research proposals by generating scientific hypotheses and designing genomics-based experiments
Assessment Tasks:
- Online Quizzes Online: 21%% (Hurdle: False)
- In-semester - In-person invigilated paper exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 48%% (Hurdle: True)
- Employability Reflection: 1%% (Hurdle: False)
- Research Proposal: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Contact 2 Hours/ Week Practical 2 Hours/ Week

Credit Points: 2
Overview: A managerial perspective of controls and audit procedures associated with preserving authenticity, accuracy, completeness, timeliness and privacy of business electronic transactions over the Internet; quality assurance for electronic commerce/online business applications for business managers.
