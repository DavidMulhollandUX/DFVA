## Graduate Certificate of Personal Injury Management
##### M4035
##### 24 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
This course, offered by the School of Public Health and Preventive Medicine, provides personal injury management professionals with a fundamental knowledge of personal injury management. Study areas include legislative and regulatory design, claims management and public health.

### AQF level
Level 8 - Graduate Certificate
### Post-nominals
GradCertPersInjMgmt

### Structure
The course comprises 24 credit points undertaken as a combination of two 6 credit point units, plus two of the following Monash University assessed short courses. Each assessed short course is equivalent to a 6 credit point unit.

  * Principles of Personal Injury Scheme Design
  * Personal Injury Strategic Claims Management
  * Personal Injury Scheme Evaluation

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Define the legislative and operational framework governing personal injury management.
**2.**
Identify the critical issues faced by various approaches to personal injury insurance schemes and identify the strengths and weaknesses of each major approach.
**3.**
Analyse contemporary issues in the personal injury industry.
**4.**
Distinguish key principles of personal injury management, including scheme design, scheme financing, performance measurement, case management, rehabilitation and return to work.
**5.**
Describe the historical, social and political context of personal injury in Australian society and appraise recent developments in personal injury policy and practice.
**6.**
Identify the necessary components of an injury management program.
**7.**
Generate personal injury management strategies and/or programs, and/or research and apply public health principles and methods, to reduce the impact of personal injury in the community.

### AHEGS
The Graduate Certificate of Personal Injury Management is a postgraduate course. It is taught in English. Graduates have demonstrated fundamental theoretical and technical knowledge in personal injury management.  Graduates at this level will have fundamental cognitive, technical and communication skills. Graduates at this level will apply knowledge and skills to demonstrate autonomy, well-developed judgement, adaptability and responsibility as a practitioner or learner. The Graduate Certificate of Personal Injury Management is a Level 8 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification.  The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Introductory epidemiology (MPH5040)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit is taken concurrently with MPH5041, as together they are prerequisites for clinical epidemiology elective units. Upon completion of this unit you will be able to measure frequencies of disease i.e. incidence, prevalence, "population at risk"; and measure associations in epidemiological studies; different study designs i.e. observational studies, experimental study designs; bias associated with study designs, misclassification, confounding and effect modification. You will critically appraise different epidemiological studies, apply diagnostic and screening tests, and examine outbreak investigation.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain and contextualise the purposes of descriptive and analytical epidemiology;
- Interpret and communicate the results of epidemiological studies; and
- Evaluate the adequacy of exposure assessment in epidemiological studies;
- Solve complex problems relating to the use of epidemiological concepts and study designs.
- Critically analyse epidemiological papers from the medical research and OHS literature;
- Analyse the strengths and weaknesses of different epidemiological study designs;
- Explain and contextualise the concepts of study questions, population, sampling, exposure assessment, bias and confounding;
Assessment Tasks:
- MCQ (30 Minutes): 10% (Hurdle: None)
- Development activities: 35% (Hurdle: None)
- Critical appraisal (2,100 words): 35% (Hurdle: None)
- Infographic (1 page of graphics): 20% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 12 hours per week including two-hour weekly workshops for 10 weeks.

Subject: Health and safety risk management leadership and governance (MPH5222)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this unit you will undertake a comprehensive exploration of core Health and Safety risk management concepts, risk assessment techniques, and decision-making processes. You will gain a deep understanding of the principles, framework, and process of ISO 31000 risk management standards, including assurance activities. The unit emphasises the comparison and contrast of ISO 31000 risk assessment processes with existing governance and compliance approaches. Additionally, you will learn to communicate the critical importance of engaging stakeholders in risk assessment and the implementation of effective controls. Upon successful completion of this unit, you will be proficient in demonstrating, explaining, comparing, and communicating key aspects of risk management, preparing them for advanced roles in Health and Safety risk management.
Learning Outcomes:
- Communicate the importance of engaging stakeholders in Health and Safety risk assessment activities including implementing effective controls.
- Plan, critically evaluate and apply various Health and Safety ISO 31000 risk assessment processes in alignment with existing governance and compliance approaches.
- Describe core Health and Safety risk management concepts, risk assessment techniques and decision-making processes.
- Critically evaluate and apply the principles, framework, and process of ISO 31000 risk management standards including assurance activities.
Assessment Tasks:
- Workshop-based critical reflection activity (1,800 words): 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Case analysis report (2,400 words): 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Integrated case-based learning and assessment (ICLA) (up to 1,800 words): 30% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 10 hours of off-campus study per week, including: 6 hours self-directed-online learning & 4 hours teacher-directed online learning per week; and 3-day block of face-to-face learning, on-campus.

Subject: Injury epidemiology and prevention (MPH5256)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit will cover the principles of injury epidemiology, prevention and control. The unit will provide an introduction to the injury epidemiology and prevention field with a particular focus on issues facing injury surveillance, countermeasure development, injury policy and injury burden estimates.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and contextualise the concept of injury as a preventable health condition;
- Analyse and evaluate issues in the measurement of injury severity, definition, burden and outcome.
- Identify requirements for and conduct injury surveillance;
- Analyse and evaluate the strengths and limitations of a variety of research methods used to define the nature and extent of injury;
- Develop, implement and evaluate injury prevention programs and frameworks for countermeasure development;
- Theorise and analyse approaches to injury policy and legislation; and
- Synthesise the principles of injury prevention and control and related models;
- Identify and describe the context and consequence of burden of injury;

Subject: Principles of health care quality improvement (MPH5267)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this unit you will learn how historical, political & social factors impact on quality measurement in health care; the relationship of industrial and health care quality monitoring; epidemiological and statistical quality measurement principles; the strengths and limitations of current monitoring techniques; different sources of health care quality data; principles of clinical indicator programs; adverse event monitoring, satisfaction surveys and benchmarking; and the relationship between evidence based medicine, clinical practice guidelines and quality improvement. You will appreciate the complexities in designing, implementing and evaluating quality improvement programs in the clinical setting. The content aligns well with other management units in the program such as MPH5266 (Clinical Leadership and Management) and MPH5020 (Introduction to Biostatistics and Epidemiology). It is a core unit of the Graduate Diploma and Master of Health Services Management.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the key driving forces that underpin the need for healthcare quality improvement.
- Contribute to debates on safety and quality problems and potential solutions.
- Formulate practical solutions to safety and quality problems leading to improvement in a healthcare context.
- Apply safety and quality literature, research findings to healthcare practice.
- Apply quality improvement tools and methods to healthcare systems, processes and outcomes.
Assessment Tasks:
- Written report (2,400 words): 40% (Hurdle: Threshold)
- Critical reflections (5 x 480 words each. Total 2,400 words): 40% (Hurdle: Threshold)
- Letter to the editor (Letter x 600 words and supporting rationale x 600 words): 20% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 15 contact hours over 2 block day On average 10 hours per week to participate in online tasks and complete assignments
