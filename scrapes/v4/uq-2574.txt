Graduates will have in-depth knowledge and be prepared for leadership in the information technology profession. Graduates will display effective communication skills, independence and creativity, critical judgment and ethical and social understanding. The objective of the Bachelor of Information Technology is to train students in- the study of digital computer systems and their applications; the ability to process data or information in order to solve problems; the study of programming languages, algorithms and information structures.
The Bachelor of Arts component allows students to choose from a wide variety of majors to either complement their information technology studies, or to broaden their knowledge in another field, thus expanding their opportunities to work in different areas.

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=COMP2200 =====
Subject: Ethical Practice in Computing (COMP2200)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course introduces students to the ethical dimensions of computing practice, the responsibilities of computing professionals and the critical role of responsible decision-making. Through a case study centred approach, students will explore how everyday design and development decisions can lead to profound social, legal and ethical consequences. Topics include professional codes of conduct, frameworks for ethical decision-making, bias and inclusion, cybersecurity and privacy, sustainability and the ethical challenges of emerging technologies. Students will gain the tools to anticipate the consequences of technological choices beyond mere legal compliance, understand relevant Australian laws and global standards. Students will develop the moral sensitivity and principled reasoning needed for responsible computing.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the foundation principles and regulatory frameworks that guide and govern professional practice in computing, including relevant codes of conduct and Australian laws pertaining to technology.
- Apply ethical decision-making frameworks to anticipate the social, environmental and ethical impact of technological products and development processes.
- Explain legal & ethical responsibilities associated with issues related to privacy, security, diversity, inclusion and systems reliability in computing practice.
- Identify and critically reflect on personal biases and their potential influence on technological design, development and deployment.
Assessment Tasks:
- Weekly Tasks: x 2% (Hurdle: False)
- Case Study Hurdle Identity Verified: x 5% (Hurdle: True)
- Final Examination Hurdle Identity Verified: x 3% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Seminar 3 Hours/ Week Applied Class 2 Hours/ Week

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=COMP3820 =====
Subject: Digital Health Software Project (COMP3820)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course will help you learn the basic fundamentals of digital health technology through hands-on experience, and through exposure to the real-world use of data and informatics in the health industry. The course includes a MOOC component that provides a broad introduction to healthcare informatics. There will also be a number of guest lecturers from industry talking about the current digital health landscape. A major part of the course is composed of a group software development project building a FHIR app for use in a clinical (or clinical education) setting, with real-world clients. FHIR (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources) is the new standard for sharing health data that is revolutionising how clinicians and patients are interacting with digital technology and has been adopted by major global IT companies (e.g., Apple, Google, Microsoft, etc.) and digital health vendors (e.g., Cerner, EPIC, etc.). Be at the forefront of healthcare application development by learning about and developing new FHIR and SMART-on-FHIR applications.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and explain key aspects of the digital health landscape.
- Apply the Software Development Life Cycle and human-centred design principles to design, develop, and document a functional digital health application using FHIR and interoperability standards.
- Analyse and address user experience challenges in the design and development of a digital health application.
- Communicate effectively with stakeholders to gather requirements and manage expectations over the course of the project.
- Demonstrate an ability to collaborate effectively within a team to achieve shared project goals, while managing individual responsibilities.
- Evaluate and justify software development processes and design decisions within the context of building digital health applications.
Assessment Tasks:
- Team FHIR Project Team or group-based: 55% (Team)% (Hurdle: False)
- Individual Course Review Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: Pass/Fail (Individual)% (Hurdle: True)
- Individual Portfolio Hurdle: 30% (Individual)% (Hurdle: True)
- Team Final Demo Team or group-based In-person: 15% (Team)% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Studio 4 Hours/ Week

Credit Points: 2
Overview: As a major computing infrastructure, Cloud Computing provides the modern on-demand services for management and usage of large and shared computing resources including storage, computations and communications. This course covers a wide range of cloud computing-related X-as-a-Service technologies, including Software-as-a-Service (SaaS), Platform-as-a-Service (PaaS), Infrastructure-as-a-Service (IaaS), Data-as-a-Service (DaaS), and related technologies such as Cloud Computing Ecosystem. Learning Outcomes:

- Build applications based on Cloud Computing architecture and tools.
- Implement Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) with a recommended Cloud Computing platform.
- Demonstrate and solve the performance and technical issues in Cloud Computing such as cloud storage, cloud data management, scalability, privacy preservation and security.
- Explain the ecosystem of Cloud Computing.
- Explain implementation techniques of Cloud Computing such as clusters and in-memory computing.
- Discuss the roles played by Cloud Computing in Big Data Analytics and identify the trends in Cloud Computing.
