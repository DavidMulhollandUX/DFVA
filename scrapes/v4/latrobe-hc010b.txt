## Graduate Certificate of Rural Nursing Practice - Bendigo
##### HC010B
##### 60 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
Take the next step in your nursing career and equip yourself with the specialist skills required to support the unique health needs of patients living in rural locations.

Developed by experienced rural nurses, our industry-focused program will equip you with the skills and knowledge needed to work confidentially in any rural healthcare setting.

Gain the expertise to effectively assess, treat and manage all patients from paediatrics through to aged care. Extend your existing communication skills and learn how to problem solve and build a strong rapport with multidisciplinary teams.

In this course you'll:

  * Take your existing qualifications as a registered nurse and specialise in rural nursing practices.
  * Learn from experienced rural nursing academics who are also practicing clinicians.
  * Develop in-demand clinical skills and knowledge in urgent care, advanced life support, palliative care, rural aged care practice and remote emergency care.
  * Develop advanced work-based skills in communication, conflict resolution as well as effective teamwork and workplace culture.
  * Have the flexibility of studying online, from anywhere and join us for a short intensive for Advanced Life Support training.
  * Gain the skills required by registered nurses working in rural settings as identified by regional and rural health peak body CRANA+
  * Upon completion, use your qualification to apply for our Graduate Diploma in Rural Nursing Practice.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Apply advanced nursing practice knowledge and skills to contribute to informed clinical judgements for rural nursing practice.
**2.**
Generate evidence-based approaches to plan, implement and evaluate culturally appropriate person/client-centred rural nursing care.
**3.**
Perform independently and as a team member in a variety of clinical contexts within rural healthcare settings in accordance with professional ethical standards.
**4.**
Communicate effectively with the community and as a member of a geographically dispersed multidisciplinary team.

Subject: FOUNDATIONS OF URGENT CARE NURSING (NRS4001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Rural urgent care is a specialist rural nursing competency. This subject will equip you to assess and appropriately manage patients in the rural emergency setting. You will apply your knowledge to specialty population groups such as paediatrics and workers in primary industries and mining. You will learn to adapt your practice to low resource settings as well as being able to use the technology and equipment in contemporary rural health care environments. This subject will equip you to prepare and respond, as part of a multidisciplinary team, to high risk, infrequent situations. Apply Advanced Life Support (ALS) within the rural urgent care setting. Collaboratively stabilise, manage and transfer the acutely unwell patient out of the rural urgent care setting. Emergency maternity care for unplanned birth at term or prematurely. Urgent care emergency preparedness response in relation to natural disasters and pandemics. The subject has both theoretical and clinical components to support advanced skill development.
Learning Outcomes:
- Adapt a comprehensive urgent care plan to the resources available in a variety of rural and remote health care contexts
- Critically evaluate rural and remote urgent care nursing practice against research and current literature, applying a practice development framework for quality improvement.
- Initiate, plan and evaluate related clinical skills and equipment/technology to address patient care needs within the rural and remote urgent care setting.
- Manage care for identified speciality population groups such as bariatric, paediatric, obstetric and critically ill patients, as well as patients with difficult behaviours; or occupational/traumatic injury within rural and remote urgent care settings.

Subject: COMPREHENSIVE HEALTH ASSESSMENT (NRS4002)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will develop advanced health assessment skills to support evidence-based nursing care of people across the lifespan in rural health care contexts. You will integrate knowledge of pathophysiology, assessment and diagnostic findings to make informed clinical decisions and prioritise nursing care. You will build on your capability to respond to the deteriorating patient, and you will develop your non-technical skills to take a leadership role in collaborative care.
Learning Outcomes:
- Integrate assessment and diagnostic findings to make informed clinical decisions, to prioritise care and management, and complete appropriate documentation.
- Evaluate risk and appropriately respond to the deteriorating patient in varied rural and remote contexts.
- Adapt non-technical skills such as leadership, communication, teamwork, and situational awareness to provide effective collaborative nursing care in a rural and remote context.
- Evaluate comprehensive health assessment to diverse rural and remote contexts caring for patients across the life span.

Subject: COMMUNICATION FOR RURAL NURSING PRACTICE (NRS4003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will analyse, critique, and reflect on different styles and methods of professional communication used in healthcare. This subject will build on your knowledge of effective teamwork, workplace culture and conflict resolution, and will explore the differences in healthcare communication strategies used by registered nurses, rural health services and human resources. You will develop an advanced level of understanding related to challenging interactions, including the impact that highly developed professional communication skills have on patient and healthcare outcomes.
Learning Outcomes:
- Compare and contrast the professional communication styles that are used in healthcare.
- Appraise and apply the principles of effective workplace culture within written and verbal communications in healthcare.
- Evaluate communication theory and its application in rural healthcare.
- Critically reflect on the influence of your own communication and the relationship this has to your learning needs in providing optimal patient care, interprofessional communication and teamwork in the rural health context.
- Critically analyse and apply conceptual and technical skills which are used to demonstrate professional communication and apply to challenging interactions.

Subject: AGEING IN PLACE IN RURAL COMMUNITIES (NRS4004)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Taking a strengths-based perspective, this subject explores models of care for older people as they transition through home based, acute, sub-acute and residential care in the context of complex financial, regulatory, policy and political frameworks. In this subject you will develop your nursing practice to promote healthy ageing. You will assess, plan, deliver and evaluate person centred nursing care for older adults in rural health care settings. In this subject you will also develop your assessment skills in the management of dementia and delirium, frailty, geriatric syndromes, wound management and palliative care.
Learning Outcomes:
- Coordinate the transition of the older person through acute, sub-acute, community and residential care in the context of regulatory, policy frameworks relevant to rural health.
- Identify the signs of cognitive deterioration and impairment in older people and identify services and evidence based interventions for their care and referral.
- Demonstrate advanced communication skills to maintain positive therapeutic relationships with patients/residents, their significant others, and the multidisciplinary team.
- Apply advanced knowledge, skills and attitudes in the assessment and management of people at the end stages of life.
- Apply principles of wound prevention, wound assesssment and wound management, including the evaluation and referral of patients with non-healing wounds.
