## Master of Management and Master of Public Policy
##### B6064
##### 96 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
The Master of Management and Master of Public Policy offers you a multidisciplinary perspective on management and public policy. This double degree combines your knowledge of contemporary business and management leadership with practical knowledge and skills in policy-making and public sector management. By pursuing this double degree, you will develop into a leader capable of inspiring positive social and organisational change, as well as the effective delivery of public services and programs.

### Structure
Double degree courses include all the features of the component degree courses, except that electives may be reduced or redirected.

Master of Management

The Master of Management is structured in three parts: Part A. Core studies, Part B. Discipline studies and Part C. Elective studies. In the double degree course you complete: 

Part A. Core studies

These units will provide you with a comprehensive understanding of core management principles, contemporary practice and leadership and their impact on multi-discipline decision-making in organisations. These units will develop your capacity as an innovative future-ready professional who is able to apply specialist knowledge to complex management issues.

Part B. Discipline studies

These units develop your capacity as a critical and creative professional who can apply advanced management knowledge to analyse complex organisational issues. You will build the skills to evaluate theories, apply evidence-based practices, and design innovative solutions that enhance leadership, strategy, and sustainable performance in diverse organisational contexts. Selected units within this part are accredited by the Australian HR Institute (AHRI).

Master of Public Policy

The Master of Public Policy is structured in four parts: Part A. Core studies, Part B. Professional practice studies, Part C. Applied studies, and Part D. Specified elective studies. In the double degree course you complete: 

Part A. Core studies 

These studies will introduce you to public policy theory and practice at an advanced graduate level. Throughout these studies, you will gain practical knowledge and skills that will provide a solid foundation for the specialist studies units undertaken in the program.  

Part C. Applied studies

The focus of these studies is professional and project work, focused locally or globally, that can contribute to a portfolio of professional development. In this part of the program you can also undertake higher level research.

Part D. Specified elective studies

The focus of these studies is to develop your expertise in a specialisation: Policy studies or Global studies. These will provide you with the knowledge and skills needed for the capacity to work and communicate across the borders of context or discipline.

You must complete 96 points, of which 48 points are from the Master of Management (including all the requirements in Part A and Part B for the single degree) and 48 points from the Master of Public Policy (including Part A: APG5101 Foundations of public policy, APG5102 Governance, APG5103 Policy analysis and APG5129 Evaluation (24 credit points), Part C (12 credit points) and Part D (12 credit points) for the single degree).

Subject: Foundations of public policy (APG5101)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Understanding public policy is a vital skill in any contemporary organisation. The unit introduces you to the basic principles of how public policy is made, how it emerges from competing interests and debates, and how stakeholders inside and outside government participate in this process. Lessons are drawn from practice in international experience.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply knowledge and skills to a real case of public policy.
- Analyse the core concepts and theories that help explain how public policy is made;
- Evaluate the role of government institutions and other stakeholders in the processes in policy making;
- Critically assess how policy makers contribute to the development of policies, make decisions and choose strategies to implement policy decisions;
Assessment Tasks:
- Case study: 35% (Hurdle: None)
- Reflections: 15% (Hurdle: None)
- Quizzes: 15% (Hurdle: None)
- Case study: 35% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Scheduled activities may include a combination of teacher directed learning, peer directed learning and online engagement.

Subject: Governance (APG5102)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The unit introduces you to public, private and not for profit institutions at international, national and local levels and how they impact upon the governance of communities in a range of international settings. The roles, relationships and networks of state and non-state institutions, and their influence on public policy, will be examined through the prism of international indicators and benchmarks of 'good' and 'bad' governance. The political, economic and regulatory dimensions of governance will be explored utilising contemporary issues in public policy.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate relationships between the public sector, the private sector and civil society and how these can be utilised to foster good governance outcomes;
- Create and develop governance strategies that reflect leadership capacity in cross-cultural competence, effective team engagement and ethical values.
- Analyse and communicate concepts of governance and their application to issues affecting states, international regions and the global community;
Assessment Tasks:
- Report: 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Practical Worksheet: 5% (Hurdle: None)
- In class test: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Case Study: 25% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Scheduled activities may include a combination of teacher directed learning, peer directed learning and online engagement.

Subject: Policy analysis (APG5103)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The unit provides you with understandings of the conceptual and theoretical frameworks for policy analysis which are necessary to present effective policy advice to the highest levels of government. Using real-life examples of public policy, the unit explores the challenges of problem definition, analysing and assessing evidence, choosing policy instruments, developing policy options, and preparing and submitting policy advice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Employ expert knowledge and specialist skills in policy analysis to enable discipline-based solutions relevant to diverse business, professional and public policy communities;
- Communicate policy analysis and options with clarity and purpose.
- Devise and deliver innovative solutions to policy-related challenges employing advanced critical thinking and research skills;
Assessment Tasks:
- Analytical exercise: 45% (Hurdle: None)
- Presentation: 15% (Hurdle: None)
- Policy brief: 25% (Hurdle: None)
- Analytical exercise: 15% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Scheduled activities may include a combination of teacher directed learning, peer directed learning and online engagement.

Subject: Evaluation (APG5129)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Evaluation plays a vital role in providing government ministers, public managers, policy advisers and other interested parties with quality information on the success or otherwise of specific programs. This unit introduces you to program evaluation techniques and how they can be used to generate quality information about program performance. The unit provides you with the knowledge and tools needed to effectively commission and conduct program evaluations. Topics include: identifying issues and formulating questions for effective program evaluation; assessing and monitoring program processes, program outcomes and program impact; measurement issues; and alternative strategies for program evaluation.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop and implement a suitable research design;
- Scope and plan an evaluation study;
- Critically analyse the focus, scope and purpose of evaluation;
- Utilise evaluation research to support evidence-based policy development.
Assessment Tasks:
- Research Proposal: 45% (Hurdle: None)
- Analytical exercise: 25% (Hurdle: None)
- Presentation: 15% (Hurdle: None)
- Report: 15% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. A unit requires on average three/four hours of scheduled activities per week. Scheduled activities may include a combination of teacher directed learning, peer directed learning and online engagement.
