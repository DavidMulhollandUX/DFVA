##### LMIBUS

You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
La Trobe's Master of International Business has been collaboratively designed with industry experts to advance your career and equip you with the skills necessary for thriving in emerging roles worldwide. Throughout this course, you'll not only acquire the expertise to effectively manage multinational corporations and formulate successful global business strategies, but also develop the ability to navigate the intricate international political and economic landscape where businesses operate.

This course encompasses a diverse range of subjects, including international trade, international finance, international corporate governance, international marketing, global human resource management, and international logistics and transportation. As a multidisciplinary program, the Master of International Business ensures that you gain the knowledge and skills demanded by organisations to succeed in the globalised economy.

Throughout your journey in this master's course, you will develop a global mindset, enhance your intercultural competencies, and learn how to effectively connect, communicate, and negotiate across national borders and diverse cultural contexts. The course allows you to tailor your career trajectory by engaging in projects specific to countries, regions, or industries of your preference while enabling you to become a catalyst for change, adept at designing, developing, and implementing strategies that drive sustainability across international business endeavours.

While doing this master's course, you will collaborate closely with internationally renowned researchers, practitioners, and global enterprises. The course includes company visits, real-world industry projects, and valuable networking opportunities. Moreover, the program provides a chance for you to embark on a global study tour, and offers the valuable experience of completing a business work placement within an organisation actively engaged in international business.

Overall, the La Trobe's Master of International Business offers a dynamic and comprehensive educational journey, designed to elevate your career prospects and empower you to excel in the intricate landscape of global business. Through a combination of international exposure, practical experiences, and immersive learning opportunities, you will emerge as a well-rounded professional capable of making a significant impact on the global stage.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Collaborate effectively in multicultural teams to develop solutions to international business problems or challenges.
**2.**
Demonstrate strategic thinking in global business scenarios.
**3.**
Effectively communicate complex international business information and ideas in a variety of forms to a wide range of multicultural audiences.
**4.**
Critically examine the role of international businesses and organisations in supporting the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and how SDGs’ initiatives affect the international business environment.
**5.**
Critically analyse the current and emerging global business environment forces that shape how organisations conduct international business.
**6.**
Critically review and explain the role of the multinational firm in the global market and appraise its handling of transactions in cross-border trade and investments.

### Course features
### Work based learning (placement) requirements
During this course, you will have the opportunity to participate in a Work Based Learning (WBL) placement experience, designed to allow you to extend your formal academic learning beyond the classroom. The WBL experience will provide the opportunity to bring your learning from the university into a work environment and test out your knowledge in a professional real-world environment. Some courses include compulsory WBL subjects, however, we also offer a range of elective WBL subjects, both cross-discipline and subject-specific. La Trobe University will source placements for some subjects, while others will require you to source your own placement. If you are required to source your own placement, dedicated staff will guide and support you through the process. The WBL experience can be undertaken via a core choice subject, which generally involves an application process and Subject Coordinator approval. The location and number of hours undertaken can vary considerably depending on the activity and the discipline area. Please refer to the individual subject entries for more information.
### Work integrated learning opportunities
Industry presentations, Industry informed curriculum, Industry-informed projects, Field trips, Professional simulations
### Career outcomes
After graduation, you could work across a range of industries all around the world.

Possible roles include:

  * Global business development manager
  * Partner with multidisciplinary teams around the world to deliver strategic outcomes for businesses.

  * International business consultant
  * Advise organisations on international strategy, operational improvements, and new market opportunities.

  * International Business Analyst
  * Provide data and evidence-driven insight to assist in the development of innovative approaches for operational and strategic changes.

  * International trade specialist
  * Work in government foreign trade offices to analyse foreign trade and the global economy to maximise trade and investment opportunities.

  * International Sales Manager
  * Maximise sales revenue in existing markets, expanding sales into new territories, and researching trends to develop accurate sales projections.

Overview: Accounting and finance are critical skills for all professionals in the modern global environment. In this subject, you will be provided with an introduction to key concepts, theories and practical applications associated with accounting and finance in diverse organisational contexts. Subject: ECONOMICS FOR GLOBAL LEADERS (BUA4003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Economics impacts on all aspects of business and society. Key individual, organisational and government decisions around the allocation of scarce resources are at the core of economics, and an understanding of economic concepts, frameworks and language can greatly enhance a leader’s capacity to make sound decisions and influence the decisions of those around them. This subject develops students’ knowledge of economic concepts and provides the opportunity to apply this knowledge to real world situations. Students will apply economic concepts and frameworks to current issues of concern for organisations and society. Students will learn how to evaluate and apply critical aspects of economics such as the main market forces of supply and demand; the choices producers make in relation to their costs; revenues and profit; competitive and monopoly market structures and how these affect market prices; the use of game theory to make more strategic economic decisions; measuring a nation's income and the cost of living; the drivers of economic growth and the causes of unemployment.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply economic frameworks to business and social problems for policy and decision-making.
- Construct and present logical and persuasive economic arguments and communicate concepts professionally.
- Research and apply economic concepts to predict how changes in economic conditions may impact individuals, businesses and industries within market structures.
- Critically analyse economic data to explain relationships between economic variables and their impacts on policy, business and individuals.

Subject: INTERNATIONAL FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT (FIN5IFM)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Financial globalisation, a heightened geopolitical environment, economic uncertainties, and increased ESG focus present unique challenges and opportunities for investors and corporations worldwide. International Financial Management develops your conceptual and applied knowledge and skills to manage the critical value drivers of firms operating globally. You will examine the structure and operations of global financial architecture, evaluate the economics, institutions, policies, and uncertainties governing exchange rate behavior, forecast exchange rates, and measure and manage exchange rate risk exposures. You will be trained to critically evaluate the relative merits and risks associated with the strategic financial management decisions of multinational companies, including financing, investment, and asset allocation decisions in a competitive global economic setting.
Learning Outcomes:
- Assess foreign exchange behaviour - exchange rate regimes, exchange rate determination, international parity relations - and their implications for forecasting exchange rates and managing exposures.
- Design and manage strategic financial decisions, including country risk analysis, foreign direct investment, financing, asset allocation, and tax management in international settings to deliver sustainable value.
- Critically evaluate contemporary global financial systems and current issues in international finance that affect the global flow of funds and financial management functions of global firms.

Subject: INTERNATIONAL TRADE (IBU5IBE)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In an increasingly globalised economy, trade and investment decisions made by any country can have significant repercussions for businesses worldwide. Public debates frequently arise concerning this ever-evolving economic landscape, and we must be prepared to engage actively in these changes. Central to these debates is the United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 8, which aims to promote decent work and economic growth. By examining the global economy through this lens, we can better understand how international trade may foster economic growth that benefits everyone. In this subject, we will analyse the complex process of globalisation and investigate the economic factors that shape the environment in which businesses operate and nations interact. We will examine the economic foundations behind the trade and investment decisions made by nations and firms. Through the analysis of international trade models and real-world cases, you will explore critical questions such as: What are the gains from free trade and foreign investment? What determines the trade patterns or investment choices of countries? Are there winners and losers in international trade? Which trade policies benefit my business or the country as a whole?
Learning Outcomes:
- Use economic reasoning to critically evaluate the consequences of international trade, investment, migration and policy interventions to countries' welfare, production specialisation and the distribution of income.
- Analyse the patterns of international trade and the flow of production factors across countries with the effective use of complex data and the creation of key indicators in international economics.
- Apply economic theories to identify and discuss the fundamental features of international trade and the movement of production factors across countries.
- Work effectively in groups to examine how countries integrate to the global economy.

Subject: INTERNATIONAL TRANSPORT AND LOGISTICS (MGT5004)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: When cargo flows and supply chains extend beyond national borders, the complexity of international transport and logistics activities increases. This subject focuses on the global transport of goods, which heavily relies on ships, aircraft, and trains. As a result, more stakeholders become involved, additional documentation is generated and transferred, financial payment risks intensify, and disruptions and uncertainties can impact supply chains. In this subject, you will acquire essential knowledge and develop job-ready skills necessary for roles such as international transport and logistics coordinators and freight forwarders. This includes gaining familiarity with trade and customs policies, understanding documentation requirements for efficient cargo flows and physical distribution, developing the ability to design international transport and logistics plans for various business scenarios, creating recovery plans to address changes and disruptions in international transport and logistics, and effectively communicating information and potential changes to stakeholders. Additionally, the subject explores sustainability concerns, data analytics, and emerging technologies related to digitalisation.
Learning Outcomes:
- Design international transport and logistics plans for efficient, profitable, sustainable and resilient supply chains.
- Exercise professional judgement and evaluate international logistics management practices for a collaborative supply chain.
- Critically analyse the impact of international transport and logistics on a company's performance and collaborations within global supply chains.
- Critically analyse the implications of trade policies, international conventions, sustainability commitments, and digitalisation on global transport, logistics, and supply chains.
