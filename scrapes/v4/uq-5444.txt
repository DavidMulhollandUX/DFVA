## Prerequisites
A bachelor's degree (or equivalent), plus current coaching qualifications endorsed by a recognised sporting body (e.g. a national sporting organisation) or equivalent and one year's coaching experience.
You must have a grade point average (GPA) of 4.0 on a 7-point scale in your previous qualification.
OR
A current coaching qualification endorsed by a recognised sporting body (e.g. a national sporting organisation) or equivalent, and five years coaching/high performance sporting experience, comprising--
(a) 150 contact hours per year, and
(b) 450 hours per year in planning, preparation, self-reflection and learning, and continuing professional development.
High performance sporting experience
means experience in performance sport contexts that are primarily focused on peak performances at major competitions. These contexts will typically be characterised by intensive preparation over prolonged periods involving extensive intervention and interpersonal contact.
Applicants who identify as Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander who meet the minimum entry requirements for this program will be offered a place. On admission to the program, you will need to provide evidence of your Aboriginal and/or Torres Strait Islander status.

Subject: Sport Science (Anatomy, Biomechanics, Physiology) (PHYL6000)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Knowledge and understanding of the anatomy, biomechanics and physiology as it applies to the human body and its application to sports performance, training and injury. Application of principles and skills in understanding and assessing human performance in sports settings.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand and apply bisic principles of human training
- Understand the relevant anatomy of the musculoskeletal system and its general function.
- Understand how the physical structure of the musculoskeletal system is closely linked to the physical function.
- Understand basic kinematics as they apply to sporting movements.
- Understand basic kinetics as they apply to performance and injury in sport.
- Understand how kinematic and kinetic information enables more in-depth analysis of sporting movements.
- Develop and apply anatomical and biomechanical knowledge to analyse technique, training or injury mechanics associated with a particular sport.
- Apply physiological principles to understand methods that are typically used to assess fitness and performance in athletes.
- Apply physiological principles to develop and evaluate appropriate training programs for athletes in specific sports.
- Apply training and periodization principles to improve sport and exercise performance

- Online Quiz Series Online: 30% (3 x quizzes worth 10% each)% (Hurdle: False)
- Anatomy and Biomechanics Assignment Online: 35%% (Hurdle: False)
- Discussion Board Interaction Online: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Physiology Assignment Online: 25%% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Movement and Performance Analysis (SPCG6001)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course offers coaches with a detailed insight to the theory underpinning skill learning in sport, emphasising its practical importance in developing, analysing and periodising effective learning environments.
Learning Outcomes:
- Performance Analysis - Explain the key principles of the various movement analysis methods relevant to coaching and human movement studies
- Performance Analysis - Measure and analyse movement data across a range of human movement disciplines using appropriate measurement and statistical techniques
- Performance Analysis - Apply knowledge of how performance analysis can be used in the development of reliable and valid training and testing programs in coaching and human movement studies.
- Movement Analysis - Explain the key principles of the various movement analysis methods relevant to coaching and human movement studies
- Movement Analysis - Measure and analyse movement data across a range of human movement disciplines using appropriate measurement and statistical techniques
- Acquisition of Skills - Describe how motor skills are learnt and implement various coaching methods to promote the creation of new motor pathways to enhance performance.
- Provision of Feedback - Apply knowledge about movement analysis as part of specific developmental and learning stages to the design and prescription of training.
Assessment Tasks:
- Multiple Choice Quiz Online: 30% (3 x MCQ each worth 10%)% (Hurdle: False)

- Case Report 2: 30%% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Applied Sport Psychology for Coaches (SPCG6002)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Primarily concerned with how psychological factors influence performance in elite sport. This course will focus on theories, principles, and applications of sport psychology that can be utilised by coaches to enhance athletes, experiences and performance. Coaches will gain an understanding of psycho-social factors (e.g., personality, motivation, emotions, leadership) that can be applied to how they understand their athlete(s); as well as providing opportunity for self-reflection and enhanced understanding of themselves as coaches and their coaching practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate the ability to clearly convey ideas about psychological concepts/constructs and theories related to applied practice, especially relevant to elite performance in sport.
- Demonstrate the ability to critically reflect upon research examining psychological influences on performance in sport settings.
- Demonstrate the ability to retrieve, analyse, synthesise and critically evaluate information.
Assessment Tasks:
- So what? Theory and evidence-based practice Online: 50% 12.5% per summary% (Hurdle: False)
- Reflective Essay Online: 50%% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Quality Coaching: Understanding, Informing and Advancing Practice (SPCG6027)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course aims to broaden your knowledge and understanding of coaching work. You will be provided with opportunities to reflect upon and develop your coaching practices. The course is based around a series of seminars focused on an increased awareness and understanding of (i) what coaching is, (ii) the educational dimensions of coaching work, (iii) coaches as learners in their own right, and (iv) the ways in which coaching work is evaluated.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate an awareness and understanding of the concept of quality coaching and the associated pedagogical aspects of coaches work;
- Demonstrate an awareness and understanding of coaches roles in the learning of athletes;
- Demonstrate an awareness and understanding of the centrality of learning to coaching practice.
