## Graduate Certificate in Strength and Conditioning - Melbourne (Bundoora)
##### HC007
##### 60 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
If you’ve always wanted to work with athletes or you’re a current practitioner who’d like to enhance your skills, then La Trobe’s Graduate Certificate in Strength and Conditioning is developed in collaboration with the Australian Strength and Conditioning Association (ASCA), so you’ll learn the latest best practice.

You'll enhance your career as you gain the latest skills and knowledge in strength, speed and endurance. Study with expert academics and industry leaders as you learn how to work with elite, professional and amateur athletes, including diverse athlete populations such as para-athletes and athletes of different ages, backgrounds and identities.

Located in La Trobe Sports Park, you’ll benefit from online learning and in-person learning at La Trobe’s premier sport and exercise facilities in Melbourne using the latest technology and research facilities, including our strength and conditioning, biomechanics, physiology and performance analysis labs.

Our academic staff have a wealth of recent industry experience and extensive applied credentials, providing direct access to a network of elite sport collaborators. You'll graduate with the skills to take your coaching career into sporting organisations locally and around the world, or as a direct pathway into La Trobe’s Graduate Diploma or Master of Strength and Conditioning.

You’ll learn:

  * Assessment and development
  *   * Learn how to design and implement training plans to improve the physical characteristics of athletes in preparation for competition.

  * Training and monitoring
  *   * Identify and monitor the key determinants of sport-specific performance and how overall training workload is manipulated over time.

  * Coaching
  *   * Learn how to develop meaningful relationships with the athletes you work with and understand how to work with other stakeholders who can help improve their performance.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Communicate and interact effectively with a range of stakeholders using appropriate professional communication methods.
**2.**
Evaluate own professional practice to plan and implement appropriate continuing development activities.
**3.**
Analyse and interpret evidence to support informed decisions that improve aspects of human physical performance.
**4.**
Apply and adapt professional knowledge and skills, independently or within a team, in diverse strength and conditioning contexts to meet defined standards of practice.

### Career outcomes
Graduates will be equipped to work with both elite and amateur athletes, supporting their performance goals across a range of settings. Career opportunities include roles within:

  * professional sports teams
  * performance training organisations
  * sporting academies and institutes
  * sport-focused schools
  * private practice
  * multidisciplinary health and performance environments.

This course also serves as a direct pathway into the Graduate Diploma in Strength and Conditioning or Master of Strength and Conditioning, offering a structured route for further professional and academic advancement.

Subject: DEVELOPING STRENGTH AND POWER (SPO4001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will be provided with the tools needed to design and monitor the impact of periodised strength training plans to improve sports performance across a range of contexts. This subject will also give you the opportunity to perform and coach weightlifting and powerlifting actions in addition to their variants. Further, you will explore the acute and chronic response to strength training programs. Advanced methods of resistance training will also be discussed. This subject includes professional learning, practical experiences or portfolio activities which contribute to the completion of the Silver Career Ready AdvantageTM award.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain and critique the effects of manipulating strength training program variables on the acute and chronic physiological responses to training.
- Design and implement methods of testing strength qualities to improve athletic performance
- Perform and evaluate the correct technique for weightlifting and powerlifting actions, in addition to their variations.
- Design and critique periodised resistance training programs to improve sport performance across a range of sporting contexts for developing strength qualities.
- Justify the integration of advanced methods of resistance training into training programs and demonstrate its effects.

Subject: DEVELOPING SPEED, AGILITY AND ENDURANCE (SPO4002)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will explore, both practically and theoretically, the various speed and endurance qualities along with change of direction and agility. You will learn to design and implement training plans to improve and assess speed, agility and endurance performance across several sporting contexts. You will engage in practical scenarios where you will learn to coach the mechanics of running and apply training tasks to improve speed, agility and endurance performance.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critique the effects of manipulating speed, agility and endurance program variables on the acute and chronic physiological responses to training.
- Perform and evaluate the correct technique for sprinting and related actions
- Design and implement methods of testing forms of agility, speed and endurance and to improve athletic performance
- Explain advanced methods of developing speed, agility and endurance, and justify how they can be integrated into the training program.
- Design and critique periodised training programs to improve speed, agility and endurance across a range of sporting contexts.

Subject: PRINCIPLES OF TRAINING AND MONITORING  (SPO4003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will be given the opportunity to develop advanced knowledge in relation to how overall training workload is manipulated over time to achieve competition goals. You will also learn to identify and monitor the key determinants of sport-specific performance and the role and scope of practice of the strength and conditioning coach. This subject includes professional learning, practical experiences or portfolio activities which contribute to the completion of the Career Ready AdvantageTM award.
Learning Outcomes:
- Outline the scope of practice and ethical responsibilities for the strength and conditioning coach including the provision nutritional recommendations to athletes
- Organise overall training workload across range of sporting contexts and monitor athlete responses
- Critique, create and integrate flexibility and mobility programs for athletes
- Classify the physical characteristics and determinants of sport-specific performance and understand the role of the strength and conditioning coach in this process
- Identify and benchmark aspects of sports performance

Subject: COACHING PRINCIPLES, PRACTICE AND PROCESSES (SPO4004)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will be given the opportunity to develop interpersonal, intrapersonal and professional knowledge in relation to coaching pedagogy. You will also learn key skills related to reflexivity, self-regulation and athlete-centred coaching in relation to your role as a coach. The purpose of this subject is to enable you to be able to develop meaningful relationships with the athletes you work with and understand the broader stakeholder engagement required to improve their performance and participation. This subject includes professional learning, practical experiences or portfolio activities which contribute to the completion of the Silver Career Ready AdvantageTM award.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe and critique own coaching philosophy and the impact this has on their athletes
- Effectively deploy critical incident analysis to inform and improve holistic athlete development practice
- Triage theoretical constructs that are fit for purpose and apply into sport specific practice to support a range of athletes
- Mediate effectively between key stakeholders responsible for supporting athlete performance
- Critically reflect on own and other’s coaching expertise
