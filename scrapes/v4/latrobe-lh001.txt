## Bachelor of Laws (Honours) - Melbourne (Bundoora)
##### LH001
##### 480 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
Stand out with a law degree committed to social impact and community engagement. Our dedicated four-year Law (Honours) degree gives you a strong academic and professional foundation with the ability to match your legal career to your area of interest through a range of specialised electives.

Put your legal skills to the test with hands-on practical learning opportunities through our Legal Internship Program, work-based learning, the Law Clinic, overseas study programs, and leading mooting program.

You'll also apply your training into the real world with internships, clerkships, and practical placements at legal firms, government bodies, and community legal centres – giving you enhanced employability and professional readiness through our network of industry and community partnerships.

Combine intellectual and skills-based legal training with a progressive approach to law. With a strong focus on practical skills like dispute resolution, criminal procedure, and contract law, you'll learn what it is like to work on real cases and develop the professional skills to meet the demands of legal practice.

By studying La Trobe's Bachelor of Laws (Honours), you will:

  * develop key skills in negotiation and examine the processes of arbitration, conciliation and mediation in private and commercial disputes
  * build your understanding of the criminal justice system, apply criminal law to real-world scenarios and provide legal advice
  * apply the legal principles governing the creation, interpretation and termination of a contract
  * hone your skills in critical thinking, research, writing and teamwork, then apply them with work-integrated learning opportunities in community legal centres, law firms and government agencies.
  * join our leading moot court program, with opportunities to take part in competitions both nationally and internationally.
  * benefit from a supportive learning environment that prioritises student wellbeing and academic success.
  * complete the subjects required by the Victorian Legal Admissions Board (VLAB) to meet the requirements for admission to the legal profession in Victoria.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Coherently communicate legal knowledge, research, and analysis using a variety of modes to both legal and non-legal actors.
**2.**
Critically analyse, synthesise relevant legal sources and conduct research independently using technologies where appropriate.
**3.**
Demonstrate awareness of and critically reflect on lawyers' professional and ethical responsibilities and the importance of integrity and service in legal analysis and practice.
**4.**
Apply analytical and professional judgement in identifying legal issues, critically evaluating legal doctrine and providing innovative solutions to complex legal problems.
**5.**
Demonstrate appropriate understanding and competence in the prescribed areas of knowledge required for admission into legal practice in Victoria, Australia, as determined by the Victorian Legal Admissions Board.
**6.**
Demonstrate awareness of and critically reflect on the historical, social, economic and cultural contexts and implications of laws and the role of lawyers in promoting justice and equity.
**7.**
Demonstrate advanced knowledge of Australian legal systems, institutions and procedures, including fundamental legal principles and concepts in substantive areas of law.
**8.**
Demonstrate  an advanced capacity to work independently, self-manage workload, foster personal and professional development, and collaborate effectively in a team environment.

### Work based learning (placement) requirements
Elective placement opportunities (Work Based Learning)
During this course, you will have the opportunity to participate in a Work Based Learning (WBL) placement experience, designed to allow you to extend your formal academic learning beyond the classroom. The WBL experience will provide the opportunity to bring your learning from the university into a work environment and test out your knowledge in a professional real-world environment. Some courses include compulsory WBL subjects, however, we also offer a range of elective WBL subjects, both cross-discipline and subject-specific. La Trobe University will source placements for some subjects, while others will require you to source your own placement. If you are required to source your own placement, dedicated staff will guide and support you through the process. Elective WBL subjects generally involve an application process and Subject Coordinator approval. The location and number of hours undertaken can vary considerably depending on the activity and the discipline area. Please refer to the individual subject entries for more information. Regional students are eligible to access additional support under the Regional Reward program

Possible roles include:

Subject: PRINCIPLES OF CRIMINAL LAW (LAW1CRL)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will examine key aspects of the substantive criminal law. You will analyse the basic concepts and principles in the criminal law such as the onus and standard of proof, evidentiary and persuasive burdens, objective and subjective tests, and principles of offence classification. Learning Outcomes:
- Work co-operatively in small teams to produce joint work and develop a reflective approach to learning.
- Demonstrate well-developed skills in problem analysis, logical argumentation, effective communication, and the interpretation of statutory provisions and case law.
- Compose a legal opinion, involving the identification and application of relevant laws, in answer to problems raising a range of criminal law issues
- Identify and apply the structure of a range of criminal offences and their underlying general principles.

Subject: PRINCIPLES OF PUBLIC LAW (LAW1PPL)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will explore the fundamental principles of public law in Australian and foreign jurisdictions, with a focus on human rights. 'Public law' can be defined as the body of legal rules that govern the relationship between the State and its citizens and the relationship between the institutions of the State (i.e. the legislative, executive, and judicial arms of government). Public law concerns itself with the nature, sources, and extent of State power to ensure that power exercised by the State is authorised and legitimate. Within this subject, there is also a focus on industry-informed career development learning activities to support setting goals for personal and professional development.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and critique scholarly materials discussing the principles of public law.
- Develop career management strategies, including the ability to work collaboratively in a group
- Critically discuss contemporary public law issues with a focus on human rights in Australian and foreign jurisdictions.
- Compose arguments based on, and accurately cite, scholarly materials in a legal essay.
- Locate, by means of legal research, scholarly materials discussing the principles of public law in Australian and foreign jurisdictions.

Subject: PRINCIPLES OF CONSTITUTIONAL LAW (LAW2CSL)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will acquire a general knowledge of federal and state constitutional law in Australia and of the principles of constitutional interpretation. Topics covered in this subject include the sources and limits of Commonwealth and state legislative power, examples of specific Commonwealth powers under the Commonwealth Constitution, and restrictions on Commonwealth power, such as Chapter III judicial power and express and implied constitutional rights and freedoms. You will also learn how constitutional law deals with federal/state relations, including the interpretative approaches that apply where federal and state laws are inconsistent. This subject will develop your constitutional problem-solving skills and your historical and theoretical understanding of Australian constitutionalism.
Learning Outcomes:
- Advise a client on their legal position with respect to a factual situation, where the problem calls for the use of constitutional method and the application of relevant constitutional law.

Subject: CONTRACT LAW A (LAW2CTA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will be introduced to the common law of contract in Australia. After considering the basic theory and history of private law and contractual obligations, you will use case law to analyse and apply the legal principles on contract formation: agreement, consideration, intention, capacity and formalities. We then consider vitiating factors: the doctrines around setting aside a contract that has not been properly formed under common law and statute (eg because of duress, undue influence). You will use case law to analyse and apply the legal principles governing the creation of a contract. This involves the development of techniques to write case analysis, for application of legal rules of contract to fact situations, and for communication of legal opinion to advise a range of audiences. This subject, in combination with Contract Law B, meets the requirements of the Victorian Legal Admissions Board to practice in Victoria in the area of Law of Contracts
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify relevant common law principles governing contracts as they apply to a given fact situation
- Identify the outcomes arising from alternative arguments and argue strategically on the basis of the needs of a client in a fictional scenario
- Demonstrate an understanding of the language of Contract Law and how to use it in written and oral communications
- Demonstrate general legal analysis skills and identify and locate Australian legal information in a range of primary and secondary sources
- Write a legal analysis and opinion on a contractual problem
Workload:

Subject: CONTRACT LAW B (LAW2CTB)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject examines the interpretation, discharge and remedies around contracts. Whereas Contract A examined the formation and setting aside of contracts, this unit starts from there being a valid contract between the parties, and examines how to interpret that contract, the interaction of consumer law and contract terms, and rights around termination. We then consider contractual remedies and end with current issues in the law of contract. This subject reinforces skills in case law and statutory analysis. This subject, in combination with Contract Law A, meets the requirements of the Victorian Legal Admissions Board to practice in Victoria in the area of Law of Contracts.
Learning Outcomes:
- Interpret statutory provisions and associated case law relating to contracts.
- Identify the outcomes arising from alternative arguments and argue strategically on the basis of the needs of a client in a fictional scenario, including identification of the opportunities and risks of these alternative arguments.
- Analyse the social, economic and ethical context within which the law relating to commercial and consumer contracts operates.
- Analyse common law principles at common law and in equity.
- Use legal problem-solving skills to compose a legal opinion which applies the appropriate common law and statutory principles to factual scenarios.

Subject: PRINCIPLES OF TORT LAW (LAW2TOT)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject introduces you to the principles of tort law. Tort law provides a private, civil law action in pursuit of reparation (normally in the form of a monetary award of damages) as compensation for harm inflicted by another, with respect to one of a number of recognised interests of a personal and economic nature. In modern society, most of these interests are vindicated by the pivotal action in negligence, hence the majority of attention is devoted to an understanding of that tort. This subject will also introduce you to other torts including nuisance and trespass.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and explain case-based and statutory tortious principles.
- Apply case-based and statutory tortious principles to arrive at a solution to a problem raised in a given fact situation.
- Demonstrate an understanding of the language of tort law and its appropriate use.
- Demonstrate an ability to write effectively using the principles of plain legal English.

Subject: PRINCIPLES OF EQUITY AND TRUSTS (LAW4003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will be introduced to the doctrines and institutions of equity. Approximately half the subject is about trusts: what they are, what they are used for, how to create them, how to administer them, and how to clean up when things go wrong. Other topics covered are fiduciary relationships, confidentiality, and equitable remedies. Together these topics make up "equity", which is often treated as different from other law. You will explore equity by using equitable principles to solve problems and will learn research skills that empower you to find answers to complex problems yourself.
Learning Outcomes:
- Effectively structure and coherently communicate a legal opinion to a standard which accords with the professional conventions of legal writing
- Formulate arguments that give legal meaning to people's interactions and review the persuasiveness of those arguments with reference to equitable principles and legal sources.
- Apply advanced legal research skills to identify and analyse the statutory provisions and case law as they relate to a specific factual scenario in equity.
