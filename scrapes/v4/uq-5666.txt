## Career Opportunities & Employment Outcomes
Career opportunities vary according to your specialty.
Counselling Psychology
Help individuals, families and groups in areas related to personal well-being, interpersonal relationships, work, recreation and health in both acute and chronic life crises.
Health Psychology
You can work on health promotion campaigns with the government and NGOs, or work with clients in hospital settings or private practice to assist clients who may have chronic pain, chronic disease or require palliative care.
Sport and Exercise Psychology
You can work in sports academies such as the Australian Institute of Sport as a psychologist for weight management programs, sport development officers or run your own practice with a focus on sport and exercise psychology.
For more information about careers visit www.psychology.org.au.

Subject: Assessment in Psychological Practice (PSYC7112)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Aims to familiarise students with the practical details of psychological test administration, interpretation and report writing relating to most commonly presenting assessment questions in clinical psychology. Hypothesis testing approach to clinical assessment is stressed. Students expected to achieve competence in administration and interpretation of limited number of commonly used assessment devices.
Learning Outcomes:
- Competently administer the WISC-V and WAIS-5 batteries.
- Effectively interpret test results and report these results in an accurate and meaningful way (e.g., report writing and providing verbal feedback).
- Understand the purpose, administration, and interpretation of a variety of psychological and neuropsychological tests designed for children, adolescents, adults and older adults
- Have working knowledge of ethical and dissemination issues in psychological testing
Assessment Tasks:
- WAIS-5/WISC-V Test Administration Competency Hurdle: 40%% (Hurdle: True)
- WISC-V/WAIS-5 Report and Video Feedback Session Hurdle: 60%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Workshop 8 Hours/ Week Lecture 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: Adult Psychopathology (PSYC7113)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides an exploration of psychological disorders, with a strong focus on diagnostic systems, assessment methods, and understanding of treatment approaches. Through both theoretical and applied learning, students will develop a thorough and critical understanding of widely used classificatory systems, and gain hands-on experience in clinical interviewing, mental status examinations, and diagnostic assessments when working with adults. Special emphasis is placed on the biopsychosocial approach to diagnosis, case formulation and treatment planning, considering developmental, cultural, and ethical factors. Throughout the course, students will engage in critical analysis of the research evidence-base, fostering a reflexive and evidence-informed approach to their professional development.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply and critically evaluate diagnostic classification systems and their role in diagnosing psychological disorders in adults.
- Demonstrate competency in clinical assessment techniques.
- Apply biopsychosocial approaches to diagnostic case formulation and evidence-based interventions.
- Understand the psychosocial and pharmacological management for common psychological disorders.
- Understand how to apply ethical principles, cultural considerations when working with diverse populations, and reflective/reflexive practice to formulation and communication of clinical findings in multidisciplinary team settings (relevant to accurate documentation, case report writing, and verbal presentation of case summaries).
- Understand the impact of inequitable access to healthcare from a human rights perspective and its implications for working with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples.
- Critically engage with the research evidence base to inform clinical decision-making and competence in all (including digital) environments to foster ongoing professional development in psychological practice.
Assessment Tasks:
- Role play: Client Descriptions, Formulations and Reflection Hurdle: Pass/Fail Recycle% (Hurdle: True)
- Written Case Study Report: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Indigenous Health Modules and Reflection: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 45%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Psychological Skills & Evidence-Based Therapies (PSYC7114)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This foundational course introduces students to evidence-based therapeutic approaches and the core psychological skills needed to apply them. It covers fundamental concepts of intake and risk assessment, case planning and treatment, and covers Cognitive Behaviour Therapy (CBT), Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), trauma-informed care, and Motivational Interviewing. Students will develop key therapeutic skills for case formulation, risk assessment, and intervention planning while integrating research evidence into psychological practice. Emphasis in working across diverse populations, including Indigenous peoples, and considering the social determinants of mental health. Through theoretical learning, case studies, observation, modelling, role-playing, and practical applications, students will gain competencies and a strong foundation in evidence-based psychological practices.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the foundations of evidence-based therapy and demonstrate knowledge of core principles in psychological interventions, that include face to face and digital applications
- Develop therapeutic skills and apply foundational therapeutic strategies in practice
- Perform psychological risk assessments: assess and manage suicide risk and other psychological risks effectively.
- Develop structured case formulations and treatment plans using evidence-based principles
- Engage in self-reflection for professional practice and self-care
- Apply culturally responsive practices in their work with clients from culturally and linguistically diverse and Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander backgrounds.
Assessment Tasks:
- Intake interview and self-reflection Hurdle: Pass/Recycle/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- Risk Assessment Competency Hurdle In-person: 20%% (Hurdle: True)
- Skill Demonstration and Clinical Reasoning Competency Hurdle: 50% Each component is weighted 25%% (Hurdle: True)
- Self-Practice/Self-reflection Tasks Hurdle: 30%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Workshop 3 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week Workshop 8 Hours/ Semester

Subject: Ethical Practice and Research Approaches (PSYC7231)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The purpose of this course is to familiarise students with relevant codes of ethics and to examine ethical and professional issues and standards related to the practice of psychology within the areas of Clinical Psychology, Clinical Neuropsychology, Counselling Psychology, Health Psychology, and Sport and Exercise Psychology. The course also aims to examine a range of research approaches and issues and to train students to communicate about their research, both in terms of presenting papers on their thesis projects, and in writing research documents. A combined academic and practical approach will be adopted
Learning Outcomes:
- Know and understand the legislative requirements and obligations of being a psychologist
- Have a good knowledge of the APS Code of Ethics and the associated APS Guidelines
- Demonstrate ethical analysis and decision-making in relation to clients, colleagues, the profession of psychology and other health professionals
- Apply the theoretical principles of the codes of practice to daily situations arising in psychology practice
- Understand the key factors to be considered in research design
- Identify potential ethical issues in a research context
- Formulate specific research questions
- Recognise and reflect on interprofessional practice skills including role clarification, team functioning, interprofessional communication, conflict management, collaborative leadership, and client-centred care.
Assessment Tasks:
- GEMS IPCP Curriculum Assessment Hurdle Team or group-based In-person: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- Workshop Preparation Hurdle: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- Peer Supervision Case Presentation Hurdle: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- Research Proposal Presentation Hurdle: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- Competency Assessment - The Ethical Clinic Hurdle: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Workshop 4 Hours/ Week Tutorial 3 Hours/ One Off 2W

Subject: Interpersonal Skills in Psychology (PSYC7801)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course will introduce students to the basic foundational knowledge and skills of counselling. Students will be introduced to the various foundational counselling skills through direct teaching and demonstrations as well as being given supervised practice in the performance of these skills and their integration into a respectful approach to counselling. A link between skills and theoretical underpinnings is an important part of the course. It encourages the student to consider the balance between the science and the practice of psychology, and the value of interviewing skills in the the process of assessment and case formulation. A satisfactory level of proficiency is expected before students can pass this course.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the underlying biological, social and intra-personal aspects that influence the use of interpersonal skills in counselling.
- Explore the various forms of interpersonal communication that can exist within a counselling relationship.
- Develop foundational counselling skills that will enhance the effective of the counselling relationship and enable therapeutic action.
- Examine the significance of the therapeutic relationship and its impact on clinical outcomes as evidenced in the literature.
- Demonstrate the establishment of an effective therapeutic counselling encounter.
- Critically reflect on the influence of culture and diversity on the counselling process.
- Appraise the suitability of difference therapy modes of delivery for different contexts.
Assessment Tasks:
- Tutorial and Reflections: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Submission of Video and Commentary: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
- Competency Interview: 25% Pass/Fail (hurdle)% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week 2L1T

Subject: Psych Plcmnt 1 (PSYC7810)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Supervised placement course to develop professional skills in applied psychology. Theories and techniques are applied to build core competencies as a psychologist.
Learning Outcomes:
- Applies and builds scientific knowledge of psychology to inform safe and effective practice Demonstrates foundational knowledge of psychological theories, research methods, and evidence-based practice. Critically evaluates scientific literature, research principles, and culturally informed practice to guide clinical decision-making and professional practice.
- Practises ethically and professionally Takes responsibility for professional conduct aligned with ethical standards and legal requirements. Practises within boundaries of competence, manages risks appropriately, and seeks consultation when needed. Demonstrates digital competence.
- Exercises professional reflexivity, purposeful and deliberate practice, and self-care Engages in critical self-reflection regarding one’s own culture, values, biases, and their impact on practice. Actively identifies areas for professional development and maintains self-care to sustain effective functioning.
- Conducts psychological assessments Selects, administers, and interprets culturally appropriate assessment methods across the lifespan. Collaboratively develops goals with clients, formulates results, and provides meaningful feedback based on results.
- Conducts psychological interventions Implements evidence-based, culturally safe interventions appropriate to client needs and context. Outlines risks and benefits to clients. Regularly evaluates intervention effectiveness and adjusts approaches based on client progress and feedback.
- Communicates and relates to others effectively and appropriately Establishes and maintains professional, safe, respectful relationships with clients, colleagues, and stakeholders. Communicates clearly using appropriate modes of delivery (includes digital) and works collaboratively with other professionals.
- Demonstrates a health equity and human rights approach when working with people from diverse groups Works inclusively and respectfully with people from diverse backgrounds, recognising intersecting forms of diversity. Understands and applies cultural responsiveness, trauma-informed care, and adapts practice to meet diverse client needs.
- Demonstrates a health equity and human rights approach when working with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples, families and communities Understands the historical and ongoing impacts of colonisation and applies culturally safe, trauma-informed practice. Works in partnership with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples, respecting self-determination and consulting appropriately.
Assessment Tasks:
- Placement Contract Hurdle: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- Mid-Point Review Hurdle: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- Professional Development Log Hurdle: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- End-Point Review Hurdle: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- Placement Folio Hurdle: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: 20 Contact hours

Subject: Master of Psychology Dissertation (PSYC7811)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The Master of Psychology Dissertation is an essential part of the MPsych program, developing skills in research design, implementation and reporting. Each student's topic must be approved by the program director and should be field-based. Students enrol in PSYC7811 for 2 consecutive semesters.
Learning Outcomes:
- Design, plan, execute, analyse, and report on a determined research question
- Demonstrate: originality; critical thought; capacity to undertake independent research; logical interpretation of data
- Recognize and accommodate ethical practice within research
- Determine, and express, the theoretical and applied implications of the research
- Demonstrate insight that allows for informed judgements about the limitations of ones own work
- To communicate their research planning and findings to appropriate audiences
- Participate in a constructive manner in the process of research supervision
- Recognise and demonstrate interprofessional practice skills including role clarification, team functioning, interprofessional communication, conflict management, collaborative leadership and client-centred care
