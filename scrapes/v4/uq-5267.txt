Physiotherapists assist people with a range of injuries and impairments across the lifespan; from office workers with stiff necks and sore backs, elite athletes with chronic health issues, children with developmental delays, people with heart and lung conditions, and those who have had neurological injury. Whatever the cause of pain or impairment, physiotherapists help to get people moving. They are key health professionals in the prevention of injury and disease. Study the Master of Physiotherapy Studies at UQ and you will learn from award-winning teachers, researchers and clinical educators who are among the best in Australia. This program will give you the theoretical knowledge, professional attributes, and clinical and research skill necessary for a career in physiotherapy. Be a leader in your field, gain an internationally renowned qualification, and become a skilled healthcare professional who helps people improve their physical capacity.

Subject: Anatomical Basis of Human Movement (ANAT1005)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Integrated course in systematic gross anatomy basic to further study in field of human movement. Introduction to musculoskeletal system, neuroanatomy & all visceral systems.
Learning Outcomes:
- ANATOMICAL TERMINOLOGY: Communicate effectively using correct anatomical terminology.
- ANATOMICAL IDENTIFICATION: Identify the principal components, relevant anatomical features and relationships of each of the major human organ systems on real human anatomical (cadaveric) specimens.
- ANATOMICAL STRUCTURE: Describe the structure of the tissues and organs that constitute each major human organ system.
- ANATOMICAL RELATIONSHIPS: Summarise the relationships between the various organ systems.
- ANATOMICAL ORGAN FUNCTION: Define the principal function/s of each major human organ system.
- FORM-FUNCTION INTERDEPENDENCE: Apply the concept of interdependent 'form and function' to illustrate how the structure of an organ system accomplishes its functions.
- STRUCTURAL & FUNCTIONAL CHANGE: Demonstrate the ability to predict some functional deficits that may occur as a result of altered structure.
Assessment Tasks:
- Online Quizzes Online: 18%% (Hurdle: False)
- In-Semester Exam (Practical) Identity Verified In-person: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- In-Semester Exam (Theory) Identity Verified In-person: 28%% (Hurdle: False)
- Practice Spotter Tests (minimum in-class participation requirement) Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- End of Semester Exam Identity Verified In-person Online: 34%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 3 Hours/ Week Practical 4 Hours/ Week

Subject: Functional Musculoskeletal Anatomy - Human Movement (ANAT2029)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides a broad overview of human functional anatomy with a focus on the muscular and skeletal systems. Topics include i) tissue organization and mechanical properties; ii) integration of the muscular, skeletal & nervous systems for movement; and iii) the influence of maturation, ageing, exercise, immobilisation and injury on components of the musculoskeletal system.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify musculoskeletal structures and peripheral nerves on human cadaveric specimens and/or images.
- Describe the relationship between the anatomy and function of the skeletal, muscular and nervous systems in the various regions of the human body.
- Relate the biomechanics of the musculoskeletal system, at the micro and macro levels of organisation, to human movement and mechanisms of injury.
- Explain how skeletal and musculotendinous anatomy is affected by exercise, immobilisation, disease, and aging.
- Integrate knowledge of the interdependence of normal anatomy and function to predict and explain functional deficits resulting from altered anatomy.
Assessment Tasks:
- Tissue Biomechanics quizzes Online: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Tissue biomechanics exam Identity Verified In-person Online: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Regional Anatomy - Upper limb Assessment Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Lower limb and Trunk assessment Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 3 Hours/ Week Practical 1.5 Hours/ Week

Subject: Systems Physiology (BIOM2012)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Students will develop an understanding of how the major organ systems of the body perform their normal functions & how these are regulated & integrated in order to maintain homeostasis.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe physiological mechanisms that support the functions of the major organ systems in the human body.
- Explain how nervous and endocrine signals are essential to whole-body physiological responses.
- Identify normal physiological responses and distinguish them from abnormal responses associated with human disease.
- Investigate how the human body responds to challenges and attempts to maintain homeostasis.
- Perform key observational and experimental techniques in human physiology, including acquisition and analysis of quantitative data.
- Design and implement a physiological experiment, and critically analyse and communicate its findings, with supporting evidence from the scientific literature.
- Reflect on your learning and skill development in scientific methods and communication
- Work and learn collaboratively, capitalising on the diverse abilities, backgrounds and perspectives within the group
Assessment Tasks:
- Laboratory Assessment Series: Hypothesis, Outline & Bibliography (group work) In-person: 4%% (Hurdle: False)
- Laboratory Assessment Series: Experiment Design Proposal (group work) Team or group-based In-person: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Laboratory Assessment Series: Meta-learning task 1 (individual task) Online: 3%% (Hurdle: False)
- Laboratory Assessment Series: Lab Report (individual task) Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Laboratory Assessment Series: Meta-learning task 2 (individual) Online: 3%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person Online: 60%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 3 Hours/ Week Contact 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: Physiology for Human Movement Studies (PHYL1007)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course extends students' basic understanding of cells & organisms to the level of systems physiology & pharmacology with an emphasis on the integrated regulation of the human body.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain and demonstrate understanding of major human physiological systems through analysis and interpretation.
- Recognise the functions and needs of major tissues, organs and systems.
- Integrate molecular mechanisms with homeostatic mechanisms at the systems level.
- Apply physiological principles to practical aspects of Human Movement Studies.
- Evaluate abnormal physiological functions and infer underlying disruptions to healthy physiological functioning.
- Reflect effectively on your learning in biomedical science.
Assessment Tasks:
- Meta-learning Tasks Hurdle Online: 10% (3.33% each)% (Hurdle: True)
- Workshop Quizzes Identity Verified In-person Online: 50% (10% each - best 5 scores out of 7 quizzes available count towards 50%)% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Exam Identity Verified In-person Online: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 3 Hours/ Week Workshop 2 Hours/ Fortnight Practical 3 Hours/ Month
