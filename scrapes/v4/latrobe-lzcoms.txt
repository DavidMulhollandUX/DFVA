Explore how the world of science interacts with the commercial sphere and how to build business structures that power scientific innovation with La Trobe's Bachelor of Commerce/Bachelor of Science.
Co-designed with industry leaders, our double degree gives you a multidisciplinary set of skills and knowledge sought after by employers and policymakers – from learning how to successfully manage research and development projects to commercialising intellectual property from a scientific breakthrough.
By completing La Trobe's Bachelor of Commerce/Bachelor of Science, you will:
  * develop critical capabilities in independent research, innovative thinking, cultural intelligence, oral and written communication, teamwork and leadership
  * study alongside researchers at our Centre for AgriBioscience and La Trobe Business School, and benefit from facilities like the La Trobe Institute for Molecular Science and finance trading room
  * choose from five majors in commerce – including business analytics, finance and marketing – and sixteen majors across a wide range of scientific interest areas – including artificial intelligence, chemistry, ecology, genetics and physics.
  * gain real-world experience through industry placements, work-integrated learning projects and overseas exchange opportunities.

Course intended learning outcomes 
Collapse all
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Analyse real-world challenges confronting business and society, and propose innovative solutions.
_keyboard_arrow_down_
**2.**
Evaluate potential solutions to real-world challenges and negotiate them with stakeholders.
_keyboard_arrow_down_
**3.**
Implement and manage optimal solutions in a responsible and sustainable manner.
_keyboard_arrow_down_
**4.**
Explain the nature and methods of contemporary scientific practice, and the interrelationship between science and society.
_keyboard_arrow_down_
**5.**
Critically evaluate information from different sources to effectively communicate scientific reasoning and results to diverse audiences. 
_keyboard_arrow_down_
**6.**
Work and learn both independently and collaboratively, and demonstrate effective teamwork to complete tasks professionally.
_keyboard_arrow_down_
**7.**
Employ academic and professional integrity and scientific rigour in the conduct, evaluation and reporting of scientific research and practice.
_keyboard_arrow_down_
**8.**
Utilise digital technologies effectively in identifying and executing managerial, policy and scientific solutions. 
_keyboard_arrow_down_
**9.**
Productively apply disciplinary knowledge and skills in the area of the chosen major(s) and minor(s).

Other opportunities 
Student exchange and overseas short programs offer a unique opportunity for a meaningful learning and educational experience abroad. La Trobe University encourages all students to make the world their campus by participating in an exchange or overseas short program that fits within your degree structure.
### 
Career outcomes 
After graduation, you could pursue a wide range of specialist careers across business and science. Whether it's finding new treatments for diseases, developing sustainable ways to grow food, pioneering research in AI or building commercial structures that power scientific innovations, you'll be prepared to pursue your dream career. Depending on your major, possible roles include:
  * business analyst
  * chemist
  * data scientist
  * economist
  * geneticist
  * project manager
  * systems analyst.


### 
Professional recognition 
The Accounting major in the Bachelor of Commerce is accredited by CPA Australia and Chartered Accountants Australia and New Zealand (CAANZ). The Marketing major in the Bachelor of Commerce is accredited by the Australian Marketing Institute (AMI). Professional membership may require an application to the professional body and may have additional or ongoing requirements beyond the completion of the degree. Please contact the relevant professional body for details.
The Chemistry major in the Bachelor of Science is accredited by the Royal Australian Chemical Institute (RACI). The Physics major in the Bachelor of Science is accredited by the Australian Institute of Physics (AIP). The Statistics major, with an additional subject (STA2ASM), in the Bachelor of Science is accredited by the Statistical Society of Australia (SSA). Professional membership may require an application to the professional body and may have additional or ongoing requirements beyond the completion of the degree. Contact the relevant professional body for details.

Subject: BUSINESS ANALYSIS AND MODELLING (BUS1002)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject introduces you to basic statistics and quantitative techniques and their application to decision making and problem solving in business and commerce disciplines such as Accounting, Economics, Finance, Data Analytics, Management and Marketing. You will be exposed to working with business data and employing spreadsheet and modelling tools for analysis, visualisation, interpretation and decision-making purposes. The topics covered in this subject include numerical descriptive measures and graphical descriptive techniques; types of data and sampling techniques; probability and uncertainty measurement, variable and sample distribution identification and visualisation; hypothesis testing and linear regression analysis. You will be introduced to this content with a focus on the understanding and interpretation of statistical information and application to business, market and industry scenarios and problem solving.
Learning Outcomes:
- Present business information using appropriate digital tools and platforms that facilitate informed decision-making
- Engage professionally and effectively to contribute to the success of a diverse team, including use of a variety of modes of communication and appropriate digital tools
- Quantify business risk / uncertainty using probability theory and statistical measurement and relate this to business, consumer or investment outcomes
- Apply appropriate descriptive measures and visualisation techniques to summarise and interpret different types of business information

Subject: FUNDAMENTALS OF FINANCE (FIN1FOF)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Finance defines the process by which individuals and businesses raise and manage funds. Financial skills are a prerequisite for success not only if you are seeking a career in banking and finance, but equally for success in any business career. Indeed, financial literacy is a skill required to successfully manage your personal financial well-being. In this subject, through lessons in our financial trading room and industry-related content, you will examine the nature of the financial environment and the types of financial decision-making required to succeed, both from a business and an individual perspective. You will develop an understanding of the concept of the time value of money, methods of valuing various financial assets such as shares and bonds, and methods of project evaluation. You will consider the trade-off between risk and return in relation to investment assets, and its importance in understanding portfolio theory, and the cost of capital for a business. Finally, you will be able to define a study plan and potential financial career paths.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the current financial environment and define a study plan and potential career path.
- Apply statistical techniques, the capital asset pricing model and modern portfolio theory to analyse and compare the risk-return characteristics of investment portfolios.
- Apply the concept of the time value of money to the valuation of annuities, perpetuities, bonds and equity securities.
- Compare the sources of finance available to a business and estimate the cost of capital, cash flows and financial viability of different investment projects.

Subject: BIG IDEAS IN SCIENCE: LIFE, THE UNIVERSE AND EVERYTHING (SCI1LUE)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: What happened during the first microseconds of the universe? Why is the cosmos filled with galaxies, stars and planets? How did that lead to life on earth? How do we know what happened and when? Why is all life built from cells using information in DNA? Why does it matter? How does this tie into cutting-edge research at La Trobe and internationally? The big ideas of science are a set of cross-cutting concepts that are fundamental to our understanding of the world around us - and how science works. In this subject, you will be introduced to these concepts, which link seemingly different phenomena via fundamental principles and laws of nature and form the basis of the major scientific disciplines. Using cutting-edge research as examples, you will see how scientific evidence is produced, and how that can lead to controversy, conflict - and scientific progress.
Learning Outcomes:
- Reflect on the social nature of science, ethics in science, and scientific progress.
- Work independently and collaboratively in teams
- Communicate basic scientific ideas clearly in written and oral formats
- Illustrate how scientific knowledge is generated and used
- Demonstrate academic integrity by appropriately acknowledging sources of information
- Apply basic understanding of big ideas in science across a range of disciplines
