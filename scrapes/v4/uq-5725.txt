Subject: Recovery & Nutrition for Elite Athlete (NUTR7009)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The course examines recent research on recovery, regeneration and nutrition and its application to the specific needs of an athlete. Strategies for practical problem-solving in specific sport contexts.
Learning Outcomes:
- NUTRITION - Understand and discuss the macronutrient and micronutrient recommendations for athletes involved in selected individual and team sports.
- NUTRITION - Describe how various nutrition strategies can enhance training adaptation and performance for athletes involved in selected team and individual sports.
- NUTRITION - Describe how various nutrition strategies can be used to enhance recovery for athletes involved in selected team and individual sports.
- NUTRITION - Understand and describe the nutrition needs for specific athlete groups involved in selected individual and team sports.
- NUTRITION - Demonstrate an understanding of the principles of nutrition in sport, and implement nutrition systems in your own coaching practice.
- NUTRITION - Demonstrate the ability to clearly convey ideas and information about the science supporting nutrition and recovery in an applied sense relevant to selected sporting contexts.
- RECOVERY - Understand the concepts underlying the principle of recovery and its role in training.
- RECOVERY - Understand, design and implement various recovery strategies (physical and psychological) in your own sporting context.
- RECOVERY - Provide athletes with the skills and resources to monitor their adaptive responses to stress.
- RECOVERY - Apply monitoring methods to track an athlete's adaptive responses to training and competition.
- RECOVERY - Identify appropriate monitoring methods for the coach and athlete.
- RECOVERY - Identify the various types of fatigue that affect training and performance
- RECOVERY - Demonstrate the integration of recovery strategies into the annual training plan.
- RECOVERY - Evaluate the effectiveness of sport specific recovery strategies using various performance indicators.
Assessment Tasks:
- Nutrition Education Series: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Athlete self-monitoring log: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Competition Recovery Program & Nutrition Plan: 50%% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Sport Science (Anatomy, Biomechanics, Physiology) (PHYL6000)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Knowledge and understanding of the anatomy, biomechanics and physiology as it applies to the human body and its application to sports performance, training and injury. Application of principles and skills in understanding and assessing human performance in sports settings.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand and apply bisic principles of human training
- Understand the relevant anatomy of the musculoskeletal system and its general function.
- Understand how the physical structure of the musculoskeletal system is closely linked to the physical function.
- Understand basic kinematics as they apply to sporting movements.
- Understand basic kinetics as they apply to performance and injury in sport.
- Understand how kinematic and kinetic information enables more in-depth analysis of sporting movements.
- Develop and apply anatomical and biomechanical knowledge to analyse technique, training or injury mechanics associated with a particular sport.
- Apply physiological principles to understand methods that are typically used to assess fitness and performance in athletes.
- Apply physiological principles to develop and evaluate appropriate training programs for athletes in specific sports.
- Apply training and periodization principles to improve sport and exercise performance

Subject: Developing the Elite Athlete (SPCG7020)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: In recent times, particularly in Australia, there has been an emphasis on athlete pathways including coaching and player development frameworks. This course recognises the challenges that confront coaches and pathway managers in negotiating the journey around developing elite athletes. The course focuses on two key areas, firstly the complexity in implementing talent development programs and some implications around long term athlete development. Secondly, the course focuses on the role of the coach as a key driver in designing 'optimal' environments intended to support athlete development. This includes, but is not de-limited to the integration of contemporary pedagogical frameworks intended to support athlete skill development. Both of these key areas, in this course, are driven by the ecological meta-theory. The course aims to enhance the coaches understanding of the issues associated with talent and its ensuing components, as well as develop the coaches¿ skills in navigating the difficulties of athlete development across both able-bodied and Paralympic programs. Finally capturing the talent development environment and its application to the coach's practice is explored across to case reports.¿
Learning Outcomes:
- understand the general principles of talent identification and development including long term athlete development models
- understand the role of a range of sub-disciplines within the high performance support team or program including sports science and specialist coaching
- be able to interpret physiological; biomechanical and psychological test data to assess the strengths and weaknesses of an athlete
- develop ‘optimal’ learning environments to support and accelerate athlete development by drawing upon contemporary views of athlete performance
- design and implement training programs using contemporary pedagogical frameworks that positively implicate aspects of athletic performance within an elite or developmental training environment
Assessment Tasks:
- Multiple-Choice Quizzes (MCQ) Online: 30% (2 x quizzes each worth 15%)% (Hurdle: False)
- Case Report 1: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Case Report 2: 30%% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Performance Psychology for Coaches (SPCG7023)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Coaches, athletes, and sport administrators now recognise that physical talent alone does not guarantee success. It is the "mental game" that often differentiates performers, especially at the elite level. The role of a high performance coach has evolved beyond instruction in technical, tactical, and physical skills to that which now includes the application of psychological principles to enhancing performance. In this course, students are introduced to the field of performance psychology and will become knowledgeable in training, practice, and research skills that will enable them to get the best out of themselves and others. There is emphasis on both theoretical understanding and personal practice in developing the student's knowledge and application of areas including: learning styles; basic neuroscience; emotion and emotional intelligence; leadership; performance culture; passion; mindset; solution-focused coaching; mindfulness; and acceptance-commitment therapy. With the primary focus of this course being applied in nature, a core outcome of this unit is the development of an individualised program that can be readily implemented into the student's current or future practice. Each seminar has a lecture component and an experiential learning component, which requires students to evaluate each topic in relation to their own personal life experiences and to participate in group discussion and coaching practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate the ability to clearly convey ideas about psychological theories and applied practice relevant to elite performance in sport.
- Demonstrate the ability to critically reflect upon personal development as a coach as well as coaching practices.
- Demonstrate the ability to retrieve, analyse, synthesise, and critically evaluate information.
- Select strategies and approaches to establish, integrate, and manage a performance psychology program in a coaching environment.
- Demonstrate an understanding of the role that psycho-social variables play in enhancing performance.
Assessment Tasks:
- Contribution to online discussions Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Portfolio of Seminar Summaries Online: 40% (5 x summaries worth 8% each)% (Hurdle: False)
- Final Essay Online: 40%% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Advanced Training Programs (SPCG7028)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course aims to develop your training plan skills and in particular your management skills in relation to handling issues associated with concurrent training, prioritising modes of training in order to enhance performance, monitoring the impact of training and competition on athlete/s and providing appropriate feedback in order to maintain a continuous level of improvement. The continual demands of modern sport, particularly in relation to competition scheduling, means that training programs and loads need to be constantly adjusted to ensure athletes are able to adapt to and maintain or improve performance. The main objective of this course is to assist you in developing a training program management instrument which is dynamic and able to respond effectively to the dynamics of training elite athletes.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the characteristics of an elite performer within their chosen sport and outline some relevant strategies and concepts applicable to the design of a high performance program
- Conduct a performance review of a chosen athlete, team or position/discipline in order to identify key developmental interventions for high performance
- Design a training program which is individualised to meet the performance goals of the individual and reflects best practice in training principles
- Design an appropriate planning system using Microsoft Excel and use this system to prepare an annual training program which includes: o Individual goals o Training Session Plans o Microcycle and Macrocycle Plans o Identifies key competitions (peaking) and reflects an appropriate training phase design
- Design a comprehensive and useful electronic coaching tool for future use in the planning of training programs

Extended Project are conducted over two semesters with the format of final submissions to be negotiated with the supervisor (and Course Coordinator where appropriate) (e.g. publishable journal paper, formal report).Topics must relate to H&PE or coaching and approval will be dependent on the availability of a suitable supervisor.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically reflect on your previous experiences in coaching and in higher education to identify areas of potential interest for further investigation
- Demonstrate an understanding of methodological approaches and select those most appropriate for their given topic
- Understand ethical clearance procedures and conduct research in ethically sound ways
- Independently research and critically evaluate an area of inquiry relevant to your own and others' coaching contexts
- Critically evaluate relevant theory and previous empirical research to make informed, convincing and justified decisions
Assessment Tasks:
- Formulating your topic Online: 5%% (Hurdle: False)
- Research proposal Online: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Ethical clearance Online: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: False)
- Progress report Online: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Thesis Online: 60%% (Hurdle: False)
