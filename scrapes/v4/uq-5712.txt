Subject: Calculus & Linear Algebra II (MATH7000)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Second order differential equations; undetermined coefficients, variation of parameters. Multi-dimensional calculus; surface & volume integrals, cylindrical, spherical and general coordinate transformations. Stoke's & Green's theorems, applications (flux, heat equations). Linear algebra, diagonalisation, quadratic forms, elementary numerical linear algebra. Taylor series, maxima, minima and saddle points in N-dimensions. Method of least squares for functions. Vector spaces, norms and inner products (for square-integrable functions). Gram-Schmidt orthogonalisation and orthogonal matrices.
Learning Outcomes:
- Solve a variety of first order and second order Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs)
- Derive properties of the hyperbolic functions and apply them in various mathematical contexts.
- Understand and apply aspects of the theory of multi-dimensional integrals, using a variety of coordinate systems.
- Apply differential calculus to the context of vector functions. Specifically, work with Grad, Div and Curl and understand their significance.
- Apply integral calculus to the context of vector functions. Specifically, use surface and flux integrals, and the theorems of Green, Gauss and Stokes.
- Understand and apply eigenvalues, eigenvectors and diagonalisation of matrices in a variety of contexts.
- Apply concepts of orthogonality and inner product spaces to the method of least squares, best fit and function approximation
- Model and solve problems with real-world application using mathematical techniques covered in this course.
- Present clear and concise mathematical arguments in assignments and exams.
Assessment Tasks:
- 4 Assignments: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Examination Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 60%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 3 Hours/ Week Applied Class 1 Hour/ Week Summer Semester only: 6L2A

Subject: Applied Mathematical Analysis (MATH7100)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: ODE's - Systems: variation of constants, fundamental matrix. Laplace transform, transform for systems, transfer function. Stability, asymptotic stability; phase-lane analysis. PDE's - Fourier series. Wave, heat, Laplace's equations. Simple maximum & uniqueness principles. Separation of variables in rectangular & polar coordinates.
Learning Outcomes:
- Ordinary Differential equations - use the software package Mathematica to solve systems of differential equations and plot the solutions
- Ordinary Differential equations - solve linear systems of ODEs and to be able to interpret their behaviour in the phase plane.
- Ordinary Differential equations - find equilibrium solutions to nonlinear systems of ODE's and analyse their stability characteristics.
- Ordinary Differential equations - use the Laplace Transform to solve linear ODE's and systems of ODE's with continuous and discontinuous forcing.
- Ordinary Differential equations - use the methods described above to analyse systems from Biology, Chemistry, Physics and Engineering (mass spring systems, mixing problems, simple electrical circuit systems, simple chemical rate equations and simple predator prey systems).
- Partial Differential Equations - Use ideas such as linear equations, the superposition principle and separation of variables to solve a range partial differential equations.
- Partial Differential Equations - Use various notions related to Fourier analysis to find and manipulate Fourier series for a range of functions.
- Partial Differential Equations - Solve a range of boundary- and initial-value problems, among them the wave equation, the heat equation and Laplace's equation.
Assessment Tasks:
- 4 Assignments: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Examination Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 60%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 3 Hours/ Week Applied Class 1 Hour/ Week 3L1A

Subject: Mathematical Analysis (MATH7400)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The course is a rigorous, precise and exact introduction to the central concept in mathematical analysis: limits. This concept is used to develop further notions such as continuity, differential and integral calculus of functions of single and several variables, the implicit and inverse function theorems, uniform continuity of functions and the uniform convergence of sequences of functions. The emphasis of this course is on understanding rather than mere computation or rote memorisation.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the precise mathematical meaning of limits, derivatives, and integrals, as well as, continuous and differentiable functions
- Formulate proofs of results similar to those covered in class, with an appropriate level of detail and rigour.
- Summarise important ideas from mathematical analysis and communicate these ideas clearly using appropriate mathematical terminology.
- Examine how ideas from this course arise in other areas of mathematics.
Assessment Tasks:

Subject: Mathematical Analysis and Advanced Topics (MATH7407)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Sequences & series of real functions. Intermediate value theorem. Extreme value theorem. Taylor's theorem & error estimates. Criteria for integrability. Vector functions, continuity & differentials. Implicit & inverse function theorems & applications. Metric spaces: compactness, completeness, continuity, contraction mapping principle. Ascoli-Arzela theorem.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand proofs of deep results in single variable real analysis. Students should be able to provide proofs of results close to those covered in class, with an appropriate level of detail and rigour. Moreover, students should demonstrate substantive content knowledge of single variable real analysis.
- Understand constructions in multivariable real analysis. Students should be able to provide proofs of results close to those covered in class, with an appropriate level of detail and rigour. Moreover, students should demonstrate substantive content knowledge of multiple variable real analysis.

Subject: Thermodynamics & Condensed Matter Physics (PHYS7120)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Theoretical understanding of general properties of macroscopic sized material systems that apply irrespective of the detailed behaviour of microscopic particles constituting the system. Understanding of matter in condensed (liquid or solid) states.
Learning Outcomes:
- Use thermodynamics concepts to reason about the behaviour of macroscopic properties of physical systems. In particular, you should develop an appreciation of the power of thermodynamics to understand a diverse range of systems relevant to physics, chemistry, biology, engineering, and earth sciences.
- Apply the laws of thermodynamics to physical, chemical and engineering applications, deriving relationships between macroscopic quantities and making quantitative predictions of thermodynamic behaviour. You should get a feel for order of magnitude estimates of thermodynamic quantities in typical systems and be able to perform 'back of the envelope' calculations, in addition to well-reasoned analytic and computational work.
- Ability to use a computer to investigate thermodynamic phenomena and simulate complex processes
- Investigate thermodynamic phenomena experimentally, applying laboratory skills and error analysis to test theoretical predictions
- Develop report writing and communication skills
