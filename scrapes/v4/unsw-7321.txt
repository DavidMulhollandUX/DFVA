## Description
The Graduate Certificate in Taxation is designed for taxation specialists and practitioners wanting to gain deeper tax knowledge and advanced professional skills.

The program may also be suitable, if you do not meet the entry requirements, as a pathway into the Master of Taxation.

## Program Learning Outcomes (CILOs)
On successful completion of this program, students will be able to:

1. Graduates will be able to identify, research and analyse complex taxation law issues and problems in business and will be able to propose well justified solutions to them.
4. Graduates will be able to participate collaboratively and reasonably in teams, will reflect on their own teamwork, on the team's processes and the team's ability to achieve outcomes.
2. Graduates will be able to identify and apply current knowledge and professional practice in taxation law in a business context.
3. Graduates will be able to produce written and oral presentations that communicate complex ideas and information in relation to taxation law effectively for the intended audience and purpose.
5. Gradutes will identify and assess ethical and social implications of taxation law as applied in the business context.

## Program Structure & Degree Requirements
Students must complete 24 Units of Credit (UOC) structured across the following academic components:

### Taxation Law (0 UOC)
Students who did not complete a university level course of study in taxation as part of their bachelor degree, are required to complete TABL5559 (online delivery mode)/TABL5551 (in-person delivery mode*) before undertaking any other courses in the program. This will ensure they are equipped with the level of knowledge required to successfully complete Taxation courses in the program. Completion of TABL5559/TABL5551 will count as one of the four courses students need to complete. Students who have 10 or more years professional experience in relevant services firms or in government departments, or have competed taxation courses as part of their undergraduate degree are not required to complete this course.

## Progression & Academic Rules

## Admission Requirements
**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
Category A

A recognised bachelor degree (or equivalent qualification) in Taxation, Law, Accounting, Business or Commerce, as determined by UNSW Business School; and a minimum of two (2) years relevant full-time professional experience in professional services firms or in government departments.

Category B

A recognised bachelor degree (or equivalent qualification) in any discipline, for example in Arts, Science, Social Sciences, Engineering, etc, as determined by UNSW Business School; and a minimum of two (2) years relevant full-time professional experience in professional services firms or in government departments. Applicants must provide a detailed resume (outlining relevant competencies and achievements) and provide evidence of other professional qualifications or studies if applying under the 'equivalent qualification' criteria.

**Limitations on Recognition of Prior Learning**:
Credit may be granted for up to 6 units of credit (one course) for the Graduate Certificate in Taxation for the below:

- TABL6506 – 6 Units of Credit of Unspecified Option

Granted if student has completed 3 ATO micro-credentials through AGSM.

Exemption may be granted for TABL5559/ TABL5551:

May be granted if student has 10 or more years professional experience in relevant professional services firms or in government departments, or has completed taxation courses as part of their undergraduate studies. If granted, the student would be exempt from completing TABL5559/TABL5551, and would need to complete an alternative elective course instead.

No other RPL can be considered for the Graduate Certificate in Taxation.

Students who successfully articulate to the Master of Taxation from the Graduate Certificate in Taxation may be granted full credit from the course completed in the Graduate Certificate and may be eligible for up to an additional 12 UOC RPL for elective courses, subject to course fit within the 9250 program.

Subject: Taxation of Commercial Transactions (TABL5501)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course offers an in-depth analysis of the tax implications for commercial transactions occurring mainly within the small and medium enterprise (SME) sector. The course is guided by a comprehensive conceptual framework (CF). The framework focuses on accurately determining and evaluating the tax treatment of various key commercial activities, integrating high-level tax policy principles and legislative intents. The course is ideal for students with prior knowledge or experience of assessable income and deductions in ongoing business operations. The course progresses to examine complex transactions not in the ordinary course of business, such as isolated profit-making endeavours and cases where the replacement principle is relevant, including compensation receipts. The program also covers the intricate allocation of commercial costs for tax purposes, whether as general deductions, repairs or costs for trading stock, depreciating assets or capital gains. The course also looks at specific expenditures like financing and legal fees. The course concludes by exploring the timing of tax recognition for income and expenses, addressing prepaid expenses, earn-out arrangements, and the interplay between capital asset rules and deductions, ensuring a thorough grasp of the taxation landscape for commercial transactions mainly in the Australian SME context.
Learning Outcomes:
- Engage in tax problem solving techniques in complex commercial transactions.
- Demonstrate lateral thinking in taxation law and links to tax accounting.
- Demonstrate expert understanding of capital gains tax rules applicable to complex commercial transactions.
- Demonstrate expert understanding of tax rules outside of capital gains tax applicable to complex transactions.

Subject: Advanced Taxation of Trusts (TABL5505)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course aims to equip you with a comprehensive understanding of trust taxation, enhancing your proficiency in navigating the Australian tax environment and your ability to advise on complex trust issues or manage compliance and tax risk. This course delves into the complex role that trusts play within the Australian financial landscape, serving as pivotal instruments for managing investment assets, running businesses, protecting and transferring family wealth, and facilitating income splitting. The course covers the nuances of various trust structures, including fixed public trusts—now broadly recognized as public unit trusts or collective investment vehicles—and the significant presence of trusts in superannuation funds. Discretionary trusts widely used for family businesses will be considered in some depth. Unlike distinct legal entities, trusts are not directly taxed; taxation obligations primarily fall to the beneficiaries, otherwise to the trustee. This curriculum is designed to unravel the intricacies of when the general taxation rule for trusts applies and when it is overridden by exceptions. Leading cases and ‘cutting edge’ issues will be explored. The course is essential for those of you seeking to deepen or refine your grasp of the Australian tax system, offering a structured exploration through five modules: an introduction to, and overview of, trust law relevant to trust taxation, taxation of trust net income, the application of the CGT provisions to trusts, trust loss measures and family trust elections, and general anti-avoidance along with other integrity measures.
Learning Outcomes:
- Recognise and resolve legal and tax problems related to trusts, delivering well-reasoned tax consequence analyses in practical scenarios with proper citation and referencing.
- Explain trust taxation principles, policy considerations, and develop the ability to independently resolve tax issues related to trusts.
- Exhibit critical thinking and originality in Master’s level research papers on trust taxation, reflecting a profound understanding and independent thought on the subject.
- Critically assess how trusts are taxed, understand the implications for investors, and enhance skills in statutory interpretation and legislative review concerning trust taxation.
- Actively contribute to discussions, applying legal principles to facts, reaching conclusions on specific trust taxation problems, and engaging constructively with the contributions of peers.
- Conduct comprehensive literature reviews to identify relevant sources and apply these in constructing legal arguments, demonstrating an ability to analyse trust taxation issues deeply.

Subject: Tax Administration (TABL5506)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course does a deep dive into the administration of Australia's self-assessment income tax system, focusing on the policy, and on the practical challenges of complying with our tax laws. - Deliver clear, effective analysis of the challenge process, including the Commissioner’s information gathering powers and the critical issues involved.
- Explain Australia's self-assessment tax system, focusing on challenging the Commissioner of Taxation’s decisions and the process of amendment.
- Demonstrate academic and professional integrity in communicating tax administration challenges, adhering to correct citation and referencing conventions.
- Assess and select effective methods to contest acts or omissions by the Commissioner of Taxation, grounded in procedural fairness and legal strategy.
- Apply theoretical knowledge to frame and resolve tax disputes through the Administrative Appeals Tribunal and courts, incorporating relevant law and research.

Subject: Taxation and Regulation of Superannuation (TABL5510)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Do you want to gain a comprehensive and practical understanding of the taxation and regulation of superannuation in Australia? Superannuation is a form of retirement savings supported by the government through various tax concessions and regulations. The superannuation system is critical to the Australian economy and currently has over $3.5 trillion in assets (1.4 times the market capitalisation of the ASX!) and is projected to almost triple to over $9 trillion in 2041. In this course, you will explore the Australian superannuation system (what superannuation is and how it works, the purpose of superannuation, types of superannuation funds including SMSFs), and the underlying government policy of how superannuation funds are taxed and regulated. The course focuses on the taxation and regulation of the three key elements of superannuation – contributions, investment earnings and benefit payments. You will develop critical thinking and problem-solving skills in relation to superannuation issues and scenarios as application of the rules to real world scenarios is a key learning tool in this course.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply correct citation and referencing conventions in properly communicating research of issues relating to the taxation and regulation of superannuation.
- Evaluate both the regulatory framework for, and tax treatment of, superannuation to ascertain whether current and proposed legislation properly reflects policy intent in this area.
- Recognise and resolve tax problems independently and in groups and develop clear, logical, effective, and well-reasoned analysis of the tax consequences of practical scenarios in a professional way using the conventions of legal essay writing.
- Demonstrate a critical approach to course materials and other materials acquired during the research process to provide evidence of original and independent thought.
- Apply the special taxation rules applicable to superannuation contributions, superannuation fund income and superannuation benefits in a variety of factual situations.

Subject: Tax Treaties and Multilateral Obligations (TABL5537)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course offers a comprehensive exploration of Double Taxation Agreements (DTAs), focusing on their principles, application, and the models underpinning them, without an exclusive emphasis on Australian DTAs. Through a curriculum that elucidates how DTAs function to allocate taxing rights between countries and prevent double taxation in international dealings, you will gain insights primarily via the OECD model tax convention, with comparative analyses of mainly the United Nations model tax convention. These comparisons reveal the varied approaches to balancing the interests of a taxpayer's country of residence and the source country of income, reflecting the diverse priorities of the models' authoring organizations. Structured to cover the rationale behind DTAs, their structure, interpretation protocols, and application scope, the course delves into specific income types including business profits, passive income, personal services income, property-related income and capital gains, and addresses anti-avoidance and administrative provisions. Extensive references to real-world DTAs illustrate these concepts, ensuring that by the course's conclusion, you will possess a thorough understanding of DTAs' global application and significance.`
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and address legal and tax issues by applying international taxation rules and DTAs to practical problems, using sound legal reasoning.
- Deliver well-reasoned, clear analyses of tax implications in practical scenarios by effectively utilizing legal writing conventions.
- Demonstrate hands-on experience with DTAs application in various jurisdictions, particularly in business and commercial contexts.
- Analyse diverse international tax strategies, understand the rationale behind different DTAs, and evaluate their practical and policy outcomes.
- Identify and explain various DTAs across multiple tax jurisdictions and apply this knowledge to real-world scenarios.
- Identify and explain the foundations of international taxation and the mechanics of Double Taxation Agreements (DTAs), determining their role in the allocation of taxing rights between jurisdictions and the prevention of double taxation.

Subject: Transfer Pricing and International Tax Controversy (TABL5550)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: How can a company improve its global group tax position using cross border transactions with related parties? Large multinationals (MNEs) earn significant profits from their worldwide operations and are able to manage overall tax positions through intra-group agreements for goods, services and intellectual property. Tax administrations may review and adjust cross-border tax risks based on MNE reporting, by applying alternate arm's length pricing under domestic transfer pricing rules, incorporating OECD transfer pricing guidelines. This course will allow you to understand and apply fundamental international tax principles, domestic law and guidance and provide greater insights into the mechanics of transfer pricing methodologies. You will learn to navigate jurisdictional tax differences and tax controversy. Key concepts are examined, ranging from basic transactions to more complex areas of hard-to-value services, intellectual property and various financial transactions. Practical applications of the subject are often encountered in tax practice and you will have the skills to identify such emerging tax risks and consider best outcomes.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the case law development in the international context of transfer pricing and the various requirements of revenue authorities in a range of jurisdictions in terms of preparing transfer pricing documentation.
- Apply knowledge of law, taxation and accounting in developing a sound understanding of the transfer pricing framework and the various methodologies for determining arm’s length pricing, in order to effectively apply transfer pricing principles.
- Apply transfer pricing principles in the context of specific transactions, such as intra-group financing arrangements and business restructurings.
- Recognise and resolve legal and tax problems and develop clear, effective and well-reasoned analysis of the tax consequences of practical scenarios using the conventions of legal essay writing. Apply correct citation and referencing conventions in properly acknowledging all source material used.

Subject: Taxation Law (TABL5551)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Tax affects all of business and investment. Do you want to have a good knowledge of how tax affects those involved with businesses and investments, and understand how the government raises revenue? This course will help you understand the tax regulatory framework and the tax rules operating in the context of a business and investments. You will acquire and develop analytical skills in applying tax rules to commercial transactions. Topics include: brief coverage of tax administration rules; legal obligations of tax agents; income tax formula and tax liability, and liability for other levies and charges; concept of income and assessable income including net capital gains; overview of exempt income; general deduction provision and allowable deductions generally; specific deduction regimes that either supplement the general deduction rule (e.g. capital allowance regimes) or deny a deduction otherwise available; tax accounting rules, and tax treatment of trading stock; taxation of taxable income made through partnerships, trusts and corporations; selected specific anti-avoidance rules and overview of the general anti-avoidance rule; overview of cross border transactions and international tax rules; brief coverage of goods and services tax (GST), fringe benefits tax (FBT) and how these taxes interact with income tax; and the tax treatment of superannuation.
Learning Outcomes:

- Identify the relevant tax law rules that may apply in a typical tax-related professional problem.
- Explore the tax law to identify rules that do not meet their objectives and use your critical legal reasoning skills to construct arguments for a “new” tax law rule.
- Summarise and explain clearly, in writing, how the tax law rules under Australia’s tax regime studied in this course have been applied by judges in court cases and by members of relevant tribunals.
- Construct written work which is logically and professionally presented and communicates ideas in a succinct and clear manner.
- Locate, summarise and explain the Australian tax law rules that have been studied in this course.

Subject: International Business Taxation (TABL5583)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course addresses the critical role of taxation in the context of the global economy, where international investment barriers are diminishing, yet disparities in tax systems and the lack of coordination among them remain significant hurdles. It explores key principles of international taxation, using the Australian international tax rules as a case study to illustrate various policy choices and challenges inherent in the global tax landscape. The course delves into strategies for enhancing coordination of international tax rules, including harmonization and the development of bilateral and multilateral treaties. Special focus is placed on practical tax issues related to international direct investments, offering insight into the complexities of navigating tax obligations across borders. Through this curriculum, you will gain a comprehensive understanding of international taxation principles, policy options, and the practical considerations vital for managing and strategizing international investments effectively.

- Locate and use resources that will assist you in developing a response to international business taxation issues.
- Evaluate different outcomes that result when different international tax planning strategies are used.
- Explain your response to international business taxation issues in written work which is logically and professionally presented.
