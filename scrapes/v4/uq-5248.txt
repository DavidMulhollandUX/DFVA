Subject: Financial Management for Decision Makers (FINM7409)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: How should you make financial decisions? In this course, you will investigate the many financial factors influencing business operations and project success. Through financial analysis, you will learn to make informed business decisions about how to invest in businesses, projects, and your career.
Learning Outcomes:
- Use accounting information and financial ratios to assess the financial status of a firm.
- Apply the concept of time value of money and capital budgeting techniques to evaluate firms, financial securities, and investment projects.
- Evaluate the relation between risk and return in capital markets to determine the cost of capital.
- Make business management decisions informed by financial analysis.
- Justify recommendations to influence stakeholder decision-making.
Assessment Tasks:
- Simulation Exercise Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Project Proposal Report Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Final Exam Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Tools and Techniques for Business Analysis (MGTS7301)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: What makes a `good¿ business decision? In this course, you will apply various rational and alternative tools to analyse real-world cases to explore how decisions are made and how they can be improved. By questioning perspectives, assumptions, and values, you will practice critical thinking as the most important employability skill for you to make strategic, evidence-based decisions in a complex and ambiguous world.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse real-world business problems using relevant analytical tools.
- Collaboratively evaluate business decisions from different perspectives.
- Recommend strategies to balance the needs of shareholders and other stakeholders.
- Justify positions supported by evidence and respect for the audience.
- Reflect on personal assumptions and values to question business norms and challenge the status quo.
Assessment Tasks:
- In-Semester Examination Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Site Visit Template Team or group-based: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Boardroom Roleplay Team or group-based In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Business Tools For Making Decisions: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 1.5 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1.5 Hours/ Week

Subject: Business and Society (MGTS7608)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Why should businesses care about social responsibility? This course prepares you for the economic, social, political, and cultural contexts that shape organisational decision-making and stakeholder relationships. Through a critical exploration of leadership, stakeholder engagement, and regulatory environments, you will develop the analytical tools needed to navigate the complex landscape of contemporary organisational practice and responsibility.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse the political, social, and environmental relationships of businesses.
- Collaboratively, discuss the complexities of business–society relationships to consider diverse perspectives.
- Recommend business decisions that create social impact by incorporating stakeholder perspectives.
- Evaluate ethical and sustainability considerations in business decision-making and practice.
- Apply elements of Indigenous worldview to develop solutions.
Assessment Tasks:
- Case study team presentation Identity Verified Team or group-based In-person: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Individual stakeholder and ethics analysis Online: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Final Exam Identity Verified In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Management Communication (MGTS7610)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: How do you ensure your ideas are heard? You need to know how to adjust how you communicate with different people at work to achieve your purpose. In this course, we learn how to engage and interact with people persuasively in business contexts. The course also enables you to analyse your communication reflectively, as an essential part of professional learning.
Learning Outcomes:
- Adapt your message to a range of management contexts and audiences.
- Articulate your ideas with clarity and purpose.
- Persuade relevant stakeholders to take sustainable business action.
- Engage a business audience using communication and presentation skills.
- Communicate respectfully and ethically in culturally sensitive ways with diverse colleagues, customers and communities.
- Evaluate communication critically and reflexively to improve collaboration and ethical decision-making.
Assessment Tasks:
- In-semester Exam: Business Correspondence Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Boardroom Presentation: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Structured independent study (using RiPPLE) Online: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Interactive Oral Assessment Identity Verified Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week
