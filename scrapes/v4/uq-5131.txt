Subject: First Nations Health and Wellbeing (INDH7002)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course introduces students to First Nations Health and Wellbeing, with a particular focus on Global Indigenous and Australian Indigenous peoples and communities. It foregrounds Indigenous voices, scholarship, and cultures in both global and Australian contexts, encouraging students to critically examine historical and contemporary determinants of health that contribute to racial health inequities. Utilising a strengths-based and decolonising lens , the course will explore strategies and Indigenous `ways of knowing, being and doing¿ to develop your understanding of the health and well-being of First Nations communities throughout different stages of life. Grounded in public health, the course adopts participatory, collaborative, and critical thinking approaches to explore key concepts and frameworks that support culturally safe and respectful engagement with First Nations communities. This course encourages students to critical self-reflection, examining their own cultural values and privileges in cross-cultural interactions. This process aims to foster culturally safe practices and deepen understanding of allyship and solidarity with First Nations Peoples, including Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples. Aligned with the theme of National Reconciliation Week 2026, ¿ All In ¿ - a call for all Australians to commit wholeheartedly to reconciliation every single day, this course encourages students to join a collective reconciliation effort¿stepping forward together, guided by the lessons of the past, and committed to shaping a more equitable future.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand and apply a culturally safe practice.
- Describe Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander and global Indigenous health in a historical context and analyse the impact of colonial processes on First Nation health outcomes.
- Analyse, apply and critically assess the role of the determinants of health to global and national First Nations health outcomes.
- Apply a health equity framework to critically analyse the strengths of First Nations led health initiatives.
- Critically evaluate and apply participatory, collaborative, and advocacy approaches to develop sustainable, culturally safe, equitable and responsive health policies, services, and systems that support First Nations health and wellbeing.
- Demonstrate skills in written and oral communication, advocacy, reflection, digital capabilities, and teamwork.
Assessment Tasks:
- Critical Reflexivity Portfolio Through Decolonising Lens: 30% VLog 1 is 15%; VLog 2 is 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Critical Analysis of a Contemporary Indigenous Health Equity Issue: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Health Equity Pitch: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week 2 hourly lectures, complemented with weekly Deadly Yarn Podcasts, readings and 2 hourly Weekly Tutorials

Subject: Qualitative Research: Public Health Applications (PUBH7003)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course develops knowledge and skills in the application of social thinking and qualitative methods to public health. Students will have the opportunity to plan a qualitative investigation of a selected topic and in doing so will gain skills in research design, data gathering techniques; considering ethical issues; data recording and management; data analysis and interpretation; and qualitative report writing. Students will consider how qualitative research can be used to inform public health health policy and practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the contribution of qualitative evidence to public health and critically appraise published qualitative research in public health.
- Understand qualitative research paradigms and critically reflect on how these paradigms impact the design and conduct of research.
- Critically reflect on the types of qualitative research methods and paradigms and their role in contemporary public health research
- Design and undertake a qualitative research project to answer a public health question, including devising research questions, selecting methods, sample, recruitment, data collection and analyses.
- Identify and apply the key steps in the development of qualitative data collection tools and carry out in-depth interviews, focus groups, and observation methods
- Demonstrate skills in written and oral communication and refection of qualitative research to diverse audiences.
Assessment Tasks:
- Audit Trail of Qualitative Research and Learning: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Qualitative research proposal: 35%% (Hurdle: False)
- In-depth interview and data analysis: 55%% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Preventing Disease through Healthy Environments (PUBH7027)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course is an exciting exploration of how the physical environment can protect or damage human health. Students will explore real-life current issues associated with safe drinking water, clean air and functional housing at a community level. They will build their understanding of the range of environmental health issues at a global, regional and local level- from climate change to urban green space. They will graduate from the course with a clear understanding of key terminology and a familiarity with the tools to reduce hazards, exposure and vulnerability to environmental health risks that can be applied in any position with an environmental health dimension.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate an understanding of the major biological, chemical, and physical hazards associated with human exposure to the environment and the likely impact of environment and climate change on the risks and outcomes of these hazards.
- Understand current prevention and control strategies for major environmental hazards.
- Understand the role of health risk assessment in the identification and management of environmental health issues.
- Investigate how environmental health fits into the scope of public health practice.
- Critically reflect how the social, political, economic, and institutional context of society influences environmental health and our capacity to respond to environmental hazards.
- Demonstrate effective written and oral communication skills, and skills in group work including collaboration, teamwork, self-reflection, and group negotiation

Subject: Foundations of Public Health (PUBH7033)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Your role as a student and (future) public health practitioner is to improve the health of populations in a changing and inequitable world. Through the Foundations of Public Health course, you will establish a strong grounding in public health, gaining a critical overview of the history, principles, philosophy, and conceptual underpinnings of public health, and the scientific disciplines that make up the public health 'toolbox'. Health equity, social justice, and human rights are at the core of public health and underpin all that we do in this course. Together we will investigate health inequalities, one of the most fundamental challenges in public health, and the structural, social, environmental, commercial, political, and biological determinants that shape the health of individuals, groups, and populations. We will also examine what constitutes relevant evidence and different ways of 'knowing' in public health, not just in a disciplinary sense, but also through the lens of culture and context. Significant public health achievements and complex contemporary challenges will be explored from local/global and multidisciplinary perspectives. The critical thinking, reflection and analytical skills developed in this course will ensure you are well equipped to continue your study in public health and for your future as a leader in public health practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the core philosophical values, principles, conceptual underpinnings, strategies and approaches of public health
- Apply principles of equity, diversity and inclusion to contemporary public health challenges within the context of current and future action
- Critically reflect on the history, context and different ways of knowing in public health and consider how these have influenced public health action
- Understand and analyse the role and impact of the determinants of health in different settings and contexts across time
- Critically reflect on your own social, cultural, and professional backgrounds and how this contributes to your understanding of public health practice
- Demonstrate skills in written and oral communication, reflection and digital capabilities
Assessment Tasks:
- Public health critical reflection journal: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Social analysis of a public health issue: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Social Determinants Analysis and Innovation Hurdle: 50%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Workshop 4 Hours/ Week A 3 hour workshop is held weekly plus an extra hour for the Masterclass program

Subject: Wastewater Surveillance: basics and applications in public health (PUBH7050)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides an in-depth understanding of wastewater surveillance as an emerging tool for public health surveillance. Through a combination of lectures, case studies, debates and practical activities students will examine the principles and basics of wastewater surveillance compared with traditional surveillance methods and how wastewater surveillance could further enhance the monitoring of public health. Additionally, students will explore a range of methodologies and real-word applications of wastewater analysis, within diverse contexts related to chemical and pathogen surveillance. The course will explore how wastewater samples are collected, analysed, and used to track indicators of chemical or disease spread, such as virus RNA, antibiotic resistance genes, or environmental toxins. Students will engage with ethical, logistical and technical considerations and be exposed to a range of laboratory techniques, statistical methods for data interpretation, and will be encouraged to evaluate and critical consider how surveillance systems can be integrated into public health infrastructures.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain and discuss the principles of wastewater surveillance including the prerequisite for its application.
- Examine the advantages and limitations of wastewater surveillance compared to traditional forms of surveillance.
- Critically apply wastewater surveillance methods to different scenarios in Australia and around the world.
- Use wastewater surveillance to examine and develop responses a range of public health questions.
- Demonstrate skills in research, communication (written, oral and digital), group work/collaboration and reflection.
Assessment Tasks:
- Individual reflection vlog: 8%% (Hurdle: False)
- Allocated debate topic Identity Verified Team or group-based In-person Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Written personal meta-reflection: 12%% (Hurdle: False)
- Implementation plan: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Workshop 40 Hours/ Block intensive for 5 days

Subject: Cost Effectiveness in Health and Medicine (PUBH7618)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course is designed for public health students who want to develop the skills and confidence to use economic thinking in real-world policy, planning, and program evaluation. Throughout the course, we will explore the core economic concepts and methods that are applicable to generate economic evidence to design, implement and evaluate public health policies, programmes and interventions. You will learn why, when, and how to conduct an economic evaluation, how to interpret their findings, and how to critically appraise the evidence they produce. Emphasis will be placed on real-world case studies, policy relevance, and the ethical and equity dimensions of economic reasoning. The course balances conceptual depth with practical application . You will: · Explore how economic incentives shape public health systems and decisions, and develop insights into how economics can be used responsibly to improve public health · Participate in budgeting and resource allocation simulations to experience the real-world trade-offs that decision-makers face when allocating limited resources across competing public health priorities · Undertake a basic cost-effectiveness analysis using Excel, and develop judgement about when economic analysis tools are relevant and when they are not, · Build skills in critical appraisal, written and verbal communication, and collaborative problem-solving, preparing you to interpret, question, and convey economic evidence with clarity and policy relevance.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate knowledge about role of economic thinking in public health policy, planning, and priority-setting
- Describe and distinguish key economic methods used in public health evaluation
- Apply core concepts of economic evaluation to conduct and interpret a basic cost-effectiveness analysis using decision-analytic modelling.
- Critically assess the quality, transparency, and policy relevance of published economic evaluations using established reporting standards
- Communicate economic evidence clearly and persuasively to diverse stakeholders
