Overview

If you’re passionate about sharing your love of learning, La Trobe’s Master of Teaching (Primary) will give you the tools to thrive in a rewarding teaching career.
Feel completely prepared from your first day in the classroom. Through a combination of practical and evidence-based learning, you’ll gain the confidence and knowledge to help shape the lives of our next generation.
Learn the most up-to-date skills for the modern classroom. Understand the needs of diverse learners and gain the skills and experience to succeed in any classroom. You’ll prepare for classroom teaching with extensive in-school experience and guidance from our highly experienced and dedicated teaching academics.
Become the best teacher you can be. With extensive real-world teaching experience, you'll learn how to manage classrooms, design class plans, guide students, and create a safe and engaging learning space for your students.
We focus on practise-based learning and our students learn about creating relationships from day one. You’ll be embedded in your community from your first semester and will be mentored by community leaders. Understand how to connect with every student as an individual and develop meaningful relationships with students, parents and the wider school community.
We are committed to social justice and diversity. With campuses across metropolitan, regional and rural Victoria, we collaborate with a range of communities on the things that matter most to them and us.
Your study experience will be flexible and suited to your needs. You’ll enjoy a combination of online, on campus and in-schools learning, making it easier to balance study and life commitments.
Study in a supportive learning environment. You’ll be mentored by our experienced and dedicated teaching staff. Learn from leading academics and researchers in the field of learning sciences and develop strong professional networks. Throughout your studies, you'll learn the latest evidence-based teaching techniques and explore innovative pedagogies and learning sciences.
Graduate ready to convert your love of learning into a rewarding career.
You’ll learn:
  * **Foundations of primary education**
    * Learn essential skills to effectively teach English, maths, science, art, humanities and physical education to primary-aged children.
  * **Classroom management**
    * Learn how to confidently manage a classroom. Prepare for classroom teaching with extensive in-school experience and guidance from our highly experienced and passionate teaching academics.
  * **Inclusion and diversity**
    * Learn how to create safe, diverse learning environments for people of different abilities and backgrounds. Develop skills to connect with parents and the wider school community.
  * **Indigenous perspectives**
    * Understand the histories, cultures, traditions, languages and diversity of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples, and learn how to provide education that’s most appropriate to their needs.
  * **Digital learning**
    * Explore the world of digital learning in a way that's creative, collaborative and innovative.

Work based learning (placement) requirements 
**_Compulsory placements (Work Based Learning)_**  
During this course, you will participate in one or more supervised Work Based Learning (WBL) placements designed to allow you to practice newly acquired skills, behaviours and knowledge while integrating theory with practice. The placements will immerse you in professional work culture, enabling you to develop and further enhance your skills. WBL provides you with the opportunity to gain valuable industry experience at one or more of our local, interstate or international industry partners or agencies. WBL placements will generally be arranged by the Partnerships Operations team at La Trobe University. The number of hours undertaken can vary considerably, depending on the requirements of the course. Students may incur additional costs and it may be necessary to travel or relocate to complete a specific placement. Please refer to the individual subject entries for more information.

Subject: UNDERSTANDING LEARNING AND DIFFERENTIATION (EDU5010)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject is concerned with how humans learn new information, from infancy to early adulthood, in circumstances of both typical and atypical development. You will learn about development of the human brain and the physical, cognitive, linguistic, and social processes supported by this; you will examine the role of cultural and linguistic diversity in learning and achievement at school and will learn to apply Response to Intervention principles across all tiers of instruction and support. You will differentiate evidence-informed practices from those that are pseudoscientific and fail to deliver on robust outcomes.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate the ability to design whole class instructional and progress monitoring activities that align with the principles of contemporary learning science frameworks.
- Apply knowledge of typical and atypical neurodevelopment to the instructional context to respond to the needs of diverse learners in the classroom in a Response to Intervention (RTI) framework.
- Differentiate between teaching and intervention approaches that are well-supported by rigorous research evidence and those that are of low efficacy and impact.
- Demonstrate the ability to identify students requiring intervention and the role of family-school connections, community agencies, representatives and external professionals in meeting such needs.

Subject: UNDERSTANDING AND ASSESSING INTEGRATED CURRICULUM (EDU5011)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject provides Pre-Service Teachers (PSTs) with a deep understanding of how to design and assess integrated curriculum that acknowledges that meaningful and transferable learning occurs across discipline areas and stages of schooling (F-12). PSTs will identify and synthesise unifying concepts across learning areas (including cross-curriculum priority areas) and general capabilities (e.g., competencies). Primary-focused PSTs will learn disciplinary and pedagogical knowledges across Health and Physical Education (e.g., PDHPE), Humanities, English, and Mathematics, and secondary-focused PSTs will explore opportunities to apply an integrated curriculum approach to their discipline/learning area. PSTs will learn how to make meaningful connections between these learning areas as well as cross-curricular priorities and general capabilities. Furthermore, PSTs will learn how to explicitly teach reading, writing and numeracy within discipline-specific content, justifying their approach with research relating to how this improves student engagement and academic achievement. In addition to learning how to design an integrated lesson and/or unit, PSTs will also develop their understanding of evidence-based assessment practices relating to diagnostic, formative and summative assessment. Drawing upon a developmental assessment approach, PSTs will learn how to select a range of evidence-based assessment practices to evaluate progress, adjust instruction, provide targeted feedback, and support learning. Through developing familiarity with curriculum documents and key resources, students will understand the strength and potential of integrated learning for their ongoing teaching practice. This subject includes evidence-informed instructional and assessment practices in reading, writing, numeracy and multimodal learning.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate core disciplinary knowledge in Humanities, Health and Physical Education/PDHPE, English and Mathematics
- Design learning and assessment sequences that incorporate integration across curriculum areas
- Select a range of evidence-based assessment practices to evaluate progress, adjust instruction, provide targeted feedback, and support learning.
- Demonstrate understanding of integrated curriculum and how it contributes to learning and disciplinary knowledge in Humanities, Health and Physical Education/PDHPE, English and Mathematics

Subject: FRAMING IDENTITIES AND CULTURES (EDU5012)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will develop core knowledge, skills and dispositions required to be equity-oriented teachers. The subject foregrounds intercultural understanding and the development and deepening of students’ understanding of the diversity among Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander languages, societies, cultures, world views, histories and contemporary experiences. You will examine your own cultural identities, representations of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities and become critically reflective of the historical circumstances that have impacted people in Australia. You will develop an understanding of theories, strategies, and pedagogical approaches that align with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander knowledge and perspectives.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate understanding of culturally safe and inclusive resources and pedagogies from an Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspective.
- Demonstrate and apply knowledge of and respect for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander cultures and histories
- Illustrate and evaluate how media representations and personal histories and experiences shape and influence perceptions of and assumptions about Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples and community life.
- Critically reflect on ethics, beliefs and emerging teaching philosophies.
- Explore and analyse theories of cultural identity, and productively engage in challenging conversations around equity, diversity and Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander education.

Subject: LEARNING DESIGN AND CLASSROOM MANAGEMENT  (EDU5013)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject introduces Pre-Service Teachers (PSTs) to knowledge and skills that educators need for impactful pedagogies in their teaching. PSTs will examine and engage with national and state-based curricula and learn evidence-based instructional and assessment strategies. Guided by the framework of Universal Design for Learning (UDL), PSTs will learn how to sequence learning experiences for learners across a range of ability levels and contexts, including digital learning environments. PSTs will also learn about the nature and efficacy of both proactive and responsive positive behaviour management practices, and plan and rehearse effective techniques for use in the classroom. Digital literacy is embedded throughout, with PSTs developing understanding of teaching and learning using digital tools, alongside ethical and legal concerns. This subject involves 15 or 20 days of placement. Successfully completing this placement is a requirement to pass this subject.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate understanding of a broad range of evidence that can be used to inform teaching and learning, including for managing students with challenging behaviours.
- Design learning sequences that align with universal design for learning principles.
- Plan learning sequences that include classroom rules and routines and other proactive practices for establishing a structured, safe, and positive learning environment to enhance student engagement.
- Demonstrate integration of digital skills and tools to enhance learning experiences, demonstrating understanding of the relevant issues and the strategies available to support the safe, responsible and ethical use of ICT in learning.
- Evaluate student data for the purpose of providing feedback and setting learning goals.
- Demonstrate understanding of the relationship between curriculum, pedagogy, assessment and classroom management.

Subject: FOUNDATIONS OF LANGUAGE AND EARLY READING (EDU5014)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will learn about the process of children’s oral language acquisition and how literacy is mastered. You will critically appraise a range of theoretical models describing the process of reading and consider the implications for classroom instruction. You will also explore the structure of oral language and how this pertains to early reading instruction and progress across the school years. In particular, you will be taught about linguistic concepts that are pertinent to the teaching of reading, spelling, and writing.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the linguistic structure of the English writing system and explain how this informs instruction in the core elements of literacy: reading spelling and writing.
- List and define the components of oral language and explain how oral language skills form the foundation for learning literacy.
- Describe and contrast the processes of oral language acquisition with the mastery of literacy.
- Examine various theoretical models of reading and identify those that have empirical support for translation into classroom practice.

Subject: TEACHING MATHEMATICS (EDU5015)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, pre-service teachers will explore pedagogies appropriate for the learning and teaching of mathematics in the primary years of schooling. The subject will emphasise tailoring instruction to match learner expertise and diverse ability levels, including the importance of explicit instruction (avoiding self-directed approaches) for novice learners and independent problem-solving once a student approaches proficiency in underpinning mathematical concepts. The subject will model the implementation of rich tasks that promote mathematical proficiencies and working mathematically (communication, problem solving, reasoning, understanding and fluency), mathematical inquiry, and mathematics skill development for school students across the strands, scope and sequence documents of the Australian, Victorian and NSW Curricula. An emphasis will be placed on the relationships among teacher mathematics content knowledge, pedagogy, models of learning, resource selection, differentiation and inclusive teaching and student achievement in mathematics. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander histories and cultural understanding of Primary Mathematics will be embedded. Engagement in the subject will include pre-service teachers participating in a community of learners to design and deliver teaching episodes using digital technologies to their peers for evaluation and constructive feedback.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate an understanding of mathematical knowledge and skills appropriate for primary teaching and explain the importance of this understanding for instructing novice learners (teacher-led schema development).
- Identify and apply appropriate, inclusive pedagogies for developing and assessing mathematical concepts and skills (numerical, spatial, graphical, statistical, and algebraic) and proficiencies (understanding, fluency, problem solving and reasoning).
- Justify the selection, application, and adaptation of learning resources to address all students’ learning needs, including the progressive removal of scaffolding as learners become more proficient in mathematical concepts and skills and general thinking skills.
- Evaluate mathematics learning and teaching resources accounting for learner characteristics such as novice/expert status, development of executive functioning skills, and working memory limitations.
Workload:

Subject: SCIENCE AND TECHNOLOGY (STEAM) (EDU5016)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, pre-service teachers (PSTs) will develop the knowledge, skills and competencies needed to teach science and technology (ST) in primary schools. In addition to pedagogies that deepen student learning in ST, PSTs will explore teaching ST. PSTs will identify high quality curriculum resources for teaching ST topics, which will then be used to develop an interdisciplinary unit plan. Contemporary issues and historical and cultural contributions to ST, and implications for ST teaching, will be examined. PSTs will demonstrate developing knowledge of the general capabilities and cross curricular priorities and how these intersect with the ST Curriculum.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply digital literacy knowledge to select and design inclusive science resources.
- Apply understanding of science and technology curriculum, the general capabilities and cross-curriculum priorities to learning and assessment sequences.
- Critically select and reflect on age-appropriate knowledge of historical and cultural contributions to science and technology as well as related contemporary issues, including the concepts, substance and structure of the content and assessment.
- Identify and apply contemporary pedagogical and multidisciplinary approaches, such as the 5E instructional model or a design cycle, for planning and teaching science and technology.
- Research and justify the science and technology knowledge, methods, skills and resources required to communicate science and technology understandings and findings.
- Demonstrate understanding of assessment strategies, including informal and formal, diagnostic, formative and summative approaches to assess student learning.

Subject: PROFESSIONAL IDENTITY AND ETHICS FOR EDUCATORS (EDU5017)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, students will develop their understanding of what it means to be an ethical education professional. Students will learn about the laws, formal professional codes and informal ethical frameworks that regulate and guide professional relationships in education settings. They will explore how these formal ethical practices and regulations shape decisions regarding inclusive pedagogical practices and proactive classroom management and apply these understandings during their placement. They will engage in critical reflection to consider their own values and beliefs and how these affect their professional relationships with other people in educational contexts, including with students, staff, parents/carers and communities. This subject involves a 15 or 20 day placement based on your course commencement. Successfully completing this placement is a requirement to pass this subject.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate knowledge of legal obligations, requirements and responsibilities for education professionals.
- Demonstrate knowledge of formal codes of conduct and ethics for education professionals.
- Apply formal and informal ethical frameworks to guide and justify professional practice
- Demonstrate knowledge of education ethics theories and approaches.

Subject: UNDERSTANDING RESEARCH FOR PROFESSIONAL ENQUIRY (EDU5018)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject equips you to engage with and be discerning consumers of research.
