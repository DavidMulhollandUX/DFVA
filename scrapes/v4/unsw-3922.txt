## Description
Collaboration between the arts and sciences has the potential to create new knowledge, ideas and processes beneficial to both fields. Artists, musicians and scientists approach creativity, exploration and research in different but increasingly connected ways and perspectives; when working together they open up new ways of seeing, experiencing and interpreting the world around us.

This double degree program enables students to complete a Major sequence from those available in the Bachelor of Advanced Science (Honours) and complete a Bachelor of Fine Arts, where students can study a wide range of animation and moving image, fine art, music and media art disciplines.

The typical duration of this program is 5 years full-time. For admission to the program, students must satisfy the entry requirements to both the Bachelor of Advanced Science (Honours) (3962) and the Bachelor of Fine Arts (4830) programs.

For questions regarding the requirements for the program, students should consult staff in the the Nucleus Student Hub.

Subject: Advancing Science (SCIF1001)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course introduces the interdisciplinary and collegial nature of science in society. Big problems in science are used as a framework to help students develop foundational skills essential for their careers, including thinking like a scientist, assessing the quality of information, working in interdisciplinary teams, project management, and science communication. The problems explored are varied and change each year, but may include topics such as global pandemics and human disease, nuclear innovations, and the flammable earth. The course provides opportunities for students to network and share perspectives and experiences with peers from a range of scientific fields, and to work toward their career goals. Assumed knowledge: This course is intended for first-year students who have completed 24 units of credit toward their program. No specific disciplinary knowledge is required.
Learning Outcomes:
- Communicate project plans and outcomes in written and visual formats to expert and general audiences.
- Critically analyse relevant data, literature, and resources to address a project brief.
- Work effectively in a project team, including managing self and evaluating personal and peer performance.
- Use reflective practice in scientific thinking and professional work.
- Explain how interdisciplinary approaches are critical for addressing issues of science and society.
- Apply the scientific method, introductory project management, and problem solving strategies to address a project brief in a team-based setting.

Subject: Level 2 Research Skills (SCIF2001)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this course, students will discover the university research environment by collaborating with peers and UNSW researchers on an authentic research project. Students can choose a disciplinary-focused or interdisciplinary project, across a broad range of fields in the Faculty of Science. Students will then be placed into a project group based on their preferences and availability. Students will work independently and with their project group throughout the term and receive guidance and mentorship from active researchers with expertise in the project area. To support skill development and peer connection across research groups, students will complete a self-paced online module and participate in a 1-hour interactive workshop each week. These activities provide guidance on how to complete the project milestones and assessments (e.g., literature review, video presentation), as well as introduce key skills and activities in academic research (e.g., ethics, data analysis and communicating results). By completing this course, students will gain important research and transferable graduate skills that are highly valued across a range of graduate pathways and will prepare them for further research opportunities in their program.
Learning Outcomes:
- Communicate approaches, outcomes and implications of a scientific investigation.
- Respectfully discuss relevant scientific topics through a variety of formats.
- Refine an individual research question and approach, considering the available research tools and relevant scientific literature.
- Apply discipline-specific research and data analysis skills and capabilities to investigate problems in science.
- Evaluate personal and collaborative effectiveness and make suggestions to enhance research progress.

Subject: Graduation Portfolio (SCIF3010)
Credit Points: 0
Overview: You've spent years developing scientific knowledge and skills—but can you confidently explain your unique value to potential employers? Do you know how your science degree translates to career opportunities beyond the lab? Can you articulate what makes you stand out in a competitive job market? These questions and more will be addressed in SCIF3010 Graduation Portfolio, your final step in the Bachelor of Science or Bachelor of Advanced Science programs. SCIF3010 is a compulsory capstone course that must be completed in the final term of your enrolment . Through three focused fully online modules delivered via Moodle and the SciConnect platform, you'll explore target industries and career pathways, assess your program learning outcomes and transferable skills, and develop a compelling digital portfolio that showcases your capabilities. The course requires approximately 15 hours of engagement and uses a Satisfactory/Unsatisfactory grading basis. By completion, you'll have transformed your academic achievements into a powerful professional narrative, equipped with the tools and confidence needed to successfully transition from university student to science professional. An overall Satisfactory grade is required to progress to graduation from your Bachelor of Science or Bachelor of Advanced Science program.
Learning Outcomes:
- Research the landscape of a target industry and the professional skills used in a chosen field.
- Identify, evidence, and articulate Program Learning Outcomes, UNSW Graduate Attributes, and transferable skills through digital portfolio development.
- Use written communication skills to convey unique value as a graduate through professional branding and effective presentation of career readiness to potential employers.
