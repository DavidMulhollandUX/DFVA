## Description
Dietitians are health professionals who apply the science of food and nutrition to promote health, prevent and treat disease and optimise the health of individuals and communities. The Master of Dietetics and Food Innovation at UNSW is an Australian-first, combining studies in dietetics and food innovation to ensure you develop a holistic understanding of food: where it comes from, how it is made available to people, and the influence of these factors on people’s food choices, health and wellbeing.

In addition to learning the scientific and clinical skills you require for practice, you will develop your skills in ethical and empathetic care, collaborative teamwork, cultural safety and cross-cultural communication, creative and critical thinking and innovative problem solving. These skills will enable you to influence, promote change, and solve critical contemporary and future issues in food, nutrition and equity. The skills acquired in the program allows you to seek employment in a wide range of sectors, including in patient care, health promotion, food policy, food businesses and other food-related organisations, agriculture and technology, in industry, government and not for profit organisations.

The first year of the program includes training in clinical dietetics, medical nutrition therapy and counselling, alongside training in food science. This will prepare you for the second and final year of study which includes a minimum of 100 days of workplace-based training in a variety of settings and a research project which develops investigative skills.

This program is only available as part of the integrated Bachelor of Nutrition/ Master of Dietetics and Food Innovation double-degree (3894) and is not offered as a separate standalone program for direct entry.

Subject: Food Microbiology (FOOD2320)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This lecture-laboratory course introduces students to the fundamental concepts of food microbiology, including the ecology, biochemistry, isolation, enumeration, and identification of bacteria, yeasts, fungi, and viruses associated with foods and beverages. The course is structured around three major areas: microbial ecology, foodborne microbial diseases, and food fermentation, all of which are directly linked to the quality, safety, and production efficiency of foods and beverages. • Food microbial ecology explores the associations of microorganisms with foods, focusing on major spoilage species and the chemical, physical, and sensory changes they cause. • Foodborne microbial diseases covers the characteristics of key pathogenic organisms, their pathogenicity, associations with specific foods, and strategies for controlling their occurrence. • Food fermentation examines the principles of microbial ecology and biochemistry of fermentation, with applications to major fermented foods and beverages such as wine, bakery products, dairy products, cocoa beans, and soy sauce. A strong emphasis is placed on the microbiological examination of foods through both lectures and laboratory work. Students gain a solid theoretical foundation as well as practical skills in identifying and enumerating major groups and species of food microorganisms, using both standard techniques and modern rapid methods.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse major foodborne pathogenic microorganisms, their pathogenicity, association with foods, and other factors contributing to outbreaks of diseases.
- Explain the occurrence, growth, and metabolic activities of the major groups of microorganisms in foods and how these impact on food safety and spoilage.
- Discuss the contribution of major microorganism groups and individual microorganisms to the production and quality of fermented foods and beverages.
- Perform microbiological analyses of foods using standard cultural methods and modern rapid methods.
- Create strategies and measures to control foodborne microbial diseases.

Subject: Food Products and Ingredients Technology (FOOD8010)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: As modern consumers, we have come to demand easy access to a vast and continuous array of safe, nutritious, and functional food. However, raw food materials are typically unpalatable and highly perishable. Further, ensuring a food supply that is abundant, sustainable, and safe to eat depends on using the most appropriate methods to grow, process, preserve, package, and distribute a variety raw food ingredients. As future food technologists, you must understand how food ingredients behave physically, chemically, and microbiologically in their environment to select the best ways to transform raw food materials into safe and saleable food products. In this course, you will learn about the major raw food categories within our food industry, such as meat, cereals, milk, eggs, sugar, fish/seafood, fruit and vegetables, fats and oils, soy, coffee and tea. You will investigate their physical and chemical characteristics, their preservation, and the common technologies used to process them. You will also investigate the principles of some fundamental food preservation methods including thermal processing, freezing and chilling, dehydration, modified atmosphere and chemical preservation.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain and/or predict some of the physical, chemical and biological characteristics of food products based on their constituent ingredients and product environment.
- Investigate and explain the major factors affecting the organoleptic properties and keeping quality of common food ingredients.
- Analyse and explain the underlying food preservation principles at work in common food products.
- Critique scientific literature and effectively communicate scientifically supported arguments in a collaborative peer environment.
- Demonstrate the principles of major food preservation methods and apply them in real world examples.

Subject: Health Research Project  (HLTH5004)
Credit Points: 8
Overview: In this course, you will contribute to an in-depth study of a relevant health science issue. You will apply the research skills you have acquired throughout your program to: identify a specific issue that requires investigation, formulate a research question, ethically and systematically collect relevant data, analyse, interpret and discuss your findings, draw conclusions, articulate the implications of your results, and disseminate the findings to the target audience. The research project may be a negotiated student-led proposal or be a proposal offered by an academic supervisor. Projects may be in a cross-disciplinary partnership with another School or Faculty at UNSW, or with an external stakeholder. You will be expected to carry out the research project independently, with the support of academic supervisor(s). To fulfil the research project requirements of your degree, you must enrol in this course over three terms (3 x 8UOC) to complete a 24UOC research project. The assessment tasks for your research project are spread over three terms. You will receive an ‘EC’ (enrolment continuing) on your academic transcript at the end of your first and second term of HLTH5004. Once all the assessment tasks have been completed and graded at the end of your third term, a numeric grade will be applied to your third term of HLTH5004. Further details on the UNSW grade definitions can be found here: https://www.student.unsw.edu.au/grade
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate professional and effective skills in project planning, time management, teamwork, ethical practice and reflective practice.
- Formulate a research question and propose appropriate methods to investigate the question.
- Manage the collection, analysis, interpretation and synthesis of data throughout the research process together with a supervisor(s) and within ethical standards.
- Demonstrate effective oral and written communication skills that enables information to be disseminated clearly and concisely and be of a standard acceptable to a scientific journal and conference.
