## Career Opportunities & Employment Outcomes
Graduates of the School's programs have always enjoyed an excellent reputation among translation and interpreting agencies and clients for their market-readiness and high level of professionalism. The GCAI, with its heightened emphasis on digital translation and interpreting technology trends, will further add to the marketability of our graduates in an increasingly globalised market. The program is NAATI endorsed. Students will need to seek NAATI accreditation to become a Certified Translator.

  * [ TRIN7140 Translation in a Globalised Marketplace ](https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=TRIN7140)
  * [ TRIN7210 Translation in Business Settings ](https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=TRIN7210)

Subject: Professional Skills for Translators and Interpreters (TRIN7120)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course focusses on core preparation skills for professional translators and interpreters. Students will acquire the skills to effectively research for translation and interpreting assignments, including analysing and organising information. Concordance tools, electronic corpora and glossary building will also be covered, in addition to professional ethics, business skills, networking and how to adapt to market demands. Topics covered will include government, international organisations, foreign affairs, social policy and the environment.
Learning Outcomes:
- develop a solid understanding of the core skills required for professional translators and interpreters.
- become equipped with the knowledge and tools necessary to conduct research for translation and interpreting assignments.
- effectively build and manage glossaries and navigate complex topics.
- develop an understanding of professional ethics, business skills and networking for translators and interpreters.
Assessment Tasks:
- Three Reflections Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Professional & ethical case analysis Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Essay about Australian society/culture Online: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Ad Hoc Workshop 2 Hours/ Week One 2 hour class (lecture or workshop) held per week.

Subject: Translation in a Globalised Marketplace (TRIN7140)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides students with the fundamental skills to undertake translation in both directions of non specialised texts and gives an overview of the digital tools available to translators. Students will be trained in specific translation approaches and strategies, including text analysis skills.
Learning Outcomes:
- identify and articulate the main trends of translation in a globalised market, including their commonalities and respective features.
- acquire an understanding of the different digital tools available to translators and the use of digital platforms/media in professional practice.
- apply knowledge of fundamental translation theories in analysing and solving the issues involved in translating non-specialised texts.
- develop knowledge of ethical standards and their application to real-life situations.
Assessment Tasks:
- Reflective report on translation assignments Online: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- In-class translation tests Identity Verified In-person Online: 40% (20% x 2 Tests)% (Hurdle: False)
- End-of-semester exam Identity Verified In-person Online: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=TRIN7210 =====
Subject: Translation in Business Settings (TRIN7210)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides students with the fundamental skills to translate business-related texts from a broad range of industries and sectors. Students will be trained in specific translation approaches and strategies to be able to operate within the translation marketplace with a broad range of commercial texts.
Learning Outcomes:
- identify and articulate the main trends of translation in business settings, including their commonalities and respective features
- acquire an understanding of the different digital tools available to translators and the use of digital platforms/media in professional practice in a business context
- apply knowledge of fundamental translation theories in analysing and solving the issues involved in translating business texts
- develop knowledge of ethical standards and their application to real-life situations in relation to business translation
Assessment Tasks:
- Practical translation assessment 1 (assignment) Online: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Practical translation assessment 2 (in class) Identity Verified In-person: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Practical translation assessment 3 (assignment) Online: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Practical translation assessment 4 (in class) Identity Verified In-person: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Contact 3 Hours/ Week

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=TRIN7240 =====
Subject: Translating with Digital Tools (TRIN7240)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides students with key skills required for the effective use of digital technology in translation. Areas covered will include: translation memory (TM) and other computer-assisted translation (CAT) tools, machine translation (MT) and post-editing. Self-editing and revision skills will also be covered.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop key skills for the effective use of digital technology in translation.
- Create and develop translation memory and term bases.
- Develop post-editing, self-editing and revision skills.
- Demonstrate an awareness of ethical issues in translation, including copyright, privacy and confidentiality in the age of digital technology.
Assessment Tasks:
- Post-editing assignments Online: 50% (2 x 25%)% (Hurdle: False)
- Post-editing examination Online: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week
