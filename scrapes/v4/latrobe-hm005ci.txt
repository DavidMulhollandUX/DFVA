## Master of Rehabilitation Counselling and Mental Health - City Campus
##### HM005CI
##### 240 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
If you’d like to build a career that makes a meaningful difference, La Trobe’s accredited Master of Rehabilitation Counselling and Mental Health will give you the foundations for a rewarding career that supports others through huge life changes.

Develop your professional knowledge, skills and expertise with this unique combination of rehabilitation counselling and mental health subjects.

You’ll get hands-on experience and will graduate prepared to work in rehabilitation counselling, as well as broader counselling settings such as mental health and Employee Assistance Program (EAP) counselling.

Explore a diverse range of workplaces and cultures. You’ll learn how to support individuals and communities through disability, disadvantage and injury – helping them to reach their full potential and reintegrate into work and the community.

You'll learn:

  * Professional counselling practice  * Acquire skills that are essential for effective counselling practice through examining professional, ethical and legal issues that affect counselling practice in Australia.

  *  Mental health counselling  * Understand the different types of mental health conditions and how they’re diagnosed. Learn how to effectively treat clinical and sub-clinical mental health conditions.

  *  Rehabilitation counselling  * Understand the interpersonal helping process, especially as it applies to rehabilitation and behaviour change. Explore different counselling models and techniques (e.g. Motivational Interviewing).

  * Employee Assistance Program counselling  * Develop specialised skills to work effectively and ethically as a counsellor in workplace settings.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Design, implement, evaluate and where necessary revise theoretically informed evidence-based counselling and behaviour change interventions to facilitate desired long term vocational and avocational outcomes.
**2.**
Plan, implement, and evaluate client-centred assessment to facilitate treatment outcomes in complex environments.
**3.**
Critically reflect on rehabilitation counselling and mental health counselling practice through monitoring and evaluation of own work and develop plans to meet personal and professional development needs.
**4.**
Apply advanced and specialist theory, philosophy, research and evaluation methods to facilitate rehabilitation counselling and counselling practice.
**5.**
Apply complex and specialised counselling skills and psychological interventions with creativity and initiative to meet complex patient needs in rehabilitation counselling and counselling contexts.
**6.**
Generate professional documentation and reports for various stakeholders within complex rehabilitation counselling and counselling contexts.
**7.**
Practice in an ethical and socially responsible manner as a health care practitioner and/or researcher.
**8.**
Appraise and apply legal, ethical and professional responsibilities of a counsellor and rehabilitation counsellor in complex environments and make referrals where appropriate.

### Course features
### Work based learning (placement) requirements
Compulsory placements (Work Based Learning)
During this course, you will participate in one or more supervised Work Based Learning (WBL) placements designed to allow you to practice newly acquired skills, behaviours and knowledge while integrating theory with practice. The placements will immerse you in professional work culture, enabling you to develop and further enhance your skills. WBL provides you with the opportunity to gain valuable industry experience at one or more of our local, interstate or international industry partners or agencies. WBL placements will generally be arranged by the College based Placements Operation Team or Experiential Learning Team at La Trobe University. The number of hours undertaken can vary considerably, depending on the requirements of the course. Students may incur additional costs and it may be necessary to travel or relocate to complete a specific placement. Please refer to the individual subject entries for more information.
### Work integrated learning opportunities
Industry presentations, Industry informed curriculum, Industry-informed projects, Interprofessional learning, Career development learning, Professional simulations
### Career outcomes
After graduation, you could work across a wide range of industries, including allied health, education, government, sports, and more.

Possible roles include:

  * Rehabilitation counsellor
  * Support people who are injured or experiencing disability or disadvantage, through the counselling process, to reintegrate into work and/or the community.

  * Counsellor
  * Work with individuals who are experiencing personal difficulties and help them to make meaningful and helpful changes geared toward promotion of wellbeing in their lives.

  * Mental health counsellor
  * Promote individual and/or community mental health and wellbeing through helping individuals, families, groups and/or communities to process difficult emotions, and through assessment and treatment of mental health disorders and trauma.

  * Employee Assistance Program (EAP) counsellor
  * Assist employees, through the counselling process, with personal problems impacting their work performance, health and/or wellbeing.

  * Student wellbeing coordinator
  * Provide welfare support, through the counselling process and coordinate development and wellbeing programs for students.

Subject: PROFESSIONAL COUNSELLING PRACTICE (PHE5001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Working effectively and ethically as a counselor requires the development of a core set of professional knowledge, skills and expertise. In this subject you will examine ethical, legal and professional considerations that are relevant to professional counselling practice in Australia. As well, you will develop the core meta-skills that are fundamental to effective and ethical work as a counselor. These include: the ability to conduct an effective intake interview; how to identify and address ongoing learning needs; the development of effective processes and networks for referral; and how to develop clear formulations of clients’ problems within complex systems.
Learning Outcomes:
- Conduct an effective intake interview, and critically evaluate your performance.
- Reflect critically about relevant ethical, legal, and professional considerations to counselling issues
- Develop formulations of clients’ problems within complex systems with multiple stakeholders.
- Critically draw on the evidence base and available scholarship of practice to evaluate your competence in working with clients with particular conditions in varying contexts, and access professional expertise, supervision, and other professional resources (e.g. referral).

Subject: MENTAL HEALTH COUNSELLING A (PHE5002)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Effective mental health counselling begins with a highly developed understanding of the types of mental health conditions that people present with and how they are diagnosed. In this subject you will examine how clinical and subclinical mental health conditions are assessed and diagnosed in Australia. You will critically evaluate the reliability and validity of the most common systems for diagnosing mental health conditions in Australia and the assessment tools used in diagnosing mental health conditions in primary health care settings and more broadly in community practice. You will also be able to demonstrate how to apply evidence-based knowledge to identify the presence of potential conditions that people may have not yet been diagnosed with and how to effectively use assessments of mental health, wellbeing, self-efficacy, pain, and broader social functioning.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically evaluate the reliability and validity of taxonomies of mental health conditions and assessment tools used in diagnosing mental health conditions, and the diagnoses of clients presenting with such conditions
- Synthesise knowledge of the mental health conditions people may present with in rehabilitation counselling and counselling settings, and apply this knowledge to facilitate people’s experiences, behaviour and functioning within broader social contexts
- Work collaboratively to apply evidence-based knowledge to recognise mental health conditions with which clients may presenting but not have yet been diagnosed
- Critically evaluate and employ assessments of mental health, wellbeing, self-efficacy, pain, and broader social functioning

Subject: MENTAL HEALTH COUNSELLING B (PHE5003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Once a person has been assessed and diagnosed, the focus of mental health counselling turns to the effective treatment of clinical and sub-clinical mental health conditions. Training for this requires the development of knowledge base and skills that enable the counsellor to design and implement evidence-based counselling interventions that will meet complex client needs. In this subject you will examine how to develop case formulations and appropriate treatment plans and will develop the knowledge and skills to be able to design and evaluate intervention plans utilising strategies and principles from some core evidence based counselling therapies including cognitive behavioural therapy, motivational interviewing, and acceptance and commitment therapy delivered within a person-centred therapeutic framework.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically evaluate evidence-based counselling interventions to meet complex client needs, including indications and contraindications
- Construct and appraise case formulations using evidence-based psychosocial concepts and literature and clinical understanding of relevant cultural and social factors
- Design and evaluate intervention plans utilising cognitive behavioural therapy, motivational interviewing, and acceptance and commitment therapy principles and strategies within a person-centred therapeutic framework

Subject: FUNDAMENTALS OF EMPLOYEE ASSISTANCE PROGRAM COUNSELLING (PHE5004)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Working effectively as an Employee Assistance Program (EAP) counsellor requires a highly developed set of inter- and intrapersonal skills that are common to all counselling settings. However, it also requires the development of a nuanced set of skills that are needed to work effectively and ethically in these settings. In this subject you will examine the ethical and practical challenges of EAP work which include working with multiple stakeholders and their competing demands, and developing the knowledge and skills to be able to design and implement short-term effective counselling interventions that includes a well-resourced referral network capable of addressing the complex needs of clients.
Learning Outcomes:
- Work collaboratively to assess the ethical challenges of EAP work, and develop plans to manage the inevitable conflicts between stakeholder demands and ethical practice.
- Critically reflect on the practical complexities of working with multiple clients from a single workplace, to be able to counsel effectively and ethically.
- Design and plan for the use of different counselling modalities (onsite, offsite, telehealth) to meet the needs of multiple stakeholders in complex environments.
- Design and plan to implement short-term counselling interventions and the effective referral networks required to meet the needs of working in the EAP environment.

Subject: THE SERVICE USER PERSPECTIVE IN HEALTH CARE (PHE5SUP)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you focus on the development and implementation of client-centred services for people with disability or chronic illness. You are given the opportunity to explore disability and chronic illness from the perspective of the person with the disability or chronic illness and how this perspective can be reflected in effective support services. Content is organised into three modules: (i) client related psychological and social dimensions of disability and chronic illness, and their relationship to effective service provision, (ii) self-management models of service delivery: theoretical approaches, strategies and interventions which facilitate involvement of the person in the management of their own condition, and (iii) the service user as a consumer: models and strategies regarding service user participation in health service planning and delivery.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse, generate and implement solutions to sSolve complex problems related to how social support can impact on behaviour in rehabilitation settings and implementation of social support needs assessments
- Identify and evaluate concepts of personal control impacts on behaviour in rehabilitation settings and apply to provision of effective client-centred services in these settings.
- Identify, analyse and critically assess the concept of adjustment to chronic conditions and a client's coping style and attitudes can impact on behaviour in rehabilitation settings and demonstrate evidence-based practice to provide more effective practice, i.e. client-centred services, in these settings
- Critique and apply an advanced and specialised body of conceptual, theoretical and practical evidence base to the rehabilitation counselling environment within a changing health care environment
- Identify, analyse and synthesise application of contemporary evidence-based theories, concepts and practices for service user involvement.
