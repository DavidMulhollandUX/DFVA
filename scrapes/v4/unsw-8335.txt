## Description
The Master of Mining Engineering is a postgraduate degree program designed to provide advanced education in mining engineering, combining both theoretical knowledge and practical industry experience. It is ideal for individuals who are either already working in the mining sector or planning to enter this dynamic field. This program offers a flexible structure, allowing students to complete the degree on either a full-time or part-time basis, depending on their professional and personal commitments.

The Master of Mining Engineering will be awarded after successful completion of 72 Units of Credit (UOC), consisting of a combination of core courses, prescribed electives, and a research project. The program is designed to meet the needs of working professionals in the mining industry, offering many of the courses in modular formats (block periods), which allows students to attend face-to-face or online lectures without interrupting their work schedules. A number of electives may only be offered every two years, providing flexibility for students in terms of course selection.

NOTE:
Some electives may only be offered every two years.
Courses are offered in intensive block mode or fully distance. Students should contact the school on mere.admin@unsw.edu.au for a copy of the latest MINE PG Coursework Calendar.
International students on a student visa are required to study no more than one third of the program online.

## Admission Requirements
**Limitations on Recognition of Prior Learning**:
Recognised prior learning (RPL) may be available towards the program award for selected courses within the program. A maximum of 24 UOC of RPL will be considered on application to the Program Authority, and according to the UNSW RPL Procedure. Criteria for approval will include: equivalent course duration at a recognised institution, equivalent course content and assessment.

All courses completed towards the Graduate Diploma in Mining Engineering at UNSW will be recognised for the purpose of advanced standing for the Master of Mining Engineering.

**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
Entry to the UNSW Master of Mining Engineering requires one of the following:

- Successful completion of the UNSW Graduate Diploma in Mining Engineering with 65 WAM, or

- a Bachelor (Honours) degree in Mining Engineering, or

- a Bachelor (Honours) degree in a cognate discipline such as Engineering Geology, Civil Engineering or Geotechnical Engineering, with a minimum of one (1) year industry experience in Mining.

Subject: Mining Geomechanics (MINE8140)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course is designed to introduce engineers and geologists to the major geomechanics components associated with mining operations, primarily underground. The course will cover the rock mass responses to excavations in both coal and metalliferous operations. It is, therefore, ideally suited to engineers or geologists who have an understanding and experience in the mining industry but are seeking to develop more specialist skills in the geomechanics field. The course content will include but not limited to the following components: Fundamentals of rock mechanics Overview of soil mechanics Rock mass classification Coupled rock behaviour Numerical methods in mine geomechanics Rock testing Managing risks in geotechnical engineering Geotechnical design principles and design acceptance The course is structured to provide an initial overview of basic principles applied to rock mechanics and geotechnical engineering, plus the major geomechanical properties and behavioural characteristics of rock material. This then leads to the application of these principles to the practical issues of site investigation, excavation design and ground reinforcement. An important component will be an emphasis on the interdependencies between geotechnical parameters and mine design/operational decisions and requirements. The link between geological and engineering disciplines is an important component in successfully managing these dependencies. The course will be presented through a series of lectures, facilitated discussion sessions and case studies, but will also include practical tutorial sessions and laboratory exercises.
Learning Outcomes:
- Show a good understanding of key numerical methods used in mining rock mechanics.
- Describe the basic mechanical properties of rock and how these are applied to analyse problems in mining geomechanics.
- Demonstrate a sound working knowledge of fundamental mechanisms and geotechnical principles within the context of practical mining applications.
- Recognise the role and importance of these principles in a comprehensive range of mining applications, both from a technical perspective, and from the risk and operational management perspective.

- Evaluate the implications of mineralogical characteristics for processing requirements.
- Analyse basic mass balance calculations.
- Analyse commonly used mineral and hydrometallurgical processing routes in base metals and precious metals processing.
- Analyse the fundamental principles of crushing and grinding in mineral processing.

Subject: Space Resources Engineering (MINE8870)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In-situ resource utilisation (ISRU) — the concept of using local materials in space to support human activity - is widely recognised as one of the most transformative ideas for the future of space exploration. It underpins the emerging field of space mining, which has so far primarily focused on overcoming engineering challenges. Many scientists agree that, in the near future, we may begin extracting resources from off-Earth bodies such as asteroids, comets, the Moon, Mars, and its moons. These celestial bodies represent some of the most distant and untapped sources of materials ever considered for human use. For humanity to establish a sustained presence in deep space, including potential colonisation, it will be essential to produce key materials and products locally - making ISRU a critical enabler of space settlement. To succeed, space mining operations will need to function autonomously or be remotely operated from orbiting stations, spacecraft, or Earth. The uncertainties and technical challenges involved are significant and will require the development of new knowledge and capabilities. As a result, mining expertise will play a central role in enabling ISRU, and engineers will need to acquire new or specialised skills to meet the demands of this frontier. In this course, students will be taken through the engineering lifecycle of new technologies and encouraged to think about how their unique skills can be applied to these projects. The course aims to provide information on space resources, develop a knowledge of technical and business risks and develop a scoping study for space mining operations. The course will be in short course format (5 days) including a series of lectures from research and industry experts, tutorials, and individual and group projects.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse technical and financial risks of potential space mining operations.
- Develop a scoping study level of financial and technical models for space mining operations for different commodities.
- Review the current status of space resource utilisation.
- Apply systems engineering principles for feasibility of potential space mining operations.
- Employ team skills, and advanced written and oral communication skills.

Mining methods considered for efficient and safe extraction of uranium will include open cut, underground and In Situ Recovery (ISR) leach methods.
