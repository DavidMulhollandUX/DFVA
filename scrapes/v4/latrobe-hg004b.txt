## Graduate Diploma in Midwifery - Bendigo
##### HG004B
##### 120 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
If you're a registered nurse with a passion for helping new parents safely navigate pregnancy, birth and early parenthood, fast-track your entry to  midwifery practice with La Trobe's Graduate Diploma in Midwifery.

Our flexible 12-month course focuses on contemporary issues in Australian midwifery practice by combining lectures, workshops, labs and simulations with hands-on clinical placements in some of Melbourne and regional Victoria's most respected maternity services. 

By completing La Trobe's Graduate Diploma in Midwifery, you will:

  * Develop the skills and knowledge to provide best-practice, woman-centred and evidence-based midwifery care.
  * Apply your skills and knowledge in a real-world clinical settings by taking advantage of La Trobe's long-standing partnerships with providers like Mercy Health, The Royal Women's Hospital, Bendigo Health, Goulburn Valley Health and Albury-Wodonga Health.
  * Promote the role of the midwife and become an agent of change for maternity care reform.
  * Understand the impacts of digital health in a maternity care context
  * Connect with a community of practicing clinicians and world-leading researchers in women's and maternal health in the School of Nursing and Midwifery and the Judith Lumley Centre.
  * Graduate eligible to apply to become a registered midwife.
  * Upon completion, use your qualification to apply for our Master of Midwifery Practice.

### Completion requirements
To qualify for the award of Graduate Diploma in Midwifery, students must complete 120 credit points consisting of:

120 core credit points 

Students will attend a minimum of 100 antenatal visits with women

Under the supervision of a midwife, the student must act as the primary birth attendant for 30 women who experience a spontaneous vaginal birth.  Additionally, provide direct and indirect care to an additional 10 women throughout the first stage of labour, where possible, during birth (regardless of mode).

Students will attend a minimum of 100 postnatal visits with women and, where possible, their babies

Students will gain experience in caring for 40 women with complex needs across pregnancy, labour, birth or the postnatal period

Students will undertake 20 full examinations of a newborn infant

Students must complete 10 Continuity of Care Experiences. A Continuity of Care Experience involves engaging with 10 women and attending four antenatal and two postnatal episodes of care and, for the majority of women, the labour and birth. This involves being “on call” to attend these births.

Occasionally, the duration of the program may be extended beyond the 12 months to support the completion of the course clinical requirements.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Perform comprehensive assessments in partnership with the woman by collecting and analysing information to plan and evaluate care
**2.**
Promote inclusive and responsive professional midwifery practice in partnership with women from diverse backgrounds with sensitivity to culture, values and beliefs and support the woman’s capacity for self-determination
**3.**
Demonstrate strategies for sustainable professional practice including ongoing professional learning and development and monitoring the health and wellbeing of self and colleagues and through reflection, action and feedback
**4.**
Demonstrate midwifery practice that aligns with relevant legal parameters and professional standards, ethical frameworks, codes and guidelines
**5.**
Appraise and critique research evidence and apply this to midwifery practice to provide safe, high quality, woman-centred midwifery care
**6.**
Provide holistic midwifery care in collaboration with the intra and interdisciplinary health care team, in order to optimise health and wellbeing of women and baby
**7.**
Advocate for the role of the midwife, the midwifery profession and continuity of midwifery care to improve perinatal outcomes for women and families

### Work based learning (placement) requirements
Compulsory placements (Work Based Learning)
During this course, you will participate in one or more supervised Work Based Learning (WBL) placements designed to allow you to practice newly acquired skills, behaviours and knowledge while integrating theory with practice. The placements will immerse you in professional work culture, enabling you to develop and further enhance your skills. WBL provides you with the opportunity to gain valuable industry experience at one or more of our local industry partners. WBL placements will be arranged by the student in conjunction with the Placements Operation Team. The number of hours undertaken can vary considerably, depending on the requirements of the course. Students may incur additional costs and it may be necessary to travel or relocate to complete a specific placement. Please refer to the individual subject entries for more information.

### Additional costs
Prior to commencing a placement, students are required to meet all of the requirements of the degree in relation to immunisation. Students must visit a medical practitioner who will complete and sign the Immunisation and Health Record form.

Depending on a students immunisation history, the overall costs for vaccination may be up to AUD$1030. Students are responsible for all costs incurred. Further information will be provided post enrolment.
Applicants will be required to hold a valid Police Check which is renewed annually. Students will have access to a reduced fee when applying via a La Trobe University provider. For more information on what a police check entails, refer to the Victoria Police website.
Students studying at the Bendigo campus will undertake their clinical placements throughout regional Victoria. This may involve travelling to health services outside their contracted clinical placement to complete rotations in higher acuity settings. Students are responsible for the expenses associated with completing these placements including travel and accommodation, but may be eligible for available bursaries or scholarships.
Applicants will be required to hold a valid Working with Children Check (WWCC). There is no cost for a volunteer WWCC. For more information and costs refer to the Working with Children Check Victoria website.

Subject: FOUNDATIONS OF MIDWIFERY CARE (MID4001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will be introduced to the foundations of midwifery care including professional issues, midwifery philosophies that underpin midwifery practice as well as the different governance frameworks in place to support safe, collaborative midwifery care. You will also be introduced to the fundamentals of pregnancy care through a primary health care lens. Using a blended learning approach including lectures, online activities, skills labs and clinical placement, you will develop skills and attributes for providing evidence-based woman-centred care during pregnancy.
Learning Outcomes:
- Perform thorough assessments to enable appropriate consultation and referral to provide safe and effective midwifery care to women during pregnancy
- Critically evaluate and apply available evidence to inform best practice in midwifery during the pregnancy period
- Analyse the contribution of the midwife in improving health outcomes for women and babies
- Plan, provide and reflect on woman-centred midwifery care with women, their families and colleagues during the pregnancy period
- Apply relevant legal and ethical frameworks to midwifery care

Subject: LABOUR, BIRTH AND POSTNATAL CARE (MID4002)
Credit Points: 30
Overview: In this subject you will be introduced to the fundamentals of midwifery care of a woman during labour and birth, as well as postnatal care up to six weeks postpartum. Care of the neonate including optimal infant feeding will be explored. Your learning will encompass common labour and birth interventions and care of the infant requiring additional care in the postnatal setting. Using a blended learning approach comprising of lectures, online activities, skills labs, clinical simulation, and clinical placement, you will develop skills and attributes for providing evidence-based woman-centred care during the intrapartum and postnatal period.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically evaluate and apply available evidence to inform best practice in midwifery during labour, birth and the postnatal periods
- Plan, provide and reflect on safe and effective neonatal care in the postnatal setting, including collaborative care for infants as required
- Promote breastfeeding in accordance with the principles of the Baby Friendly Hospital Initiative
- Perform thorough midwifery assessments to enable appropriate consultation and referral to provide safe and effective care to women during labour, birth, and the postnatal period
- Plan, provide and apply evidence-based information, assistance and advice to the woman on nutrition needs and feeding of her infant
- Plan, provide and reflect on woman-centred midwifery care with women, their families and interdisciplinary colleagues during labour, birth and the postnatal period

Subject: COMPLEX MATERNAL AND NEONATAL CARE (MID4003)
Credit Points: 30
Overview: In this subject you will build on the knowledge and skills gained in previous subjects to care for women experiencing maternity complications and emergencies, and infants experiencing complexities. You will incorporate evidence-based practice to develop skills in critical thinking, situational management, inter- and intra-professional collaborative relationships and leadership. The subject is delivered using a blended learning approach comprising of lectures, online activities, skills labs, clinical simulation and clinical placement.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically evaluate and apply current evidence to inform best practice in complex maternity and neonatal scenarios
- Identify, interpret and communicate complex clinical data to optimise care of the woman experiencing complexities
- Plan, provide and reflect on woman-centred care in collaboration with the inter- and intra-professional team in response to complex scenarios
- Critically reflect on the role of the midwife in relation to their scope of practice when managing complex scenarios
- Plan, provide and reflect on family-centred care of the deteriorating infant requiring stabilisation in collaboration with the inter- and intra-professional team

Subject: CONTEXT OF WOMEN'S LIVES AND COMPREHENSIVE MATERNITY CARE (MID4004)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will develop skills to work in partnership with women in the context of their lives. You will explore the global midwifery landscape and consider political issues that influence the maternity care women receive. You will learn effective communication skills to apply a strengths-based approach to providing individualised care to women. In preparation for entering the midwifery workforce you will also explore strategies for self-care, mitigating personal risk and building resilience to support you throughout your midwifery career. The subject is delivered using a blended learning approach, comprising face-to-face teaching, workshops and online learning modules via LMS and is supported by clinical placement.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate and justify the importance of incorporating a strengths-based approach to support women’s wellbeing
- Critically evaluate and apply available evidence to support the role of the midwife in improving health outcomes
- Demonstrate effective, sensitive communication and collaboration with colleagues, women, families and the community in a variety of professional and social contexts
- Evaluate the concepts of self-care, mitigating personal risk and building resilience in relation to midwifery practice
- Explain the importance of high-quality midwifery care in improving public health outcomes in both a local and global context

Subject: FIRST NATIONS PERINATAL HEALTH (NSM4IPH)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will reflect on aspects of your own cultural self-awareness to advance your knowledge and skills in providing effective and safe care to First Nations Australian families in the maternity context. You will explore Australian history, concepts related to traditional and contemporary Indigenous cultures, the rights of individuals and groups to self-determination, and consider how culturally safe clinical practices can contribute to closing the gap in health outcomes. The skills gained in this subject will enable you to recognise how socio-cultural diversity may impact maternity outcomes equity and access. This subject is delivered online using online activities, and synchronous lectures and workshops to support your learning.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate health inequity, the factors contributing to this, and analyse ways to close the gap in birth outcomes between First Nations and non-First Nations Australians
- Evaluate the interconnections between physical, social, historical, political and cultural factors and their impact on perinatal health outcomes by understanding First Nations concepts of health and healing.
- Development of culturally competent communication skills to facilitate therapeutic relationships, and support partnerships with First Nations families, health professionals, organisations and communities.
- Critically analyse the role of cultural safety in improving maternal and child health outcomes for First Nations Australians and how cultural safety influences practice.
- Critically reflect on personal and professional beliefs and attitudes in relation to working alongside First Nations women, babies and families.

Subject: RESEARCH AND EVIDENCE IN MIDWIFERY (NSM4REM)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will be introduced to the research and evidence in midwifery practice. You will learn the skills needed to be able to interpret and understand research evidence and draw on real life examples of midwifery related research. You will gain a broad understanding of various research designs including the application of quantitative and qualitative approaches. You will develop skills in critiquing evidence for application to maternity care. Using an online teaching mode including, online activities, and synchronous lectures and workshops you will develop skills in reviewing the literature, explore the ethics of research and apply the appropriate design and methods to specific clinical and social issues related to maternity care and practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically appraise levels of evidence and how this impacts the relevance of research to a clinical question
- Develop a search strategy and conduct a literature review related to a specific topic
- Explain the ethical issues inherent in midwifery research
- Formulate a research question based on a clinical issue related to midwifery
- Explain key principles that underpin evidence- based practice
- Justify the choice of different research designs and data collection methods
