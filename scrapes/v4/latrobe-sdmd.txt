## Diploma of Biomedicine - Melbourne (Bundoora)
##### SDMD
##### 120 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
La Trobe’s Diploma of Biomedicine is a course that focuses on the foundations of biomedicine: chemistry, biology, and human structure and function. This course is designed to provide you with a theoretical and practical grounding in the biomedicine discipline. During your one-year diploma, you’ll develop technical and practical skills and gain experience in generating and analysing scientific data and communicating findings to different audiences.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Discuss fundamental knowledge from scientific disciplines that underpin biomedicine and apply this knowledge within known contexts.
**2.**
Effectively communicate biomedical information to a defined audience in specified formats.
**3.**
Generate, record, and interpret scientific data from scientific disciplines that underpin biomedicine.
**4.**
Demonstrate and explain safe laboratory practices.

Subject: MOLECULES, GENES AND CELLS (BIO1MGC)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Living organisms, with their many intricate and intriguing processes, are composed of lifeless molecules. BIO1MGC takes a look at how those molecules are organised into the smallest unit of life, cells, across a range of organisms. BIO1MGC also covers how those cells capture light energy, break down molecules to release energy, synthesise new molecules, communicate with other cells, and how the instructions to perform those functions are stored and passed on to the next generation.
Learning Outcomes:
- Name the classes of biological molecules, describe their function in cellular processes, and describe and explain typical separation technologies for those molecules.
- Describe and distinguish the features of prokaryotic and eukaryotic (i.e. plant and animal cells, and explain the function of organelles.
- Describe and explain Mendelian genetics and its role in inheritance.
- Describe and explain basic cell biology processes, including energy metabolism (photosynthesis and cellular respiration), DNA replication, transcription and translation and their regulation, and cell signalling.
- Apply critical laboratory skills such as microscopy and micro pipetting to complete scientific experiments that examine different aspects of cell biology.
- Locate and critically evaluate scientific information and effectively communicate scientific ideas in written and oral formats.

Subject: CHEMISTRY FOUNDATIONS (CHE1CHF)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Chemistry Foundations is an introductory subject designed for students who have no or little previous experience or study in chemistry. Students will be introduced to foundation concepts, knowledge and skills that will enable these students to apply chemical principles during their university degree and future employment. This one-semester subject covers topics common to senior high-school chemistry and introductory university chemistry, that prepares students to advance to second semester chemistry. Students will encounter atomic and molecular structure, bonding, chemical reactions, organic chemistry, and physical chemistry.
Learning Outcomes:
- Communicate in written form using the language of chemistry; interpreting and using chemical symbols, conventions and nomenclature.
- Classify types of chemical reactions and predict the chemical products.
- Safely employ the scientific method to observe and measure chemical phenomena in the laboratory and communicate findings by written report.
- Manipulate quantitative mathematical relationships to solve chemistry problems such as the stoichiometric relationships that determine the quantitative behaviour of chemical systems
- Interpret thermochemistry data to determine whether reactions will occur and if they do, to what extent.
- Explain the properties of matter using concepts of chemical structure.

Subject: BURDEN OF DISEASE (MED1001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject explores the current and historical health burden posed by major communicable and non-communicable diseases, in Australia and globally. You will interrogate the worldwide causation and prevalence of high-burden diseases and learn about current and emerging prevention and treatment strategies, and expected clinical outcomes. Skills you will develop include sourcing, interrogating and describing health data.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the approaches used to prevent, diagnose and treat clinically significant diseases and injuries
- Describe the aetiology and symptoms of conditions that cause significant mortality and/or morbidity in Australia
- Extract and interpret data regarding the incidence and mortality of major health conditions
- Compare the impact on populations of medical conditions that pose major health burdens in Australia versus worldwide, and over time
- Outline factors that influence human lifespans and healthspans
- Communicate medical and scientific information coherently using audience-appropriate language individually or as part of a team

Subject: FOUNDATIONS OF BIOMEDICAL SCIENCE (MED1BSF)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Foundations in Biomedical Science introduces students to fundamental concepts for their future studies in biomedical science. These include the evidence-based and evolving nature of biomedical science and the important role of data in its evolution. You will be encouraged to develop skills in quantitative literacy and apply those skills to solve problems. Topics covered will include the emergence of evidence-based medicine, quantitative units in a biological context, conventions in describing and presenting data and an introduction to using data to solve problems. You will also establish an e-portfolio to encourage development of metacognition and reflection as they develop as biomedical scientists.
Learning Outcomes:
- Effectively communicate scientific ideas together with supporting evidence in both oral and written forms.
- Identify, analyse, interpret and represent quantitative data to solve problems in biomedical science.
- Work in teams to evaluate contemporary issues in biomedical science by making accurate observations and developing reasonable inferences.
- Describe the evidence-based nature of Biomedical Science and recognise the evolving nature and diversity of the current consensus.

Subject: INFECTIONS, PANDEMICS AND EPIDEMICS  (MIC1IPE)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Infectious diseases, both new and ancient, continue to threaten wellbeing by causing localised, epidemic or pandemic disease outbreaks. In this subject students will learn about the chain of infection, immunity and vaccination, disease prevention, and disease surveillance. Selected microorganisms will then be described and compared: the main focus is the natural habitat of the organisms (reservoirs of infection), the ways in which humans can encounter the organisms (routes of infection) and the strategies available at the individual, community and global levels to prevent disease and, in the diseased patient, to cure disease. In parallel, workshops will focus on laboratory techniques that can be used for disease diagnosis including culture-based methods, molecular methods, and immunological approaches. Workshops will culminate in an infectious disease case study.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate and interpret results from various diagnostic microbiology techniques.
- Analyse factors that can influence the emergence, re-emergence and/or spread of specific infectious diseases.
- Apply knowledge of different laboratory techniques that are used to isolate and identify microorganisms, and diagnose infectious diseases caused by these microorganisms.
- Describe and compare infectious agents, their biological features, epidemiology, disease effects and disease management, using appropriate microbiological terms and scientific concepts.
- Evaluate the different modes of transmission of infectious agents and the effectiveness of corresponding control and prevention strategies.
- Work collaboratively with peers in a small group to complete an infectious disease case study, including disease diagnosis, treatment options, and prognosis for recovery.
