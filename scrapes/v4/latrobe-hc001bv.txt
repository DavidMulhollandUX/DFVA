##### 60 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
Enhance your skills in dealing professionally with First Nations families with La Trobe's Graduate Certificate in Family Therapy: First Nations.

Delivered in partnership with The Bouverie Centre in Brunswick and grounded in the 'Black and White' teaching model that keeps culture at the heart of the process, our course gives you a thorough introduction to family therapy theories and practice, with an emphasis on maintaining sensitivity to First Nations families’ needs and experiences.

By studying La Trobe's Graduate Certificate in Family Therapy: First Nations, you will:

  * explore foundational family therapy theories and skills, utilising these ideas in the context of cultural safety and responsiveness
  * examine the importance of First Nations' family and community connections in conducting therapy in a range of contexts
  * apply theory and skills in real-world scenarios by conducting family therapy sessions
  * develop a deeper understanding of the ongoing impact of transgenerational trauma on First Nations families and communities, and apply culturally and trauma-informed practices
  * explore your own culture and experiences, identifying constraints in your work with families to increase your effectiveness in your professional practice
  * graduate eligible to apply to progress with credit to the Master of Clinical Family Therapy.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Implement the application of ethical practices that are culturally congruent
**2.**
Translate family therapy knowledge to promote positive changes for communities, workers and the broader system to support the implementation of sustainable culturally congruent systemic practice
**3.**
Appraise the congruence of systemic knowledge and practice for First Nations cultures and its contemporary application
**4.**
Critically reflect on own practice and professional development to ensure best outcomes
**5.**
Apply Systems theory and cybernetic principles in culturally congruent family therapy frameworks.

### Work based learning (placement) requirements
Compulsory placements (Work Based Learning)
During this course, you will participate in one or more supervised Work Based Learning (WBL) placements designed to allow you to practice newly acquired skills, behaviours and knowledge while integrating theory with practice. The placements will immerse you in professional work culture, enabling you to develop and further enhance your skills. WBL provides you with the opportunity to gain valuable industry experience at one or more of our local, interstate or international industry partners or agencies. WBL placements will generally be arranged by the Office of the Provost Placements and WBL Team at La Trobe University. The number of hours undertaken can vary considerably, depending on the requirements of the course. Students may incur additional costs and it may be necessary to travel or relocate to complete a specific placement. Please refer to the individual subject entries for more information.
### Career outcomes
Graduates may find work supporting families in the public and private sectors in family violence, disability, drugs and alcohol, residential care and mental health. Those already employed in these areas may find increased opportunities to extend and enhance their work with families and other systems.

Possible roles include:

  * family therapist
  * counsellor
  * case manager
  * social worker
  * family therapy teacher or supervisor.

Subject: FIRST STEPS IN FAMILY WORK (FTH4003)
Credit Points: 30
Overview: In this subject you will examine the importance of First Nations' family and community connections in conducting systemic therapy. You will explore foundational family therapy theories and skills, utilising these ideas in the context of cultural safety (working in cross cultural work contexts). You will integrate theory and skills to conduct a first family session. You will also develop a deeper understanding of the ongoing impact of transgenerational trauma on First Nations families and Communities, and apply culturally and trauma-informed practices.
Learning Outcomes:
- Translate and integrate a set of foundational family therapy concepts and skills in conducting a first session of systemic work with First Nations peoples
- Develop systemic case conceptualisations for a range of presenting issues and challenges
- Working collaboratively and individually, create a safe, respectful and family sensitive context for working with First Nations families with consideration for culture and other diversity issues.
- Working collaboratively and individually, using evidence-based knowledge and traditional and digital methods and technologies, develop and present strategies for effective and appropriate responses to the impact of a range of traumagenic factors on First Nations people.

Subject: TRANSFORMING SYSTEMIC PRACTICES FOR WORK WITH FIRST NATIONS FAMILIES (FTH4004)
Credit Points: 30
Overview: In this subject you will consolidate your theoretical knowledge and develop skills to apply your knowledge across the life cycle as well as in relevant work contexts. You will learn additional family therapy frameworks and how to translate these for use with First Nations families. You will explore your own culture and experiences, identifying constraints in your work with First Nations peoples to increase your effectiveness in your professional practice. You will be supported to safely reflect on your family of origin, current life circumstances, professional history, and other contextual influences. You will deepen your knowledge of relevant theoretical frameworks that are useful for conceptualising and appraising the relationship between the influence of your own self- factors and your interactions with clients. Therapist self- care and ethics are also discussed.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse the influence of family, community and life experiences on personal values, interpersonal style, and capacities as a family therapist/counsellor/advocate with First Nations families.
- Develop systemic case conceptualisations and ways of working that creatively integrate First Nations ways of knowing, being and doing with family therapy theories for a range of presenting issues and challenges.
- Implement a developmentally appropriate, culturally sensitive and trauma informed approach to working with First Nations peoples across the lifespan in the context of family and community.
- Implement strategies to address practice challenges posed by the intersection of identities within the contexts of community and professional life.
- Effectively manage challenging family interactions.

Overview: The academic integrity module introduces academic integrity standards to inform and support students in avoiding plagiarism and academic misconduct.
