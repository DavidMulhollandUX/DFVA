You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
The Diploma of International Tourism and Hospitality Management is an International Collaborative Programme in partnership with Sheffield Hallam University. This course is only available to Sheffield Hallam students that have completed their first year of the Bachelor of Science (Honours) International Hospitality and Tourism Management in the UK and will study their second year at La Trobe University to obtain the Diploma of International Tourism and Hospitality Management which will be presented to the student on completion of the degree.

Please note: overseas programs may be impacted by travel restrictions.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Apply problem-solving approaches, knowledge and skills to the context of tourism and hospitality services
**2.**
Identify and collect information to develop and evaluate business solutions for tourism and hospitality challenges
**3.**
Explain and apply operational knowledge of tourism
**4.**
Examine the impact of digital technologies across business, tourism and hospitality challenges
**5.**
Communicate professionally using knowledge and operational skills of the tourism sector, communicate professionally to contribute to the success of a multicultural team
**6.**
Discuss a range of alternate solutions to business and social challenges, and account for potentially competing economic, ethical, social and commercial perspectives in a responsible manner
**7.**
Communicate professionally and effectively with a range of stakeholders, using a variety of modes of communications and appropriate tools to achieve shared goals in a global context

Subject: WOMINJEKA LA TROBE: INDIGENOUS CULTURAL LITERACY FOR HIGHER EDUCATION (ABS0WOM)
Credit Points: 0
Overview: Wominjeka La Trobe is a short one-hour compulsory online subject that introduces all La Trobe students to Indigenous Australian history, culture and customs. A rich and relevant cultural heritage education is important for everyone. This topic highlights the link between Indigenous knowledge and values and broader cultural literacy. It asks you to think critically about your own attitudes, values and beliefs.
Learning Outcomes:
- Reflect critically on your own cultural attitudes, values and beliefs
- Demonstrate knowledge of and respect for Indigenous Australian perspectives and experiences, history, culture and customs, including an appreciation of the local people and country
- Recall important details regarding the history, culture, customs and people of Indigenous Australia
- Understand the need for and value of appropriate and effective intercultural communication

The work placement offers a unique opportunity to bridge the gap between theory and practice by immersing yourself in a business environment. Working alongside experienced professionals and undertaking specific business tasks you will gain valuable insights into local, national or multinational workplaces. - Apply relevant theoretical perspectives to practical work tasks.

- Demonstrate comprehensive knowledge about your host organisation and articulate how you will apply your degree knowledge to tasks and projects
- Use critical thinking, problem-solving, collaboration, and technology to address business issues.

Subject: INTERNATIONAL HUMAN RESOURCE MANAGEMENT (MGT2003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: The phenomena of internationalisation and globalisation have substantial implications for the human resource management of organisations. In addition to broad strategic concerns, issues such as understanding and appreciating differences between local environments and international staff placement are complex and have far-reaching implications. Even organisations that choose not to operate across national boundaries are affected directly by internationalisation and globalisation. In this subject, you will build on knowledge acquired in the 'Human Resource Management' subject and apply this in an international context using both theoretical and applied approaches. Thereby, you will gain an understanding of the strategies and policies available to organisations in this increasingly important area.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the important role, the objectives and purposes of international human resource management (HRM) in multinational organisations
- Define and develop international HRM plans/strategies in a variety of industries both individually and in a team environment
- Critically analyse the dynamic nature of national and international context and emerging trends and disruptive forces in the marketplace and their impact on the management of people in organisations
- Critically analyse theoretical and applied approaches to international HRM

Subject: DESTINATION MARKETING AND MANAGEMENT (THS3DMM)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will examine the management and marketing of tourism destinations at supra-national, national, state, regional and local/city levels. You will cover a variety of topics such as the tourism demand; destination choice behaviour; destination competitiveness; the role of Destination Marketing Organisations (DMOs); and the role of partnerships and alliances to promote the destination; destination image, branding and marketing. You will learn about the important link between destination marketing and strategic planning for development.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate, propose, and communicate approaches to destination marketing and management
- Analyse the strategic challenges, complexities, and special issues involved in destination marketing and management
- Analyse the role of Destination Management Organisations (DMOs) and other relevant stakeholders in destination marketing and management

Subject: TOURISM, HOSPITALITY AND EVENT FUTURES (THS3FUT)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This cutting edge subject challenges you by anticipating the future of the tourism, hospitality and events sector. By the end of this subject you will be expected to understand the range of factors likely to be influential in the future of this industry. Such factors include the demographic, political, economic and technological changes which will influence the nature, trends and participation in tourism, hospitality and events. These factors are important to be recognised to ensure the long-term sustainability of tourism in different countries and destinations.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse current and future trends in tourism, hospitality and events to inform decision-making
- Evaluate tourism, hospitality, and event strategies with respect to current and future trends
- Create innovative, plausible ideas for tourism, hospitality and events that align with current and future trends
