## Master of Architecture
##### F6001

You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
The Master of Architecture offers a coursework postgraduate program of education in the discipline of architecture and provides the second of two degrees required for professional registration.

The course addresses the timely and pressing challenges of our age and our profession, in an enquiring, sophisticated, creative, ethical, inclusive, material and architectural way. Through research-led design studios and supporting professional subjects, we provide our graduates with the confidence and capabilities to respond to the complexities of the world in which they will operate. Our project-based learning operates at a range of scales, working with diverse teams and in live social and cultural contexts to help shape our evolving built environments. With commitments to social and spatial justice, environmental sustainability, and the creation of thriving communities, we equip graduates to operate as change agents through architectural practice.

The Master of Architecture is professionally accredited and upon completion of the Master of Architecture, you will have demonstrated the (AACA) National Competency Standards as applicable to university courses.

### Structure
The course is structured in five parts: Part A. Foundations, Part B. Studio practices, Part C. Critical contexts, Part D. Disciplinary practices, and Part E. Discipline electives.

Part A. Foundations
These predominantly project based studies, required for entry point 1, provide advanced preparatory knowledge and skills of architectural design, architectural performance, history and theory, and communication strategies.

Part B. Studio practices
These studies will focus on core concepts and skills relevant to architecture design at a Master's level. Through project-based studio classes, you will gain exposure to leading design practices.Studies culminate in the final year with a rich integrated project that draws on and advances prior learning.

You will also complete occupational health, safety and environmental training particularly within the context of studying art, design and architecture.

Part C. Critical contexts
These studies focus on critical and ethical engagement with multiple histories and theoretical approaches across the disciplines of architecture, urban design and urban planning.

Part D. Disciplinary practices
These studies focus on the core business and regulatory aspects of practice as an architect within the context of multi-disciplinary collaborations and evolving construction methods and practices.

Part E. Discipline electives
These studies will provide you with the opportunity to select from a range of advanced architecture studies units, which are complementary depth units. These studies will introduce you to the methods and tactics of architectural design research and you will engage with the multidisciplinary socio-cultural, political, economic, historical, theoretical and technical dimensions relevant to architecture and urbanism.

Master's entry points

Depending on prior qualifications you may receive entry level credit (a form of block credit) which determines your point of entry to the course:

  * If you are admitted at entry level 1 you complete 144 credit points, comprising Part A, Part B, Part C, Part D and Part E, including a minimum of 48 credit points at Level 5.
  * If you are admitted at entry level 2 you complete 96 credit points, comprising Part B, Part C, Part D and Part E, including a minimum of 48 credit points at Level 5.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
acquire and apply the knowledge and range of design, technical and communication skills necessary to become a professional architect according to the relevant accreditation bodies
**2.**
collaborate in multi-disciplinary teams in diverse contexts responsive to local cultures and conditions
**3.**
identify and apply occupational health and safety measures appropriate to the area of study
**4.**
contribute to the built environment with design that advocates and responds to environmental, technical, social and cultural challenges
**5.**
contribute to advancing design research relevant to the professional practice of architecture
**6.**
integrate knowledge and skills in the design, communication and implementation of architectural projects

### Progression to further studies
Successful completion of this course with distinction (70%) average may provide a pathway to a graduate research degree.

Bachelor of Architectural Design (F2001)

### AHEGS
The Master of Architecture is a postgraduate degree. The course is taught in English and normally takes two or three years of full-time study to complete. The major study is the architectural design studio which supports graduates in gaining proficiency in the process of architectural design. In addition graduates select from a range of advanced architecture studies units, which are complementary depth units, that introduce them to the methods and tactics of architectural design research and engage with the multidisciplinary socio-cultural, political, economic, historical, theoretical and technical dimensions relevant to architecture and urbanism. Studies culminate in a final year self-directed project. The Master of Architecture is a Level 9 Masters Degree (Extended) in the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification.  The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.
