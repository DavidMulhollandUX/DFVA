## Description
The Graduate Certificate in Economics is designed for professionals wishing to upskill or those seeking to start a new career in the area of Economics. This program will provide students with a solid foundation in economic principles, quantitative reasoning and decision-making.

This program is offered in a blended on-campus/online mode. Students are required to complete 4 courses (24 UOC) comprised of 2 core courses and 2 elective course. All courses are 6 UOC.

Students who have completed the Graduate Certificate with a minimum WAM of 65 can apply to articulate into the Master of Applied Economics program and will receive 24 UOC of credit transfer.

### Enrolment guidance for waiver recipients (0 UOC)
Students who receive a waiver for ECON5103 and/or COMM5005, are required to take electives (as specified in the Prescribed Electives list) to ensure that the total UOC for the program has been completed. However, please be advised that all electives have prerequisite requirements. Students are advised to first complete all core courses. In addition, many PG Economics courses require ECON5101, ECON5102 or ECON5205 as prerequisites. As such, students are advised to contact the Program Authority for further advice on enrolment and program requirements. For students considering a progression into Master of Applied Economics (MAppEc), please note that ECON5101, ECON5102 and ECON5205 are core courses for MAppEc.

Below are recommended enrolment pathways for students who receive waiver(s):
1.	Waiver for ECON5103: Take COMM5005, ECON5101, 5102 and 5205.
2.	Waiver for COMM5005: Take ECON5103, ECON5101, 5102 and 5205.
3.	Waiver for both ECON5103 and COMM5005: Take ECON5101, 5102 and 5205 plus one elective offered by the School of Economics except ECON5258.

## Progression & Academic Rules
Students completing the Graduate Certificate in Economics (7412) with a minimum WAM of 65 can articulate to the 96 UOC Master of Applied Economics (8429) with 24 UOC of course credit applied to program (reducing program length to 72 UOC).

## Admission Requirements
**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
The minimum entry requirement is:

- a bachelor degree from an Australian university or equivalent overseas institution; and

- satisfying the UNSW Business School English Language requirement of IELTS 7.0 overall (min 6.0 in each subtest) or equivalent test.

NOTE: Assumed knowledge - A level of knowledge equivalent to a first-year undergraduate mathematics subject is assumed. Students without such knowledge are advised to contact the Postgraduate Coursework Director in Economics, complete an appropriate self-assessment of university mathematics knowledge before the commencement of their first term and complete a Maths bridging course where appropriate.

Subject: Quantitative Methods for Business (COMM5005)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Modern business decisions rely on data-driven insights and analytical precision. This course builds the mathematical and statistical toolkit needed to interpret data, model complex problems, and make informed, evidence-based decisions—skills that are indispensable in today’s data-centric economy. Students will learn to address economic and business problems using analytical and computational methods. The first half of the course develops core techniques in algebra, calculus, financial mathematics, and optimization. The second half introduces probability, descriptive statistics, and inferential methods such as hypothesis testing and linear regression. By the end of the course, students will be able to apply quantitative reasoning to analyse complex business problems, interpret data effectively, and communicate insights with clarity and precision.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify ethical issues in business practice and statistical reporting.
- Engage in independent and reflective learning to analyse business data and problems using critical thinking.
- Work collaboratively to discuss and solve problems.
- Solve problems using a variety of mathematical and statistical techniques and produce an associated business report.
- Use a calculator and a spreadsheet program (Microsoft Excel) effectively to perform calculations.

Subject: Microeconomics (ECON5101)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Decision-making by consumers, producers, and governments lies at the heart of economic analysis. It determines how scarce resources are allocated, how markets function, and how welfare is distributed across society. The first half of this microeconomics course is devoted to the study of decision-making and the modelling of associated market behaviour. We then study how individual economic decisions interact in markets with varying degrees of competition, paying substantial attention to the comparative welfare outcomes produced by different market forms. Additional topics include externalities, game theory and its applications, and asymmetric information. There is an emphasis on the use of analytical tools and mathematical models, which underpin much of current research in microeconomics. By the end of the course, students will be able to apply core microeconomic models to analyse decision-making and market outcomes, critically evaluate efficiency and welfare implications across market structures, and use formal tools to address issues such as externalities, strategic interaction, and information asymmetries.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop and present arguments to demonstrate an understanding of key concepts of microeconomics and their appropriate usage.
- Develop succinct and clear work that communicates ideas in a logical, professional manner.
- Analyse choice problems and markets by identifying and applying appropriate mathematical techniques to design and solve economic models.
- Evaluate environmental and sustainability problems arising from non-cooperative decision making among agents/ firms/countries.

Subject: Macroeconomics (ECON5102)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The macroeconomy of a country is a complex network consisting of millions of interacting agents, including consumers, ﬁrms, banks and government institutions. This course introduces you to some of the models employed by economists to understand how these agents interact and contribute to economic growth, the business cycle and other macroeconomic variables. The course covers a range of topics, including ﬁscal and monetary policies, the business cycle, inflation and unemployment and economic growth models. Models developed in the unit are applied to real-world data and are used to analyse observed developments across different economies. By the end of the course, you will develop the tools, skills and knowledge necessary for you to operate as a practicing macroeconomist.
Learning Outcomes:
- Work collaboratively to complete a complex task.
- Synthesise theory and evidence to assess macroeconomic outcomes related to fairness and inequality in an economic context.
- Employ advanced analytical methods to apply macroeconomic models to interpret and analyse real-world economic issues.
- Synthesise ideas through succinct and clear work that communicates ideas in a logical, professional manner.
- Articulate and evaluate the assumptions, strengths, and limitations of standard macroeconomic models.

Subject: Econometrics (ECON5205)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: What can we learn from data, and how can we separate correlation and causality? This course introduces the use of econometrics to explore and estimate economic relationships using linear regression models. Emphasis will be placed on cross-sectional data and on understanding the conditions required for inferring causal relationships. We will also consider extensions that address common data analysis complications, including heteroskedasticity, proxy variables, and other data limitations. And we will consider regression models for time series and panel data, highlighting their relevance for economic analysis. Practical computer-based exercises are integrated throughout, enabling students to gain hands-on experience working with real-world data and econometric software. By the end of the course, you will have the skills to critically interpret empirical relationships and a foundation for more advanced courses in econometrics.
Learning Outcomes:
- Synthesise ideas through succinct and clear work that communicates ideas in a logical, professional manner.
- Analyse and articulate the assumptions underlying regression models.
- Employ and apply statistical software to analyse data analyse and present regression results appropriately.
- Identify and assess environmental and sustainability considerations in problems in economics and business.
- Employ econometric models and methods to interpret real data in economics, finance, and other social sciences.

Subject: Introductory Economics and Data Analysis for Business Professionals (ECON5258)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Successful business leadership depends on understanding both the economic forces that shape markets and the data that inform strategic choices. This course equips students with the ability to interpret economic trends, apply decision-making frameworks, and use data-driven insights to guide policy and business strategy. It covers the policy and strategic aspects of business management and also provides a practical and analytical approach to economic policy issues. It is structured into two parts. The first part (Economics) provides you with the tools to use economic principles in decision-making and an understanding of the broader economic environment in which business decisions must be made. The second part (Data Analysis) introduces the basic statistical tools and quantitative methods useful in understanding the types of data encountered in business. By the end of the course, students will be able to analyse economic and business data, evaluate policy implications, and make informed, evidence-based decisions in complex business environments.
Learning Outcomes:
- Use the concepts, theories and techniques of statistical analysis to present data and evaluate business and economic problems.
- Use standard statistical techniques to analyse and interpret real-world problems.
- Describe key micro- and macro-economic concepts and construct economic arguments in terms of these concepts.
- Apply simple economic models to the analysis of the relevant economic issues.
- Develop succinct and clear work that communicates ideas in a logical, professional manner.
