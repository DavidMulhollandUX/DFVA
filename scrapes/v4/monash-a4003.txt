## Graduate Certificate of Bioethics
##### A4003
##### 24 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
This course is particularly well suited if you are a health care professional or scientist who faces complex ethical issues in your working life. The course is also valuable if you are involved in the development of public policy and law, or simply wish to explore issues of public concern. The course is designed in ways to suit you with little or no prior educational background in ethics or philosophy, and is also suitable for philosophy graduates with an interest in further studies of bioethics.

Teaching in the course is designed to improve your analytical skills, encourage you to think critically about the ethical issues raised, and to develop independent and well-informed views about the ethical and philosophical questions addressed in the course.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
recognise and discuss contemporary best practice in applied ethics, healthcare ethics, research ethics and policy;
**2.**
communicate bioethical issues to professional and non-professional audiences.
**3.**
analyse and critically evaluate the key texts and cultural products pertaining to the field of bioethics;
**4.**
identify and explain global debates surrounding ethical issues in policy, governance and research;

### Progression to further studies
Successful completion of this course may provide a pathway to the Master of Bioethics (A6002).

### AHEGS
The Graduate Certificate of Bioethics is a postgraduate course. It is taught in English and normally takes six months of full time study or one year of part-time study or equivalent. This course provides an interdisciplinary education that is concerned with the ethical issues raised by medicine and the biomedical sciences. Graduates develop advanced knowledge to enable them to participate in critical debates surrounding ethical issues in policy, governance and research at a global level. Graduates also develop knowledge of best practice in applied ethics, healthcare ethics, research ethics and related policy issues. The Graduate Certificate of Bioethics is a Level 8 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Research ethics (APG5079)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit examines key ethical issues arising in biomedical research. It covers topics such as the history of controversial research, "therapeutic misconception", informed consent, acceptable and unacceptable risks to research participants, research involving "vulnerable" populations, and research in developing countries. Throughout the unit use will be made of case studies, ethical frameworks and principles--and participants will have ample opportunity to discuss their own experiences with human research ethics.
Learning Outcomes:
- Use ethical principles and reasoning to arrive at well-argued conclusions about ethical dilemmas in biomedical research;
- Critically engage with the research ethics literature;
- Identify and critically evaluate key ethical concepts and principles used to address issues in research involving humans;
- Construct arguments for novel conclusions in relation to that literature.
Assessment Tasks:
- Essay: 45% (Hurdle: None)
- Research task: 10% (Hurdle: None)
- Participation: 45% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Scheduled activities may include a combination of teacher directed learning, peer directed learning and online engagement.

Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit focuses on the role of communication campaigns and advocacy as a particular form of strategic communication. Credit Points: 12
Overview: In this unit, you will explore the key elements of ethical theory, as a foundation for your studies in bioethics. You will consider several key perspectives in normative ethics, including Utilitarianism, Kantian ethics, and Virtue Ethics, and examine several key problems for each of these approaches. Subject: Ethics of life and death (APG5394)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: In this unit you will explore ethical issues that arise in healthcare at the beginning and end of life. You will examine core concepts that shape ethical debates, including the sanctity of life doctrine, potentiality and the nature of personhood, killing and letting die, and the definition of death. You will apply these concepts to practical issues in life and death decision-making in healthcare, which could include embryo experimentation, abortion, neonatal resuscitation, advanced directives, voluntary assisted dying and brain death.
Learning Outcomes:
- Discuss and critically evaluate the relevance of these concepts to core issues in contemporary biomedical practice;
- Use well-developed research skills to undertake rigorous, in-depth analysis of bioethical issues.

Subject: Clinical ethics (APG5729)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit focuses initially on three ethical principles used to justify decisions in patient care: autonomy, beneficence, and justice. These principles are then applied to a variety of ethical issues in health care practice, such as the allocation of health care resources, the justifiability of paternalistic interventions, breaches of confidentiality, assisted reproductive technologies, surrogate motherhood, and euthanasia. The role of health professionals is also considered, in relation to issues in family care giving, and conscientious refusals to treat patients.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and critically analyse central concepts in clinical ethics;

- Apply those ethical concepts to core issues that arise in healthcare practice;

Subject: Foundations of health policy (MPH5269)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Delivery of health services is underpinned by a framework of health policies and other health system elements. Health professional leaders and managers, and those who aspire to these roles, need to know about these policies and about the process of policy making so that they can understand why a policy is what it is, and how to engage in the policy making process. This unit involves a structured review of policy making processes, key challenges and major issues confronting governments and health systems around the world, and how policy can help address these. Throughout the unit, current Australian policies and senior health policy experts will be drawn on to apply theory to practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify the key components of policy development.
- Effectively communicate complex information related to health policy and its implications.
- Identify relevant stakeholders and critically analyse and interpret their policy interests.
- Describe the application of policy theory and process to relevant examples.
- Analyse formal and informal sources of information guiding policy development.
- Critically evaluate and explain the political context of policy development.
Assessment Tasks:
- Written assignment - critical analysis report of policy (1,800 words): 35% (Hurdle: None)
- Written assignment - development of policy position statement (1,800 words): 35% (Hurdle: None)
- 6 x short answer questions (400 words each. Total- 2,400 words): 30% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Off-campus: 12 hours self-directed study per week, plus 2 block days.

- Identify issues and challenges in the design of national health systems.

- Effectively communicate issues and challenges associated with reforming and developing health systems to specialist and non-specialist audiences.
