## Monash Transition Program
##### A0001
##### 36 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
The Monash Transition Program is designed to support Indigenous Australians to make a successful transition to university study. It will help you develop the academic skills for success at university. Successful completion leads to a range of Monash University bachelor courses including in arts, business, information technology, pharmacy and pharmaceutical sciences, medicine, nursing and health sciences, and science.

The Monash Transition Program has two parts:

  * In Part 1 you will develop your academic writing skills, gain an understanding of the university’s requirements and expectations, and undertake a unit related to your destination degree.

  *  In Part 2 you will acquire foundational mathematics knowledge, explore the connection between knowledge and context, and undertake a unit related to your destination degree.

Depending on your educational background and the requirements of your destination course of choice, you may need to complete both Part 1 and Part 2. Advisors will work with you to develop an individual plan to meet your goals.

### Course duration (full time)


### Structure
The Monash Transition Program has two parts:

  * Part 1. Foundation studies consists of two academic skills units and one university discipline-based unit.
  * Part 2. Discipline studies consists of one maths based unit to address prerequisite studies and either one or two university units.

In both parts, your study will be structured to provide both foundation knowledge and specialist discipline knowledge.

Part 1. Foundation studies

These units will develop your skills to study at university, including academic writing and learning in a University context. Additionally, you will gain required knowledge in mathematics or sciences for entry to your course of choice.

Part 2. Discipline studies

These studies will give you the opportunity to select a unit (usually a level one/foundation unit) for your chosen destination degree course. You can select from a pre-approved list of units or choose a unit in consultation with the course leader.

### Requirements
Part 1. Foundation studies (18 credit points)

You must complete the following 18 credit points comprising:

- two units (12 credit points) from the following units; and

- one unit (6 credit points) at level 1 from the recommended list of units for your chosen destination course, or in consultation with the destination faculty..

  * EDF1010 Learning in a university context

  * ATS1297 Academic writing

  * ATS1298 Professional writing

Part 2. Discipline studies (18 credit points)

You must complete 18 credit points comprising:

- one or two units (6 or 12 credit points) from the following list of units; and

- one or two units (6 or 12 credit points) at level 1 from the recommended list of units for your chosen destination course, or in consultation with the destination faculty.

  * EDF1011 Knowledge and context

  * EDF1012 Mathematics for tertiary study

  * ETF2700 Mathematics for business

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
demonstrate confidence in your intellectual abilities and have an appreciation for study and scholarship;
**2.**
demonstrate an understanding of the requirements and expectations of university study at a bachelor level;
**3.**
demonstrate that you are a proficient tertiary learner through the explicit development of cognitive, analytical, problem-solving, oral and writing skills.

Subject: Academic writing (ATS1297)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit introduces you to academic writing and the attitudes, values and practices of academic culture. The unit will help you to write effective essays at a tertiary level. There is a particular focus on how academic writing presents clear reasoning with evidence to support a position, and how the academic essay differs from other types of non-fiction writing, such as the opinion piece. You will learn key principles of grammar and academic style conventions, as well as how to use sources correctly (finding, referencing, quoting, and paraphrasing) and avoid problems of plagiarism. You will also learn how to plan, research, edit, and proofread essays to a high standard.
Learning Outcomes:
- Reflect critically on the differences between academic and opinion writing and apply this understanding in the composition of academic essays;
- Utilise primary, secondary and tertiary sources in digital and print in their research;
- Apply scholarly principles of referencing, quoting, paraphrasing;
- Self-edit and revise writing in an academic context.
- Identify and explain core attitudes, values and practices of academic culture and how academic writing reflects these;
- Recognise and use the fundamental concepts of grammar, punctuation, spelling, usage and style;
Assessment Tasks:
- Essay: 45% (Hurdle: None)
- Analytical Exercise 2: 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Quiz: 15% (Hurdle: None)
- Analytical exercise 1: 20% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Scheduled activities may include a combination of teacher directed learning, peer directed learning and online engagement.

Subject: Professional writing (ATS1298)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In the modern workplace, the ability to communicate well in different forms (spoken and written) and contexts (online, face to face, and formal documents) is highly valued. In this unit we will focus on developing effective communication, particularly in written form, in professional situations. The unit is based in practical exercises to develop your abilities as a writer and also an editor of professional standard texts. We will study and produce types of writing and documents that are essential for you to secure professional employment, as well as to thrive in the kind of professional employment you can expect to find as a graduate. The unit will introduce analytical and critical approaches to contemporary communication issues such as changes in literacy practices and the forms and mediums of communication. Through an understanding of professional genres, varied text types, audience, context, and language usage, the unit will help to develop your ability to judge communication expectations in new and changeable environments. We will also study principles of information, gathering, analysis and reporting in professional contexts, and put these into practice through activities, projects and assignments.
Learning Outcomes:
- Plan and design effective documents for varied situations;
- Understand and apply essential features of English usage (grammar and structure) in writing and editing documents, and oral communication in class groups;
- Apply understanding of contemporary professional communication issues.
- Identify and use appropriate English in written and oral contexts;
- Identify expectations of communication based on understanding of different professional audiences and contexts and apply these in documents and presentations;
- Understand and apply basic principles of information gathering and research (such as short online surveys) in professional contexts through practical assignments;
Assessment Tasks:
- Research task: 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Media task: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Media task: 30% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Scheduled activities may include a combination of teacher directed learning, peer directed learning and online engagement.

Subject: Learning in a university context (EDF1010)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit introduces you to the requirements of university study. It provides you with a range of specific capabilities that are essential for engaged and successful undergraduate study. The unit content is arranged around the relationships between the learner, the university's requirements and the expectations of students. You are introduced to the rigours of university learning and the ranges of scholarly traditions and practices. Emphasis is given to the importance of independent learning in university studies.
Learning Outcomes:
- employ relevant theoretical and practical knowledge in a range of skills central to university learning, including critical reading and writing
- understand the principles and expectations of university scholarship
- produce essays and reports that present a critical argument and adhere to the conventions of academic presentation.
- exercise the skills necessary to maximise their learning from lectures, tutorials and teaching materials
Assessment Tasks:
- Research and reflection (1600 words or equivalent): 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Referenced essay (2400 words or equivalent): 60% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: The minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per teaching period comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Usually, you can expect to engage with: • 24 hours of scheduled learning delivered on campus via weekly/regular directed learning activities; and • the remaining hours to be allocated towards self-directed/independent study Scheduled learning activities may include a combination of teacher directed learning, peer directed learning and online engagement.

Subject: Knowledge and context (EDF1011)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit explores the relationships between knowledge and context as you consider the knowledge developed outside the formal degree structure and examine the use of transferable knowledge at university and in other contexts. You will identify, compare, contrast and discuss a range of knowledge theories and key pedagogical approaches from a range of disciplines to build a deeper understanding of learning across different contexts.
Learning Outcomes:
- demonstrate a grasp of the relationship between knowledge and context
- prepare for and deliver an oral report.
- write well-structured essays
- demonstrate some familiarity with theories of knowledge
- demonstrate an ability to work effectively in a group
- demonstrate independent research skills
- construct a reference list and correctly use citations
Assessment Tasks:
- Written task (1600 words or equivalent): 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Tutorial presentation and paper (2400 words or equivalent): 60% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: The minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per teaching period comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Usually, you can expect to engage with: • 24 hours* of scheduled learning delivered on campus via weekly/regular directed learning activities; and • the remaining hours to be allocated towards self-directed/independent study Scheduled learning activities may include a combination of teacher directed learning, peer directed learning and online engagement. *Subject to variation due to professional experience requirements

Subject: Mathematics for tertiary study (EDF1012)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit will provide a foundation in mathematics and provide you with skills to support your future university studies. Topics covered include linear modelling, graphs of curves, financial mathematics, univariate and bivariate data, probability, matrices, and sequences and series. You will be supported to develop core concepts and skills, and to apply these to solve problems.
Learning Outcomes:
- solve problems requiring linear or curved graphing skills, including sketching, identifying key features and finding equations
- summarise, present and interpret data numerically and graphically
- calculate operation on matrices, including by-hand calculations involving identity and inverse matrices
- apply correct procedures to solve financial application problems, and interpret the solutions
- generate an arithmetic or geometric sequence or series using a recurrence relation, and solve problems in practical contexts.
- select and apply appropriate procedures for the solution of probability problems, including recognition of dependent and independent events
- apply your mathematics knowledge to solve problems in a range of contexts
Assessment Tasks:
- Take-home task (1200 words or equivalent), T2-58: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- In-class test (1200 words or equivalent), S2-01: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- In-class test (800 words or equivalent): 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Take-home assessment tasks throughout the unit (2000 words or equivalent): 50% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: The minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per teaching period comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Usually, you can expect to engage with: • 24 hours* of scheduled learning delivered on campus via weekly/regular directed learning activities; and • the remaining hours to be allocated towards self-directed/independent study Scheduled learning activities may include a combination of teacher directed learning, peer directed learning and online engagement. *Subject to variation due to professional experience requirements

Subject: Mathematics for business (ETF2700)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The topics covered in this unit include: the linear model, linear simultaneous equations, non-linear functions, index numbers, financial mathematics, derivatives and partial differentiation, integration, optimisation (constrained and non-constrained), and matrix algebra.
Learning Outcomes:
- understand the features of linear and non-linear models and their applications in economics, finance, and business
- acquire skills in solving equations and applying them to problems arising in business
- develop skills to perform differentiation, optimisation (constrained and unconstrained), basic matrix operations, and apply them to understand and solve problems in business
- solve two- and three-dimensional simultaneous equations, with applications in business
- work with arithmetic and geometric sequences and series and use them in financial applications
Assessment Tasks:
- 2 - Written: 15% (Hurdle: None)
- 1 - Quiz / Test: 25% (Hurdle: None)
- 3 - Examination: 60% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Independent study may include associated readings, assessment and preparation for scheduled activities. You are expected to complete all pre-class activities prior to your scheduled class, and post-class activities should be completed after your scheduled class. Learning activities may include a combination of teacher directed, peer directed and online engagement activities.
