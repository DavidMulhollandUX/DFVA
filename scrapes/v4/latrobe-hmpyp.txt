## Master of Physiotherapy Practice - Melbourne (Bundoora)
##### HMPYP
##### 255 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
Physiotherapists assess and treat patients with temporary or long-term physical injuries, movement disorders or disabilities, aiming to achieve the highest possible degree of recovery, activity, participation and quality of life. Physiotherapists may work independently or as members of healthcare teams. They are active in the prevention of illness and injury and in the promotion of health. They are also involved in clinical research, movement rehabilitation, ergonomics and the management of healthcare organisations.

Following a preparatory subject in the summer semester (Jan-Feb), in the first semester you will develop clinical reasoning, assessment and treatment skills in the core areas of cardiorespiratory, musculoskeletal and neurological physiotherapy across a lifespan. This is followed by 18 months of integrated theory and clinical practice subjects, when you will be primarily located in the clinical environment.

The clinical practice and theory subjects extend outside official University semester dates. The course fosters small-group learning to master the practical aspects of physiotherapy practice. You will be given the opportunity to assess and treat patients in hospitals and other clinical settings under the supervision of a qualified clinician.

Please visit our Frequently Asked Questions for more information.

The qualification awarded on graduation is recognised in the Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) as Level 9.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Critically reflect on theory and personal practice, analyse and synthesise complex ideas, creatively problem solve and apply knowledge and skills to novel professional situations
**2.**
Apply a body of theoretical, conceptual and practical knowledge, including an understanding of recent developments in physiotherapy research and practice, to physiotherapy practice in a changing environment
**3.**
Communicate effectively with specialist and non-specialist audiences to justify, evaluate, interpret and transmit complex knowledge, skills and ideas using a variety of modes and media
**4.**
Use initiative and work with a high level of personal autonomy, and as a member or leader of a team in a multidisciplinary work environment
**5.**
Make, and justify, ethical and socially responsible decisions in professional practice
**6.**
Learn new skills, implement, evaluate and theorise about developments that contribute to physiotherapy practice supporting an intellectual curiosity and commitment to continuous learning

### Course features
### Work based learning (placement) requirements
Compulsory placements (Work Based Learning)
During this course, you will participate in one or more supervised Work Based Learning (WBL) placements designed to allow you to practice newly acquired skills, behaviours and knowledge while integrating theory with practice. The placements will immerse you in professional work culture, enabling you to develop and further enhance your skills. WBL provides you with the opportunity to gain valuable industry experience at one or more of our local, interstate or international industry partners or agencies. WBL placements will generally be arranged by the College based Placements Operation Team or Experiential Learning Team at La Trobe University. The number of hours undertaken can vary considerably, depending on the requirements of the course. Students may incur additional costs and it may be necessary to travel or relocate to complete a specific placement. Please refer to the individual subject entries for more information.
### Work integrated learning opportunities
Industry presentations, Industry informed curriculum, Industry-informed projects, Professional simulations
### Career outcomes
Physiotherapists are employed in a wide variety of settings, including private practice, general hospitals, rehabilitation centres, community health centres, industrial clinics and specialist centres. Physiotherapists are also involved in movement rehabilitation, ergonomics and the management of healthcare organisations. You may also work in education and clinical research.

Subject: PHYSIOTHERAPY FOR NEUROLOGICAL DYSFUNCTION (PTY4NEP)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: You will learn core knowledge, skills and attitudes relevant to physiotherapy management of common neurological problems. The emphasis will be on evidence-based assessment and intervention of neurological patients across the lifespan and various health care settings. Using an enquiry-based learning approach, case examples will include, but not be limited to, Stroke, Multiple Sclerosis, Parkinson's Disease and Cerebral Palsy. The World Health Organisation International Classification of Function Disability and Health will provide a framework to demonstrate the need for person centredness. .
Learning Outcomes:
- Complete a safe, sensitive and case-specific assessment relevant to common neurological problems, and in a variety of healthcare settings.
- Safely and effectively perform a range of therapeutic interventions commonly used in neurological physiotherapy.
- Devise and justify an interprofessional and evidence based treatment that can be safely applied and is relevant to common neurological conditions, individually and collaboratively.
- Perform a case sensitive analysis and interpretation of assessment findings relevant to common neurological conditions to produce a prioritised problem list and patient centred goals, individually and collaboratively.

Subject: ADVANCED PHYSIOTHERAPY PRACTICE (PTY5APP)
Credit Points: 30
Overview: This subject involves three modules: (i) advanced spinal physiotherapy, (ii) professional practice, and (iii) a self-directed project. Advanced spinal physiotherapy expands on the clinical reasoning and manual therapy skills developed earlier in the course, with focus on spinal physiotherapy. The professional practice module aims to prepare you to commence practice as a newly graduated health professional, covering legislation, codes of conduct and ethical practice. In the third module, you will identify a gap in physiotherapy practice and develop a creative solution to this issue. As a self-directed project, you will develop their solution with the ultimate aim of improving physiotherapy practice within the chosen context.
Learning Outcomes:
- Design, justify and conduct safe, sensitive, advanced and highly case-specific assessments.
- Analyse and critically evaluate assessment findings and prioritise patient needs to at least the minimum standard expected for an entry level physiotherapist
- Communicate competently and clearly and coherently communicate research findings to a variety of audiences, both individually and collaboratively.
- Select, safely apply and medico legally document high velocity, low amplitude (HVLA) manipulation techniques to the lumbar, thoracic and cervical regions of the spines.
- Examine, and critically evaluate evidence to create, and enhance aspects of physiotherapy practice.
- Critically reflect in teams on personal and colleagues' professional behaviour based on relevant legislation, ethical standards and Codes of Practice.
- Prioritise and critically evaluate interventions using reliable and valid outcome measures to at least the minimum standard expected for a beginner/entry level physiotherapist.
- Design, justify and conduct complex case-specific physiotherapy management plans.

Subject: CHRONIC DISEASE MANAGEMENT (PTY5CDM)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will integrate core strands of knowledge and practice with contemporary health needs of individuals, groups, communities and populations. You will develop knowledge and skills in interventions and management plans for people with chronic conditions in both group and individual contexts as well as health leadership and change management skills. You will critique models of health, theories of health behaviour and apply innovative problem-solving skills to health-related scenarios covering a range of chronic conditions. Principles of ethical leadership, change management, social justice, cultural competence, interprofessional, person-centred, evidence-based practice and critical reflection approaches will be adopted during the exploration of topics.
Learning Outcomes:
- Collaboratively investigate and develop ethical health leadership behaviours to responsibly manage change and promote sustainable and contemporary healthcare practice.
- Collaboratively and individually evaluate factors that contribute to health inequality between Indigenous and non-Indigenous Australians and develop recommendations to improve health outcomes for Indigenous Australians.
- Critically evaluate and respond to barriers and enablers of health for clients with chronic conditions
- Critically evaluate and design evidence-based recommendations for clients with chronic conditions
- Develop and evaluate culturally sensitive, effective and safe health programs that draw on the principles of inclusion, equity and accessibility for clients with chronic conditions.

===== SOURCE: https://handbook.latrobe.edu.au/subjects/2026/PTY5EBP =====
Subject: EVIDENCE BASED PRACTICE IN HEALTH (PTY5EBP)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Evidence based practice is the use of best available evidence combined with patient preferences, available resources and therapist expertise in making decisions about the care of patients. Evidence based health care requires the physiotherapist to be able to perform the five steps of evidence based practice. In this subject students will build on the skills developed in previous research subjects in the production of a written Critically Appraised Topic (CAT) for publication in a web-based clinical resource. This resource will be peer reviewed, with the students further applying their EBP skills in order to critically appraise the work of other students. In this manner they will further develop the necessary skills to perform evidence based practice, while gaining a greater understanding of the peer review process and contributing to a valuable evidence based resource.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically appraise with colleagues the methodology of a variety of research designs to determine the relative weight of research evidence.
- Collaborate with colleagues to develop and justify research topics that will enhance clinical knowledge and systematically locate evidence.
- Collaboratively collate current, peer-reviewed evidence, to answer clinically oriented research questions.
- Critically reflect on team strengths and weaknesses in relation to evidence-based-practice.
- Critically appraise published work in a process of peer review.
- Critically analyse and interpret research findings with colleagues to justify answers to clinical research questions.

===== SOURCE: https://handbook.latrobe.edu.au/subjects/2026/PTY5PPB =====
Subject: PROFESSIONAL PRACTICUM B (PTY5PPB)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject is the second of 6 professional practicum subjects across different specific areas of practice. Students are required to meet competency in 6 specific practice areas to meet professional accreditation requirements. In this subject students are in supervised clinical practice for 20 days over 5 weeks and prescribed independent learning (total subject hours 160). Clinical practicums are supervised by registered physiotherapists and may be organised outside normal teaching weeks. Students will develop specific competencies in the assessment of clients' problems and needs, analysis of assessment findings, formulation of short and long term goals and development, implementation and evaluation of intervention plans. Students will apply specialised knowledge, cognitive and technical skills to demonstrate autonomy, expert judgement, adaptability and responsibility as a learner.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate evidence to select, perform, monitor and progress interventions safely in a specific area of practice and appropriately in collaboration with clients.
- Communicate effectively and appropriately with clients, carers, family and health professional colleagues in one specific area of practice to at least the minimum standard expected for a beginner/entry level physiotherapist.
- Implement discharge planning to address client and carer's needs for ongoing care in one specific area of practice.
- Analyse and interpret assessment findings accurately to identify client's problems and set realistic short and long terms goals for a specific practice area.
- Plan and initiate effective education/health promotion in one specific area of practice with clients and caregivers.
- Conduct sensitive and case specific client interviews to at least the minimum standard expected for a beginner/entry level physiotherapist in a specific practice area.
- Practice responsibly, applying professional behaviour in one specific area of practice appropriate to at least the minimum standard expected for a beginner/entry level physiotherapist.
- Perform safe, systematic, efficient and accurate physical assessments.
