If you're looking to study a postgraduate degree at La Trobe, but don't quite meet the academic or English requirements, our Postgraduate Qualifying Program is designed for you.
In this program, you'll gain a pathway into a range of business and IT postgraduate coursework degrees at La Trobe. While you learn the academic skills you need to succeed, you'll benefit from a supportive learning environment with smaller class sizes and personalised attention.
By completing La Trobe's Postgraduate Qualifying Program, you will:
  * build a transferable skillset in critical thinking, innovation, problem-solving, research, analysis and communication
  * choose from streams in business or information technology and explore topics such as finance, managerial strategic planning and pricing decisions, global business, fundamental concepts of computer science and more
  * master professional and effective engagement that recognises the diverse needs, values, personalities and capabilities of others and contributes to the success of multicultural teams.


Please note: the Postgraduate Qualifying Program is a non-award program.

Subject: ACCOUNTING AND FINANCE FOR BUSINESS (BUS4001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject introduces you to the fundamental components of accounting and finance and their role in business. You will learn to record and report business transactions, assess financial health, and manage business assets. You will also learn the ethical requirements underpinning financial statements, how to compile and interpret financial statements and critically assess different sources of financing. You will develop your critical thinking and ability to make strategic planning and pricing decisions.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and interpret financial statements and prepare a budget for managing business finances
- Critically assess the relationship between risk and return and how these impact on using different sources of capital to fund business operations
- Communicate to stakeholders the business investment options and rules as they relate to the funding and investment decisions of the business.
- Explain the importance of accounting and financial reporting and ethical and corporate governance issues as they apply to managerial decisions .

Subject: BUSINESS ESSENTIALS (BUS4002)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject introduces you to contemporary business operations in local, regional and global environments where you will consider both current and future operational needs. You will engage actively with current business issues, focusing particularly on the impact of external and internal environments on businesses and individuals. In addition, this subject is designed to develop your critical thinking and creative problem-solving with a particular focus on management, marketing and human resource practices. You will also examine and address contemporary business social issues drawn from various organisational and cultural contexts.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically analyse an existing local, regional, or global businesses environment and make informed strategic decisions.
- Communicate effectively to culturally-diverse audiences through structured written arguments.
- Demonstrate and justify the use of management skills to solve a contemporary social problem in a dynamic business environment

Subject: PEOPLE, CULTURE AND COMMUNICATION (LTU4001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject prepares students for postgraduate study in the Australian context. Students will build their competence in effective communication, participation and interaction in Australia and at university. Through an exploration of the people (structures of universities), culture (student body, staff and communities), students will improve their agency to navigate higher education systems and respond appropriately in varied multicultural and social environments.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop familiarity with the cultural context of Australian higher education.
- Reflect on personal worldview, skills and attributes.
- Demonstrate skills in reflective and critical writing for academic purposes.
- Understand the responsibilities and expectations of students and staff in Australian universities.

Subject: PREPARING FOR POSTGRADUATE CRITICAL THINKING (LTU4002)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject supports students to extend their critical and analytical thinking skills ahead of postgraduate studies. Students will be introduced to critical thinking methods that are applied to scholarship by critically engaging with, and evaluating, the arguments of others. Students are encouraged to not only challenge the views of others, but also communicate their own position based on reasoning and effectively formulating an argument.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and formulate questions for academic inquiry.
- Identify, source, critically analyse, organise and reference research materials to support an opinion.
- Analyse a question, form an opinion and develop an academic argument in response.
- Critically compare different viewpoints by using critical reading and note-taking strategies.

Subject: LITERACY FOR POSTGRADUATE STUDY (LTU4003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject provides students from a range of educational, linguistic and cultural backgrounds with tools to develop the academic voice and language skills necessary for postgraduate studies. Students will explore academic conventions and practices to complete tasks in a range of academic genres and text types such as writing essays, reports and case studies, formal presentation and delivery of academic material as well as participating actively in university lectures, seminars and tutorials.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate skills in argumentative and report writing for academic purposes.
- Apply knowledge of a range of information types and genres to the analysis and construction of academic texts.
- Compare different academic genres and styles and identify their requirements required for specific purposes.
- Critically review written work using proofreading skills and selecting and applying feedback.
