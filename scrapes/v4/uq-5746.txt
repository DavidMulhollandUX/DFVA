Subject: Aerospace Composites (AERO4300)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The course provides an introduction to composite materials, manufacturing and design with a primary focus on applications in the aerospace industry. Students will gain insight into the commonly used constituent materials, intermediates, and resulting composites and understand the many choices available in this hierarchy. The course will then introduce the students to the most common composite manufacturing processes for both thermoplastic and thermosetting matrix composites. Emphasis is put on enabling students to select the most appropriate manufacturing process for a given application and material combination. The design component of the course covers the key concepts of structural design and evaluation of composite materials. This includes introducing students to micromechanics, classical-laminate theory, and failure theory of composite materials using both analytical as well as numerical methods. In the final two weeks of the semester, the course ventures into advanced topics, including non-destructive testing, quality assurance, and ceramic matrix composite materials. The learning experience is enhanced through a hands-on manufacturing practical in which students work in teams, under the guidance of teaching staff, to manufacture aerospace-grade composite components.
Learning Outcomes:
- Select appropriate composite material systems to meet the requirements of specific applications.
- Determine suitable manufacturing methods for composite materials based on application, cost, and performance considerations.
- Apply composite mechanics and design principles to engineer composite structures.
- Specify appropriate destructive and non-destructive testing methods for quality assurance during manufacturing and through-life support.
Assessment Tasks:
- Materials and Manufacturing Poster: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Assignment Composite Mechanics and Design: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Workshop and NDT Studio participation assessment In-person: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Exam Hurdle Identity Verified: 60%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Studio 3 Hours/ Week Practical 4 Hours/ Ad Hoc

Subject: Aerospace Propulsion (AERO4450)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Air-breathing propulsion systems; rocket propulsion systems; combustion applied to aerospace propulsion systems.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the operating principles of air-breathing propulsion systems, rockets and combustion systems
- Analyse aircraft and spacecraft engines by synthesising concepts introduced in fluid mechanics and thermodynamics to develop system models
- Analyse combustion systems by synthesising concepts introduced in chemistry, fluid mechanics and thermodynamics to develop system models
- Predict and evaluate the performance of new and novel engine designs using the analysis techniques described above
- Justify which engine performance measures are most relevant for different applications
- Design an optimal propulsion system for a given application, using appropriate performance measures to rationally select the system type and optimise its parameters
- Experimentally investigate propulsion system performance as a team and present findings as a professional engineering report.

Credit Points: 2
Overview: Corrosion fundamentals, design against corrosion, corrosion protection principles and practice, corrosion in common environments, corrosion resistant alloys, corrosion mechanisms, environment assisted fracture and fatigue.

- Explain the interaction between corrosion and stress in causing failure of materials.
- Demonstrate familiarity with industry-relevant corrosion mitigation principles and techniques for practical engineering applications.
- Design corrosion protection systems for assets exposed to corrosive environments by applying key concepts of corrosion science.
- Research and analyse a corrosion‑related topic and deliver an oral presentation that effectively communicates your findings, individually or in a group.
- Identify and assess real-world corrosion issues and apply corrosion knowledge to develop a comprehensive written technical report.

Subject: Advanced Manufacturing (MATE7013)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Current global problems require increasingly sophisticated materials and appropriate advanced approaches and methodologies for their manufacture. This course will look at materials design for device manufacture, manufacturing techniques and manufacturing systems that are used to deliver innovative products and devices from the laboratory to industrial production. Several key manufacturing techniques, such as nano-, electronic and sustainable manufacturing will be covered as case studies illuminating how materials and manufacturing processes affect the end performance of the product, the economics of production and the impact on society and the environment. - Understand the fundamental principles of semiconductor and superconductor behaviour, and classify these systems based on their functional properties.

- Assess current manufacturing processes for electronics packaging and interpret their role in enabling emerging technologies including IoT, mobile devices, and advanced digital systems essential to Industry 4.0.
- Design and evaluate prototype magnet and electronic subsystems by integrating knowledge of material behaviour, structural/thermal engineering, manufacturability, and lifecycle cost considerations.
- Identify limitations in conventional electronic packaging and manufacturing systems, propose innovative solutions, and critically review relevant literature and peer presentations.
- Understand persistent-mode magnetic system operation and analyse mechanisms to maintain stable current flow for next generation MRI magnet applications.
- Collaboratively engage in practical and in-class team activities in device fabrication and testing, including mini-projects and prototype-based learning, and demonstrate effective teamwork, technical integration, and problem-solving skills.
- Critically evaluate global research and industry developments in electronic, superconducting, and semiconducting manufacturing, identify emerging trends, and assess their implications for future research and Australia’s manufacturing sector.

This course will provide students with an in-depth understanding of the various AM technologies, including their limitations, along with the skills required for their successful implementation.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand additive manufacturing processes - Develop a deep understanding of the 7 ASTM families of additive manufacturing technologies including fundamental processes, advantages, limitations and applications.
- Understand additive manufacturing processes - Learn when and how additive manufacturing processes are most applicable including understanding the cost and design drivers, which determine if additive manufacturing is an appropriate manufacturing route.
- Understand additive manufacturing processes - Analyse potential additive manufacturing product opportunities and evaluate appropriate processes.
- Understand additive manufacturing processes - Develop knowledge about the materials suitable for additive manufacturing and the physics behind their processing.
