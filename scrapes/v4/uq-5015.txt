This program provides students with knowledge, skills and resources to practise effectively as a clinical pharmacy practitioner involved in the quality use of medicines and pharmaceutical care. These skills include the capacity to individualise, review and monitor a patient's medication regimen; demonstrate effective communication skills in clinical and non-clinical scenarios; critically appraise medical and pharmaceutical literature, recognise and describe social issues relating to medicine use. Regular updates of course content integrate emerging clinical evidence and current controversies. A major strength of the program is the emphasis on developing clinical practice and the integration of evidence based medicine.

Subject: Advanced Skills for Clinical Pharmacy Practice (PHRM7010)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course aims to develop specific skills which will enable the student to improve their practice of pharmacy and will be integrated with knowledge gained in pharmacotherapy courses. This course introduces the student to advanced clinical pharmacy practice, with a focus on critical appraisal, drug information, evidence-based medicine (EBM) and patient-centred care.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify appropriate health care strategies based on patient-focused and evidence-based frameworks.
- Develop and employ search strategies to extract relevant information from the literature using various bibliographic databases.
- Efficiently access and identify relevant clinical resources and information and critically appraise and analyse clinical and therapeutic literature.
- Access, utilise and critically appraise clinical information about diagnostic tests, screening and monitoring critical to medication management.
- Apply concepts underpinning personalised drug regimen design, drug response and dosage adjustments.
- Use effective, oral and written professional communication and presentation skills.
- Develop the ability to work collaboratively with peers to critically evaluate clinical evidence.
- Identify, acknowledge, and analyse one’s own beliefs, values and attitudes, and reflect on how they may influence your work with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people in the context of delivering culturally safe health care.
Assessment Tasks:
- Module Assessments Identity Verified Team or group-based: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Cultural Safety in Practice Modules 1 & 2 Hurdle: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- Journal Club Critical Appraisal: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Evidence-based Practice Presentation and Report Identity Verified: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Tutorial 1 Hour/ Semester Workshop 6 Hours/ Semester

Subject: Evaluation and Research in Pharmacy Practice (PHRM7021)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course develops knowledge and skills necessary for clinical pharmacists to develop and conduct research and evaluation projects within their practice. The course covers concepts of planning and design of research and evaluation projects, and will help student to develop skills in both qualitative and quantitative methodologies. The relevance of research and evaluation to pharmacists is supported by practical learning tasks linked to clinical practice and the workplace, including drug utilisation evaluation.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically appraise and synthesise current evidence in a particular context
- Identify a gap in practice or knowledge that can be addressed through research and evaluation
- Develop focused, feasible, answerable research and evaluation questions
- Demonstrate an understanding of the principles of quantitative and qualitative data analysis
- Collect and analyse qualitative or quantitative data while ensuring the rigour and quality of that data
- Develop a project proposal in the area of pharmacy practice covering study design, methodology, data analysis and ethical considerations
- Communicate research ideas and project plans to a knowledgeable, non-expert audience
- Work collaboratively with your peers
Assessment Tasks:
- Module Activities Identity Verified Team or group-based: 21% (7% per module)% (Hurdle: False)
- Project Outline: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Analysis Assignment: 24%% (Hurdle: False)
- Project proposal: 35%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Tutorial 2 Hours/ Ad Hoc

Subject: Pharmacotherapy in Patient Care A (PHRM7031)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course aims to develop the student in the practice of clinical pharmacy, integrating skills and attitudes with knowledge to enable the pharmacist to take responsibility in ensuring optimal drug management in people. Key clinical areas covered in the course include: cardiovascular, respiratory, endocrinology and neurology.
Learning Outcomes:
- Access, interpret, critically appraise, and communicate current best evidence to apply to the treatment of patients with cardiovascular diseases, respiratory conditions, endocrine disorders and epilepsy.
- Apply pharmacological knowledge, evidence-based practice, clinical reasoning and ethical principles to individualise pharmaceutical care for patients with cardiovascular diseases, respiratory conditions, endocrine disorders and epilepsy in situations where evidence is limited or conflicting.
- Communicate with patients, peers and interdisciplinary health professionals across a range of communication technologies and formats to improve medication management in patients with cardiovascular diseases, respiratory conditions, endocrine disorders and epilepsy.
- Collaborate, share learnings and provide constructive feedback to and with peers around medication management in patients with cardiovascular diseases, respiratory conditions, endocrine disorders and epilepsy.
- Demonstrate an ability to disseminate learned knowledge to the wider community through contribution to online resources, in a manner appropriate for various levels of health literacy.
Assessment Tasks:
- Module Assessments Team or group-based Online: 24% (8% per module)% (Hurdle: False)
- Wikipedia page review and update: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Oral VIVA Hurdle Identity Verified Online: 56%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Tutorial 2 Hours/ Ad Hoc

Subject: Leading and Managing the Future in Pharmacy Practice (PHRM7051)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides skills in pharmacy management and leadership, including designing and leading service initiatives and change management in hospital and community settings. Students will gain insight into leadership, management and organisational dynamics, quality assurance in health systems, and human resource management basics, offering them skills and knowledge for advancing their career in the future.
Learning Outcomes:
- Design and evaluate pharmacy services that address value-based care and organisational objectives.
- Apply strategic planning principles to the development, resourcing, and implementation of pharmacy services within the healthcare system.
- Develop and communicate a strategic business plan that aligns with healthcare priorities, demonstrates financial viability, and incorporates effective marketing strategies to engage stakeholders and support implementation.
- Apply leadership and team management strategies to foster collaboration, and enhance workforce performance through recruitment, staff induction, and performance appraisal processes.
- Analyse and address organisational challenges to change management to ensure the sustainability and impact of pharmacy services.
Assessment Tasks:
- Business or Service Development Plan (Plan on 2 pages): 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Tutorial assessments (Group presentations and Elevator Pitch) Identity Verified Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Business Case: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Change Management Strategy: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Tutorial 1 Hour/ Semester Workshop 6 Hours/ Semester
