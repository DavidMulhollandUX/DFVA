## Graduate Diploma of Reproductive Sciences
##### M5010
##### 48 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
This course is offered by the Education Program in Reproduction and Development (EPRD), in the Monash Department of Obstetrics and Gynaecology with significant input from the Ritchie Centre for Baby Health Research and other centres in the Hudson Institute of Medical Research. Other Monash departments that participate in the course include anatomy and developmental biology, paediatrics, physiology, pharmacology and the School of Philosophical, Historical and International Studies. The program is also supported by Monash IVF, Family Planning Victoria, the Victorian Assisted Reproductive Treatment Authority, the University of Melbourne, Zoos Victoria, Genetics Australia, and Melbourne Aquarium. The primary aim of the EPRD is to foster education and research into reproductive biology and medicine. 

The course will provide you with a broad theoretical understanding of, the applications of reproductive biology to current human and veterinary medicine, assisted reproduction and conservation, and consideration of the social and ethical issues raised by the use of these techniques.

### Structure
The course consists of six units (two 12 credit point units and four 6 credit point units). You will develop a broad knowledge of reproductive biology for current human clinical and veterinary medicine, and comparative reproduction, captive breeding and wildlife conservation. You will also explore the associated social and ethical issues in regard to fertility regulation and treatment of infertility and the associated assisted reproductive and genetic technologies. 

Your theoretical learning is supported by exposure to practical and research skills delivered online and through face-to-face lab modules.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
evaluate the impact of research, policy and practice in reproductive science.
**2.**
integrate problem-solving, organisational, and teamwork skills to navigate professional practice in reproductive science.
**3.**
generate and interpret data, critically analysing its significance in the field of reproductive science.
**4.**
apply advanced theoretical knowledge of reproductive science to analyse contemporary challenges and formulate evidence-based solutions.
**5.**
appraise the ethical considerations and broader implications of reproductive science on individual health, public health, and planetary health.
**6.**
communicate complex concepts of reproductive science to peers, professionals, and the public.
**7.**
create research-informed approaches to resolving challenges within the reproductive sciences.

### Progression to further studies
Students who pass all units for this course and achieve an overall grade average of distinction (70%) or above may qualify for admission into higher degrees by research (HDR) and the Master of Clinical Embryology (M6010). Note: This course is not suitable for PhD entry outside of Monash University.

### AHEGS
The Graduate Diploma of Reproductive Sciences is a postgraduate award. The course is taught in English and normally takes one year of full-time study. Graduates have gained: broad theoretical and practical knowledge of reproductive biology for human clinical and veterinary medicine; theoretical and practical knowledge of comparative reproduction, captive breeding and wildlife conservation; an understanding of infertility treatment techniques; and developed an understanding of the social, ethical and legal issues associated with fertility regulation and infertility treatment. The Graduate Diploma of Reproductive Sciences is a Level 8 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Fundamentals of reproductive biology (GRS4000)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: The ability to reproduce is critical for the continuation of species. In this unit, you will develop a deep understanding of the complex processes that govern reproductive function across different animal species throughout their lifespan. We begin by identifying and examining the anatomical structures of the reproductive system, as well as the chemical components that play essential roles in its functioning. You will learn how gametes are made and the mechanisms underlying gametogenesis, elucidating the significance of the cell cycle and apoptosis in this process. You will explore sex determination in various animals, examining the genetic and hormonal pathways that determine the commitment of embryonic stem cells to the male or female germ cell lineage, and how these are involved in the differentiation and development of the male and female reproductive tracts across different animal species. Building on these gene and hormone interactions you will explore different disorders of sexual development, identifying their symptoms and elucidating their biological origins. Upon completing this unit, you will have developed the foundational knowledge needed to explore the spectrum of topics encompassed within the reproductive sciences.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify the anatomical and chemical components of the reproductive system
- Describe how hormones produced by the pituitary, hypothalamus and gonads contribute to reproductive function across the lifespan of different animals
- Define the concepts underpinning sex determination in different animals
- Distinguish between the genetic and hormonal pathways that result in an embryonic stem cell committing to the male or female germ cell lineage
- Apply knowledge of reproductive function to formulate evidence-based theories, opinions and diagnoses
- Appraise the work of yourself and others (including peers, professionals and artificial intelligence), and apply feedback to improve individual and team performance in reproductive science
- Compare the genetic and hormonal pathways that result in the differentiation and development of the male and female tract in different animals
- Explain how the processes of the cell cycle and apoptosis contribute to the production of functional gametes
- Communicate effectively with a range of audiences and in a variety of formats
Assessment Tasks:
- Critical analysis (1,500 words): 25% (Hurdle: None)
- Portfolio (1 x peer feedback 3 x 600-word articles): 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Oral presentation (10 min presentation & round table discussion): 25% (Hurdle: None)
- Problem solving activity and presentation (90 mins): 20% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 6 hours of teacher directed study per week, including 3 hours of lectures and 3 hours of tutorials. 18 hours per week of self-directed study dedicated to the review of course content and completion of assessments. Total per week = 24 hours

Subject: Pregnancy and parturition (GRS4103)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this unit, you will develop an in-depth understanding of human pregnancy and birth as well as pathophysiological changes within the maternal-placental-fetal unit. You will also cultivate higher-order thinking skills through analysing and critiquing recent research developments in this field, refining your communication abilities to effectively convey complex scientific ideas.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the normal physiology of human pregnancy, fetal development and birth.
- Explain pathophysiological aspects related to gestation, fetal growth, and parturition.
- Interpret recent research developments in perinatal processes.
- Analyse information provided to society regarding maternal and fetal health and effectively communicate inconsistencies.
Assessment Tasks:
- Tests (Total duration 1 hour 30 mins): 45% (Hurdle: None)
- Critical appraisal (4,000 words): 55% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 6 hours of teacher directed study per week, including ~3 hours of lecture content and ~3 hours of tutorials 12 hours of self-directed study per week = 18 hours per week over an 8-week period for a total unit requirement of 144 hours.

Subject: Reproductive health and fertility regulations (GRS4104)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this unit, you will apply your knowledge of reproductive health to recognise reproductive disorders such as sexually transmitted infections, reproductive cancers, erectile dysfunction and infertility and evaluate how these afflictions are treated, managed and prevented. You will also consider the means through which humans regulate their reproduction through the use of contraceptives and family planning. Applying your learning, you will respond to public stigmas and misconceptions about reproductive health and analyse contemporary and emerging challenges to maintaining reproductive health on local and global scales.
Learning Outcomes:
- Communicate effectively with a range of audiences and in a variety of formats.
- Appraise current and emerging methods to manage, treat and prevent reproductive health concerns.
- Design evidence-based health promotion materials that address public stigmas and misconceptions of reproductive health.
- Apply knowledge of reproductive health to recognise reproductive dysfunction and formulate evidence-based diagnoses.
- Analyse contemporary and emerging challenges in reproductive health on local and global scales.
Assessment Tasks:
- Essay (2,000 words): 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Examination (2 hours): 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Promotional communication: 30% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 6 hours of teacher directed study per week, including ~3 hours of lecture content and ~3 hours of tutorials 12 hours of self-directed study per week = 18 hours per week over an 8-week period for a total unit requirement of 144 hours.

Subject: Research in reproductive science  (GRS4200)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: In this unit you will be introduced to the common methods being used to study reproductive science and become familiar with the research process by undertaking your own research project. Practical sessions will introduce you to the lab and key elements of experimental design. You will then complete tuition in biostatistics and the key components of research theory. Under the guidance of a supervisor, you will follow the standardised method of scientific enquiry to identify gaps in our understanding of a subject area and propose an experimental design to test your hypotheses. You will then statistically analyse data and report your findings in a research report.
Learning Outcomes:
- Reflect on the experimental process and on group participation and learning.
- Compose a research plan to solve a problem using well-established experimental design and measurable outcomes
- Analyse current scientific literature to formulate new research questions and propose an experimental design to investigate these questions
- Communicate effectively with a range of audiences in a variety of formats
- Describe common scientific methods used in research within reproductive biology
- Work effectively as a team to solve a problem using scientific theory
- Employ the appropriate use of statistics to analyse data in reproductive science
Assessment Tasks:
- Project milestone meeting summaries (500 words x 2) (Pass/Fail): 0% (Hurdle: Competency)
- Workshop (1.5 hours, 500 words): 25% (Hurdle: None)
- Research plan (1,500 words) (Pass/Fail): 0% (Hurdle: Competency)
- Scenario based test: 25% (Hurdle: None)
- Project report (4,000 words): 50% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 8 hours of teacher directed study per week, which may be dedicated to lectures, tutorials, practical sessions, or working on research projects. 16 hours per week of self-directed study dedicated to the completion of the research project and preparation of assessments. Total per week = 24 hours

Subject: Reproduction and planetary health  (GRS4201)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit will focus on the reproductive health of animals and its influence on Planetary Health. Planetary Health refers to the health of natural systems which humans rely on for clean air, water, and food. Lectures and tutorials will introduce the concepts of Planetary Health and how healthy reproduction is key to maintaining healthy ecosystems. You will explore the process through which different anthropogenic influences disrupt reproduction in animals, and how humans as a species manipulate reproduction to improve breeding of endangered and agriculturally significant species for ecological and economic gain. This unit includes trips into the field to experience how fundamentals in theoretical reproductive biology can be applied to achieve environmental and/or commercial outcomes.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate knowledge of reproductive systems through risk mitigation relating to the principles of Planetary Health.
- Explain how anthropogenic influence impacts Planetary health and subsequently the reproductive systems of animals;
- Analyse how socioeconomic and cultural factors on local and global scales influence the use of reproductive technologies in animal agriculture and conservation;
- Recognise how aberrant reproduction results in species decline;
- Identify current and emerging techniques being used to manipulate reproduction in animals and critique their use in agriculture and conservation;
- Evaluate the economic impact of manipulating animal reproduction;
Assessment Tasks:
- Proposal: 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Expert report: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Case based test (2 hours): 30% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 6 hours of teacher directed study per week, including ~3 hours of lecture content and ~3 hours of tutorials. 12 hours of self-directed study per week = 18 hours per week over an 8-week period for a total unit requirement of 144 hours.

Subject: Clinical embryology: Introduction (GRS4202)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit covers the patient assisted reproductive technology (ART) experience from a requirement for accessing this technology, seeking treatment options, pre-cycle preparation and ovarian stimulation. You will learn the theory behind all key embryology laboratory principles, including oocyte pick-up, sperm preparation, embryo culture, vitrification and embryo transfer. You will learn how the principles of embryo culture (temperature, pH, osmolarity and air quality) are applied in a human IVF laboratory to ensure embryo growth and development is not compromised. You will be able to confidently perform embryo selection based on morphology, and will have an appreciation for emerging embryo selection strategies. Embryo biopsy and the advanced field of preimplantation genetic testing of embryos will also be covered. You will be exposed to the latest research and technology in the field through experienced lecturers and industry experts and will be encouraged to consider the evolution and implementation of reproductive technologies from an ethical perspective. In an Australian context, you will evaluate the industry from a variety of stakeholder perspectives, including the current legislation and regulation that governs IVF practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically evaluate the assisted reproductive technology (ART) industry from the perspective of various stakeholders.
- Apply principles of embryo culture to optimise embryo growth and potential.
- Explain key procedures and processes used in clinical embryology.
- Create an effective way of communicating scientific information to ART patients.
- Demonstrate evidence-based reasoning to critically review a controversial ART issue.
Assessment Tasks:
- Examination (2 hours): 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Simulation (15 minutes): 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Critical review (4 minutes, 1,500 words): 40% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: This unit is held over a 6-week block in Semester 2. You are required to attend all lectures and interactives sessions, including a week long practical block. Expect 12 hours of teacher directed and self-directed study per week.
