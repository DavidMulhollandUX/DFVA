## Graduate Diploma in Engineering Management - Melbourne (Bundoora)
##### LGEMT
##### 120 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
If you’re determined to create sustainable change in the world of engineering, La Trobe’s Graduate Diploma in Engineering Management will give you the advanced skills and experience to take the next step in your career. Develop the commercial expertise to set yourself apart in this evolving industry.

Update your core knowledge in modern engineering practice and advance your career as a leader in a wide range of engineering disciplines. Delve into specialist subjects in management, innovation and sustainability – designed specifically for engineers – while you develop your understanding of core business principles in marketing, finance, organisational behaviour and business statistics.

In this interdisciplinary course, you’ll learn from experienced engineers and business professionals at the top of their fields. All your teachers have extensive industry experience and up-to-date knowledge of best practice. 

After successfully completing the Graduate Diploma in Engineering Management, you’ll be eligible to apply for La Trobe’s Master of Engineering Management. You’ll gain up to one year of advanced standing for the Master’s, so you could commence studying in second year. 

Want to create a career in sustainable engineering? Graduate ready to become an engineering leader. 

You'll learn:

  * Advanced engineering management skills  * Develop specialist skills in innovation, environmental sustainability, computer-aided engineering, and risk and cost engineering.

  * Australian engineering environment and sustainability practices  * Understand the sustainability practices used in the Australian engineering industry. Gain the skills to take the next big step in your career.

  * Transferable skills in business and management  * Learn how to lead and develop organisations, teams and individuals. Understand how to develop financial documents and take a strategic approach to management decisions.

### Career outcomes

Subject: ACCOUNTING AND FINANCE FOR MANAGERS (BUA4002)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Accounting and finance are critical skills for all professionals in the modern global environment. In this subject, you will be provided with an introduction to key concepts, theories and practical applications associated with accounting and finance in diverse organisational contexts. You will be exposed to content which will cover the nature of the accounting and financial environment and the types of accounting and financial practice and decision-making required from a business perspective.
Learning Outcomes:
- Construct, interpret, analyse and communicate information contained in a firms financial statements using appropriate digital and information tools.
- Examine and apply financial mathematics tools and techniques to determine cash flows, cost of capital, capital investments.
- Prepare budgets, manage working capital, and make appropriate pricing and production decisions.
- Examine the importance of basic accounting and finance principles, and differentiate the appropriate sources of finance for a firm.

Subject: ECONOMICS FOR GLOBAL LEADERS (BUA4003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Economics impacts on all aspects of business and society. Key individual, organisational and government decisions around the allocation of scarce resources are at the core of economics, and an understanding of economic concepts, frameworks and language can greatly enhance a leader’s capacity to make sound decisions and influence the decisions of those around them. This subject develops students’ knowledge of economic concepts and provides the opportunity to apply this knowledge to real world situations. Students will apply economic concepts and frameworks to current issues of concern for organisations and society. Students will learn how to evaluate and apply critical aspects of economics such as the main market forces of supply and demand; the choices producers make in relation to their costs; revenues and profit; competitive and monopoly market structures and how these affect market prices; the use of game theory to make more strategic economic decisions; measuring a nation's income and the cost of living; the drivers of economic growth and the causes of unemployment.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply economic frameworks to business and social problems for policy and decision-making.
- Construct and present logical and persuasive economic arguments and communicate concepts professionally.
- Research and apply economic concepts to predict how changes in economic conditions may impact individuals, businesses and industries within market structures.
- Critically analyse economic data to explain relationships between economic variables and their impacts on policy, business and individuals.

Subject: STATISTICS FOR BUSINESS AND FINANCE (BUA4004)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will develop basic data-analysis skills to investigate real-life problems in business and finance. You will focus on basic concepts in statistics and probability theory, methods of statistical inference, measures of linear relationship between variables including linear regression analysis. There is a strong emphasis on the application of these techniques to practical problems in business and finance using Microsoft Excel. The contents of the subject are in line with the Chartered Financial Analyst (CFA) quantitative analysis curriculum. Successful completion of this subject will lay a solid foundation for the further study of data analysis in business and finance.
Learning Outcomes:
- Ability to present work in a professional manner and to communicate effectively to a professional audience
- Use statistical principles and research skills in a collaborative team environment to solve practical problems and communicate the findings to people with various viewpoints
- Apply probability theory in business and financial decision making
- Apply and interpret concepts of descriptive statistics to real life data and interpretation of results
- Conduct estimating linear regression models and testing hypothesis to aid decision-making

- Effectively prepare, present and evaluate short, engaging speeches for a non-technical audience, using advanced communication principles

Subject: ADVANCED ENGINEERING INNOVATION (EMS5AEI)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject introduces students to design thinking methodology, where students will discover and define problems, conduct primary and secondary research, analyse data and discover insights, use ideation methods to develop ideas, use selection methods to select ideas, consider the marketing of an idea, evaluate the social, legal and environmental impacts, and prototype an idea at different levels of fidelity. Students will be guided through the workshops and lectures to develop a solution to a problem as a team using design thinking methodology and produce a portfolio documenting, discussing, and evaluating the approach taken.
Learning Outcomes:
- Conduct effective brainstorming and selection methods as part of the design thinking methodology to develop potential solutions to problems.
- Identify and evaluate problems that can be solved by innovation.
- Comprehensively investigate and report on the impact of technical, financial, societal, legal and environmental impact and viability of innovations and ideas.
- Effectively communicate innovative solutions from a developed portfolio, presenting technical and non-technical information.
- Use rapid prototyping techniques to develop concept work and prototypes.
- Investigate and apply understanding of current and future key areas of innovation in Australia and worldwide.
- Demonstrate effective verbal communication by presenting a pitch in a clear, concise and confident manner.
- Collect and analyse primary and secondary research.

Subject: APPLIED MANAGEMENT FOR ENGINEERS (EMS5EMT)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject introduces business concepts in theory and practice for engineers and technologists. The subject covers these five major areas: business strategies for engineers, management tools and resources, legal system, marketing and finances. Students will gain insight into business/company models and structures, innovation/technology adoption life cycle and business plan development. Emphasis is given to leadership and management for engineers and information/decision making tools. Students will be exposed to the Australian legal system including law of tort, professional negligence, industrial relations, insurance/risk, the ACCC and legislation. Important elements of marketing of services/products, marketing research, consumer behaviour and market segmentation are covered. Students are provided with an introduction to accounting, basic financial analysis and budget management.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop and appraise the function of business strategy, marketing, innovation, corporate finance, workplace laws, work, health and safety, and business ethics as applied to an engineering enterprise.
- Assess the financial performance of an engineering enterprise and conduct a comparative analysis of the performance of firms in a selected engineering sector.
- Work as a member of a team to develop a comprehensive business plan that includes marketing, budgeting, human resource management, and project management for an engineering enterprise and present this professionally

Subject: CIRCULAR ECONOMY IN PRACTICE (MGT5006)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: The circular economy is an economic system designed to minimise waste and continually use resources. The circular economy aims to generate economic and social value while ensuring environmental sustainability by creating closed-loop systems that minimise resource input and waste output. As businesses transition towards this model, job requirements are evolving to prioritise sustainability and circular economy practices. This subject explores the interplay between the circular economy and value chain management. In consultation with industry partners, it is designed to provide an engaging learning journey to understand, strategize, and apply circular economy principles, including sustainable resource utilization, waste minimization, and the integration of repair, reuse, and recycling through the lens of value chain management. Students will have the opportunity to work on selected industry projects to enhance their practical skills in identifying and addressing circular economy challenges, utilizing various tools and data-driven methods. Emphasizing the significance and urgency of circular value chain strategies, this subject aligns with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals, preparing students to make informed and responsible decisions in various business settings, both individually and collectively. The applied nature of the subject delivery enables the development of critical business skills essential for roles in the circular economy and sustainability domains.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate teamwork and leadership skills to collectively strategise, communicate, and execute practical circular economy practices in various business settings.
- Exercise professional judgment in addressing complex circular economy challenges.
- Critically analyse circular economy principles within value chains to enhance competitive advantage of organisations in various settings.

Subject: PROJECT LEADERSHIP (MGT5PLS)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject will provide you with an introduction to the competencies required to support effective leadership and governance for project management. In this subject you will examine a broad range of project management and leadership concepts to illustrate the significant role that effective project management leadership can have in project and organisational success. You will also examine projects as vehicles of strategic change and transformation. A range of issues associated with delivering project success will be covered, including: leadership styles and how effective leaders facilitate project success and change; project team planning; human resources in the project context; the role of effective governance, stakeholder management and engagement, and understanding people management practices to align team performance with project outcomes.
Learning Outcomes:
- Determine and address the diverse needs of project stakeholders, formulate innovative solutions and demonstrate sound decision making related to project management.
- Develop high level ability to recommend differentiated actions for leaders in different phases of the project lifecycle.
- As a project leader, demonstrate a high level of effective and appropriate communication and collaboration with project team members and different stakeholders.
- Examine and critically analyse major streams of leadership theory and how leadership relates to various aspects of project effectiveness and efficiency.
