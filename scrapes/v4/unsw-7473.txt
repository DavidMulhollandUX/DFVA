## Description
The Graduate Certificate in Public Service Implementation is designed for postgraduate scholars and professional public sector managers with appropriate undergraduate qualifications in a public administration, management or related discipline and/or extensive relevant professional experience. The program is designed for those involved in policy development or delivery wishing to gain an understanding of the concepts and principles that underpin effective public service implementation.

The program is particularly suitable for those who have moved into public policy or administration roles but have not yet got qualifications in the domain area. Potential learners can undertake a flexible entry with credit from specified microcredentials.

## Program Learning Outcomes (CILOs)
On successful completion of this program, students will be able to:

1. Undertake independent and collaborative inquiry so as to communicate effective public service implementation advice to a range of audiences.
2. Synthesise disciplinary knowledge and skills to achieve government and organisational objectives in a range of policy contexts.
4. Support policy implementation issues in a range of government contexts so as to ensure the best outcomes longer term.
3. Analyse  implementation issues and develop appropriate solutions, through critical thinking and problem solving skills that ensure efficient and effective outcomes.

## Admission Requirements
**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
To gain entry into a Graduate Certificate in Public Service Implementation, an applicant must meet one of the following entry requirements:

- Completion of a Bachelor degree with honours in the same or a related discipline* from a recognised institution; or

- Completion of a Graduate Diploma or Graduate Certificate in a related discipline* from a recognised institution; or

- Completion of a Bachelor degree in the same or a related discipline* from a recognised institution; and completion of at least three years relevant full-time professional experience; or

- Completion of a Bachelor degree in a non-related discipline; and completion of at least four years relevant full-time professional experience; or

- Evidence of other qualifications and professional experience to be assessed as acceptable grounds for admission into the program by the relevant Program Authority. In certain circumstances, students may be required to undertake and successfully complete a relevant non-award course as a condition for admission into the program. This non-award course may also be credited towards the program upon admission.

*Related Discipline/s: Business, Management, Economics, Accounting, Information Technology, Information Systems, Logistics, Human Resource Management, Project Management, Public Policy, Marketing Management, Law.

Subject: Approaches to Change (AGSM9271)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The course focuses on: concepts, frameworks and theories that you can use to guide your thinking and practice as a change agent tools and methods that you can use in critically important change processes such as: diagnosing what needs to be changed deciding on the best change to make building the required level of commitment to support and enact the change understanding and handling resistance.

- Clearly articulate the concepts, frameworks and theories that guide your understanding of organisational change and your practices in managing change.

- Work flexibly with a more expansive and diverse repertoire of ideas and perspectives - sometimes selecting particular perspectives for particular purposes, at other times working with multiple perspectives.
- Apply ideas and tools from the course to analyse the needs and opportunities for change, design changes and change processes, and devise effective ways of managing change.
- Identify and consider ethical, environmental and/or sustainability issues in making decisions about organisational change.
- Understand others' approaches to leading and managing change.
- Analyse and constructively critique a variety of different approaches to managing and leading change, and the ideas and assumptions that underpin them.
- Consider cultural and social dimensions of organisational change in your change-management practice.

Subject: People and Systems (ZCBU8102)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The People and Systems course considers the role of people in delivering organisational effectiveness and high performance, in conjunction with the impact of their managers on the outcomes. The concept of systems thinking and the role of people as part of those systems will be introduced. The course then considers the role of people and reflects on the way people will behave within the systems. The key course outcome is to ensure that managers recognise their role in enabling and supporting others to achieve their goals, and the consequences of not doing this well. The course will take students through a number of real world case studies and present the challenges faced by managers when first taking responsibility for a team of people within an organisation.
Learning Outcomes:
- Synthesise a range of theories about individuals and groups in order to evaluate a range of possibilities for implementing given strategies
- Determine the drivers of individual motivation and engagement and apply this knowledge to develop implementation plans and create effective teams
- Recognise the determinants of a organisational performance and the role of people achieving organisational performance
- Explain to others how people fit into systems theory, assessing the implications for middle manager decision making and recognising the elements of the people system, which will create instability or support adaptability.

Subject: Public Service Implementation (ZCBU8103)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: It is widely accepted that developing good policy is only one part of delivering effective outcomes; effective implementation is also critical. In this course students are introduced to an implementation cycle, which stresses an end user focus, and the elements provide the basis for the curriculum. Topics include: the impact of internal and external context; ways of engaging with stakeholders; success identification; system level change; capability identification and development; risk assessment and mitigation; performance and accountability management; measurement review and evaluation; enacting implementation; and continuous review. The aim is to prepare students for the messy reality of implementation through introducing the range of issues involved, and the inevitable complexity of the interactions of different public service stakeholders and organisations.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and analyse elements of context that shape successful implementation processes
- Synthesise and apply a range of theoretical perspectives on implementation in order to critically evaluate implementation effectiveness
- Recognise the interconnected nature of challenges and opportunities in implementation
- Discuss the components of implementation processes and the capabilities required to support them

Subject: Public Service Performance and Evaluation (ZCBU8104)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course is designed for those working in and around the public sector, at all levels of government, who wish or have responsibility for improving organisational performance. It will explore a range of performance monitoring and evaluation strategies, approaches and frameworks. It will examine the use of programme theories in public service evaluation and change processes as well as consider different types of available evidence and the contexts in which they are best suited. Qualitative and quantitative approaches to data collection including focus groups, interviews, surveys, and participatory research will be discussed. Students will develop skills in both designing and undertaking the analysis of data to create formative and summative evaluations, and skills in designing data collections useful for program managers to monitor program implementation including identifying emerging implementation risks.
Learning Outcomes:
- Distinguish between monitoring and evaluation and articulate their roles in the context of government policy and program implementation.
- Create arguments to support and apply the strategic use of evaluation and programme theories of change using a range of evaluation and monitoring frameworks and techniques.
- Analyse elements of context that are relevant to proposed change processes and develop appropriate evaluation designs.
- Synthesise a range of data and mid-range theory sources to prepare an evaluation strategy ready to be communicated to inform implementation/change managers.

Subject: Defence Logistics Management (ZCBU8105)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The United States Airforce has operated the B52 strategic bomber for over 70 years. It is expected that this capability will operate up to 100 years. The strategies, procedures and know how to operate such a high-end technical capability for so long demonstrate a bureaucratic discipline that has delivered great outcomes for the US Government. This course will provide students with a high-level overview of these types of strategies and how organisations are best placed to procure and sustain defence materiel to ensure the greatest capability possible for each taxpayer dollar spent. This includes bringing differing stakeholders on the procurement journey including non-defence related bureaucracy, defence industry and our political masters through the procurement process, ensuring best asset availability for minimal cost once the capability is online and ensuring a supply chain that can support these assets in challenging circumstances such as a warlike situation.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically appraise and make practical contributions to the body of knowledge necessary for the establishment and operation of efficient and effective supply chains of defence materiel.
- Reflect upon best practice for the procurement and sustainment of defence materiel through a thorough examination of an appropriate case study.
- Critically appraise methods for the identification of maintenance, inventory requirements and configuration management inputs to defence materiel to ensure the efficient and effective asset availability in the sustainment phase of the physical asset life cycle.
- Critically reflect on use of Life Cycle Costing (LCC) principles to manage defence materiel efficiently and effectively.
