## Description
Reproductive medicine is an expanding field. It is at the forefront of emerging medico-scientific technology offering career opportunities to those with demonstrable skills and knowledge in both clinical and non-clinical fields. Increasing numbers of medical practitioners are developing special interests in this area - particularly GPs, family planning practitioners and specialist gynaecologists working in menopause and infertility. In addition, there are similar specialisations emerging in nursing, counselling and allied health professions, as well as amongst biological scientists.

The Master of Reproductive Medicine integrates advanced scientific understanding of reproductive physiology, diagnoses of fertility challenges, and advanced infertility treatments including Assisted Reproductive Technologies (ART) using an evidence based, clinically focused curriculum. The program provides an opportunity for healthcare professionals to develop specialised knowledge and expertise in reproductive medicine.

This program is offered fully online, enabling health professionals to integrate their study program into their daily professional lives.

## Program Learning Outcomes (CILOs)
On successful completion of this program, students will be able to:

1. Demonstrate advanced professional practice through the synthesis of the ethical, social, and professional frameworks required to deliver patient-centred care in reproductive medicine across complex clinical contexts.
2. Apply advanced knowledge of appropriate research principles, methods and skills to evidence-based clinical practice in reproductive medicine.
3. Critically analyse and evaluate clinical research evidence to enhance clinical decision-making and improve patient outcomes in the field of reproductive medicine.
4. Demonstrate effective and adaptive communication skills and strategies applicable to diverse professional and patient contexts.

## Admission Requirements
**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
Entry into this program requires an undergraduate degree in medicine, nursing, or other health or medical science-related disciplines* AND one of the following:

- Honours or postgraduate qualifications in medicine, nursing or other health or medical science-related disciplines*; OR

- Commencement of a recognised postgraduate medical specialist training program (e.g. general practice, obstetrics & gynaecology); OR

- Minimum 1 year full-time equivalent of postgraduate professional experience in medicine, nursing or other health or medical science-related fields*.

* health or medical science-related disciplines include, but are not restricted to laboratory scientists, and allied health professionals.

Subject: Reproductive Anatomy and Physiology (SWCH9000)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This postgraduate course aims to provide you with an in-depth understanding of the anatomy and physiology of the reproductive systems of both males and females, essential for clinical practice in reproductive medicine and women’s health. In this course you will develop comprehensive knowledge of the reproductive system development, function, and dysfunction across the human lifespan, with emphasis on clinical applications and evidence-based practice. - Analyse the mechanisms of sexual determination, differentiation, and the developmental stages required to achieve reproductive competence.
- Critically evaluate the physiological processes which underpin fertility and infertility in context of clinical relevance
- Integrate the anatomical, physiological and psychosocial processes that influence sexual function and dysfunction in males and females.
- Explain the anatomy and physiology of the male and female reproductive systems and identify the hormonal mechanisms that regulate their functions

Subject: Contraception (SWCH9002)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course examines the clinical, social, epidemiological and ethical aspects of contraception and family planning. Students critically consider the various methods of contraception, sterilisation and pregnancy termination. Through case-based and evidence-informed learning, the course develops student’s capacity to provide nuanced, person-centred contraceptive counselling across diverse clinical, social, and demographic contexts.
Learning Outcomes:
- Appraise the mechanisms of action of contemporary and emerging contraceptive methods, and apply to individual patient needs.
- Critically analyse global contraceptive needs and evaluate how social, cultural, economic and policy factors shape equitable access to contraceptives across diverse populations.
- Evaluate and compare the various methods of contraception in terms of efficacy, benefits, risks and side-effects to support clinical decision-making.
- Develop and justify evidence-based contraceptive advice tailored to a range of clinical scenarios and individual patient/client’s needs.
- Integrate medical, psychosocial, cultural, and ethical considerations to determine the suitability of contraceptive options for individuals across a range of clinical needs, life stages, and reproductive goals
- Describe and justify the recommended management of complications or side effects associated with various methods of contraception.

Subject: Laboratory Aspects of ART (SWCH9006)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course deals solely with the laboratory aspects of Assisted Reproductive Treatment, from oocyte collection, gamete preparation and embryo culture up to and including methods of presenting results. It introduces the use of Quality Systems and risk management in human embryo culture, and the rationales for choosing various laboratory techniques and their potential complications. This course will provide a better understanding of the complexities of the laboratory and culture technique requirements currently in use. It has five main modules. The first two introduce the basic aspects of setting up an ART laboratory and highlight the importance of quality management systems including Benchmarking and evaluation of Performance Indicators to support meaningful self-assessment of laboratory outcomes. The third covers sperm preparation for ART, and in the fourth, we look at the biological processes that can affect laboratory outcomes, and briefly cover the oocyte retrieval process. The final module covers the practical aspects of an ART laboratory, such as insemination methods, fertilisation, embryo culture, embryo transfer, and cryopreservation. Assessments in this course are a formative MCQs, two summative essays and finished with a summative MCQs.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify common risks in ART laboratories, devise methods to control these risks and evaluate their effectiveness
- Identify the biological events that determine the overall outcome of an IVF cycle and discuss how they can be supported by the laboratory systems
- Outline the systems needed to create a functional ART Laboratory
- Evaluate the scientific principles, process management and risk minimisation methodologies necessary to maintain a functional ART laboratory

Subject: Ethics and Law In Reproductive Medicine (SWCH9009)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The focus of this course is primarily upon ethical, social and legal aspects of reproductive medicine, with particular reference to new reproductive technologies. The first part of the course focuses mainly upon ethical issues, relating to conceptuses, and fetuses, genetic engineering, pre-natal testing, genetic discrimination and commercialisation. The second part of the course looks at the legal issues associated with the regulation of reproductive technologies, with particular reference to access to assisted reproductive services, the use and disposal of gametes, surrogacy and posthumous reproduction.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify major ethical theories and principles related to reproductive medicine.
- Describe the laws regulating assisted reproduction.
- Discuss the social and political aspects of the law regulating reproductive technologies.
- Examine and relate to your own moral commitments and decisions, in addition to those of others.
- Apply skills of ethical reasoning and analysis to current issues in reproductive medicine and medical research.
- Formulate critical responses to the existing regulatory framework.

Subject: Reproductive, Perinatal Epidemiology and Biostatistics (SWCH9011)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course aims to provide an understanding of statistics methodology and clinical appraisal for students undertaking study in a reproductive and perinatal-health related program. This course is a core subject for the Programs 9014 Masters of Women's Health Medicine and 9065 Masters of Reproductive Medicine. This course aims to introduce students to research methodology and statistical analysis. Using examples drawn specifically from the field of reproductive and perinatal medicine this course will also develop in students the ability to critically analyse/appraise literature pertinent to that area. It is likely to be most relevant to those working primarily in a reproductive and perinatal health setting-in particular gynaecologists in training, sexual health physicians, primary care physicians and family planning practitioners as well as nurses, counsellors and scientists with a special interest in this field.
Learning Outcomes:
- Perform statistical data analyses using SPSS software including calculating measures of disease occurrence and association, statistical significance tests in hypothesis testing, correlation and simple linear regression and proportion differences.
- Distinguish between common study designs that can be used to address descriptive and analytic epidemiology research questions about health status, determinants of health and effectiveness of health services with particular reference to reproductive and perinatal medicine.
- Critically appraise the quality of published studies in reproductive and perinatal health, demonstrating the ability to assess study design and methods of data analysis, as well as interpret study methods, results and conclusions.
- Describe the concept ‘study validity’ and explain how factors could affect study validity, including selection bias, measurement error and confounding.
- Describe key statistical concepts used in common epidemiological studies (including hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, study power and significance levels).
- Describe key epidemiological issues related to reproductive and perinatal health (including infant and maternal mortality rates, perinatal terms and definitions)

Subject: Research Project in Reproductive or Women's Health (SWCH9019)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The project comprises enquiry-based learning in an area of Reproductive Medicine or Women's Health Medicine. Students are expected to demonstrate their ability to apply knowledge and skills gained in the course, through: identifying and defining a significant issue; systematically collecting relevant, up-to date information about the issue; analysing, interpreting and discussing the information; drawing conclusions; making recommendations; and writing a report in a manner consistent with academic standards at Master's level. The project may be in the form of a small-scale research study, a case study, or a clinical audit. Although candidates are advised to start planning early in their undertaking of the Masters Program, the project is normally undertaken in the last term of the Masters Program. Prior to enrolment, students need to identify a topic area, find a relevant and willing supervisor, contact the course convenor, fine-tune the project proposal, and ideally also obtain necessary ethics approvals. These are all time-limiting factors requiring 6-12 months of pre-planning. Please Note: It is a requirement of enrolling into SWCH9019 that students have achieved a Credit or higher in the SWCH9011 Introduction to Perinatal Epidemiology and Biostatistics course. Enrolment is by application, please contact Student HUB.
Learning Outcomes:
- Write a report which is consistent with academic standards at Masters level
- Identify and define a significant issue
- Draw conclusions, make recommendations where applicable
- Analyse, interpret and discuss the information
- Systematically collect relevant, up-to-date information about the issue
