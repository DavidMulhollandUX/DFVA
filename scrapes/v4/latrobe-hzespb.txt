### Overview
This degree delivers you a postgraduate qualification that combines the study of human movement with an understanding of how exercise is used for rehabilitation, disease management and injury prevention. You'll gain hands-on experience through at least 500 hours of clinical placement.

From an exercise science perspective, you'll investigate motor control, exercise physiology, biomechanics and anatomy. You'll gain an understanding of how to modify health behaviours in the community and how exercise programs can be used to optimise performance. You'll also learn how to work with groups such as the elderly, people with disabilities and athletes undergoing rehabilitation.

You'll explore how exercise is used to promote health and well-being, including the preventative and rehabilitative benefits of exercise for those with chronic illnesses or serious injuries.

Your clinical placements will teach you to develop and implement exercise, lifestyle and behavioural programs for people of all ages. This gives you with the clinical skills and confidence to make a genuine difference to the quality of people's lives.

The course structure lets you complete the required subjects for this 4.5 year degree in only four years.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Have the ability to develop and deliver individualised exercise programmes, to include pre-participation screening, risk appraisal, and exercise and performance assessments
**2.**
Understand the many physiological, psychological, social and environmental factors influencing participation and adherence to a physically active lifestyle
**3.**
Determine how age, gender, culture, socioeconomic and development status can influence the individual's exercise capacity and motivation to participate in regular physical activity; and how physical activity, in turn, influence growth and development
**4.**
Combine general nutritional principles with exercise advice to increase the effectiveness of health and wellbeing interventions
**5.**
Apply knowledge of anatomy and biomechanics to evaluate movement and devise safe and effective fitness programs, improve athletic performance, correct improper technique, prevent injuries and regain physical fitness after injury
**6.**
Undertake, evaluate and present an independent research project in the discipline of Exercise Science
**7.**
Evaluate the acute chronic physiological responses to exercise and apply this knowledge to develop effective programs that encourage individuals to incorporate regular physical activity into a healthy lifestyle
**8.**
Determine the relationships between physical activity, sedentary behaviours and lifestyle-related diseases, such as cardiovascular disease, obesity, diabetes mellitus, asthma, osteoporosis and osteoarthritis

Compulsory placements (Work Based Learning)

Student exchange and overseas short programs offer a unique opportunity for a meaningful learning and educational experience abroad. La Trobe University encourages all students to make the world their campus by participating in an exchange or overseas short program that fits within your degree structure.
### Career outcomes
Accredited Exercise physiologists are specialists who use exercise interventions to condition clients for optimal health/performance and to treat patients with, or at elevated risk of developing, chronic illness or injury. The demand for accredited exercise physiologists is high, and expected to increase in future years, due to various factors including the ageing Australian population and an increased demand on the health care system due to chronic diseases such as obesity and diabetes.

Subject: ADVANCED EXERCISE BIOMECHANICS (EXS3AEB)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject will advance your theoretical and practical understanding of exercise and sports biomechanics. In this subject you will explore the relationship between biomechanical principles of human movement that underpin common activities of daily living to observe, describe, measure and analyse gross body movements relevant to performance in sport and exercise. This subject will examine the biomechanics of human walking and running gait and the changes which occur with development and ageing. This subject will introduce you to principles of tissue mechanics and will explore the contribution of biomechanics to the field of sports injury prevention.
Learning Outcomes:
- Interpret the biomechanical properties of human tissue and analyse adaptations to loading, over and under use, aging and pathological function.
- Evaluate injury occurrence and predisposing factors across sport and exercise performance including assessment of injury mechanisms.
- Analyse and evaluate (individually and in groups) biomechanical data in order to solve quantitative problems involving linear and angular kinematic and kinetic quantities.
- Critically assess appropriate measurement methods and perform and report biomechanical measurements involving temporal- spatial, kinematic and kinetic quantities.
- Evaluate the stages of development and patterns of muscular activity for normal gait, and identify biomechanical parameters that are commonly assessed in clinical gait analysis, including aging and pathological function.

Subject: SKILL ACQUISITION (EXS3SKA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Success in exercise and sport depends on your ability to develop a specific set of perceptual, cognitive and motor skills. This subject will expand your theoretical and practical understanding of motor performance and skill learning. Overview: In this subject you will complete 140 hours of professional placement within an approved health organisation. These hours will contribute to the requirements for registration as an Accredited Exercise Physiologist with Exercise and Sports Science Australia. You will need to prove your professional skills, including communication and patient assessment. You will demonstrate your independent clinical exercise prescription skills under minimal supervision from appropriately qualified and experienced clinicians working in a range of healthcare settings. In this subject you will refine and finalize your skills and knowledge necessary for competent practice. This subject will prepare you for graduate practice; you will create your professional portfolio and prepare a cover letter for a job application. A practical competency exam (simulated patient exam) is an important part of this subject.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply advanced skills in exercise assessment to inform management strategies in diverse clinical populations..
- Use evidence based practice to justify the implementation of effective exercise-based interventions for diverse clinical populations based on specialised theoretical knowledge.
- Adapt strategies for communicating with patients, medical and allied health professionals across different healthcare settings.
- Critical reflect upon theory, contemporary research, and professional practice to identify areas of professional practice for development
- Apply advanced knowledge and skills to demonstrate autonomy, expert judgement, adaptability and responsibility across diverse health care settings and clinical populations.

Subject: RESEARCH STUDY (EXS5RES)
Credit Points: 30
Overview: In this subject, you will develop professional practice as an allied health professional by using research to inform clinical reasoning. This subject aims to develop your research skills and apply discipline-specific knowledge and techniques to address a clinically relevant question. Both individually and as part of a small group, you will work on a research project that will include: planning, project management, data collection/cleaning, data analysis, critical interpretation/evaluation and dissemination of findings. These are essential skills to support evidence-based clinical practice. You will be expected to work independently in self-directed learning.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically examine, analyse and interpret data using appropriate quantitative and/or qualitative methods to inform clinical reasoning and implications for practice.
- Disseminate and defend findings, actions and recommendations using oral and written formats.
- Synthesise and critique relevant literature to inform clinical practice or areas for further research.
- Conduct independent and collaborative scholarly work to review and evaluate the efficacy and effectiveness of clinical interventions to health and healthcare.

Subject: HUMAN BIOSCIENCES A (HBS1HBA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject introduces you to human physiology - how the body works. You'll learn how the body is organised and the basics of cell structure and function, and how processes in the body are controlled. This will provide the foundation for the study of the major body systems important for support and movement (muscular system); regulation, integration and control (nervous and endocrine systems); fluids and transport (blood, heart, cardiovascular and immune systems); energy, maintenance and environmental exchange (respiratory, digestive and renal systems, metabolism); and the continuity of life (reproductive systems).
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the basic physiology of major organ systems of the human body.
- Apply knowledge of physiology concepts to clinical and disease states.
- Demonstrate oral and written communication skills.
- Demonstrate the skills for self-managing, reflection, and successful learning at university.
- Describe the structure and function of body cells, tissues, and organs.
- Apply specialist terms and language associated with physiology and anatomy to describe the human body.

Subject: HUMAN BIOSCIENCES B (HBS1HBB)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you are introduced to the study of anatomy. An overview of anatomical terminology, basic tissue types and a variety of techniques used to visualize the human body will be given followed by a more detailed study of the anatomy of the musculoskeletal and nervous systems. Anatomical principles and terminology will be applied to relevant body systems and the concept of integrated function of multiple systems in one body region will be introduced through the study of the trunk. You will be taught via an online-only OR blended (on-line and face-to-face) approach.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the anatomical structure and function of major body systems.
- Use appropriate anatomical terminology to describe anatomical structures and events.
- Apply observational and communication skills to make accurate observations of anatomical structures or events and infer their relationship to function using various anatomical resources, the living body or medical imaging.

Subject: RESEARCH AND EVIDENCE IN PRACTICE (HLT1RAE)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject is an introduction to the use of research-based evidence in professional health care practice. Working in interprofessional teams and using a range of case scenarios, you will develop research skills in areas relevant to your field of practice. Through online activities and workshops, you will learn about the role of evidence-based practice in health. Areas of study include systematic approaches to acquiring evidence, critical appraisal of the literature, interpretation of research design, descriptive and inferential statistics and assessment of research outcomes. You will learn how an evidence-based approach in health informs clinical practice. You will develop research skills to determine the most appropriate intervention techniques for application in a given clinical population, while understanding the complex interaction between social, economic and environmental influences that contribute to sustainability thinking in health research.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically appraise some components of the validity of health research evidence
- Explain the relative merits and uses of the different types of evidence used in practice
- Communicate research outcomes appropriate to peers
- Develop an effective search strategy to address practice based research questions

Subject: SOCIAL DETERMINANTS OF HEALTH (PHE1SDH)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will examine the ways health, well-being and illness are experienced, understood, and responded to by people as social beings who participate in various groups, organisations, communities, and societies. Theories and frameworks will be examined and assessed so that you can develop an ability to become aware of and interpret various life concerns both personally and professionally and gain a more nuanced awareness of the dynamics operating between the personal and public domains.

- Research and use different types of data and information to understand and explain health and illness issues within a diverse social and cultural context.
- Compare health and illness outcomes experienced by groups from different social and cultural contexts and explain why differences occur.
- Examine how social and cultural factors shape and determine people's experience of health and illness in different parts of the world.
- Discuss the interactions between health and illness, social perceptions of disease, culture and other societal structures and institutions.

Subject: SPORT AND EXERCISE PSYCHOLOGY (PHE3SEP)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will focus on the scope, aims and techniques of sport and exercise psychology. The scope of sport psychology is vast and expanding and goes beyond the sporting field. Sport and exercise psychology aims to enhance both performance and wellbeing through the application of psychological principles. This field goes beyond the goal of winning, focusing on improving various aspects of life, including sport, fitness, health and overall enjoyment. This subject combines theory with practical application, employing cognitive-behavioural techniques to boost motivation, reduce competition anxiety, foster teamwork and manage stress in various areas of life such as work, study and family. It also addresses issues such as injury management, promoting physical activity, substance use awareness, working with diverse populations and navigating life transitions. This subject is designed for third-year students. To maximise your chances of success in achieving the intended learning outcomes, it is recommended that you complete this subject in your third- year.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse performance scenarios and identify tailored solutions to influence positive behaviours in sport and exercise settings.
- Demonstrate an understanding of the psychological influences on injury risk, rehabilitation, and performance to improve outcomes for individuals and multi-disciplinary teams.
- Collaborate as part of a team to analyse the impact of psychological factors on athlete performance, and develop an evidence-based intervention to improve performance.
- Understand and apply Sport and Exercise Psychology theories and evidence to increase performance in sport and exercise settings.

Subject: CHRONIC DISEASE MANAGEMENT (PTY5CDM)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will integrate core strands of knowledge and practice with contemporary health needs of individuals, groups, communities and populations. You will develop knowledge and skills in interventions and management plans for people with chronic conditions in both group and individual contexts as well as health leadership and change management skills. You will critique models of health, theories of health behaviour and apply innovative problem-solving skills to health-related scenarios covering a range of chronic conditions. Principles of ethical leadership, change management, social justice, cultural competence, interprofessional, person-centred, evidence-based practice and critical reflection approaches will be adopted during the exploration of topics.
Learning Outcomes:
- Collaboratively investigate and develop ethical health leadership behaviours to responsibly manage change and promote sustainable and contemporary healthcare practice.
- Collaboratively and individually evaluate factors that contribute to health inequality between Indigenous and non-Indigenous Australians and develop recommendations to improve health outcomes for Indigenous Australians.
- Critically evaluate and respond to barriers and enablers of health for clients with chronic conditions
- Critically evaluate and design evidence-based recommendations for clients with chronic conditions
- Develop and evaluate culturally sensitive, effective and safe health programs that draw on the principles of inclusion, equity and accessibility for clients with chronic conditions.
