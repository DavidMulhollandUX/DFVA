You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
Equitable and sustainable human development remains a pressing global concern. Complex socio-political, health, economic, cultural, humanitarian, and ecological challenges contribute to extreme poverty, reduced human development, fragile governance and environmental vulnerability affecting the well-being of millions of people worldwide. These complex, multidimensional challenges require adaptable professionals with the practical skills and analytical capacity to strategically and ethically address them at community, national and global levels. Our graduates can adapt rapidly to changing circumstances while understanding that at the core of development practice are enduring principles of social justice, equity, inclusion, and sustainability

The Master of International Development Practice offers a multi-disciplinary, applied approach to the theory and practice of sustainable human development. Delivered by specialists across the humanities and social sciences, medicine, business and economics, education and law Faculties, the program delivers rigorous research training alongside the critical and practical skills of project design and management and leadership skills. 

You may choose to focus your electives in one of the following areas:

  * democracy, justice and governance

  * gender, peace and security

  * crisis, change management and leadership

  * sustainable resource management

  * sustainable economies

  * Leading in multicultural and intercultural contexts

Opportunities for fieldwork, study abroad and internship engagement include options to engage in field-schools. 

Graduate employment opportunities may include local and international non-government organisations, human rights advocacy, aid agencies, government, social enterprise, corporate social responsibility, the business sector, universities, community organisations, and international institutions such as the United Nations,  World Bank and International Migration Organisation.

### Structure
The course is structured in four parts. Part A. Core studies, Part B. Applied studies, Part C. Professional practice studies, Part D. Elective studies. 

A research pathway is also available subject to meeting eligibility requirements.

Part A. Core studies 

These studies will immerse you in international development practice at graduate level and draw on best practices within the broad realm of international development theory, practice and research to address questions ranging from the causes of inequality and disparity, evolving aid and development thought and practice, to the impact of politics, economics, culture, history and natural resources human development.

Part B. Applied studies  

The focus of these studies is professional or scholarly work that can contribute to a portfolio of professional development.

Part C. Professional practice studies

Designed in association with industry experts, select from this suite of professional enhancement offerings to build essential competencies for your career pathway

Part D. Elective studies 

Select from a range of options designed to enhance specific knowledge and skills in international development practice and related fields.

Master's entry points

Depending on prior qualifications you may receive entry level credit (a form of block credit) which determines your point of entry to the course: 

  * If you are admitted at entry level 1, you complete 96 credit points, comprising Part A, Part B, Part C and Part D.
  * If you are admitted at entry level 2, you complete 72 credit points, comprising Part A, Part B, Part C (6 credit points), Part. D (18 credit points).
  * If you are admitted at entry level 3, you complete 48 credit  points, comprising Part A and Part B.

Note: Students eligible for credit for prior studies may elect not to receive the credit and complete one of the higher credit-point options.

Course progression maps

If you are eligible to enrol in the 1.5-year entry point, you have the option of completing this course intensively over a 12-month period by following the intensive course progression map.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
communicate research findings effectively and perceptively in a range of formats to a variety of audiences.
**2.**
critically analyse the political, economic, cultural and environmental challenges contributing to human inequalities;
**3.**
employ ethics research methodology to plan and conduct an independent research project;
**4.**
critically appraise the range of organisations and institutions involved in international development, their working culture and funding processes;
**5.**
apply theoretical learning and best practice within the broad professional and practical context  of international development practice, policy, governance and research;
**6.**
explain and evaluate complex sustainable development challenges and debates across diverse cultural and geo-political contexts;

### AHEGS
The Master of International Development Practice is a postgraduate course. It is taught in English and normally takes two years of full-time study or four years of part-time study. The course offers a multi-disciplinary applied approach to the theory and practice of sustainable development through research training, project management and leadership skills development within an interdisciplinary curriculum. Graduates will demonstrate a critical understanding of the political, economic, cultural and environmental challenges contributing to human inequalities and be able to articulate and apply knowledge of best practice within the broad realm of international development practice, policy, governance and research. The Master of International Development Practice is a Level 9 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Professional internship (APG5044)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: You will conduct an internship with a domestic or international host partner organisation. Partner organisations may be from a diverse range of industries and sectors, including government departments, private industry and not-for-profit organisations. While undertaking your internship, you will undertake tasks and/or contribute to a workplace project as defined by the host partner organisation. Internship tasks, such as research, may be carried out on an affiliated arrangement where physical location at the host organisation is not required. The host partner organisation will appoint a field supervisor for the project and the Unit Coordinator and/or Chief Examiner at Monash University will assess your assignments for the unit.
Learning Outcomes:
- Communicate in a clear and coherent way that is effective for the purpose and the intended audience
- Reflect on workplace experiences and craft a meaningful and insightful response to these reflections
- Apply broad discipline knowledge to find solutions to complex problems
- Show technical skill in designing, conducting and reporting on an independent research project
- Collaborate with others on a project in a workplace setting
- Exercise critical thinking and professional judgement in developing new understandings
Assessment Tasks:
- Exercise: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Presentation: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Report: 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Applied Practice Project - Analysis: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Career development task: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Applied Practice Project - Reflection: 40% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 288 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Scheduled activities may include a combination of teacher directed learning, peer directed learning and online engagement.

Subject: Gender and development (APG5045)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The unit will introduce you to the main debates, concepts and practices in the field of gender and international development. You will explore broader debates and conceptual frameworks related to the study of gender, gender equity, and gender justice, including how gender operates with other relations of power, such as race, ethnicity, class, and disabilities, that inform current development practice. Using available datasets and indicators, we will examine historical and contemporary trends in gender inequality and analyse why and how gender inequality and injustice are pervasive, albeit variegated, worldwide. We will identify approaches to reduce gender inequality and promote social inclusion in development practice. You will gain an overview of gender analysis methods, tools and frameworks that can be applied across a range of sectors. You will be given the opportunity to apply these skills to real case studies drawn from contemporary non-government organisations, government and social movements grappling with complex geopolitical and environmental realities. The unit will conclude by examining the drivers and actors involved in transformative change. You will have the opportunity to develop reflexive skills that are essential for navigating the complex terrain of working in international development.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse how individual and collective agency is exercised to advocate for and organise for change in various ways;
- identify, assess, and apply gender analysis methods, tools and frameworks commonly used in development practice;
- Develop and apply reflexive thinking that can be applied to a wide variety of professional environments.
- Develop an understanding of strategies to work with policymakers when pursuing policy or legislative change pertaining to gender equality;
- identify and explain theories and concepts on gender and gender relations as they pertain to development practice;
