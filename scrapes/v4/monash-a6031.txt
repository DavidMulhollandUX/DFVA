## Master of Strategic Communications Management (Double Masters with Shanghai Jiao Tong University)
##### A6031
##### 96 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
This international double master's program enables you to complete the requirements of the Monash Master of Strategic Communications Management as well as a second degree with Shanghai Jiao Tong University (SJTU).

You will study one year at Monash University in Australia and one year at the partner university. On successful completion of the academic program at Monash and the academic program at the partner university, you will be eligible to graduate with the degrees from both institutions, awarded separately.

This double degree course is subject to maximum quotas for each partner university. If you are interested you must inquire directly to the course coordinator for Master of Strategic Communications Management in the Faculty of Arts at Monash, and if eligible, will be invited to apply.

The Master of Strategic Communications Management provides you with professional skills, specialist knowledge and key capabilities relevant to career pathways in a wide range of strategic communication related fields. This industry-focused degree develops core competencies in critical areas of advocacy and persuasion, public relations and crisis communications, audience research and data analytics across government, corporate, not-for-profit and community sectors. Drawing on best practices locally and globally, graduates will be equipped to build effective and ethical communication strategies across local and international cultures and be responsive to a digitally integrated world.

### Structure
The course is structured in three parts. Part A. Core studies, Part B. Applied studies and Part C. Partner studies. 

Part A. Core studies and Part B. Applied studies
These studies draw on best practices within the broad realm of strategic communications studies practice and research to further your understanding of strategic communications management both locally and globally. Studies focus on the challenges posed and how communication management can play a key role in an increasingly global and digital environment.

Part C. Partner studies 
On transferring to SJTU you will complete studies, as approved by the Department of Journalism and Communication in the Faculty of Media and Design SJTU, equivalent to 48 credit points of electives in the Monash Master of Strategic Communications Management.

### Requirements
The course comprises 96 points structured into three parts: Part A. Core studies (36 points), Part B. Applied studies (12 points) and Part C. Partner studies (48 points). 

Monash students will normally commence the first year at Monash (in July), and the second year at SJTU. Following completion of the academic program at Monash comprising 48 credit points (one year full-time) of study towards the Master of Strategic Communications Management, selected students will travel to China to complete a further 48 points (one year full-time) study toward the Master of Journalism and Communication from Shanghai Jiao Tong University (SJTU). The thesis will take place at the end of the second year at SJTU (normally around early June). 

SJTU students will normally commence the first year at SJTU, and the second year at Monash (July). They will then return to SJTU to complete the requirements of the thesis.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
apply a variety of research methodologies to real-world problems of communication management, and develop effective solutions within an ethical framework;
**2.**
enter professional practice equipped with industry-relevant specialist skills that demonstrate intercultural and global competence in a world of constant technological and social change.
**3.**
critically interpret and evaluate secondary literature in the field of study, and the key texts and cultural products pertaining to strategic communication;
**4.**
demonstrate and analyse the possibilities, challenges and impact of strategic communication;
**5.**
apply advanced reading, communication and analytical skills, both written and verbal;

Subject: Professional internship (APG5044)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: You will conduct an internship with a domestic or international host partner organisation. Partner organisations may be from a diverse range of industries and sectors, including government departments, private industry and not-for-profit organisations. While undertaking your internship, you will undertake tasks and/or contribute to a workplace project as defined by the host partner organisation. Internship tasks, such as research, may be carried out on an affiliated arrangement where physical location at the host organisation is not required. The host partner organisation will appoint a field supervisor for the project and the Unit Coordinator and/or Chief Examiner at Monash University will assess your assignments for the unit.
Learning Outcomes:
- Communicate in a clear and coherent way that is effective for the purpose and the intended audience
- Reflect on workplace experiences and craft a meaningful and insightful response to these reflections
- Apply broad discipline knowledge to find solutions to complex problems
- Show technical skill in designing, conducting and reporting on an independent research project
- Collaborate with others on a project in a workplace setting
- Exercise critical thinking and professional judgement in developing new understandings
Assessment Tasks:
- Exercise: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Presentation: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Report: 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Applied Practice Project - Analysis: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Career development task: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Applied Practice Project - Reflection: 40% (Hurdle: None)

Subject: Data analytics in communications (APG5374)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The strategic application of ‘big data’ is transforming the field of communications. As our everyday communication practices increasingly become digital, mobile and social, there has been an explosion of data that is routinely collected and analysed for strategic purposes. Building on APG5373, this unit explores the intersection between audiences and publics, and digital data. In this unit, you will learn how to make use of data analytics to examine the dynamics, flows and effectiveness of communication processes across digital and social media platforms. You will acquire skills in gathering, analysing and visualising digital data to gain a better understanding of audience sentiment, behaviour and engagement. Drawing on case studies and practical research, you will understand the value of data analytics in informing communication strategies. You will also engage with the ethical and legal concerns at the heart of public debates relating to the collection and use of ‘big data’.
Learning Outcomes:
- gather, analyse and visualise digital data in the context of communication flows;
- apply advanced communication, research and analytical skills.
- assess the distinct ethical, legal and public interest implications raised by the use of big data;
- discuss and interpret the results of data analytics to provide insights to processes of communication;
- critique the role of big data in the field of strategic communications;
