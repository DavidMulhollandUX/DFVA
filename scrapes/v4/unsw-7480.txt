## Description
The Graduate Certificate in Actuarial Analytics provides specialised training in data analytics, financial mathematics, and risk management, equipping students with the quantitative and analytical skills essential for actuarial and financial careers. The program develops expertise in actuarial models, probability, machine learning, and artificial intelligence, preparing graduates for high-demand roles in financial services, insurance, superannuation, and risk management.

All actuarial courses are accredited by the Actuaries Institute, ensuring they meet professional standards and support your career progression in actuarial science, finance, and business.

## Career Opportunities & Employment Areas
The Certificate of Actuarial Analytics allows you to obtain and develop the required competencies to enter an actuarial career and provides data analytics and quantitative risk management training for entry into the financial services industry. The program covers the professional actuarial subjects and includes options in actuarial studies, data analytics, quantitative risk management, and other related disciplines.

## Progression & Academic Rules
The Graduate Certificate in Actuarial Analytics is associated with the Master of Actuarial Studies and Master of Actuarial Studies (Extension) as similar programs, both of which have a higher entry requirement of a WAM of 65 or above.

Students who complete the Graduate Certificate in Actuarial Analytics with a WAM of 65 or above are eligible to transfer into either the Master of Actuarial Studies or the Master of Actuarial Studies (Extension).

This program provides a pathway for students who narrowly missed the initial WAM requirement of 65, offering them an opportunity to progress into the Master of Actuarial Studies (MAct) upon successful completion.

## Admission Requirements
**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
To be eligible for the program, you must have:

- A recognised bachelor degree (or equivalent qualification) in mathematics, statistics, econometrics, actuarial studies, financial engineering, data science, data engineering, physics, engineering, or other related quantitative field with a minimum WAM of 60.

Assumed knowledge: mathematical background in calculus and linear algebra at first-year university undergraduate level

Notes:

Students without an Actuarial background are advised to begin their studies in Term 1, as core courses are only offered in Term 1 each year.

**Limitations on Recognition of Prior Learning**:
Students with an Actuarial background may be granted exemptions for core courses based on their previous Actuarial studies (bachelor's degree or higher). Students who receive exemptions must complete additional electives to meet the program’s UOC requirements.

Subject: Survival Analysis and Prediction of Life and Health Related Risks (ACTL5104)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course covers the actuarial profession syllabus for survival models, focusing on understanding and predicting mortality, morbidity, and longevity risks. It applies these techniques to life, health, and disability insurance, superannuation, finance, and epidemiology. These models help forecast risks related to events such as mortality, health changes, and credit defaults, leading to better risk quantification, pricing, and management. Key topics include: survival models and actuarial notation; Estimation of lifetime distributions; Maximum likelihood estimation of transition intensities in multi-state models, considering age and duration; graduation of crude estimates and tests for fidelity and smoothness; and models for projecting mortality trends. The course emphasizes implementing these concepts using numerical computer packages. Additionally, it will address ethical considerations and issues related to discrimination and heterogeneity in mortality and health risks.
Learning Outcomes:
- Devise exact and census approximation methods to estimate transition intensities dependent on age,
- Apply estimation procedures for lifetime distributions.
- Explain and critically appraise the ethical dimensions and implications of the modelling introduced in the course.
- Explain the concepts of survival models.
- Explain the Binomial and Poisson models of mortality and contrast these models with multiple state models.
- Apply and contrast alternative graduation methods and graduation tests.
- Apply and contrast the main methods of projecting/forecasting mortality rates.
- Derive maximum likelihood estimators for the transition intensities of a multiple state model.

Subject: Quantitative Risk Management Techniques (ACTL5301)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Recent occurrences of significant losses in the financial and insurance markets associated with catastrophic events such as heatwaves, floods, cyclones, bushfires, and earthquakes have intensified the need for a systematic approach to modelling, measuring, and managing catastrophe risks. Culprits behind catastrophe risks include frequency, severity, and dependence. This course introduces students to advanced statistical and actuarial models for a variety of risks in financial institutions such as banks, insurance/reinsurance companies, and superannuation funds. Traditional and newly emerging risks encountered therein will be studied, with a special focus on extreme losses, extreme dependence, and loss aggregation over time. Topics covered include: risk measures; multivariate models; copulas and dependence; financial time series; extreme value theory; market credit and operational risk. Students will be implementing the models using computer software.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop and refine skills in presenting, discussing, and writing about risk-modeling concepts, ensuring clarity and effectiveness in communicating quantitative risk management.
- Systematically search, identify, and review recent research on quantitative risk modeling to stay updated with cutting-edge advances.
- Estimate using diverse models and implement these models in real-world applications, demonstrating their practical use.
- Critically assess various models used in risk management, identifying their benefits and limitations to enhance their practical applications.
- Conduct a comprehensive review of advanced risk models to enhance understanding of complex modeling techniques.
- Identify both theoretical and practical aspects of quantitative risk modeling as applied to insurance and financial risks.

Subject: Risk Analytics (RISK5002)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course covers risk analytics tools and techniques that individuals and organisations can use to manage a wide range of risks, including strategic, operational, environmental, health and safety, engineering reliability and cyber risks. Based on the ISO 31000 and IEC/ISO 31010 international standards for risk management, this course introduces analytics tools and techniques to measure, quantify and analyse risks. The course focuses on the application of these tools to risk assessment, which is a key component of the risk management process. Emphasis is placed on the practical applications of these approaches using qualitative, semi-quantitative and quantitative tools and techniques, including Scenario Methods, Risk Maps, Cybersecurity Risk Assessment, Human Reliability Analysis, Environmental Risk Assessment, and approaches to dealing with extremes.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the main types of risks faced by financial institutions and banks, and realise that many of the ideas and approaches are equally applicable to non-financial corporations
- Develop an understanding of the need for quantification and practical issues in quantifying risks relevant for the operating environment of the corporation
- Locate and evaluate the research literature on current developments in risk management strategies, for example stress testing and liquidity risks are receiving more attention
- Present and discuss simulation based approaches to analyse risks in complex systems

Subject: Risk Decision Analytics (RISK5003)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Risk Decisions provide a systematic, quantitative, and visual approach to addressing and evaluating important choices confronted by decision makers. They enable individuals and organisations to make better decisions by using a variety of Decision Analysis tools. This course introduces Decision Analysis procedures, techniques, and tools which enable individuals and organisations to make better decisions by assessing all relevant information and evaluating decision outcomes to aid in the decision-making process. It focuses on the application of these structured approaches to develop solutions to decision problems involving uncertainty and multiple objectives. The course covers Decision Analysis tools used to model decisions, uncertainty, and preferences, including Decision Trees, Influence Diagrams, Sensitivity Analysis, Bayesian Networks, and Monte Carlo Simulation. It also covers the Decision Analysis approaches to incorporating risk attitudes in decision making and using Multi-criteria Decision Analysis.

Subject: Risk Management Strategies (RISK5009)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Navigating the volatile world of financial markets demands a precise and efficient risk assessment framework. This course empowers the participants with various risk measurement techniques which are commonly employed by financial industry professionals. Topics include the foundational concepts for risk analysis, such as probability distributions, statistical inference, linear regression, time series analysis, and risk modelling techniques. Furthermore, key measurement methodologies, such as VaR, expected shortfall, parametric and non-parametric estimation, default likelihood, credit VaR, expected and unexpected loss for various risks will be introduced.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply a range of quantitative methods to measure various risks
- Explain the advantages and disadvantage of the measurement approaches
- Understand the foundational statistical concepts for risk analysis
- Connect theoretical concepts with real-world risk modelling applications

Subject: Generative AI for Risk Management (RISK5110)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course positions generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) as a transformative tool for identifying, assessing and mitigating risks across financial services and related sectors. Through research-infused seminars, hands-on labs and real-world case studies, students will learn to: integrate GenAI into enterprise risk management frameworks; apply stochastic and simulation techniques to quantify uncertainty in model outputs; generate synthetic risk scenarios for stress-testing and capital planning; and design responsible prompt-engineering workflows. Ethical, governance and regulatory considerations—including transparency, bias mitigation and auditability—are embedded throughout. Graduates will be equipped to lead the responsible adoption of GenAI in high-stakes risk management environments.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop responsible prompt-engineering pipelines tailored to risk-management use cases, balancing model performance with compliance requirements.
- Critically assess emerging research and tools in generative AI for risk applications, synthesising findings to inform best practices.
- Evaluate and mitigate ethical, governance, and regulatory risks in GenAI workflows, ensuring transparency, bias control, and auditability.
- Design and execute stochastic simulations and synthetic scenario generators to quantify tail risks and stress-test exposures.
- Integrate generative AI techniques into end-to-end enterprise risk-management frameworks, from risk identification to monitoring
- Communicate complex GenAI-driven risk analyses effectively in professional reports and executive presentations for both technical and non-technical audiences.
