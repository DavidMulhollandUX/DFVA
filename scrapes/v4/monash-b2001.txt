### Overview
The Bachelor of Commerce is a transformative course designed to equip you with the knowledge and skills needed to thrive in today’s dynamic business landscape. Through a blend of theoretical insights and practical applications, you will develop expertise in an area of commerce, crafting ethical solutions that resonate with business, not-for-profit, professional, and public policy communities. From investigating complex situations in organisations to generating innovative solutions using quantitative analysis and techniques, you will emerge as a strategic thinker capable of addressing challenges and driving positive change in organisations and societies.

In addition to rigorous academic training, you can choose to take part in exciting opportunities through study abroad and industry experience programs. Immerse yourself in different cultures, gain global perspectives, and enhance your cross- cultural competency through our study abroad programs. Furthermore, our industry partnerships provide invaluable hands-on experience, allowing you to apply your knowledge and skills in real-world settings and build connections with leading professionals in the field.

Career prospects are diverse and exciting, ranging from management consulting, financial analysis, marketing, international business, and sustainability management. Whether your passion lies in the realm of corporate enterprise or not- for-profit organisations, the course prepares you to make a meaningful impact and excel in your chosen path.

### Structure
This is a comprehensive course, structured in four parts: 

Part A. Core studies

These studies will provide you with a broad foundation for your study of commerce and expose you to several commerce disciplines. This will contribute breadth to your knowledge of commerce and address the graduate course outcomes. It will also give you the opportunity to learn more about each discipline before finalising your choice of major.

Part B. Major studies

Your major studies will provide you with a focused program of study that will develop your expertise in one discipline area. You will develop, apply and communicate an advanced level of understanding of the concepts and theoretical frameworks that constitute the knowledge base of your major area of study.

Part C. Capstone studies

This is designed to consolidate the knowledge and skills acquired throughout the study of your course.

Part D. Elective studies

This will enable you to further develop your knowledge of your chosen major, or commerce more broadly, through a second major or a minor. Alternatively, you can select units from across the University in which you are eligible to enrol.

Masters Accelerated Pathway

The Bachelor of Commerce offers an Accelerated Pathway option into the Master of Business, Master of Global Business, Master of Management, Master of Applied Marketing and Master of Regulation and Compliance. This Accelerated Pathway allows you to complete a master’s degree with one additional year of full-time study.

If you have completed at least 96 credit points and have achieved a high credit (65%) average, you can apply to the Course Director for the master’s accelerated pathway. If admitted to the accelerated pathway, you will use four units (24 credit points) of your Elective studies (Part D) to complete master-level units from one of the destination courses.

Note: You still need to meet the entry requirement of having an overall 65% weighted average mark at the end of your degree to enter the master’s program.

demonstrate relevant knowledge and technical skills in at least one area of commerce, apply them in providing ethical discipline-based solutions relevant to the business, professional and public policy communities that you serve

**8.**
communicate complex data, analytical insights, strategies, solutions and decisions to effectively engage with stakeholders in professional contexts

## ACC1100 - Introduction to financial accounting
Faculty: Department of Accounting | School: Faculty of Business and Economics | Credit points: 6

### Overview
This unit introduces you to fundamental accounting concepts and rules to provide a foundation for further study within the accounting discipline. Emphasis is placed on developing meaningful understanding of both accounting terminology and technical rules for recording, guided by the Conceptual Framework and selected Accounting Standards. Applying this knowledge along with critical thinking skills will facilitate evidence-based decision-making using accounting data, and the preparation and interpretation of financial statements.

### Learning Outcomes
1. FIRST SEMESTER: identify and analyse measurement systems and their relationship with the accounting Conceptual Framework SECOND SEMESTER: explain fundamental accounting concepts and rules by justifying how and why economic transactions are recorded in accounting systems
2. FIRST SEMESTER: apply the principles of double-entry and accrual accounting SECOND SEMESTER: apply the Conceptual Framework and selected Accounting Standards to record transactions and prepare basic financial statements
3. FIRST SEMESTER: describe and apply definitions and recognition criteria for assets, liabilities, incomes and expenses as specified in the Conceptual Framework SECOND SEMESTER: interpret accounting data to derive and communicate meaningful and relevant business insights
4. FIRST SEMESTER: apply principles of selected Accounting Standards and prepare financial statements for business entities including service and retail organisations SECOND SEMESTER: collaborate effectively in team-based accounting tasks to contribute disciplinary knowledge and support collective problem solving
5. FIRST SEMESTER: apply critical thinking, problem solving, and communication skills within corporate financial reporting topics. SECOND SEMESTER: analyse accounting data to identify issues and support reasoned conclusions in business contexts.

### Assessment Tasks

- 2 - Project: 29%
- 4 - Exercise: 24%
- 5 - Project: 26%

- 6 - Examination: 50%

## BTC1110 - Commercial law
Faculty: Department of Business Law and Taxation | School: Faculty of Business and Economics | Credit points: 6

### Overview
As the law affects all kinds of business activities, learning about the law as a non-lawyer is vital for everyone working in business. There are serious legal consequences for failing to comply with the law which in turn can impact upon business profitability and value. Understanding the law will enable you to appreciate how businesses are set up, to understand business risks, and to seize upon business opportunities, while also recognising when professional legal advice might be required. This unit covers essential topics in the Australian business world, including the Australian legal system, the law of contract, consumer protection, and the law of torts (including negligence as it affects business and as it applies to professionals). Privacy and data protection laws and the law of business organisations, including the law of agency, partnerships, and an introduction to company law, will also be covered.

### Learning Outcomes
1. FIRST SEMESTER: explain the nature of the Australian legal system and its role in regulating business activity SECOND SEMESTER: explain foundational aspects and institutional arrangements of the Australian legal system and its role in regulating business activity
2. FIRST SEMESTER: assess common legal scenarios arising in business and identify relevant legal principles to predict possible legal risks and outcomes SECOND SEMESTER: compare legal obligations and ethical considerations in the context of business decision-making and conduct
3. FIRST SEMESTER: understand key legal principles from contract law, consumer protection law, tort law, privacy and data protection law, and the law of business organisations, and the impact these principles have on business conduct SECOND SEMESTER: apply key areas of law, including contract law and tort law, to common business problems
4. FIRST SEMESTER: demonstrate an understanding of legal reasoning and apply this skill set to solve legal problems and inform business decision-making and conduct SECOND SEMESTER: employ legal reasoning to communicate professionally on legal issues.
5. FIRST SEMESTER: recall business ethical obligations arising under the law relevant to business activity and conduct.

### Assessment Tasks

## ECC1000 - Principles of microeconomics

### Overview

You will learn key tools such as supply, demand and market equilibrium, and apply them to everyday economic situations. ## ETC1000 - Business and economic statistics
