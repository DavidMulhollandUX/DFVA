## Master of Marketing and Digital Communications
##### A6039
##### 72 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
The Master of Marketing and Digital Communications provides you with professional skills, specialist knowledge and key capabilities relevant to career pathways in a wide range of strategic communication and marketing related fields. This industry focused degree is designed for those seeking cutting edge theoretical knowledge and problem solving skills in marketing and communications. It develops core competencies in critical areas of digital marketing, strategic and crisis communications, audience research and data analytics which can be applied to the government, corporate, not-for-profit and community sectors.

Drawing on best practices locally and globally, you will be equipped to build effective and ethical communication and marketing strategies across local and international cultures and be responsive to a digitally integrated world. Taught by a combination of leading academics and practitioners in the field, you can also choose to develop deep knowledge in key elective disciplines from across the Indonesia campus.

### Structure
The course comprises 72 credit points structured into three parts: Part A. Core studies, Part B. Applied studies and Part C. Discipline elective studies.

Part A. Core studies

These studies draw on best practices within the broad realm of communications and marketing practice and research to further your understanding of communications and marketing systems both locally and globally. These studies engage with theoretical and industry based perspectives to provide an understanding of how organisations can connect and engage with their different stakeholders to achieve specific communication and marketing objectives. Studies focus on the opportunities and challenges posed by the emergence of digital media, globalisation and increasing levels of cross-cultural exchange. The impact of technological change on marketing and communication behaviour will also be critically analysed.

Part B. Applied studies

The focus of these studies is professional and project work, focused locally or globally, that can contribute to a portfolio of professional development. In this part of the program you can also undertake higher level research.

Part C. Discipline elective studies

The focus of these studies is to develop your expertise in disciplines from across the Indonesia campus. These will provide you with the knowledge and skills needed to work and communicate across different industry and disciplinary contexts.

Masters entry points

Depending on prior qualifications you may receive entry level credit (a form of block credit) which determines your point of entry to the course:

  * If you are admitted at entry level 1 you complete 72 credit points, comprising Part A, Part B and Part C.

  * If you are admitted at entry level 2 you complete 48 credit points, comprising Part A (24 credit points), Part B (12 credit points) and Part C (12 credit points).

Note: Students eligible for credit for prior studies may elect not to receive the credit and complete one of the higher credit-point options.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
apply advanced research skills to communicate findings in a range of formats to professional and non-professional audiences;
**2.**
recognise and develop skills that demonstrate intercultural and global competence to undertake work in a world of constant digital disruption.
**3.**
critically interpret and evaluate secondary literature in the field of study, and the key texts and cultural products pertaining to marketing and digital communication;
**4.**
analyse and discuss the possibilities, challenges and impact of marketing and digital communication;
**5.**
apply a variety of research methodologies to real world problems of marketing and digital communication, and develop effective solutions within an ethical framework;

### AHEGS
The Master of Marketing and Digital Communications is a postgraduate degree. The course is taught in English and takes 1.5 years of full-time equivalent study to complete. Graduates develop expert critical knowledge and skills to deal with marketing and digital communications analysis issues and resolutions across the spectrum of public and private sector activities. The Master of Marketing and Digital Communications is a Level 9 Australian Qualification Framework (AQF) qualification. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Overview: The unit introduces you to public, private and not for profit institutions at international, national and local levels and how they impact upon the governance of communities in a range of international settings. Subject: Environmental governance and citizenship (APG5428)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this unit you will explore social and institutional frameworks for environmental governance. You will consider the role, structure and processes of government, market and civil society in relation to the environment and sustainability agenda. The emphasis is on exploring the character of contemporary environmental governance, evaluating the assumptions on which it is based, and taking a critically informed view of the strengths and limitations of different approaches. You will use Australian case studies e.g. biodiversity, urban water management, waste management, to examine the mix of policy instruments where different kinds of agencies and organisations collaborate to implement a range of interesting and innovative approaches to environmental governance. In this unit you will attend a full day field trip and a full day workshop involving policy-makers and practitioners from state and local government, and representatives from from NGOs and community organisations.
Learning Outcomes:
- critique the roles and responsibilities of government, markets and civil society in environmental policy and governance initiatives;
- develop and communicate insights into challenges that arise when implementing governance approaches on the ground.
- recognise and analyse the theoretical perspectives of the shift from government to governance in environmental management and sustainability;
- analyse and assess the strengths and weaknesses of command-and-control regulation, the use of market-based instruments, citizen empowerment and community participation, and their compatibility in policy mixes;
Assessment Tasks:
- Reflection: 35% (Hurdle: None)
- Workshop presentation and facilitation: 15% (Hurdle: None)

Overview: The MIG is an intensive unit designed to equip you with the holistic skills and mindsets required to lead and implement viable innovation solutions in industry.
