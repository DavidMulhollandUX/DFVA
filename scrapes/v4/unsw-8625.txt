## Description
The MBA (Part-time) degree, or ‘MBAX’, allows you to choose a specialisation that aligns with your individual career aspirations and personal goals. This program is unique as it can be completed entirely online. While courses are offered in a variety of delivery modes, the online format allows you to gain an MBA from a leading business school with a flexibility that fits in with your lifestyle whatever your location.

You will be able to apply the concepts and tools to your professional life immediately, while preparing for great things in the future – accelerating your current career, following a new path or building a new enterprise.

The program is designed to be undertaken part-time in combination with full-time employment.

Subject: Accounting and Financial Management (AGSM6210)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: NOTE: This course was previously identified as MNGT6210. The aim of this course is to show how the effective use of financial information can improve organisational decision-making. The course provides a broad introduction to how accounting contributes to an organisation. You will examine how accounting can help managers measure and analyse their organisation's economic performance, to improve resource allocation, and build accountability for performance through effective governance. The users of accounting information are typically classified as either external (such as investors or lenders) or internal (such as operational managers). Different users have different information needs. This course deals with the needs of these two main user groups and how senior managers can more effectively use accounting data to connect with them. The course explores accounting techniques and terminology, to equip you with sufficient conceptual and practical skills to make you confident in your ability to understand and communicate complex financial strategies. You will also examine the design and operation of accounting systems. As a result, the course integrates ideas and concepts from accounting with a range of other disciplines and reveals how the effective use of financial information provided by such systems can improve organisational decision-making. You will also examine how firms use non-financial information such as Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) to communicate their performance and strategies. It is assumed that participants have little prior knowledge of accounting and financial management.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the objectives, concepts and relationships that underpin both internal and external financial reports
- Apply analytical tools, techniques and frameworks to evaluate and critically examine the contents of both internal and external financial reports
- Communicate financial information to interested stakeholders in an ethical and professional manner
- Research the financial performance and position of reporting entities

Subject: Data Analytics and Decision-making (AGSM6234)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course is designed to provide a broad strategic perspective on issues and challenges related to incorporating data analytics and AI into business decision-making for strategic gain and meaningful change. Due to the complex and multi-faceted nature of the world of analytics, leaders and managers need some basic and abstract familiarity with the technical building blocks of analytics. This enables them to speak the language of analytics with fluency. Also, since emerging tools and objects in the world of AI and analytics are underpinned by these technical building blocks, this familiarity enables leaders and managers to separate hype from reality with confidence and make better decisions when integrating analytics/AI into their organisations. We deliver this course in three phases to provide an integrated strategic–technical–execution view of data analytics. In Phase 1, we will explore the concept of data strategy and its connection to business strategy. In Phase 2, we aim to help you understand basic analytics and statistical concepts, building technical confidence to distinguish between hype and reality in analytics and AI, while learning to speak the language of analytics fluently. In Phase 3, we will address the challenges of incorporating analytics and AI into organisations and explore their potential to create business value and drive innovative changes in business processes.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop basic data literacy and explain how data analytics can support decision-making in business contexts.
- Explain the importance of ethics and governance in order to apply relevant frameworks in data-driven organisations, and evaluate the social and ethical implications of data collection and analysis.
- Select and appropriately apply various data-analytics techniques to identify business problems, trends and patterns used to inform business decision-making.
- Develop and integrate data-driven recommendations and solutions to enhance and support business strategies and decision-making through well-reasoned analysis.
- Use visual and digital mediums to communicate clear analytical insights and develop convincing narratives from complex datasets.

Subject: Managing People and Organisations (AGSM6275)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The organisations we work in are rich and complex entities in which many interactions, relationships and processes are played out every day. The complexity of these elements often poses challenges in understanding and management. Managing People and Organisations (MPO) is focused on managers who manage people – some you manage formally, such as your subordinates, and others informally, such as your peers and bosses. Central to the course is the characterisation and discussion of everyday functions and roles in which managers take part. These include sense-making, decision-making, leading, motivating, planning, organising, mentoring, monitoring and influencing. We also address the context in which managers function and examine the effect of current issues they face. These include an increased emphasis on entrepreneurship; managing with social responsibility and the role of ethics; leading diverse, equitable and inclusive teams, and managing in a global environment. Each Unit in this course covers a topic that contributes to your undertaking these managerial roles more effectively so that you can become a better manager of yourself, others, teams and organisations. In this course, we will ask you to test your understanding of the concepts that we introduce by examining your working life. This could involve analysing your actions, the actions of others, processes that occur within your organisation and key organisational elements. You can use these activities to evaluate the concepts by asking yourself: how useful is this idea to me? how can I modify this idea in light of my own experiences? how can I apply this idea to my own organisation? MPO will introduce you to some of the key concepts, issues and debates in management, offering a glimpse into various topics that could individually constitute full courses. It is intended to serve as a foundation for your understanding of management and managing others, marking the beginning of your learning journey rather than its conclusion.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate the role of the environment within and outside of the organisation in managing and leading others, teams and the organisation.
- Appraise potential biases in decision-making to make defendable decisions based on critical evidence.
- Apply the principles of ethics and responsible management and discuss the role they play in driving sustainable outcomes for organisations.
- Explore different frameworks and models that inform the role of a manager inside an organisation.
- Apply course frameworks to critically reflect on your practical experience in prior or existing professional or managerial roles.
- Analyse and discuss how teams work in organisations to enable you to design, manage and lead effective teams.

Subject: Social Impact Investment (COMM5703)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course is managed by the Centre for Social Impact. The landscape of social finance and impact investment has undergone a transformation – driven by global economic realignment, the privatisation of community and government services, shifting patterns of wealth distribution and intergenerational wealth transfer, and the rising expectations of corporate citizenship and philanthropy. These forces have accelerated the growth of social impact investment: a field in which commercial enterprise, blended finance, and philanthropic capital are increasingly deployed together to address complex social and environmental challenges. COMM5703 Social Impact Investment develops critical understanding of the history, core concepts, and current issues shaping philanthropy, fundraising, and social impact investment – both in Australia and globally. Students will examine the full capital spectrum, from philanthropic giving to impact bonds and ESG-aligned investment vehicles, exploring how these instruments are structured, deployed, and evaluated across diverse economic regulatory, and cultural contexts. Ethical and philosophical perspectives, impact measurement, and the legal dimensions of social investment programs are also examined, equipping students to engage as informed and strategic practitioners in this field.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and apply key instruments of social impact investment
- Identify and critically evaluate the roles and interrelationships of diverse players in the social impact investment ecosystem
- Explain the trends, drivers and challenges shaping the dynamics of the social impact investment market in Australia and globally
- Discuss the drivers of the emergence of social impact investment and the role it plays within the capital spectrum

Credit Points: 6
Overview: Corporate Responsibility and Accountability is a core course for the Global Sustainability and Social Impact specialisation in the Master of Commerce, and an elective for those undertaking the Graduate Certificate in Social Impact, Graduate Diploma in Social Impact or MBA Programs. It examines how business manages its social, environmental and economic impact on society. The course first reviews the historical evolution and development of the concept of Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) and Corporate Citizenship (CC) and why it is now a key part of managing the business enterprise. Second, the course examines what acting responsibly means for corporations in terms of the market, community, environment, workplace and government. Issues covered include business and human rights, business-community partnerships, corporate philanthropy, the human resource management implications of CSR, business and the environment and businesses' relationship with government. Third, the course examines how business accounts for its behaviour and impact in society through sustainability reporting and the role of external assurance and outlines debates on the appropriate role of government policy in either regulating and/or enabling responsible corporate behaviour.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate and apply ethical and accountability issues and frameworks in business
- Describe the key drivers behind its adoption by business
- Analyse and understand a corporation's impact in the social, workplace, market, environment and political spheres;
- Apply Corporate Responsibility theories and frameworks to critically evaluate corporate responsibility performance;
- Describe and critically evaluate the concepts of Corporate Responsibility and Accountability, and future trends in the role of the corporation in society

Subject: Managing Just Transitions (COMM5710)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Managing Just Transitions introduces students to the emerging framework of Just Transitions , to examine how leaders can manage complex socio-ecological transitions in the face of systemic challenges such as climate change, economic inequality, and unsustainable development. It focuses on the governance of change across interconnected systems, where decisions in one domain can create significant implications for markets, organisations, communities, and the environment. A central question is: how can systemic transitions be governed effectively and equitably, while balancing competing interests, risks, and opportunities? This course adopts a systems-level perspective that investigates decisions of justice, fairness, and inclusion in navigating structural changes such as decarbonisation, digitalisation, artificial intelligence, and automation. Students will critically examine how businesses, investors, governments, and civil society can work together to reimagine economic futures that are both environmentally sustainable and socially inclusive . Through interdisciplinary theories and real-world case studies, students will explore Just Transitions across five key conceptual domains: System Thinking – A system value domain sits at the core of this course, recognising that just transition occur within interconnected systems shaped by power, institutions, stakeholder relationships and competing ideas of justice. Civic Wealth Creation – Examining how community-driven entrepreneurship, participation, and coalition-building generate economic, social, and communal value. Place-Based Development and Investment – Investigating financial models that acquire, absorb, and deploy capital equitably across different communities and geographies. Indigenous Knowledge and Systems – Exploring the Indigenous business sector and the role of cultural values in fostering sustainable, community-led development. Ethical Infrastructure and Intergenerational Equity – Engaging with long-term governance frameworks that consider who benefits from change, who bears the costs, and how future generations are impacted. Designed for future leaders, strategists, and change agents, this course equips students with the critical tools to design, assess, and lead transitions that align business innovation with societal and environmental resilience.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically evaluate the range of theoretical and conceptual frameworks that inform Just Transition strategies across economic, social, and environmental domains.
- Demonstrate advanced research, analytical, and communication skills relevant to professional and academic work in the field of Just Transitions.
- Explain the concept of Just Transitions, including its origins, evolution, and significance in contemporary business and policy contexts.
- Analyse emerging trends, drivers, and governance challenges shaping Just Transitions at local, national, and global levels.
- Identify and apply the core design elements, stakeholder considerations, and measurement approaches required to develop effective Just Transition initiatives.

Subject: Collaboration and Policy Making for Social Impact (COMM5713)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course examines key concepts and frameworks of cross-sector collaboration and develops your capacities to undertake effective collaboration with partners and policy makers. The course is taught using experiential processes to learn about collaboration in real time at personal, organisational and system levels. This requires students to be willing to reflect on their own approaches and attitudes within a collaboration. In completing this course, you will gain a deeper understanding of the knowledge and frameworks required to develop and sustain collaborations and make policy to achieve social impact. You will also have the opportunity to develop a range of skills and competencies that can be applied to build collaborative partnerships for policy making with government, business, community and nonprofit.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate effective communication skills and strategies, verbally and in written format, to advance collaborative partnerships to advance social change and impact.
- Apply critical thinking and problem solving skills to diagnose complex problems within collaborative partnerships
- Demonstrate effective inter/intra-sectoral collaborative practice to advance social change.
- Demonstrate a strong awareness of, and capacity to, sustain partnerships that build ethical relationships of trust and collaboration
- Demonstrate current inter-disciplinary knowledge applicable to local and global contexts
- Demonstrate social leadership to support dynamic collaborative practice required for social change.
