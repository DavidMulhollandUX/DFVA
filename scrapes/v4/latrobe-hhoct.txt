## Bachelor of Occupational Therapy (Honours) - Melbourne (Bundoora)
##### HHOCT
##### 480 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
Help people maximise their quality of life and reach their full potential with La Trobe's Bachelor of Occupational Therapy (Honours).

Discover the science behind human activity and get the know-how to conduct evidence-based occupational therapy assessments and interventions for people of all ages and backgrounds. Learn with a combination of face-to-face tutorials and workshops, online learning and hands-on experience, including 1,000 hours of professional placements.

By studying La Trobe's Bachelor of Occupational Therapy (Honours), you will:

  * gain essential knowledge in occupational science, anatomy, physiology and psychology
  * uncover the best treatment options for people with special needs and chronic conditions
  * explore communication as a foundation to your practice as a health professional
  * learn about group theory, process and development
  * understand cultural identity and responsiveness in relation to health and wellbeing, including First Nations' perspectives
  * apply your learning with individuals and families and collaborate with employers, policymakers and other health professionals
  * build skills in both qualitative and quantitative research design, then pursue an applied research project or industry-based thesis in your embedded Honours year
  * graduate eligible to apply for registration with the Occupational Therapy Board of Australia (OTBA).

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Exercise personal autonomy and collaborative decision making in the application of professional reasoning and skills in a range of health related contexts
**2.**
Independently plan and execute project or research work and interpret findings correctly.
**3.**
Evaluate knowledge and practice using appropriate technologies to support continued professional and personal development
**4.**
Critically evaluate research evidence to enable knowledge transformation for ethically informed and evidence-based occupational therapy practice
**5.**
Collaborate and communicate effectively with professional and non professional stakeholders using advanced and culturally safe, verbal, non verbal and written skills
**6.**
Integrate knowledge of the biosciences and public health with specialist discipline knowledge in the practice of occupational therapy
**7.**
Critically analyse and apply occupational, biopsychosocial and cultural understandings in order to plan, implement and evaluate occupational therapy practice in diverse and changing environment

Applicants will be required to hold a valid Police Check which is renewed annually. Students will have access to a reduced fee when applying via a La Trobe University provider. For more information on what a police check entails, refer to the Victoria Police website.
Students should allow approximately AUD$60 for a uniform, name badge and equipment. Students can purchase these items from any supplier; however La Trobe University branded uniform must be purchased from a university approved vendor. These items remain the student's personal property.
Prior to commencing a placement, students are required to meet all of the requirements of the degree in relation to immunisation. Students must visit a medical practitioner who will complete and sign the Immunisation and Health Record form.

Depending on a students immunisation history, the overall costs for vaccination may be up to AUD$1030. Students are responsible for all costs incurred. Further information will be provided post enrolment.
Applicants will be required to hold a valid Working with Children Check (WWCC). There is no cost for a volunteer WWCC. For more information and costs refer to the Working with Children Check Victoria website.
Prior to commencing a placement, students will be required to complete a National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS) worker screening check. For information please refer to the Services Victoria website. Further information will be provided post enrolment.

Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will explore the fundamentals of human anatomy. You will develop a strong foundation in anatomical terminology, including concepts such as anatomical position, planes, directional terms, and movements. Through focused study of the major body systems—particularly the musculoskeletal and nervous systems—you will learn to apply anatomical knowledge to real-world scenarios and communicate effectively using precise anatomical language.

- Describe the anatomical structure and function of major body systems using the appropriate anatomical terminology.
- Analyse the role of the musculoskeletal system in facilitating movement and assess the impacts following injury.

Subject: APPLIED HUMAN ANATOMY (ANA2001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, students will build on previously learned anatomical principles and terminology, to cover the applied regional anatomy of the upper limb, lower limb and the trunk (including the vertebral column, as well as the thorax and its role in breathing), and neuroanatomy in relation to sensation and motor control. The concept of integrated function of multiple body systems will be developed in each region. A strong focus on functional anatomy and on applying anatomy to a living body will prepare students for using this knowledge in a professional context.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify musculoskeletal structures through surface anatomy.
- Describe normal structure and function of the central nervous system including neural pathways for sensation and motor control.
- Describe normal structure and function of the musculoskeletal and nervous systems within the upper limb, lower limb, and trunk (vertebral column, and the thorax in relation to mechanisms of respiration).
- Analyse movements and exercises in terms of muscle activation and function, and joint range of motion.
- Apply observational and communication skills, using appropriate anatomical and lay terminology for use with clients or other health care professionals, to make accurate observations of anatomical structures or events and infer their relationship to function using cadaveric material, the living body or medical imaging.

Subject: HUMAN PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (HBS3001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will examine disorders which may be experienced by individuals over the life-span, emphasizing the basic pathological processes that underlie diseases and the risk factors and contributing causes of disorders. Specifically, general pathological processes will be discussed with emphasis on disturbed function, adaptation to injury, inflammation, tissue healing, immunological disorders, neoplasia, fluid imbalance, thrombosis, embolism and vascular disorders. In addition to general pathological processes, specific pathological conditions of body systems e.g. cardiovascular, respiratory, nervous, gastrointestinal and skeletal systems along with diabetes will be addressed. You will consolidate and apply their knowledge of pathophysiology by communicating a disease state in authentic format to scientific peers and non-expert audiences.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain cellular and biochemical changes that underlie the development of systemic diseases (i.e. Systemic Pathophysiology and relate these changes to the likely clinical course and outcomes of an afflicted individual.
- Apply the principles of General Pathophysiology to selected human disorders and reparative states, describing how these general pathophysiologic processes impact on possible clinical courses and outcomes in an afflicted individual.
- Demonstrate effective communication skills by communicating scientific results, information, or arguments to a range of audiences, for a range of purposes, and using a variety of modes.
