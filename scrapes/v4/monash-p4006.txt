### Overview

### AQF level
Level 8 - Graduate Certificate

### Structure
The course comprises 24 points structured into two parts: Part A. Core prescribing skills studies and Part B. Core prescribing microcredentials. You can complete Part A and Part B in any order or concurrently. 

Part A. Core prescribing skills studies

In this part of the course, you will complete two 6-credit point units of study focused on developing your prescribing skills - PGC5301 Pharmacist prescribing skills 1 and PGC5302 Pharmacist prescribing skills 2. 

You will develop skills relevant to the four step prescribing process of information gathering, clinical decision-making, communicating and documenting treatment decisions and monitoring responses to treatment. You will also develop skills to support prescribing practice within the professional scope of practice of Australian pharmacists such as person-centred care, ethical/legal practice, clinical governance, quality of use medicines, shared-decision making and evidence based practice. In order to apply these skills, you will also learn about the diagnosis and management of acute and chronic primary care conditions (e.g. asthma, gastrointestinal conditions, hypertension and diabetes) and will make prescribing decisions for simulated cases involving such conditions. 

PGC5302 Pharmacist prescribing skills 2 includes assessments to demonstrate your prescribing competency that are completed in a work-integrated learning environment under the supervision of an approved registered prescriber (i.e. designated prescriber).  

Part A of this course is accredited with conditions by the Australian Pharmacy Council.

Part B. Core prescribing microcredentials

In this part of the course, you will complete two microcredentials focused on Pharmacist Prescribing in specific clinical conditions - Pharmacist prescribing: Clinical conditions 1 and Pharmacist prescribing: Clinical conditions 2. You will learn how to diagnose and manage preventative health conditions (e.g. contraception, travel health and smoking cessation) as well as acute and chronic musculoskeletal and dermatological conditions (including wound care management). 

You may complete the microcredentials in either order, prior to or during your enrolment in this course.

Subject: Pharmacist prescribing skills 1 (PGC5301)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit will equip you with the knowledge and skills to make safe person-centred and evidence-informed prescribing decisions as a pharmacist. You will learn about and apply the principles of quality use of medicines, person-centred care, shared decision making, cultural competency, interprofessional collaborative care, evidence based practice and clinical governance to manage simulated prescribing cases. You will also apply principles of legal and ethical practice to make safe prescribing decisions within a pharmacists’ professional scope of practice. Throughout this unit, you will learn about the signs, symptoms, diagnostic features and management options relevant to acute primary care ear, nose and throat conditions, gastrointestinal conditions and chronic respiratory conditions. Using the context of these acute and chronic primary care conditions, you will apply knowledge and skills relevant to the four step prescribing process of information gathering, clinical decision-making, communicating and documenting treatment plans and monitoring treatment responses. In the context of these conditions, this unit will also teach you how to conduct comprehensive, culturally safe patient assessments to develop a working diagnosis and make prescribing decisions that promote person-centred care. To support this, you will develop basic physical examination skills (including techniques such as otoscopic examination and chest auscultation) relevant to the diagnosis and management of acute and chronic primary care conditions. Through a series of case-based learning and simulation exercises you will start developing your prescribing competency.
Learning Outcomes:
- Prepare accurate prescriptions and identify potential sources of prescribing errors, implementing strategies to minimise risks, enhance patient safety and comply with legal requirements.
- Demonstrate cultural competency by critically reflecting on personal and workplace practices, identifying cultural biases, and proposing evidence-based strategies to improve cultural safety in healthcare delivery
- Define professional scope of practice in the context of pharmacist prescribing and develop professional development (CPD) plans to ensure safe, competent, and sustained prescribing practice.
- Engage patients in shared decision-making, respecting their needs, preferences, and cultural considerations to foster active participation in their treatment plans
- Perform and accurately document key diagnostic and physical examination procedures, including but not limited to otoscopic examination, chest examination, abdominal examination, and blood pressure measurement - demonstrating correct technique, and interpretation of findings to inform patient management.
- Describe the signs, symptoms, diagnostic features and management options relevant to acute primary care ear, nose and throat conditions, gastrointestinal conditions and chronic respiratory conditions
- Collaborate with healthcare professionals to ensure coordinated, informed, and effective prescribing practices within personal scope of practice. This includes identifying when referral to another healthcare professional is required
- Demonstrate the principles of quality use of medicines, clinical reasoning, person-centred care and evidence-based medicine within the context of the prescribing process
- In the context of common primary care acute and chronic conditions, apply the steps of the prescribing process to create person-centred and evidence informed treatment decisions: i) Conduct comprehensive, culturally safe patient assessments, including basic physical examination techniques to develop a working diagnosis (where needed) and make prescribing decisions that promote person-centred care. ii) Evaluate and apply evidence-based management options tailored to person-specific factors, ensuring alignment with clinical guidelines and therapeutic goals. iii) Formulate, document, and communicate safe, legal, effective and ethical treatment plans that comply with legislative requirements and codes and guidelines, and support person-centred care. iv) Monitor and evaluate treatment outcomes, including the identification and management of adverse outcomes

The unit requires on average 10-12 hours of learning activities per week, including teacher directed learning, independent study and reading time, participation in online workshops, research and preparation for assignments. Subject:  Pharmacist prescribing skills 2 (PGC5302)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit will consolidate your ability to make safe person-centred and evidence-informed prescribing decisions as a pharmacist. Throughout this unit, you will learn about the signs, symptoms, diagnostic features and management options for conditions related to cardiac risk reduction such as dyslipidemia, hypertension and diabetes mellitus. Using the context of these primary care conditions, you will apply knowledge and skills relevant to the four step prescribing process of information gathering, clinical decision-making, communicating and documenting treatment plans and monitoring treatment responses. You will further expand your understanding of principles relevant to safe prescribing practice such as quality use of medicines, person-centred care, shared decision-making, clinical governance and interprofessional collaboration. This includes developing an in-depth understanding of scope of practice and related principles to ensure that you make safe, legal and ethical prescribing decisions as a pharmacist prescriber. In this unit, you are required to nominate a “designated prescriber”, who is an Ahpra registered health professional currently prescribing, with at least 12 months experience as a prescriber in an area of practice relevant to your scope of practice. Your designated prescriber must formally agree to provide supervision and mentorship, and assess your prescribing competency in a work-integrated learning environment.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the signs, symptoms, diagnostic features and management options relevant to conditions that relate to cardiac risk such dyslipidaemia, hypertension and diabetes mellitus
- Engage people in shared decision-making, respecting their needs, preferences, and cultural considerations to foster active participation in their treatment plans
- Describe the legal and ethical issues associated with prescribing practice and develop strategies to prescribe legally and ethically
- In the context of common primary care acute and chronic conditions, apply the steps of the prescribing process to create person-centred and evidence informed treatment decisions: i) Conduct comprehensive, culturally safe patient assessments, including basic physical examination techniques to develop a working diagnosis (where needed) and make prescribing decisions that promote person-centred care. ii) Evaluate and apply evidence-based management options tailored to person-specific factors, ensuring alignment with clinical guidelines and therapeutic goals. iii) Formulate, document, and communicate safe, legal, effective and ethical treatment plans that comply with legislative requirements and codes and guidelines, and support person-centred care. iv) Monitor and evaluate treatment outcomes, including the identification and management of adverse outcomes
- Prepare accurate prescriptions and identify potential sources of prescribing errors, implementing strategies to minimise risks, enhance patient safety and comply with legal requirements
- Collaborate with healthcare professionals to ensure coordinated, informed, and effective prescribing practices within personal scope of practice. This includes identifying when referral to another healthcare professional is required
- Define professional scope of practice in the context of pharmacist prescribing and develop professional development (CPD) plans to ensure safe, competent, and sustained prescribing practice

The unit requires on average 10-12 hours of learning activities per week, including teacher directed learning, independent study and reading time,participation in online workshops, research and preparation for assignments. There are three milestones within the 2 unit. By each milestone, you are required to have completed a series of workplace based assessments under the supervision of your designated prescriber.
