## Description
This program is only available in part-time mode.

The Mental Health Practice program is designed to equip students with essential skills to further their career and understanding in the field of mental health. The program provides students with the opportunity for a comprehensive training in mental health by coursework. It covers the theoretical and practical aspects of mainstream mental health services, including acute inpatient and community mental health assessment and treatment. All courses are taught by mental health professionals with significant experience in the assessment and treatment of mental health disorders.

It is designed to provide training, skills and expertise required in the mental health workplace both in the private and non-private arena. This program will assist students in meeting the mental health needs of patients requiring assessment and ongoing treatment across all mental health presentations.

The program is undertaken on a part-time basis by flexible online delivery and is suitable for working health professionals. The Program is designed for mental health professionals including medical practitioners, nurses, psychologists, social workers and occupational therapists. It is also open to members of the other interested disciplines who align with the program entry requirements.

5. Evaluate your practice using professional self-management and excellence in leadership.

## Career Opportunities & Employment Areas
This program will provide students with the skills to work at an advanced level in mental health. It does not lead to registration with AHPRA.

Students must complete 24 Units of Credit (UOC) structured across the following academic components:

## Progression & Academic Rules
The courses are offered once per year, therefore completion of the program may not be possible within 12 months if one or more courses are not completed successfully.

Subject: Mental Health Assessment and Treatment (PSCY9020)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course will review key principals underpinning mental health assessment and treatment decisions, across all the mental illness category groups and treatment settings. Key assessment approaches and skills will be explored, along with a bio-psycho-social model for treatment and management decisions. This course will review the major psychiatric disorders, with reference to the DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 diagnostic classification systems. Disorder categories will include psychotic disorders, mood disorders, anxiety disorders and eating disorders, as well as an overview of personality and substance use disorders. The course will review common psychopharmacological treatment approaches (including antidepressant and antipsychotic medications) including considerations of the benefits and side-effects of each medication category. An overview of the benefits and side-effects of each medication category will be considered. This course will also provide you with a foundational understanding of key psychological modalities used in treatment, with emphasis on cognitive behavioural therapy, brief solution focused therapy and an overview of other psychological therapies such as acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT) and dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT) and others. On completion of this course, you will be able to complete a full, comprehensive mental health assessment, provide an initial diagnostic formulation and differential diagnosis, and develop a meaningful mental health treatment plan, with an understanding of the most appropriate treatment modality for each of the major mental health disorders.
Learning Outcomes:
- Compare appropriate psychopharmacotherapy treatment(s) utilised in the treatment of mental health conditions and how these apply across differing patient groups.
- Explain the key principals underlying mental health assessment to inform treatment decisions.
- Evaluate the efficacy of how major mental illnesses are treated across inpatient and outpatient services nationally and internationally.
- Formulate comprehensive psychiatric assessments, using clinical case studies and generate concise diagnostic impressions, utilising psychiatric diagnostic nosology.
- Appraise the bio-psychosocial model and its application in the treatment of mental illnesses, including strengths and limitations of this model.
- Develop well-considered comprehensive individualised treatment plans, using diagnostic nosology.
- Critically evaluate appropriate psychological treatment(s) utilised across a variety of mental health conditions.

Subject: Fundamental Psychological Therapy Skills (PSCY9021)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course will introduce psychological treatment modalities and provide a framework to select the appropriate treatment for a particular mental health disorder. The course will include various psychological therapy theories including, but not limited to, cognitive behaviour therapy (CBT), dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT), acceptance and commitment therapy (ACT), mentalisation based therapy (MBT) and transference focussed therapy (TFT) fundamentals. By the end of this course, you will understand the theoretical frameworks of these therapeutic interventions and be able to implement aspects of each in your clinical practice, providing patients with high quality psychological treatment. This course provides an opportunity for you to practice the skills you have learned throughout the course with the course convenor(s) who have expertise in the delivery of psychological therapies.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop a treatment modality, using a specific psychological therapy, chosen specifically to meet the patient's need, in both a group and individual therapy session.
- Justify an effective treatment plan using psychological therapy treatment modalities.
- Evaluate the appropriate psychological therapy approaches to treat mental health disorders, explaining their benefits and comparing how these are applied across a range of mental health disorders and differing patient needs.
- Describe psychological therapy approaches and apply the underpinning key principals and theories with clients.

Subject: Philosophies of Mental Health Care, Services and Systems (PSCY9022)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course will explore the key philosophies underpinning mental health care, services and systems within an Australian and International context. Students will be provided with a comprehensive understanding of trauma-informed care, the bio-psychosocial model and working within a multidisciplinary team, focussing on delivering least restrictive care under a recovery model. Students will obtain an understanding of the way in which mental health services and systems are constructed and the reasons for service delivery design. Students will navigate services and systems, including beyond the formal mental health services, to support patients to holistically meet the needs of this patient group.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically assess the current mental health systems, the need for more efficacious service delivery and future opportunities and explain the challenges of implementing differing approaches into current practice.
- Compare the roles and accountabilities of the multidisciplinary team employed in the provision of mental health care.
- Explain the key philosophies underpinning mental health care both nationally and internationally.
- Demonstrate advanced understanding in the delivery of trauma-informed care to a psychiatric population.
- Develop a plan of how to navigate the current mental health services and systems, including the benefits and limitations of current service offerings.
- Demonstrate advanced knowledge in how mental health systems and services are constructed and how philosophies of mental health care underpin these.

Subject: Assessing Risk and Managing Challenging Behaviour (PSCY9023)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course will provide students with a comprehensive understanding of the assessment and formulation of risk for violence and self-harm, its application to management approaches, and consideration of the strengths and limitations of different approaches. Students will gain practical experience in developing and utilising clinical skills in risk assessment and management, and in strategies for preventing and de-escalating risks. Acute emergency management of difficult behaviour will also be addressed.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the difference between risk prediction and risk management, including the benefits and limitations of these approaches to risk assessment.
- Formulate an appropriate risk assessment tool based on a mock patient, demonstrating an advanced knowledge of the tool, its benefits and limitations and explain the appropriateness of this chosen tool to a range of scenarios.
- Develop a tool for de-escalating risk and managing difficult behaviours.
- Apply several risk assessments, critically evaluate their individual benefits and limitations for use with a psychiatric population. Compare the benefits and limitations of utilising these tools in specific risk assessments.
- Demonstrate advanced knowledge of risk assessments and risk mitigation plans and how mental health services respond to risk.
