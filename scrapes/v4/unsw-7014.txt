## Description
Women's Health Medicine is a rapidly expanding and diverse field of practice. It encompasses the timely provision of sexual and reproductive care, pre-pregnancy and pregnancy care as well as the management of female specific conditions and malignancies. Increasing numbers of medical practitioners are developing special interests in this area - in particular General Practitioners, family planning practitioners and generalist gynaecologists. Significant career opportunities exist for those with demonstrable skills and knowledge in this field. In addition there are similar specialisations emerging in nursing and counselling, and amongst biological scientists.

The Graduate Certificate in Women's Health Medicine is an accessible entry point for professional development in women’s health medicine with a defined pathway to a Master of Women’s Health Medicine degree. The program provides an opportunity for healthcare professionals to develop specialised knowledge and expertise in the field. Individual courses within the Program have been developed to provide a practice-based and authentic approach to this field of study.

This program is offered entirely online, enabling health professionals to integrate their learning into their daily professional lives.

## Admission Requirements
**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
Entry into this program requires an undergraduate degree in medicine, nursing or other health or medical science related discipline* AND one of the following:

- Honours or postgraduate qualification in medicine, nursing or other health or medical science-related disciplines*; OR

- Commencement or completion of a recognised postgraduate medical specialist training program; OR

- Minimum 1 year full-time equivalent postgraduate professional experience in medicine, nursing or other health or medical science-related fields*.

* health or medical science-related disciplines include, but are not restricted to, laboratory scientists, and allied health professionals.

Subject: Reproductive Anatomy and Physiology (SWCH9000)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This postgraduate course aims to provide you with an in-depth understanding of the anatomy and physiology of the reproductive systems of both males and females, essential for clinical practice in reproductive medicine and women’s health. In this course you will develop comprehensive knowledge of the reproductive system development, function, and dysfunction across the human lifespan, with emphasis on clinical applications and evidence-based practice. This is a core course of the Masters of Reproductive Medicine and Masters in Women’s Health Medicine. Students are recommended to enrol in this course early in their program.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically assess the physical, emotional and hormonal changes occurring during puberty, pregnancy, and menopause, and evaluate their clinical implications for reproductive health.
- Analyse the mechanisms of sexual determination, differentiation, and the developmental stages required to achieve reproductive competence.
- Critically evaluate the physiological processes which underpin fertility and infertility in context of clinical relevance
- Integrate the anatomical, physiological and psychosocial processes that influence sexual function and dysfunction in males and females.
- Explain the anatomy and physiology of the male and female reproductive systems and identify the hormonal mechanisms that regulate their functions

Subject: Contraception (SWCH9002)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course examines the clinical, social, epidemiological and ethical aspects of contraception and family planning. Students critically consider the various methods of contraception, sterilisation and pregnancy termination. Through case-based and evidence-informed learning, the course develops student’s capacity to provide nuanced, person-centred contraceptive counselling across diverse clinical, social, and demographic contexts.
Learning Outcomes:
- Appraise the mechanisms of action of contemporary and emerging contraceptive methods, and apply to individual patient needs.
- Critically analyse global contraceptive needs and evaluate how social, cultural, economic and policy factors shape equitable access to contraceptives across diverse populations.
- Evaluate and compare the various methods of contraception in terms of efficacy, benefits, risks and side-effects to support clinical decision-making.
- Develop and justify evidence-based contraceptive advice tailored to a range of clinical scenarios and individual patient/client’s needs.
- Integrate medical, psychosocial, cultural, and ethical considerations to determine the suitability of contraceptive options for individuals across a range of clinical needs, life stages, and reproductive goals
- Describe and justify the recommended management of complications or side effects associated with various methods of contraception.

Subject: Reproductive, Perinatal Epidemiology and Biostatistics (SWCH9011)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course aims to provide an understanding of statistics methodology and clinical appraisal for students undertaking study in a reproductive and perinatal-health related program. This course is a core subject for the Programs 9014 Masters of Women's Health Medicine and 9065 Masters of Reproductive Medicine. This course aims to introduce students to research methodology and statistical analysis. Using examples drawn specifically from the field of reproductive and perinatal medicine this course will also develop in students the ability to critically analyse/appraise literature pertinent to that area. It is likely to be most relevant to those working primarily in a reproductive and perinatal health setting-in particular gynaecologists in training, sexual health physicians, primary care physicians and family planning practitioners as well as nurses, counsellors and scientists with a special interest in this field.
Learning Outcomes:
- Perform statistical data analyses using SPSS software including calculating measures of disease occurrence and association, statistical significance tests in hypothesis testing, correlation and simple linear regression and proportion differences.
- Distinguish between common study designs that can be used to address descriptive and analytic epidemiology research questions about health status, determinants of health and effectiveness of health services with particular reference to reproductive and perinatal medicine.
- Critically appraise the quality of published studies in reproductive and perinatal health, demonstrating the ability to assess study design and methods of data analysis, as well as interpret study methods, results and conclusions.
- Describe the concept ‘study validity’ and explain how factors could affect study validity, including selection bias, measurement error and confounding.
- Describe key statistical concepts used in common epidemiological studies (including hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, study power and significance levels).
- Describe key epidemiological issues related to reproductive and perinatal health (including infant and maternal mortality rates, perinatal terms and definitions)

Subject: Antenatal Care (SWCH9014)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this course, you will examine the issues involved in delivering a high clinical standard of pregnancy care. This course emphasises critical evidence-based analysis of current systems of care, appropriate pre-pregnancy and antenatal investigations, and management of problems during pregnancy in order to optimise health outcomes for both the pregnant woman and her fetus/child. You will also examine the impact of pregnancy complications on ongoing maternal and child health. It is likely to be most relevant to you if you work in a reproductive health setting, such as obstetricians/gynaecologists in postgraduate training, primary care physicians, midwives, and maternal/child health nurses, as well as allied health professionals with a special interest in this field. The Course will cover the following topics: Pregnancy preparation/Pre-pregnancy care Early pregnancy care Antenatal screening The pregnant woman in society Late pregnancy care Complicated pregnancy: maternal Complicated pregnancy: fetal Pregnancy as a stress test for life/Developmental Origins of Health and Disease
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically appraise and demonstrate understanding of current systems and components of pregnancy care.
- Outline and critique current evidence-based best practice, with Australian focus, in pregnancy clinical care.
- Explain the physiology of the different phases of pregnancy, and demonstrate understanding of how this impacts the development and management of common maternal and fetal complications of pregnancy.
- Describe the lifelong impact of normal and complicated pregnancy and motherhood on a woman's physical, mental, and social health and wellbeing.
- Outline the assessment of maternal and fetal risk in pregnancy, including risks arising from social determinants of health and inequitable access to care, and describe the indications and demonstrate understanding of appropriate referral pathways to appropriate medical, midwifery and/or allied health specialist care and support.

Subject: Cancer in Women (SWCH9016)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this course, you will examine the epidemiology, clinical presentation, investigation and management of cancers specifically affecting women. A major emphasis of this course is examining the potential impact of a cancer diagnosis at varying stages of a woman’s life, including impact on reproductive health, sexual health and maintaining independence in old age. It is likely to be most relevant to you if you are working primarily in women’s health across the lifespan, either in a reproductive health setting and/or with older patients . In particular, gynaecologists and primary care physicians, as well as nurses, counsellors and scientists with a special interest in this field. The following topics will be covered during this course: Hereditary Aspects of Gynaecological Cancer Uterine Cancer Cervical Cancer Vulvar/Vaginal Cancer Ovarian/Fallopian Tube Cancer Breast Cancer
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse the epidemiology of various common women’s cancers in Australia and globally and demonstrate understanding of their clinical presentations.
- Demonstrate evidence-based understanding of the main treatment options for the studied women’s cancers, and how to apply these to specific patient circumstances.
- Discuss and apply knowledge regarding how to investigate women presenting with symptoms suspicious of gynaecological/breast cancer to confirm a diagnosis.
- Evaluate how a cancer diagnosis may impact a women’s psychological and physical wellbeing, including sexual health and reproductive concerns, and how these impacts should be integrated into patient management.

Subject: Perinatal Psychology (SWCH9032)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this course, you will examine the perinatal period from the perspective of psychological adaptation of both parents and infants to the new family structure. You will adopt a clinically oriented and evidence-informed approach to perinatal mental health and wellbeing. This course is likely to be most relevant to you if you are working primarily in a pregnancy, parenthood and reproductive health setting- such as obstetricians/gynaecologists, primary care physicians, midwives, nurses, and maternal/child health nurses, as well as family planning practitioners, counsellors and allied health care practitioners with a special interest in women, child and family mental health. The following topics will be covered by this course: Pregnancy and becoming a parent Optimising the birth experience and pregnancy related trauma The infant, attachment theory and developmental psychology Perinatal Substance use, domestic violence and complex post-traumatic stress disorder Mental health conditions in pregnancy and postpartum: adaptation/adjustment disorder, unresolved grief, anxiety disorders, mood disorders, eating disorders and psychotic disorders.
Learning Outcomes:
- Discuss parenthood as a developmental process, and explain the major biological and psychological adaptive changes
- Critically evaluate the evidence base pertaining to parent-infant bonding and attachment, including the association between perinatal mental health and infant physical and emotional health
- Understand the impact of current and past trauma related disorders during the perinatal period, including trauma arising directly from pregnancy and birth events, and critically evaluate the immediate and longer-term psychological support and interventions
- Understand the major psychological risk factors and protective factors in the perinatal period, including for perinatal mental health conditions; and describe the impact of perinatal stressors, critically evaluating common psychosocial clinical scenarios.
- Explain psychological and developmental processes and changes that occur during pregnancy and early parenthood, and how these might impact on mothers/birthing parents, fathers/non-birthing parents, and families
