## Description
Digital engineering is an integrated approach that leverages authoritative sources of systems’ data and models across disciplines to support lifecycle activities from concept through disposal. Since the inception of Industry 4.0, the relevance and necessity of digital systems have skyrocketed, creating a high demand for engineers who can blend their engineering expertise with computer science skills. In Australia, both Federal and State governments are developing standards and mandating the use of digital engineering strategies and systems, further increasing the need for skilled professionals to facilitate this transition.

The Master in Digital Engineering program is designed to equip professionals with a robust foundation to capture, interpret, and integrate data within a digital work environment. Graduates with a degree in Digital Engineering will be in high demand across various industries, as digital engineering is a fundamental enabler applicable to every sector, both civil and military.

The Program provides foundational understanding of key areas such as mission engineering, data engineering, model-based systems engineering, AI-driven decision-making, and cybersecurity, making it an ideal entry point for professionals looking to upskill or transition into the field. The program also preparing graduates for leadership roles in industries such as defence, infrastructure, health, and advanced manufacturing.

This program emphasises real-world applications, industry collaboration, and emerging technologies, ensuring graduates are well-positioned to drive digital transformation in engineering practice. UNSW Graduates with a degree in Digital Engineering will be in high demand across various industries, as digital engineering is a fundamental enabler applicable to both public and private technology sectors.

## Career Opportunities & Employment Areas
The Graduate Certificate and Master’s in Digital Engineering program is designed for professionals seeking to enhance their expertise in mission engineering, digital twin technology, cybersecurity, and AI-driven decision-making. These programs are ideal for:

- Engineering Professionals looking to transition from traditional engineering roles into mission engineering, model-based systems engineering (MBSE), and digital twin technology.
- Project Managers, Technical Leaders, Acquisition Professionals and Operators who need advanced skills in mission engineering, cybersecurity, AI-driven decision-making to support capability development and acquisition.
- Infrastructure and Asset Managers interested in optimizing the lifecycle management of critical infrastructure through digital workflows.
- Advanced Manufacturing Practitioners aiming to integrate smart technologies, automation, and digital twins into manufacturing and production processes.
- Academics and Researchers working in emerging engineering technologies, systems integration, and AI-enhanced engineering, who require advanced knowledge of digital engineering principles.

## Admission Requirements
**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
Admission to this program requires one of the following:

- Have completed the Graduate Certificate of Digital Engineering (or equivalent) with a minimum WAM of 65, or

- Have completed a Bachelor degree or higher-level education in a cognate discipline* with a minimum WAM of 65, or

- Have completed a Bachelor degree or higher-level education in any discipline with a minimum WAM of 65 and a minimum of 2 years of relevant work experience**

*cognate discipline includes

1)   Communications Engineering

2)   Electrical and telecommunication Engineering

3)   Electrical Engineering

4)   Automation and Control

5)   Electronic Information

6)   Optoelectronics/Electronics Engineering

7)   Photonic Engineering

8)   Power Electronics

9)   Power Engineering

10) Telecommunications

11) Computer science

12) Information systems

13) Software engineering

** relevant experience includes being responsible for tasks within digital engineering-related roles that may include technical or governance roles. It can include experience in managing a small team, being a team leader, and managing a relevant project.

**Limitations on Recognition of Prior Learning**:
Recognition of Prior Learning of 24 UOC can be granted for a cognate Bachelor Pass AQF 7 and up to 48 UOC can be granted for cognate AQF 8 (Cognate Bachelor Honours or GCert/GDip).

Subject: Life Cycle Engineering (GSOE9340)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course is designed to provide postgraduate students with high level knowledge of Life Cycle Engineering principles and practices. The course will follow a typical product life cycle and the associated tools and techniques available. It starts with defining the concept of life cycle engineering, history and potential benefits (ecological and environmental). It then introduces the drivers behind this concept such as legislation and standards, environmental requirements. Next, the concepts of life cycle thinking, Cradle-to-Grave, Cradle-to-Cradle, Energy and Eco-efficiency are introduced. In the following section, generic environmental impact assessment tools and the concept of sustainable product development and the associated eco-design tools are introduced. The course continues with concepts related to usage and the end-of-life stages of product life cycle. These topics include Product collection, Reverse logistics and End-of-Life decision making, Tools and technologies, Disassemble sequence planning, Disassembly technologies, Reuse and remanufacturing principles, product monitoring and testing, materials recycling techniques. The course finally finishes with economics and future trends such as Economic models, Life Cycle Costing (LCC) and Product Service Systems (PSS).
Learning Outcomes:
- Have gained knowledge in the inter-disciplinary field of Life Cycle Engineering
- Develop an appreciation of the future trends in the area of triple bottom line of sustainability (economic, ecological and social)
- Develop in-depth understanding of various tools and techniques associated with engineering and managing the whole life cycle of a product

Subject: Data Security and Privacy (ZZCA9202)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Security and privacy are related but distinct concepts; they are also some of the defining terms of the 21st century. This course equips student for handling data in secure ways, understanding the nature and principles of privacy, and how to securely identify manage and respond to privacy risks across large datasets and apply current best practice in a changing global environment.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply data security and privacy principles in security engineering design of software, systems, and policies.
- Apply national and international legislation, ethical standards, and professional best practice to organisational practice and personal professional practice.
- Demonstrate a critical understanding the nature and principles of privacy, and of the potential costs, benefits, and risks under societal, commercial, individual and governmental perspectives.
- Explain historic data security and privacy failures and their causes and consequences.
- Analyse the privacy implications of systems and processes.
