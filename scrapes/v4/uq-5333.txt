## Career Opportunities & Employment Outcomes
The Master of Counselling provides career opportunities in human service settings such as health, welfare, community services, human resources and personnel management and private practice. It is well suited to people who work with individuals, families, groups and communities in many different fields and circumstances, such as loss and grief issues, mental health and well-being, domestic violence, sexual abuse, and drug and alcohol issues, relationship issues and others.

This program will provide students with in depth knowledge and practical skills for a successful career in professional counselling. The program has a strong focus on integrative approaches to counselling and applied psychotherapy that seek to effectively and respectfully enhance outcomes for people struggling with a range of challenges. Subject: Interpersonal Skills in Counselling 1 (COUN7001)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course will introduce students to the basic foundational knowledge and skills of counselling. Students will be introduced to the various foundational counselling skills through an understanding of the aspects of the counselling interactions that lead to change, direct teaching and demonstrations as well as being given supervised practice in the performance of these skills and their integration into a respectful approach to counselling. A link between skills and theoretical underpinnings is an important part of the course.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the underlying biological, social and intra-personal aspects that influence the use of interpersonal skills in counselling.
- Explore the various forms of interpersonal communication that can exist within a counselling relationship.
- Develop foundational counselling skills that will enhance the effective of the counselling relationship and enable therapeutic action.
- Examine the significance of the therapeutic relationship and its impact on clinical outcomes as evidenced in the literature.
- Demonstrate the establishment of an effective therapeutic counselling encounter.
- Critically reflect on the influence of culture and diversity on the counselling process.
- Appraise the suitability of difference therapy modes of delivery for different contexts.
Assessment Tasks:
- Journal and Reflections: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Submission of Video and Commentary: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
- Competency Interview: 25% Pass/Fail (hurdle)% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week

Subject: Theories of Counselling (COUN7002)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course will familiarise students with the major theories that underpin the counselling profession and that inform the counselling relationship. Students will be expected to demonstrate a thorough knowledge of the history and theoretical development of counselling, understanding the principles and practices behind the major models of practice. This course is taught in weekly workshop format.
Learning Outcomes:
- Discuss the role of theory in counselling practice
- Differentiate between modern, postmodern, and integrative theories
- Research the evidence supporting particular theoretical approaches
- Identify the significance each theory places on: affect; cognition; behaviour; relationships
- Outline the historical development of talk therapy from Psychoanalysis to Integrative approaches
- Apply the principles of each theory to your own life and experience
- Use theory as a framework and blueprint for your practice
- Critically analyse the strengths and limitations of each theory studied
- To synthesize and integrate theories into their own practice
- Critically review their own practice in the light of theory
Assessment Tasks:
- Online Multiple Choice Quiz Online: 30% Completion of four online learning modules through associated quizzes% (Hurdle: False)
- Position Paper: The Science & Art of Two Theories: 50% 3000 words% (Hurdle: False)
- Oral Exam: Case Conceptualisation Competency Hurdle Identity Verified: 20% Hurdle requirement% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Workshop 4 Hours/ Week

Subject: Counselling and Mental Health (COUN7005)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course will focus on various paradigms for conceptualising mental wellbeing and psychopathology. A biopsychosocial model for assessing the mental health and wellbeing of clients will be emphasised. Students will learn about the major classificatory systems for mental disorders as well as develop an understanding of the major disorders.
Learning Outcomes:
- Define and describe the key concepts, determinants, and indicators of mental health and wellbeing.
- Describe key features of major mental health disorders with reference to current diagnostic classification systems (e.g., DSM-5).
- Analyse presenting information to consider possible diagnoses and differential diagnoses using current classification systems (e.g., DSM-5).
- Integrate and apply a biopsychosocial framework to the assessment and formulation of individuals experiencing mental illness and their carers.
- Conduct a comprehensive assessment including history taking and mental status examination and synthesise findings into a written case report.
- Demonstrate the ability to select and describe culturally safe strategies for counselling practice with diverse populations.
- Synthesise assessment data and research evidence to develop coherent, client-centred treatment plans.
Assessment Tasks:
- Case Study Report: 35%% (Hurdle: False)
- In-Lecture Online Quiz Identity Verified Online: 30% 30 MCQ items% (Hurdle: False)
- Video Assessment with Reflection: 20%% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Understanding and Caring for Those Affected by Loss (COUN7014)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The course explores in a thematic and theoretically integrated manner common issues of loss and grief that are inherent in many aspects of human functioning and adverse life events. As such it provides an example of an integrative approach to counselling. The influence of loss on reactions to a situation as well as approaches to intervention are considered. Throughout the course, students are encouraged to apply the knowledge gained to their personal experiences and work practices. The course aims to enhance the ability of practitioners to empower those dealing with loss through a) a knowledge of the literature around situations of loss including problems in grieving; b) the encouragement of individual, familial, community and system strengths, and c) the prevention of the escalation of problems associated with failure to address issues of loss and grief incumbent in a particular situation.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the social, intrapersonal, and biological factors that influence reactions to loss and grief responses across the life cycle.
- Describe major definitions and theoretical models related to loss and grief.
- Differentiate complicated grieving patterns and comorbidity issues from uncomplicated grieving patterns.
- Develop counselling skills for working with loss and grief that will enhance the counselling relationship and enable therapeutic action.
- Recognise your own personal experiences of loss and grief that may affect your practice.
- Critically reflect on the influence of culture and diversity in shaping responses to loss and consider how this understanding informs professional practice
Assessment Tasks:
- Reflections on conversations about loss: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Fact sheet on an area of loss: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Case Formulation report: Applying theory and evidence to understand grief reactions: 35%% (Hurdle: False)
- Reflections of skills practice: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Workshop 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: Trauma Therapy: Theory and Practice (COUN7025)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course introduces students to the nature of crisis, trauma, and violence and approaches to mental health support. The theoretical foundations of trauma therapy are examined and applications of trauma theory is applied to a range of case studies. The nature and impact of domestic and family violence is also explored as one aspect of crisis and trauma. Students will be expected to understand the physical, psychological, and sociological impact of crisis and trauma on the individual and families. The course aims to develop in students a keen appreciation for ways of working with those who have been traumatised by life events.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the distinction between crisis interventions and psychotherapy and the different forms of support they respectively provide.
- Examine the difference between different stress and trauma conditions and disorders.
- Explain key aspects of established trauma theory and how traumatic events affect the individual.
- Recognise different forms of domestic and family violence and their impact on individuals and families.
- Apply trauma informed approaches to psychotherapy to case studies.
- Develop a keen appreciation for a range of trauma theories and interventions.
Assessment Tasks:
- Assignment 1 Part A: Group Presentation Team or group-based In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Assignment 1 Part B: Critical Reflection: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Assignment 2: Essay - Understanding Trauma Theory and Practice: 60%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week

Subject: Counselling Approaches and Mental Health Practice (COUN7026)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course introduces students to working in mental health as a psychotherapist from the perspective of interpersonal psychodynamic theory and one additional evidenced-informed psychotherapy such as CBT utilising focused psychological strategies. The course is designed to deepen students¿ understanding of mental health conditions and how to work effectively as a psychotherapist. Students will be expected to be working with clients during the course and will be provided with supervision. Students will apply the basics of assessment and engage in demonstrated application of the specific theories studied.
Learning Outcomes:
- Have an increased appreciation for the complexity of the human condition and of the neurological and psychological processes involved in human functioning.
- Understand the nature trauma conditions and the potential impact that traumatic life events have on the self.
- Appreciate the effectiveness of psychotherapy for a wide range of mental health conditions.
- Conceptualise and develop comprehensive case formulations based on at least two evidenced-based theories of psychotherapy.
- Apply a selected psychotherapy approach to the process of psychotherapy.
- Apply a range of focused psychological strategies to case presentations.
- Demonstrate critical and reflective reasoning to examine approaches to assessment, treatment planning and psychotherapy.

Subject: Group Process for Recovery and Behaviour Change (COUN7027)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course aims to introduce students to contemporary approaches to working with groups as a counsellor. The course is designed to familiarise students with the common interpersonal dynamics that occur within groups as well as the skills, structures and processes that facilitators use to construct safe and effective groups. Students will be introduced to the varieties of group work that address a range of populations and issues, as well as the theoretical foundations that inform these approaches. Students will be given examples of real-world group practice that currently occurs within counselling services. At the end of the subject, students will have the frameworks to consider how a group-based may be designed to address clinical issues that they may face in practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Compare the strengths and limitations of different models of group work to address clinical and therapeutic issues
- Understand common interpersonal dynamics that occur within groups
- Critically reflect on one’s own experiences of group processes within educational groups
- Recognise common processes, structures and skills that group facilitators use to manage group process
- Design and justify a group intervention for a chosen issue or population
Assessment Tasks:
- Focus Group Presentation and Facilitation Identity Verified: 50% 30 minutes + 1000 word reflection% (Hurdle: False)
- Group Work Proposal and Plan: 50% 2000 words + Resources% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Research Methods (HLTH7317)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course focuses on designing, developing, and articulating a well-structured research proposal. The course provides students with the necessary skills to design a research plan able to address a clear and concise research question. The course requires students to create a comprehensive research plan which includes appropriate methodology, the identification of suitable sampling techniques, data collection, analysis and the dissemination of research findings.

- Design, develop, and articulate a well-structured research proposal which incorporates an answerable research question, a research plan, the identification of appropriate sampling techniques, and an outline of data collection and analysis.
- Demonstrate advanced communication skills to articulate complex ideas clearly, structure arguments logically, and use appropriate academic conventions and referencing styles to effectively present and disseminate research findings.

Subject: Family Therapy Traditions: Basic Concepts and Skills (SWSP7267)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides a thorough overview of the basic theories which gave rise to the central schools of contemporary family therapy. Students can expect to gain knowledge of fundamental concepts and their practice derivatives with some opportunities to begin to recognise and undertake their implementation.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the significance of the shift from seeing individuals to seeing families, and to articulate key concepts in understanding problems from a systemic and contextual framework.
- Describe each of the main schools upon which family therapy is based in terms of theoretical concepts, interventions and key skills.
- Apply the theoretical positions of various schools of family therapy to family problems and conceptualise cases in a manner consistent with the main schools.
- Understand an integrative framework for understanding families and to guide family therapy practice.
- Be aware of and sensitive to the ethical issues in family therapy, including the influence of the therapist's own experience of family on his or her practice.
Assessment Tasks:
- Core Concepts of Family Therapy: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- The Experience of Family: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Structured Case Analysis: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Workshop 6 Hours/ Week 6W
