### Structure
Double degree courses include all the features of the component degree courses, except that electives may be reduced or redirected.

Master of Management

The Master of Management is structured in three parts: Part A. Core studies, Part B. Discipline studies and Part C. Elective studies. In this double degree course you will complete: 

Part A. Core studies

These units will provide you with a comprehensive understanding of core management principles, contemporary practice and leadership and their impact on multi-discipline decision-making in organisations. These units will develop your capacity as an innovative future-ready professional who is able to apply specialist knowledge to complex management issues.

Part B. Discipline studies

These units develop your capacity as a critical and creative professional who can apply advanced management knowledge to analyse complex organisational issues. You will build the skills to evaluate theories, apply evidence-based practices, and design innovative solutions that enhance leadership, strategy, and sustainable performance in diverse organisational contexts. Selected units within this part are accredited by the Australian HR Institute (AHRI).

Master of Public Policy and Management

The Master of Public Policy and Management is structured into three parts: Part A: Core studies, Part B: Applied studies and Part C: Specified elective studies. In this double degree course you will complete:

Part A. Core studies

These studies will introduce you to public policy and management theory and practice at an advanced graduate level. Throughout these studies, you will gain practical knowledge and skills that will provide a solid foundation for the specialist studies units undertaken in the program.  

Part C. Specified elective studies

The focus of these units is to develop your expertise in public policy and management studies. These will provide you with the knowledge and skills needed for the capacity to work and communicate across the borders of context or discipline.

Subject: Resource allocation and business model performance (ACI5903)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit examines the use of both financial and management accounting information for decision making. Whilst emphasising interpretation by users of accounting information, the unit will also provide you with an introduction and understanding of the preparation, presentation, and interpretation of financial statements. Similarly, the unit will provide an introduction to the preparation and presentation of various forms of management accounting information and aspects of management control. The unit will also provide an introduction to business structures and the governance and regulatory arrangements in respect of financial reporting that pertain to them.
Learning Outcomes:
- demonstrate the application of judgement, problem solving, critical thinking and analytical skills.
- apply management accounting techniques relating to full and relevant costing, cost volume profit analysis, budgeting, capital management and accounting for sustainability
- describe the merits, and regulatory and financial reporting implications of various forms of business structures
- understand the accounting process and the presentation of financial statements and critically use financial accounting information to make decisions about financial and other aspects of business performance
Assessment Tasks:
- 1 - Presentation: 15% (Hurdle: None)
- 3 - Written: 75% (Hurdle: None)
- 2 - Exercise: 10% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Independent study may include associated readings, assessment and preparation for scheduled activities. You are expected to complete all pre-class activities prior to your scheduled class, and post-class activities should be completed after your scheduled class. Learning activities may include a combination of teacher directed, peer directed and online engagement activities.

Subject: Field studies in urban policy and governance (ATI5069)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit offers field-based perspectives on the policy and governance challenges of urban transformation in developing contexts such as Indonesia. Focusing on Greater Jakarta – one of the world's largest urban agglomerations – the unit offers you a field immersion experience and practice perspectives on the challenges of urban policy making in a diverse, complex, and multilevel governance environment. You will gain first-hand knowledge of policy problems and dilemmas faced by city administrators in critical sectors such as traffic congestion and urban transportation, waste management, and flood control. Through interactions with local governments, communities, the private sector, and CSOs involved in solving such problems, as well as visits to field sites, you will gain an in-depth understanding of the policy and governance challenges of urban transformation.
Learning Outcomes:
- recognise and explain the linkages between urban policy and governance and the broader context in which decisions relating to urban policy and governance are made;
- analyse and critically reflect on key challenges and opportunities in urban policy and governance and propose innovative policy solutions.
- critically evaluate concepts and practices in urban policy and governance within an Indonesian context;
Assessment Tasks:
- Policy brief: 25% (Hurdle: None)
- Discussion: 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Analytical exercise: 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Oral presentation: 35% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Scheduled activities may include a combination of teacher directed learning, peer directed learning and online engagement.

Subject: Governance (ATI5102)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In this unit, you will examine public, private and not for profit institutions at international, national and local levels and how they impact the governance of communities in a range of international settings. Subject:  Data analytics in communications (ATI5374)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The strategic application of big data is transforming the field of communications. As our everyday communication practices increasingly become digital, mobile and social, there has been an explosion of data that is routinely collected and analysed for strategic purposes. In this unit you will explore the intersection between audiences and publics, and digital data. You will learn how to make use of data analytics to examine the dynamics, flows and effectiveness of communication processes across digital and social media platforms. You will develop skills in gathering, analysing and visualising digital data to better understand audience sentiment, behaviour and engagement. Drawing on case studies and practical research, you will understand the value of data analytics in informing communication strategies. You will also engage with the ethical and legal concerns at the heart of public debates relating to the collection and use of big data.
Learning Outcomes:
- assess the distinct ethical, legal and public interest implications raised by the use of big data;
- gather, analyse and visualise digital data in the context of communication flows;
- discuss and interpret the results of data analytics to provide insights to processes of communication;
- apply advanced communication, research and analytical skills.
- critique the role of big data in the field of strategic communications;

Subject: Crisis communications and issues management (ATI5381)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Crisis communications and issues management are an increasingly important and specialised area of expertise in contemporary organisations.
