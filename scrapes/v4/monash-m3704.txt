## Bachelor of Nursing (Honours)
##### M3704
##### 48 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
This is a prestigious program of coursework and research in either nursing or midwifery for high achieving students who have completed the Bachelor of Nursing and/or the Bachelor of Midwifery, or a comparable degree course. 

You will undertake research methodology training and carry out an independent research project in nursing or midwifery, working closely with a supervisor who will provide you with individual guidance and academic counselling. If you have both a degree in nursing and a degree in midwifery you may, with agreement of their supervisor, undertake a combined honours over the one year of full-time study in both fields.

This course offers a pathway to higher-level research in nursing for graduates with a nursing degree, or in midwifery for graduates with a midwifery degree.

### Structure
This course consists of a combination of coursework and research. You will develop advanced theoretical knowledge and research methodologies appropriate to your area of research, Nursing or Midwifery, and plan and execute a research project under the individual guidance of an academic supervisor.

In some circumstances, candidature embracing the two may be approved.

### Requirements
This course comprises 48 points including a blend of research and coursework. The focus of study within each of these units will be either nursing or midwifery or a blend of the two.

You must complete:

  * NUR4401 Clinical honours thesis 1: Applied research methods and skills

  * NUR4402 Clinical honours thesis 2: Framing a research proposal

  * NUR4403 Clinical honours thesis 3: Conducting a research project

  * NUR4404 Clinical honours thesis 4: Analysing and reporting research

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Apply cognitive and technical skills to a range of complex problems including developing and executing a research project and collecting, organising, analysing and interpreting data with some independence
**2.**
Apply advanced knowledge and technical skills including theoretical, experimental and computational approaches as appropriate for the field of research to demonstrate autonomy, well-developed judgement, adaptability and responsibility as a learner
**3.**
Communicate ideas and results perceptively and effectively to professional audiences in oral and written formats
**4.**
Identify a gap in knowledge or evidence and develop critical, creative and innovative solutions to address the research problem
**5.**
Demonstrate cognitive skills to identify, retrieve, interpret, critically review and synthesise the relevant literature in the field
**6.**
Demonstrate ethical, professional and personal responsibility and behaviours consistent with good research practice and professional codes of practice

### Progression to further studies
Successful completion of this course may provide a pathway to a Monash master's by coursework degree, and if admitted you may be eligible for credit.

Successful completion of this course may also provide a pathway to a graduate research degree.

### AHEGS
The Bachelor of Nursing (Honours) is an undergraduate award. The course is taught in English. Graduates of the course have demonstrated advanced theoretical knowledge and techniques nursing and/or midwifery and relevant research skills including the capacity to undertake an independent research project and to communicate their findings to a high standard. The course takes one year of full-time study and comprises a fourth year of study following the completion of the normal requirements of the Bachelor of Nursing or Bachelor of Midwifery degree or equivalent. The Bachelor of Nursing (Honours) is a Level 8 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification.  The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Clinical honours thesis 1: Applied research methods and skills (NUR4401)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: This unit provides you with the opportunity to encounter a challenging research environment through the interaction of philosophical paradigms and extension of theoretical knowledge and acquisitions of skills to facilitate the undertaking of a research project. The unit will build upon knowledge gained in core undergraduate research units. You will be allocated a research supervisor to begin formulating research questions and methodology for subsequent study.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop and utilise research skills in the following areas: + advanced online searching; + documentation management; + data collection methods and analysis; and
- Begin formulating research question and proposed study.
- Evaluate and utilise appropriate philosophical foundations of research;
- Distinguish and critique quantitative and qualitative methodologies;
Assessment Tasks:
- Oral Presentation (20 minutes): 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Written assessment (3,500 - 4,000 words): 60% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 24 hours per week of teacher facilitated and self-directed learning including tutorials and online activities. Please refer to Moodle for tutorial types and requirements.

Subject: Clinical honours thesis 2: Framing a research proposal (NUR4402)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: The unit provides you with the opportunity to develop an appropriate hypothesis or research question and derive an appropriate research design showing regard to the practical problems of conducting research in nursing or midwifery settings. Ethical issues in research are also considered. You will develop your own research proposal and seek ethical approval to conduct your study under the supervision of an academic staff member.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify issues related to the conduct of the proposed research;
- Identify a research topic that addresses a significant nursing or midwifery issue;
- Participate in research seminars and workshops;
- Develop a research problem, question or hypothesis;
- Describe the theoretical basis behind the topic and research design;
- Demonstrate a collaborative and critical approach to research.
- Apply to ethics committees for research approval as necessary; and
- Formulate and present a research plan;
- Develop and present a research proposal, including a literature review;
Assessment Tasks:
- Research proposal (3,500 - 4,000 words): N/A% (Hurdle: Competency)
- Oral presentation (15 minutes): N/A% (Hurdle: Competency)
Workload: 24 hours per week of teacher facilitated and self-directed learning including online activities. Please refer to Moodle for tutorial types and requirements.

Subject: Clinical honours thesis 3: Conducting a research project (NUR4403)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: The unit provides you with the opportunity to implement the research proposal developed in NUR4402. You will further develop a literature review, finalise ethical approval for their research, and collect, organise and manage the data for the study. With your research supervisors, you will explore a range of issues that can arise during the research process.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate effective progress towards research plan;
- Finalise the ethics approval process for the project;
- Discuss issues arising during the research process;
- Work collaboratively with a research supervisor to achieve research milestones (e.g. submission of ethics proposal);
- Present research findings in a seminar context.
- Demonstrate an awareness of ethical and privacy issues as they apply to their research proposal;
- Generate a critical review of the literature and theoretical frameworks relevant to their field of study;
- Accurately collect, manage and secure data for research activities;
- Write to an academic standard by incorporating existing research findings and supervisor feedback; and
- Respond appropriately to the realities of research project issues including identification of a research site (or sites) and recruitment of participants;
- Undertake data collection both accurately and ethically;
- Conduct a pilot study (if appropriate) to anticipate and address practical problems that may arise in the course of data collection;
Assessment Tasks:
- Research report (3,500 - 4,000 words): N/A% (Hurdle: Competency)
- Oral presentation (15 minutes): N/A% (Hurdle: Competency)
Workload: 24 hours per week of teacher facilitated and self-directed learning including online activities. Please refer to Moodle for tutorial types and requirements.

Subject: Clinical honours thesis 4: Analysing and reporting research (NUR4404)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: The unit provides you with the opportunity to develop appropriate research knowledge and skills as you, with the assistance of a supervisor, analyse and interpret the data collected in Clinical Honours Thesis III. Following this, the research will be written in the form of a literature review and article suitable for publication, and be presented at a research seminar. The clinical/midwifery practice in this unit will allow the you to exit the program at the level of an advanced novice practitioner.
Learning Outcomes:
- Write to an appropriate academic standard by incorporating existing research findings and supervisor feedback;
- Respond appropriately to realities of research project issues (e.g. ethical and practical issues surrounding confidentiality, data management etc.);
- Generate a report on the project, demonstrating critical analysis and discussion of findings in the context of prior research showing due regard to relevant stylistic conventions;
- Work collaboratively with a research supervisor to achieve research milestones;
- Identify strengths and limitations in their research design;
- Address practicalities of research project management.
- Demonstrate an ability to manage data collection and analysis;
- Conduct appropriate data analysis and interpretation;
- Suggest directions for future research in their specialist area; and
- Analyse and present data in a clear, concise and systematic manner;
- Present research findings in a seminar context;
Assessment Tasks:
- Submission of a portfolio consisting: 100% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: 24 hours per week of teacher facilitated and self-directed learning including online activities. Please refer to Moodle for tutorial types and requirements.
