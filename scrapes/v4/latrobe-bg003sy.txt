##### BG003SY

You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
As international markets become increasingly interconnected and complex, the demand for agile, forward-thinking professionals continues to rise. If you are seeking to rapidly expand your expertise in international business, La Trobe’s Graduate Diploma in International Business offers an ideal pathway to advance your knowledge in just one year.

The course strengthens your cross-cultural competencies and equips you to navigate the challenges of managing people and organisations in international business contexts. You will explore how organisations adapt to diverse cultural, institutional, and regulatory settings to build effective workforce and foster collaboration across borders.

A key focus lies in the management of innovation in an international setting, examining how emerging technologies, such as artificial intelligence (AI) and cybersecurity, are shaping operational practices and strategic decision-making. By integrating core business skills with insights into digital transformation and international talent dynamics, you will gain the tools to develop inclusive and future-oriented strategies for international business success.

By the end of the course, you will possess a distinctive blend of capabilities in international business, digital fluency and intercultural engagement, preparing you for impactful roles on the world stage.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Examine strategies for leading and managing individuals and teams in culturally diverse and international organisational settings.
**2.**
Evaluate practical solutions to issues in cross-border business operations.
**3.**
Apply ethical and strategic decision-making skills in international business environments.
**4.**
Demonstrate cross-cultural competence and communicate international business ideas effectively to diverse audiences.
**5.**
Critically analyse international business theories and concepts to address challenges in international business contexts.

Subject: ACCOUNTING AND FINANCE FOR MANAGERS (BUA4002)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Accounting and finance are critical skills for all professionals in the modern global environment. In this subject, you will be provided with an introduction to key concepts, theories and practical applications associated with accounting and finance in diverse organisational contexts. You will be exposed to content which will cover the nature of the accounting and financial environment and the types of accounting and financial practice and decision-making required from a business perspective.
Learning Outcomes:
- Construct, interpret, analyse and communicate information contained in a firms financial statements using appropriate digital and information tools.
- Examine and apply financial mathematics tools and techniques to determine cash flows, cost of capital, capital investments.
- Prepare budgets, manage working capital, and make appropriate pricing and production decisions.
- Examine the importance of basic accounting and finance principles, and differentiate the appropriate sources of finance for a firm.

Subject: ECONOMICS FOR GLOBAL LEADERS (BUA4003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Economics impacts on all aspects of business and society. Key individual, organisational and government decisions around the allocation of scarce resources are at the core of economics, and an understanding of economic concepts, frameworks and language can greatly enhance a leader’s capacity to make sound decisions and influence the decisions of those around them. This subject develops students’ knowledge of economic concepts and provides the opportunity to apply this knowledge to real world situations. Students will apply economic concepts and frameworks to current issues of concern for organisations and society. Students will learn how to evaluate and apply critical aspects of economics such as the main market forces of supply and demand; the choices producers make in relation to their costs; revenues and profit; competitive and monopoly market structures and how these affect market prices; the use of game theory to make more strategic economic decisions; measuring a nation's income and the cost of living; the drivers of economic growth and the causes of unemployment.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply economic frameworks to business and social problems for policy and decision-making.
- Construct and present logical and persuasive economic arguments and communicate concepts professionally.
- Research and apply economic concepts to predict how changes in economic conditions may impact individuals, businesses and industries within market structures.
- Critically analyse economic data to explain relationships between economic variables and their impacts on policy, business and individuals.

Subject: INTERNATIONAL FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT (FIN5IFM)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Financial globalisation, a heightened geopolitical environment, economic uncertainties, and increased ESG focus present unique challenges and opportunities for investors and corporations worldwide. International Financial Management develops your conceptual and applied knowledge and skills to manage the critical value drivers of firms operating globally. You will examine the structure and operations of global financial architecture, evaluate the economics, institutions, policies, and uncertainties governing exchange rate behavior, forecast exchange rates, and measure and manage exchange rate risk exposures. You will be trained to critically evaluate the relative merits and risks associated with the strategic financial management decisions of multinational companies, including financing, investment, and asset allocation decisions in a competitive global economic setting.
Learning Outcomes:
- Assess foreign exchange behaviour - exchange rate regimes, exchange rate determination, international parity relations - and their implications for forecasting exchange rates and managing exposures.
- Design and manage strategic financial decisions, including country risk analysis, foreign direct investment, financing, asset allocation, and tax management in international settings to deliver sustainable value.
- Critically evaluate contemporary global financial systems and current issues in international finance that affect the global flow of funds and financial management functions of global firms.

Subject: FOUNDATION OF LOGISTICS AND SUPPLY CHAIN MANAGEMENT (MGT4001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will be introduced to the fundamentals of logistics and supply chain management, along with their implications for operational and strategic business decisions. You will explore essential theories and concepts related to demand and supply planning, distribution network design, sustainable procurement, service supply chain and operations, international transport and logistics, as well as supply chain sustainability and the circular economy. Throughout the subject, you will have the opportunity to learn these concepts in a range of business contexts, preparing you to advance your knowledge in each of those specialised areas. Through both independent and collaborative work on a variety of authentic activities, you will develop foundational knowledge in logistics and supply chain management by solving real-world problems. Additionally, you will cultivate critical and creative thinking, quantitative, and interpersonal skills essential for success in the logistics and supply chain profession.
Learning Outcomes:
- Propose informed solutions to real-world logistics and supply chain challenges, through both individual and collaborative efforts.
- Critically analyse foundational concepts in logistics and supply chain management across various business settings.
- Examine and evaluate the principles of logistics and supply chain management.

Subject: INTERNATIONAL TRANSPORT AND LOGISTICS (MGT5004)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: When cargo flows and supply chains extend beyond national borders, the complexity of international transport and logistics activities increases. This subject focuses on the global transport of goods, which heavily relies on ships, aircraft, and trains. As a result, more stakeholders become involved, additional documentation is generated and transferred, financial payment risks intensify, and disruptions and uncertainties can impact supply chains. In this subject, you will acquire essential knowledge and develop job-ready skills necessary for roles such as international transport and logistics coordinators and freight forwarders. This includes gaining familiarity with trade and customs policies, understanding documentation requirements for efficient cargo flows and physical distribution, developing the ability to design international transport and logistics plans for various business scenarios, creating recovery plans to address changes and disruptions in international transport and logistics, and effectively communicating information and potential changes to stakeholders. Additionally, the subject explores sustainability concerns, data analytics, and emerging technologies related to digitalisation.
Learning Outcomes:
- Design international transport and logistics plans for efficient, profitable, sustainable and resilient supply chains.
- Exercise professional judgement and evaluate international logistics management practices for a collaborative supply chain.
- Critically analyse the impact of international transport and logistics on a company's performance and collaborations within global supply chains.
- Critically analyse the implications of trade policies, international conventions, sustainability commitments, and digitalisation on global transport, logistics, and supply chains.

Subject: MANAGING INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS (MGT5005)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: As the fourth industrial revolution connects people and industries throughout the world, managers and organisations must increasingly navigate issues related to globalisation, regionalisation and environmental analysis as well as assess political risk, culture and diversity, and global corporate responsibility. In this subject you will analyse and critique the interconnected issues of strategy, structure, culture, and people management in international business operations.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply relevant international management theories and research to practical management problems and challenges in multinational and transnational enterprises
- Critically analyse how local, national and global trends affect management and strategy in multinational and transnational enterprises.
- Apply relevant theories and research to align talent management with the strategic objectives and practical challenges of international management.
- Critically analyse the role of ethics and corporate governance in international management.
