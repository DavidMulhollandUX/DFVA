Subject: WOMINJEKA LA TROBE: INDIGENOUS CULTURAL LITERACY FOR HIGHER EDUCATION (ABS0WOM)
Credit Points: 0
Overview: Wominjeka La Trobe is a short one-hour compulsory online subject that introduces all La Trobe students to Indigenous Australian history, culture and customs. A rich and relevant cultural heritage education is important for everyone. This topic highlights the link between Indigenous knowledge and values and broader cultural literacy. It asks you to think critically about your own attitudes, values and beliefs.
Learning Outcomes:
- Reflect critically on your own cultural attitudes, values and beliefs
- Demonstrate knowledge of and respect for Indigenous Australian perspectives and experiences, history, culture and customs, including an appreciation of the local people and country
- Recall important details regarding the history, culture, customs and people of Indigenous Australia
- Understand the need for and value of appropriate and effective intercultural communication

Subject: INTRODUCTION TO HUMAN ANATOMY (ANA1001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will explore the fundamentals of human anatomy. You will develop a strong foundation in anatomical terminology, including concepts such as anatomical position, planes, directional terms, and movements. Through focused study of the major body systems—particularly the musculoskeletal and nervous systems—you will learn to apply anatomical knowledge to real-world scenarios and communicate effectively using precise anatomical language.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate how the nervous system integrates sensory and motor control, and the effects of its dysfunction/impairment.
- Describe the anatomical structure and function of major body systems using the appropriate anatomical terminology.
- Analyse the role of the musculoskeletal system in facilitating movement and assess the impacts following injury.

===== SOURCE: https://handbook.latrobe.edu.au/subjects/2026/ANA2001 =====
Subject: APPLIED HUMAN ANATOMY (ANA2001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, students will build on previously learned anatomical principles and terminology, to cover the applied regional anatomy of the upper limb, lower limb and the trunk (including the vertebral column, as well as the thorax and its role in breathing), and neuroanatomy in relation to sensation and motor control. The concept of integrated function of multiple body systems will be developed in each region. A strong focus on functional anatomy and on applying anatomy to a living body will prepare students for using this knowledge in a professional context.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify musculoskeletal structures through surface anatomy.
- Describe normal structure and function of the central nervous system including neural pathways for sensation and motor control.
- Describe normal structure and function of the musculoskeletal and nervous systems within the upper limb, lower limb, and trunk (vertebral column, and the thorax in relation to mechanisms of respiration).
- Analyse movements and exercises in terms of muscle activation and function, and joint range of motion.
- Apply observational and communication skills, using appropriate anatomical and lay terminology for use with clients or other health care professionals, to make accurate observations of anatomical structures or events and infer their relationship to function using cadaveric material, the living body or medical imaging.

===== SOURCE: https://handbook.latrobe.edu.au/subjects/2026/HBS1HBA =====
Subject: HUMAN BIOSCIENCES A (HBS1HBA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject introduces you to human physiology - how the body works. You'll learn how the body is organised and the basics of cell structure and function, and how processes in the body are controlled. This will provide the foundation for the study of the major body systems important for support and movement (muscular system); regulation, integration and control (nervous and endocrine systems); fluids and transport (blood, heart, cardiovascular and immune systems); energy, maintenance and environmental exchange (respiratory, digestive and renal systems, metabolism); and the continuity of life (reproductive systems).
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the basic physiology of major organ systems of the human body.
- Apply knowledge of physiology concepts to clinical and disease states.
- Demonstrate oral and written communication skills.
- Demonstrate the skills for self-managing, reflection, and successful learning at university.
- Describe the structure and function of body cells, tissues, and organs.
- Apply specialist terms and language associated with physiology and anatomy to describe the human body.

Workload: 

===== SOURCE: https://handbook.latrobe.edu.au/subjects/2026/HBS2ALF =====
Subject: ANATOMY: LOWER LIMB & FOOT (HBS2ALF)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject the anatomical principles and terminology introduced in first year anatomy will be applied to the detailed study of the anatomy of the lower limb and the foot. The concept of integrated function of multiple body systems will be developed in each region and relevant medical imaging techniques and clinical assessment skills will be incorporated. Lectures and on-line material will provide an outline of the major content of this subject, but students will be responsible for their own learning through a variety of online and practical learning activities integrated with clinical applications.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply observational and communication skills, individually and as part of a team, using appropriate medical terminology, to make accurate observations of anatomical structures or events and infer their relationship to function using cadaveric material, the living body or medical imaging in a laboratory environment
- Apply relevant anatomical principles to describe normal structure and function of body systems within the lower limb, and explain its role in support, locomotion and posture.
- Apply relevant anatomical principles to describe normal structure and function of body systems within the foot, and explain its role in support, locomotion and posture.

Subject: ANATOMY AND NEUROPHYSIOLOGY FOR SPEECH AND SWALLOWING (HBS2ANS)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, students will explore the functional anatomy and neurosciences relevant to speech and swallowing and the role of the central and peripheral nervous system in sensory and motor function. The concepts of human structure and function introduced in your first year anatomy and physiology subjects will be applied to the head and neck, respiratory and nervous systems. Emphasis will be placed on developing students' abilities to predict the effects of impaired structure and function. This subject will provide students with foundation knowledge to assist in clinical reasoning, practice and consultation in speech pathology.
Learning Outcomes:
- Work collaboratively with peers in a small group to understand the anatomical aspects of clinically relevant case studies
- Make accurate observations of anatomical structures and events and infer their relationship to function.
- Apply anatomical principles to explain normal structure and function of body systems in the nervous system involved in speech, swallowing, vision, hearing and breathing.
- Apply anatomical principles to explain normal structure and function of body systems in the head and neck involved in speech, swallowing and hearing.
- Apply anatomical principles to explain normal structure and function of body systems in the thoracic region involved in breathing and speech.
Assessment Tasks:
Workload:

Credit Points: 15

Topic materials will be presented face to face and online lectures, videoclips and worksheets, but you will be responsible for your own learning through supported face to face practical activities and online workshops, including examination of appropriate cadaver materials. - Independently source, assimilate and appraise basic anatomical principles relating to head and neck anatomy.

===== SOURCE: https://handbook.latrobe.edu.au/subjects/2026/HBS2PAT =====
Subject: PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (HBS2PAT)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject provides you with the fundamental principles of the underlining mechanisms of human disease and how these mechanisms impact on clinical outcomes. Emphasis begins with general disease processes including cell injury, cell adaptations, inflammation, immunopathology, tissue healing, neoplasia and circulatory disturbances and disorders. Specific pathological conditions of body systems e.g. nervous, respiratory, renal, gastrointestinal, hepatic and skeletal systems along with diabetes will be addressed. A variety of teaching methods; including interactive lectures, on-line video content, on-line quizzes, clicker workshops and discipline-specific workshops will be delivered in this subject.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain cellular and biochemical changes that underlie the development of systemic diseases (i.e. Systemic Pathophysiology and relate these changes to the likely clinical course and outcomes of an afflicted individual.
- Demonstrate effective communication skills by communicating scientific results, information, or arguments to a range of audiences, for a range of purposes, and using a variety of modes.
- Apply the principles of General Pathophysiology to human disorders and reparative states, describing how these general pathophysiologic processes impact on possible clinical courses and outcomes in an afflicted individual.

===== SOURCE: https://handbook.latrobe.edu.au/subjects/2026/HBS3001 =====
Subject: HUMAN PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (HBS3001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will examine disorders which may be experienced by individuals over the life-span, emphasizing the basic pathological processes that underlie diseases and the risk factors and contributing causes of disorders. Specifically, general pathological processes will be discussed with emphasis on disturbed function, adaptation to injury, inflammation, tissue healing, immunological disorders, neoplasia, fluid imbalance, thrombosis, embolism and vascular disorders. In addition to general pathological processes, specific pathological conditions of body systems e.g. cardiovascular, respiratory, nervous, gastrointestinal and skeletal systems along with diabetes will be addressed. You will consolidate and apply their knowledge of pathophysiology by communicating a disease state in authentic format to scientific peers and non-expert audiences.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain cellular and biochemical changes that underlie the development of systemic diseases (i.e. Systemic Pathophysiology and relate these changes to the likely clinical course and outcomes of an afflicted individual.
- Apply the principles of General Pathophysiology to selected human disorders and reparative states, describing how these general pathophysiologic processes impact on possible clinical courses and outcomes in an afflicted individual.
- Demonstrate effective communication skills by communicating scientific results, information, or arguments to a range of audiences, for a range of purposes, and using a variety of modes.

===== SOURCE: https://handbook.latrobe.edu.au/subjects/2026/HCS1FSP =====
Subject: FOUNDATIONS IN SPEECH PATHOLOGY (HCS1FSP)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will be introduced to the concepts of communication, eating and drinking and develop an awareness of the individual and social factors that shape human communication, eating and drinking across different populations and contexts. You will learn about the human right to communicate and the integral role that communication, eating and drinking play in the formation of individual identity. You will learn about the role of speech pathology in enabling communication, eating and drinking by exploring speech pathology practice in a range of contexts, including health, disability and education. You will also be introduced to the role of research literature in advocacy, support and the delivery of innovative, evidence-based speech pathology services. This subject includes professional learning, practical experiences or portfolio activities which contribute to the completion of the Career Ready AdvantageTM award.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the scope of speech pathology practice and its role in enabling communication, eating and drinking
