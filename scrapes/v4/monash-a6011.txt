## Master of International Relations and Master of Journalism
##### A6011
##### 96 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
This is a double degree course leading to two master's degrees: the Master of International Relations and the Master of Journalism. Our world is being shaped by big questions about global justice, war, peace, social movements and inter-state relations. These questions help define what this double degree course is all about. In short, this course offers you a window into the diverse and dynamic world of politics in the 21st century. This course provides an entry-level qualification for a professional journalism career, and a mid-career course for working journalists seeking to extend their expertise and refresh the intellectual basis of their practice. The journalism component is designed to ensure you will make an excellent contribution to their profession using a full range of media technologies and the course has a particular focus on public interest and investigative journalism.

Alongside a suite of core units covering key issues in International Relations, you will have the opportunity to curate your learning towards your own interests and career pathway by selecting units from key international relations specialist knowledge areas including:  Global business and management, Global justice and development, and Global security.

This course provides you with a comprehensive knowledge of international affairs, grounded in the key fundamental debates framing global politics and driven by in-depth empirical analysis. Through this, you will be able to make sense of the complexity of global politics by providing the analytical perspectives and skills needed to see both the 'bigger picture' and comprehend detailed aspects of specific issues.

Journalism studies focuses on finding the truth and telling the story and plays a crucial role in scrutinising and holding to account those in power and influence.

Graduates have also found careers in news media, and have become young entrepreneurs establishing their own firms both in Australia and abroad.

### Structure
Double degree courses include all the features of the component degree courses, except electives may be reduced or redirected.

A research pathway is also available subject to meeting eligibility requirements.

Master of International Relations 

The Master of International Relations is structured in four parts. In the double degree course you complete: 

Part A.  Core studies
These studies draw on best practices within the broad realm of international relations practice and research exploring the theoretical, analytical, economic, and security dimensions of global politics. 

Part B.  Applied studies
These studies cover key issues in diplomacy and global political economy, gender, peace and security, global migration, and political violence and counter-terrorism. Additionally you will undertake professional or scholarly work that can contribute to a portfolio of professional development.

Master of Journalism 

The Master of Journalism is structured in five parts. In the double degree course you complete:

Part B. Core studies 
These studies will immerse you in journalism studies at graduate level. Through a combination of academic and practice-based work, you will gain a solid foundation in creating works of journalism from mastering the written word in print through to video, audio, photography, digital production, investigative and data - in metropolitan, regional and international contexts.

Part C. Applied studies
The focus of these studies is professional or scholarly work that can contribute to a portfolio of professional development.

Part D. Professional practice studies 

Designed in association with industry experts, select from this suite of professional enhancement offerings to build essential competencies for your career pathway.

### Requirements
You must complete 96 points, of which 48 points are from the Master of International Relations; including all the requirements in Part A and Part B for the single degree; and 48 points from the Master of Journalism; including all of the requirements in Part B, Part C and Part D. (6 credit points) for the single degree.

Research pathway (optional) – 24 credit points

You can complete a research pathway in your course if:
- you have achieved a minimum weighted average mark of 80% in the units of this course;
- you have the permission of the Course Director; and 
- a suitable thesis supervisor is available.

The following unit replacements apply:
- APG5084 Designing research replaces Part D from the Master of Journalism. 
- APG5849 Research thesis A replaces 6 credit points of Part A from the Master of International Relations.
- APG5850 Research thesis B replaces 12 credit points of Applied studies from either the Master of International Relations or the Master of Journalism. 

Note 1. APG5084 Designing research is a co-requisite for enrolment in APG5849 Research thesis A and must be completed in the same semester.
Note 2. Undertaking the research pathway does not guarantee entry into a graduate research degree (e.g. Masters by Research, PhD).

You must complete 24 credit points from the following list:

  * APG5084 Designing research

  * APG5849 Research thesis A

  * APG5850 Research thesis B

Subject: Professional internship (APG5044)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: You will conduct an internship with a domestic or international host partner organisation. Partner organisations may be from a diverse range of industries and sectors, including government departments, private industry and not-for-profit organisations. While undertaking your internship, you will undertake tasks and/or contribute to a workplace project as defined by the host partner organisation. Internship tasks, such as research, may be carried out on an affiliated arrangement where physical location at the host organisation is not required. The host partner organisation will appoint a field supervisor for the project and the Unit Coordinator and/or Chief Examiner at Monash University will assess your assignments for the unit.
Learning Outcomes:
- Communicate in a clear and coherent way that is effective for the purpose and the intended audience
- Reflect on workplace experiences and craft a meaningful and insightful response to these reflections
- Apply broad discipline knowledge to find solutions to complex problems
- Show technical skill in designing, conducting and reporting on an independent research project
- Collaborate with others on a project in a workplace setting
- Exercise critical thinking and professional judgement in developing new understandings
Assessment Tasks:
- Exercise: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Presentation: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Report: 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Applied Practice Project - Analysis: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Career development task: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Applied Practice Project - Reflection: 40% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 288 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Scheduled activities may include a combination of teacher directed learning, peer directed learning and online engagement.

Subject: Designing research (APG5084)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit provides you with advanced skills in the design of research projects in the social sciences. The unit explores the core issues involved in conducting social science research and examines a variety of research methodologies and methods. Its focus on the process of research design allows you to develop skills in writing detailed research proposals for their own research topics.
Learning Outcomes:
- Initiate and design an original research project.
- Critically evaluate research design principles, data collection and data analysis techniques in existing research in the social sciences;
- Identify and select a range of research methodologies and methods for a specific discipline, issue or topic;
- Identify and apply ethical principles in research practice;
Assessment Tasks:
- Forum posts: 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Research proposal: 60% (Hurdle: None)
- Presentation: 20% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Scheduled activities may include a combination of teacher directed learning, peer directed learning and online engagement.

Subject: Advanced simulation (APG5969)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: This unit examines a number of interrelated themes dealing with the resolution of various types of complex policy problems. You will be assigned specific roles and participate in extended problem-based simulations. These theoretically grounded simulations require you to work strategically with classmates to resolve the issue according to role-specific objectives. You will explore a number of factors that may prevent or accelerate the escalation of such policy problems. To be effective, interdisciplinary student teams will have to work together to apply and integrate their disciplinary areas of expertise.
Learning Outcomes:
- Work with others on a team to formulate action plans based on insights from multiple areas of expertise
- Critically analyse the goals and motivations of various actors
- Engage in time-critical, applied research
- Communicate persuasively in a variety of professional environments
- Prepare expert briefings for non-experts on a multidisciplinary team
Assessment Tasks:
- Project: 60% (Hurdle: None)
- Simulation: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Pitch: 10% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 288 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled meetings with a supervisor and independent study.
