Become an expert with in-demand skills when you study La Trobe’s Master of Professional Accounting.
In this accredited degree, you’ll build expertise in existing and emerging accounting information systems and data analytics tools. Investigate the fields underpinning modern accounting, including business law, taxation, finance and economics. Connect with industry and professional accounting bodies during your studies and prepare for career success.
Learn how to solve accounting problems across a diverse variety of business contexts. With lectures delivered by leading academics and guest industry experts, you’ll get a well-rounded perspective on contemporary accounting issues – ranging from industry-specific measurement issues to the impact of global harmonisation on financial reporting practices.
La Trobe’s Master of Professional Accounting is a direct pathway to becoming a qualified professional accountant. If you have limited exposure to accounting or are forging a new path, this degree will develop your theoretical and technical knowledge and skills to build your dream career.
You'll learn:
  * **Accounting principles, information systems and analytics**
    * Understand how to record business transactions, prepare financial statements and evaluate business performance. Use the accounting and data analytics tools to analyse data and assist decision making.
  * **Management accounting**
    * Understand how to provide organisational decision-makers with relevant accounting information to assist with strategic planning, controlling and performance management.
  * **Financial accounting and reporting**
    * Apply international and Australian accounting standards involving measurement and professional judgement in a variety of practical scenarios.
  * **Auditing and assurance**
    * Explore the nature and purpose of auditing and assurance. Understand the responsibilities of auditors in the context of reporting quality information.
  * **Taxation**
    * Learn the fundamentals of the taxation system and its implications for individuals and businesses.

Work based learning (placement) requirements 
Students have an opportunity to undertake a work placement subject BUS5010 (Business Work Placement) or a work-based project subject BUS5009 (Business Consulting Project) as core-choice. These subjects are an opportunity to focus on the application of theoretical concepts to address workplace problems or development issues using problem-based enquiry and learning. ### 
Career outcomes 
Accountants and accountancy expertise are vital for organisations in every industry – so after graduation, you could find roles in private enterprise, government departments, the not-for-profit sector and beyond.
Possible roles include:
  * **Business analyst**
    * Assist with start-up structuring, valuation and financing. Review existing business operations to help with strategy, business planning, and identifying and implementing performance improvements.
  * **Financial accountant**
    * Take responsibility for financial reporting, including end-of-year financial reports, reconciliations and statutory obligations. Help provide stakeholders with reliable information for decision-making.
  * **Management accountant**
    * Help your organisation evaluate past performance and plan for the future. Analyse financial and non-financial data to assist in decision-making and advise on the implications of business decisions.
  * **Auditor**
    * Use modern auditing and data analytics tools to give internal and external clients assurance about an organisation’s operations.
  * **Taxation accountant**
    * Advise on taxation issues for individuals and organisations. Manage, prepare and submit reports, including tax returns and other statements, and advise clients on how to manage their income.

Subject: ACCOUNTING PRINCIPLES AND PRACTICES (ACC4001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Accounting principles are critical for ensuring consistent and reliable accounting practices. This unit develops students’ understanding of fundamental accounting principles and their application to various accounting processes and systems. Students will learn how to systematically identify and analyse business transactions, prepare financial statements, and evaluate businesses financial performance using relevant accounting tools. Students will also enhance their professional judgment and data analytics skills by exposure to real-world business scenarios.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and record business transactions, and prepare financial statements using double-entry accounting principles.
- Evaluate the accounting purpose, processes and systems within the Australian or an international regulatory environment
- Critically evaluate financial performance of firms to inform decision making using relevant information.
- Exercise professional judgement to solve accounting problems from ethical, social, and regulatory perspectives, and effectively communicate relevant information to a range of stakeholders

Subject: AUDITING AND ASSURANCE  (ACC5AAS)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Financial report audit is an independent examination of a company's financial statements and related disclosures by an auditor. This subject develops students’ knowledge of the core principles and objectives of financial report auditing, as well as the responsibilities of auditors in delivering high-quality reports to stakeholders. Students will acquire the skills to apply relevant auditing standards to effectively plan, execute, and communicate audit findings to both internal and external stakeholders. Additionally, the subject will expose students to various audit techniques used by auditors to conduct audit procedures and assess evidence. Learning Outcomes:
- Apply quantitative methods and relevant technologies in audit and assurance engagement.
- Evaluate the nature, purpose and stages of auditing, and the professional, legal and regulatory requirements for auditors.
- Evaluate the audit evidence and communicate audit opinion to relevant stakeholders.
- Critically apply relevant auditing standards, regulations and laws to audit general-purpose financial statements, and assess the risks of material misstatement in the financial statements and develop strategies for audit.

Subject: TAXATION (ACC5TAX)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Australian taxation laws and regulations are vital to the Australian way of life given its significant impact on individuals and businesses taxable income and tax responsibilities. This subject develops students’ knowledge and application of relevant tax legislation and case law principles to determine the taxable income and tax liability of individuals and companies in Australia, and taxation implications to other taxable structures including trusts, partnerships, and their associated entities. Subject: ECONOMICS FOR GLOBAL LEADERS (BUA4003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Economics impacts on all aspects of business and society. Key individual, organisational and government decisions around the allocation of scarce resources are at the core of economics, and an understanding of economic concepts, frameworks and language can greatly enhance a leader’s capacity to make sound decisions and influence the decisions of those around them. Subject: PRINCIPLES OF FINANCE (BUA4005)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will be introduced to the concepts, theories, and applications of finance. You will examine the role of the financial sector within the economy at large and the role of finance in the management of organisations. A particular focus will be on how investment and funding decisions are made within organisations, including an introduction to capital budgeting and project evaluation. This will lead to an introduction to a wide range of financial markets, including equity and debt markets. The mechanics of each market are examined, and the characteristics of the instruments traded in each market are explored. Consideration is then given to the concepts of risk and return and their application to portfolio theory and asset pricing models.
Learning Outcomes:
- Formulate the risk and return of individual financial assets and portfolios and describe the Capital Asset Pricing Model and its implications when evaluating financial assets' expected return and risk.
- Identify and review the fundamental concepts of capital structure theories and explain the critical factors which impact managerial decisions in developing capital structure and dividend policy
- Identify the financial manager's critical financial decisions, discuss the financial system's primary role, and explain how interest rates are determined.
- Compute bonds and share prices using the time value of money. Evaluate and explain the capital budget decision rules and apply these rules to a firm's investment decisions.
Assessment Tasks:

Subject: FOUNDATIONS OF COMPANY AND COMMERCIAL LAW (LST4001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will explore some of the core principles of contract law, corporations law and the law of negligence. You will discuss various business structures including the law relating to the incorporation of companies and a strong emphasis will be placed on important company law topics such as directors' duties, member rights and remedies, financing of companies and company insolvency. You will examine the purposes of these legal frameworks and analyse the practical operation of these legal principles in the business environment. This subject adopts a skills-based learning approach through teaching the basic techniques of problem-solving and promoting an understanding of the dynamic nature of the law in business and corporations. This is a core subject accredited by CA/CPA.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify legal principles, including case law and statute, relevant to company law and commercial law, applying them appropriately to problem-solving scenarios.
- Identify the key purposes and principles of contract law, the law of negligence and corporations law, including the legal status of corporations, and explain how these purposes and principles apply to commercial transactions.
- Demonstrate advanced knowledge of the key features of the Australian legal system.
- Critically analyse the ethical frameworks for the laws relating to data protection and privacy regulation in business and corporations.

Subject: ACADEMIC INTEGRITY MODULE (LTU0AIM)
Credit Points: 0
Overview: The academic integrity module introduces academic integrity standards to inform and support students in avoiding plagiarism and academic misconduct. Students complete four parts that encompass academic misconduct and academic integrity decisions, including cheating, plagiarism, and collusion. Students learn about Turnitin (the text-matching tool) used at La Trobe University, and how to get help and support their development of referencing skills.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the importance of the acknowledgment and the referencing of sources
- Locate, select, and utilise information about La Trobe's approach to academic integrity to support appropriate acknowledgement of references used.
- Recognise and understand academic integrity responsibilities.

Subject: INDUSTRY PLACEMENT (LTU5IND)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject allows students to extend their formal academic learning beyond the classroom. Students complete a minimum of 100 hours of work placement combined with academic activities that require reflection on self-efficacy, skill application and skill development. Placements are expected to be self-sourced by students and be related to their studies. Subject to approval from the University, placements can be undertaken within an organisation, remotely, or as a virtual experience. This subject provides students with opportunities to apply and adapt their skills to a real-world environment and network with employers. It brings together the expertise of industry partners and La Trobe University staff for the benefit of students' professional development.
Learning Outcomes:
- Perform at a professional level, work tasks and projects by applying discipline knowledge and employability skills in professional contexts.
- Communicate effectively with team members from different backgrounds, using in-person and technology-based communication channels.
- Collaborate with team members in professional contexts to achieve negotiated project and organisational outcomes.
- Critically appraise your professional development achievements, employability and career development needs.
- Work with an organisation to design and plan the scope and aims of a project to complete in a 100-hour work placement.
