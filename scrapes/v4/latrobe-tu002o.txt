## Undergraduate Certificate in Information Technology - On-Line
##### TU002O
##### 60 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
La Trobe's Undergraduate Certificate in Information Technolgy provides you with the foundational knowledge to enhance your employability with a digital skillset or pursue a new career in information technology. You'll build solid foundations and essential knowledge across key areas of information technology in like networking, programming, databases and more.

You'll develop the foundational knowledge in the areas of computer hardware, cybersecurity, programming, software, data communications, and computer networks. Explore the complex social and environmental impacts of technology and how to protect data, systems, and networks from cyber threats as you build the interdisciplinary skills to prepare you for future studies and employment – such as communication, critical thinking, problem solving and collaboration.

Make study work around work and life commitments with flexible online learning, giving you the freedom to study anywhere. You'll gain the confidence to directly pathway into a Bachelor of Information Technology, Cybersecurity, or Computer Science, where you can continue to build your skills in Information Technology while expanding into various sectors of the IT industry.

You'll learn:

  * Protecting data, systems and networks  * Understand the core concepts, terminology, and practices of cybersecurity, exploring common attacks, malware, real-world data breaches, and apply basic security controls and risk management approaches.

  * Programming  * Learn the foundations of the Python programming language as a means of creating executable program code to design and implement working software solutions.

  * Management of database systems  * Explore the different existing database models, including relational database analysis, design, and implementation.

  * Digital literacy  * Discover how to source information, communicate, collaborate and manage your own digital footprint when you use technology.

  * Network engineering  * Learn about architecture, structure, functions, components, and models of the Internet and other computer networks.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Apply foundational programming techniques using Python to design, test, and debug simple software solutions.
**2.**
Interpret and apply core networking concepts, including protocols, topologies, and device functions, in practical scenarios.
**3.**
Analyse common cybersecurity threats and recommend basic protective strategies to mitigate risks.
**4.**
Design and query simple relational databases using data modelling techniques and structured query language (SQL).
**5.**
Apply problem-solving techniques to simple computational and technical challenges across the domains of programming, cybersecurity, networking, and databases.

Subject: INTRODUCTION TO CYBERSECURITY (CSE1ICB)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In the modern Internet era, industries and organisations need to be prepared to defend against cyber threats and attacks. Stakeholders should be familiar with basic principles and best practices of cybersecurity to protect their businesses and personal information. In this subject, these principles and strategies for future cyber security are explored. Key topics include: information security; ethical and legal practices; mitigating cyber vulnerabilities; and the process of incident response and analysis. The subject introduces the broad discipline of cybersecurity and outlines how to ensure the privacy, reliability, confidentiality and integrity of information systems and mitigate against cyber threats and risks.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply and compare approaches to cyber risk management in addressing real-world digital security challenges.
- Demonstrate foundation skills in identifying malware, securing applications, systems, and networks
- Analyse emerging cybersecurity practices, regulations, and standards, and evaluate their implications for digital security.
- Work efficiently and effectively as a member of a team to investigate possible solutions such as cryptography to enhance data security, and web security, and digital forensics against cyber threats

Subject: DATABASE FUNDAMENTALS (CSE2DBF)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject starts with an overview of the architecture and management of database systems, and a discussion of different existing database models. The main focus includes relational database analysis, design, and implementation. This subject prepares students for subjects such as the final year industry project which require an understanding of database design and implementation. The students learn: relational algebra as the formal foundation of relational databases; relational conceptual design using an entity-relationship diagram; relational logical database design; foundation of normalization; security and integrity; and SQL implementation of relational database queries. Students are required to design a database application that meets the needs of a system requirement specification, and to implement the system using a commercial standard database system such as ORACLE or POSTGRESQL. In addition, a selection of advanced topics in databases will be introduced and discussed.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify the appropriate normalization techniques for database design.
- Identify the possible risks and ethical and social considerations relevant to a designed system.
- Implement a database system using SQL and apply the relevant knowledge in database management and administration.
- Define the underlying model of relational database operations using relational algebra.
- Apply a database modelling technique using an ER and EER diagram, and use a transformation process to change these to a relational database design.

Subject: NETWORK ENGINEERING FUNDAMENTALS (CSE2NEF)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, students are introduced to the foundational concepts of computer networking. The subject covers the OSI reference model and its associated protocols to provide a layered understanding of network communication. Students will explore the lifecycle of a data packet across a network and learn the principles of IP addressing. The subject also includes hands-on experience with basic network device configuration and an introduction to static routing, enabling students to build and manage simple network topologies.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the overall OSI model and how layers interact with adjacent layers in the model.
- Apply appropriate network physical standards for different network scenarios.
- Identify functions of each OSI layer and apply this knowledge to build a Network.
- Describe TCP/IP based network protocol suite and compare with OSI model.
- Describe the evolution of Local Area Network (LAN technology) with focus on Ethernet.
