The Master of Sustainable Energy is designed to equip students to develop transition pathways that provide affordable, reliable and sustainable energy. The program aims to provide students with a deeper understanding of energy systems, responsible business practice and ways to address threats of global temperature rise and broader climate change impacts. The MSE is designed to address complex problems across a range of disciplines and equips graduates to progress to leadership and management positions in the sector. As a graduate of the MSE you will understand the complex nature of energy generation, distribution and supply; the crucial roles of finance, technology and regulation for energy project management and business development and the critical importance of social license for assessing the energy landscape. You will develop a deep understanding of risk, innovation and sustainability. You will be working towards creating a better energy future, learning directly from leading academics, researchers and industry representatives. All courses are in week intensive blocks.

Subject: Foundations in Microeconomics (ECON7000)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides students with a basic knowledge of the core economic principles. These principles will be explained and developed using a variety of examples involving businesses, governments, and households. Topics include supply and demand analysis, imperfect competition, producer and consumer theory, marginal analysis, welfare analysis, benefits and costs.
Learning Outcomes:
- Examine advanced economic concepts and theories.
- Critically assess economic policies related to real-world contexts.
- Explain policy discussions and decision-making processes.
- Evaluate contemporary economic challenges and their outcomes.
- Communicate economic ideas through written reports to both expert and general audiences.
Assessment Tasks:
- CML Quizzes Online: 25% (Best 5 out of 6 CML count)% (Hurdle: False)
- Contextualised Learning of Economics via Authentic Reflection - Journal Entries (CLEAR-JE) Online: 20% (Best 2 out of 3 CLEAR-JE count)% (Hurdle: False)
- End-of-Semester Exam Identity Verified In-person: 55%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week

Subject: Foundations in Macroeconomics (ECON7001)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This is an introductory course in Macroeconomics. This course covers important macroeconomic issues such as growth, inflation, unemployment, interest rates, exchange rates, technological progress, and budget deficits. The course will provide a unified framework to address these issues and to study the impact of different policies, such as monetary and fiscal policies, on the aggregate behavior of individuals.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply macroeconomic terminology correctly.
- Appraise the roles and functions of macroeconomic institutions.
- Use diagrams, simple equations and words to explain key causal relationships in macroeconomics.
- Analyse contemporary macroeconomic policy issues and their impacts on business and on society.
- Identify, compile and interpret macroeconomic data.
Assessment Tasks:
- Tutorial Preparation: 10% (Best 8 of 9)% (Hurdle: False)
- Policy Analysis + Infographic Presentation (Part A) Team or group-based In-person: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Policy Brief + Infographic (Part B) Team or group-based: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- End-of-Semester Exam In-person: 35%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: The Economics of Climate Change (ECON7760)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course has a number of objectives; to outline the problem of climate change, to discuss and compare the theoretical economic solutions to combating climate change, to present existing climate change mitigation actions in an economic context and to discuss possible future climate policy issues.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate knowledge of the causes (and potential regulatory solutions) of climate change.
- Understand and apply economic concepts to the issue of climate change.
- Critically evaluate the economic outcome of climate change policy.
- Communicate economic thinking in non-technical, plain language via different formats.

- Infographic and Presentation Identity Verified: 35%% (Hurdle: False)

Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 1 Hour/ Week

Subject: Sustainable Energy Technologies and Supply Systems (ENGY7000)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course introduces students to the language of energy and key scientific principles that underpin energy systems. Students are provided with an overview of the energy challenge including supply and demand and future scenarios for 2050. Students will gain a comprehensive understanding of the suite of renewable energy and technology options available.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate an understanding of renewable and low-carbon electricity generation technologies, electricity grid infrastructure and supply systems (how electricity is transmitted and distributed between generators and load centres), and energy storage technologies including how they interact with electricity grids at different time scales
- Use analytical tools to evaluate electricity generation, grid infrastructure and energy storage technologies with respect to efficiency, resource utilisation, and environmental impact
- Analyse social, political and economic challenges of new technology deployment and provide the most effective strategies to overcome barriers to sustainable energy transition
- Effectively communicate to multiple audiences and stakeholders, sustainable solutions to multifaceted energy problems
Assessment Tasks:
- Solar Energy Problem Set: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Wind Energy Problem Set: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Net Zero Electricity System - Report: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Oral Exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Contact 12 Hours/ Ad Hoc Workshop 15 Hours/ Ad Hoc Intensive contact 12 hours over 2 days in first week, Workshops 3 hours/week for 5 weeks.

Subject: Net Zero Pathways for Business (ENGY7001)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course explains the physical science of climate change, with discussion of climate models, projections and impact scenarios. Australian and international climate policies will be presented and their implications for transitioning to a low carbon society will be explored. Students will gain a deeper understanding of potential mitigation responses, vulnerability assessment, and adaptation strategies for organisations and communities both in Australia and beyond.
Learning Outcomes:
- Articulate an evidenced-based explanation of climate change, its future impacts and risks, and appropriate and meaningful responses.
- Analyse corporate climate-related disclosures against benchmarks and standards, including science-based targets.
- Develop and analyse climate and energy policies and strategies.
- Apply scenario planning approaches to develop logical alternative forecasts and analyse the energy and climate implications.
- Lead and work in teams to develop net-zero strategies for businesses to reduce their emissions intensities.
Assessment Tasks:
- Reflection: Climate-related disclosures: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Reflection: Decarbonisation Strategies: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Group Project: Net Zero Strategy Team or group-based: 45%% (Hurdle: False)
- Oral exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 25% Hurdle% (Hurdle: True)

Subject: Energy and Development (ENGY7002)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course examines the fundamental importance of energy transitions for achieving sustainable development. During the course, students will explore a variety of global change issues including resource depletion, population, consumption, security and conflict and investigate the ensuing implications for energy. With a focus on both the developed, fast developing and developing countries by applying a range of legal, political, regulatory and behavioural frameworks, the course examines mechanisms required to achieve a transition towards a low carbon future.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand and critique the multi-dimensional role of energy in achieving sustainable development goals.
- Adopt a systems approach when examining energy access issues such as the energy trilemma.
- Critically evaluate development paradigms and the contributions of the public and private sectors in achieving sustainable energy access for all.
- Demonstrate an understanding of appropriate technology for energy access projects.
- Articulate the social and stakeholder impacts of energy proposals and management of social licence to operate.
- Reflect on sustainability leadership and working in multi-disciplinary, professional teams.
Assessment Tasks:
- Foundation Modules Quiz Online: 5%% (Hurdle: False)
- Group Presentation: Community Energy Proposal (community and stakeholders) Team or group-based In-person Online: 5%% (Hurdle: False)
- Group Report: Community Energy Proposal Online: 45%% (Hurdle: False)
- Individual Project: Energy Trilemma Case Study (Report + Interview) Hurdle Identity Verified: 45% HURDLE - MUST PASS% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Contact 12 Hours/ Ad Hoc Workshop 15 Hours/ Ad Hoc Intensive contact 12 hours over 2 days in first week, Workshops 3 hours/week for 5 weeks.

Subject: Energy Transitions in Industrial Processes (ENGY7003)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course examines current and emerging technology options to manage and reduce energy consumption, improve efficiency and lower emissions to mitigate climate change. Key low emission power generation technologies and their costs, benefits, challenges, risks and associated supply systems will be examined, including advanced power cycles, Carbon Capture & Storage (CCS), gas, cogeneration and oil production. The course provides students with a broad understanding of the challenges required in transitioning from a carbon intensive fossil fuel based energy system.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop an understanding of various processes used in the heavy industry and manufacturing sectors, including but not limited to thermal, electrical, and chemical processes.
- Gain a thorough understanding of the energy requirements for processes within the heavy industry sector. Analyse different energy sources (fossil fuels, electricity, renewables) and their implications for efficiency, cost, and environmental impact.
- Analyse social, political and economic challenges of new technology deployment and provide the most effective strategies to overcome barriers to sustainable energy transition.
- Communicate effectively with other professionals and with the community at large on the challenges in the heavy industry and manufacturing sectors, including as part of multi-disciplinary teams.
- Provide advice and leadership on evaluating energy systems and making sustainable technology recommendations, taking into account environmental, social and governance (ESG) criteria.
Assessment Tasks:
- Foundation Modules Multi-Choice Quiz Online: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Debate on Australia's Industry Future and PAF Team or group-based Online: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Part 1: Corporate Decarbonization Plan Development for a Heavy Industry Leader Hurdle Online: 40% Hurdle% (Hurdle: True)
- Part 2: Oral Presentation Hurdle Identity Verified: 20% Hurdle% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Contact 12 Hours/ Ad Hoc Workshop 15 Hours/ Ad Hoc Intensive contact 12 hours over 2 days in first week, Workshops 3 hours/week for 5 weeks.

Subject: Energy Investment and Finance (ENGY7004)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The course introduces key commercial analysis concepts and decision tools including financial concepts such as cash flows, costs of debt and equity, and rates of return, discount rates, NPV analysis for informing the development of a business case. Students will gain an excellent understanding of investment decision making under uncertainty with the application of these concepts to various energy case studies.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the principles of finance and investment decision making in a company
- Compare different power generation technologies in terms of their returns on investment and their sensitivities to key financial and technical parameters.
- Describe and analyse a potential new sustainable energy investment opportunity and communicate the value proposition, in terms of Discounted Cash Flow and Net Present Value analyses.
- Communicate (both individually and as part of a multi-disciplinary group) the implications of risk and uncertainty on new, complex, energy projects, including the impact on value creation.
- Provide advice and leadership on evaluating alternative energy development options and making an overall investment recommendation, taking into account environmental, social and governance (ESG) criteria.
Assessment Tasks:
- Foundational Module Quiz Online: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Group Presentation Identity Verified Team or group-based In-person Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Individual Project Report Hurdle: 50% Hurdle% (Hurdle: True)
- Oral Exam Hurdle Identity Verified Online: 20% Hurdle% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Contact 12 Hours/ Ad Hoc Workshop 15 Hours/ Ad Hoc Intensive contact 12 hours over 2 days in first week, Workshops 3 hours/week for 5 weeks.

Subject: Professional Project (ENGY7112)
Credit Points: 8
Overview: Students are required to complete a professional project (4 course equivalence) in their chosen field of study before the end of the third semester. Students will be encouraged to select a suitable project or a dissertation topic upon enrolling, ideally with a suitable industry partner, and to continuously consider their topic throughout the program, adding value from the different courses they undertake.
Learning Outcomes:
- Knowledge of field - Identify and scope a professional or research area of inquiry, and develop related questions, in a critical area of sustainable energy.
- Knowledge of field - Locate and evaluate relevant literature and prior art; locate, access, select and use technical and commercial information from various sources; relate prior knowledge to the problem to identify how it can best be applied to tackle the problem.
- Knowledge of field - Conceptualise, develop and implement a methodology to produce results for the Project, delivered to academic and professional standards.
- Analysis and critical thinking - Collect and analyse data; elicit project requirements and academic concepts.
- Analysis and critical thinking - Draw conclusions based on evidence and argument in a field of sustainable energy.
- Analysis and critical thinking - Contextualise outputs relevant to current research and industry practice.
- Communication - Build professional links and networks with energy practitioners as part of the Project experience.
- Communication - Produce a written report or other instrument that eloquently communicates integrated, reflective approaches to the sustainable energy challenge.
- Communication - Deliver an oral presentation at a professional level (similar to an international conference or 'board of directors' briefing).
- Communication - Confidentially discuss relevant aspects of the Project, and why it matters in the context of the sustainable energy transition, to relevant stakeholders.
Assessment Tasks:
- Milestone 1: Project Proposal Hurdle Online: 5% Hurdle% (Hurdle: True)
- Milestone 2: Interim Report Hurdle Online: 10% Hurdle% (Hurdle: True)
- Milestone 3: Oral Presentation Hurdle Identity Verified Online: 15% Hurdle% (Hurdle: True)
- Milestone 4: Final Report Hurdle: 70% Hurdle% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Activities vary according to project scope and delivery mode, and may include field work, industry placements, interview
