## Graduate Diploma in Child, Family and Community Nursing - Melbourne (Bundoora)
##### HGCFCN
##### 120 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
La Trobe's Graduate Diploma in Child, Family and Community Nursing prepares you for a broad community approach to nursing children and families, particularly those in the prenatal to school-entry stage of their life cycle.

Study community child health, including the nurturing care of mothers, children and families throughout the childbearing years. Explore the practice of nursing, midwifery research, Indigenous health, perinatal health and the care of families facing adversity. Graduate ready to apply an evidence-based approach to practice, communicate effectively, critically analyse and apply principles for optimal health outcomes.

As part of the course, you'll undertake 324 hours of clinical practice in community-based maternal, child health and early childhood education settings.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Critically examine the principles of child and family health nursing according to relevant legislation, professional standards, and program guidelines.
**2.**
Develop knowledge and skills in teleheath and artificial intelligence to support primary health care and  community-based nursing practice.
**3.**
Critically apply culturally inclusive, family-centred practices to achieve optimal health and development outcomes for children and families.
**4.**
Plan, implement and evaluate child, family and community nursing, using an evidence-based approach to address complex family concerns.
**5.**
Communicate effectively as a member of a multidisciplinary team.

### Course features
### Work based learning (placement) requirements
Compulsory placements (Work Based Learning)
During this course, you will participate in one or more supervised Work Based Learning (WBL) placements designed to allow you to practice newly acquired skills, behaviours and knowledge while integrating theory with practice. The placements will immerse you in professional work culture, enabling you to develop and further enhance your skills. WBL provides you with the opportunity to gain valuable industry experience at one or more of our local, interstate or international industry partners or agencies. WBL placements will generally be arranged by the Office of the Provost Placements and WBL Team at La Trobe University. The number of hours undertaken can vary considerably, depending on the requirements of the course. Students may incur additional costs and it may be necessary to travel or relocate to complete a specific placement. Please refer to the individual subject entries for more information.
### Work integrated learning opportunities
Industry informed curriculum, Industry-informed projects, Interprofessional learning, Career development learning
### Career outcomes
As a graduate of this course, you will be well placed to find work within the specialised area of child, family, and community nursing, as a Maternal and Child Health nurse.

### Additional costs
Prior to commencing a placement, students are required to meet all of the requirements of the degree in relation to immunisation. Students must visit a medical practitioner who will complete and sign the Immunisation and Health Record form.

Depending on a students immunisation history, the overall costs for vaccination may be up to AUD$1030. Students are responsible for all costs incurred. Further information will be provided post enrolment.
Students should allow approximately AUD$20 for a uniform, name badge and equipment. Students can purchase these items from any supplier; however La Trobe University branded uniform must be purchased from a university approved vendor. These items remain the student's personal property.
Prior to commencing a placement, students may be required to complete an annual mask fit test. Further information will be provided post enrolment.
Applicants will be required to hold a valid Police Check which is renewed annually. Students will have access to a reduced fee when applying via a La Trobe University provider. For more information on what a police check entails, refer to the Victoria Police website.
Applicants will be required to hold a valid Working with Children Check (WWCC). There is no cost for a volunteer WWCC. For more information and costs refer to the Working with Children Check Victoria website.

Subject: COMMUNITY CHILD HEALTH (NSM4CCH)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will develop public health knowledge and skills within a community-based child, family and community nursing context. You will explore the links between economic inequality and child health, and the promotion of optimal development of young children through social justice strategies. You will develop insight into culturally appropriate practice, applying this knowledge when working with families from diverse cultural backgrounds. This subject is delivered via a flexible online mode which may include online classes and online learning modules.
Learning Outcomes:
- Appraise the nurse’s role in contributing to Primary Health Care strategies in community maternal and child health settings.
- Facilitate referral to appropriate community services to connect families to the community.
- Critically analyse the application of Primary Health Care principles in community maternal and child health care practice.
- Plan culturally inclusive interventions appropriate to diverse populations using the social model of health.

Subject: FIRST NATIONS PERINATAL HEALTH (NSM4IPH)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will reflect on aspects of your own cultural self-awareness to advance your knowledge and skills in providing effective and safe care to First Nations Australian families in the maternity context. You will explore Australian history, concepts related to traditional and contemporary Indigenous cultures, the rights of individuals and groups to self-determination, and consider how culturally safe clinical practices can contribute to closing the gap in health outcomes. The skills gained in this subject will enable you to recognise how socio-cultural diversity may impact maternity outcomes equity and access. This subject is delivered online using online activities, and synchronous lectures and workshops to support your learning.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate health inequity, the factors contributing to this, and analyse ways to close the gap in birth outcomes between First Nations and non-First Nations Australians
- Evaluate the interconnections between physical, social, historical, political and cultural factors and their impact on perinatal health outcomes by understanding First Nations concepts of health and healing.
- Development of culturally competent communication skills to facilitate therapeutic relationships, and support partnerships with First Nations families, health professionals, organisations and communities.
- Critically analyse the role of cultural safety in improving maternal and child health outcomes for First Nations Australians and how cultural safety influences practice.
- Critically reflect on personal and professional beliefs and attitudes in relation to working alongside First Nations women, babies and families.

Subject: MATERNAL AND CHILD HEALTH PRACTICE (NSM4MCH)
Credit Points: 30
Overview: In this subject, you will use your clinical knowledge of women and children (0-6) to plan and implement safe and effective care for families with young children in the community. You will undertake clinical practice in community-based maternal and child health and education settings. This subject enables you to assess the health and development of children and maternal wellbeing, using screening and assessment tools. You will build on nursing and midwifery skills and knowledge, to develop advanced critical thinking and problem-solving skills and develop personal learning goals. The subject is delivered via a flexible online mode which may include on-campus or online workshops, online learning modules and clinical practice in maternal and child health settings.
Learning Outcomes:
- Undertake comprehensive infant, child, and maternal assessments according to the Key Ages and Stages Framework.
- Engage in critical reflection to develop your maternal and child health clinical nursing practice.
- Using prescribed tools, accurately assess the health and wellbeing of mothers.
- Using prescribed tools, accurately assess the health and development of children 0-6 years.

Subject: RESEARCH AND EVIDENCE IN MIDWIFERY (NSM4REM)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will be introduced to the research and evidence in midwifery practice. You will learn the skills needed to be able to interpret and understand research evidence and draw on real life examples of midwifery related research. You will gain a broad understanding of various research designs including the application of quantitative and qualitative approaches. You will develop skills in critiquing evidence for application to maternity care. Using an online teaching mode including, online activities, and synchronous lectures and workshops you will develop skills in reviewing the literature, explore the ethics of research and apply the appropriate design and methods to specific clinical and social issues related to maternity care and practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically appraise levels of evidence and how this impacts the relevance of research to a clinical question
- Develop a search strategy and conduct a literature review related to a specific topic
- Explain the ethical issues inherent in midwifery research
- Formulate a research question based on a clinical issue related to midwifery
- Explain key principles that underpin evidence- based practice
- Justify the choice of different research designs and data collection methods

- Observe legislative requirements in maternal and child health practice, including mandatory reporting, and the accurate assessment, documentation, and response to identified risk of child abuse.

Subject: RESEARCH IN NURSING AND MIDWIFERY (NSM5RES)
Credit Points: 30
Overview: In this subject, you will develop advanced knowledge of nursing and midwifery practice. You will examine the elements of the research process including identifying a research problem, formulation of a research question, research design, methods, data collection, analyses and dissemination of results. You will acquire skills required to conduct a literature review for research purposes and address the ethics of research, develop cognitive and technical skills that demonstrate a broad understanding of a body of knowledge relevant to clinical practice. You will also acquire skills required to conduct a literature review for research purposes, develop appropriate research methods and address the ethics of research.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify the important elements of the research process to critically review and report on an area that needs further exploration
- Examine appropriate strategies for dissemination of research
- Discriminate between different data collection methods and critically judge the most suitable data collection and analysis methods for different study designs
- Critically appraise available evidence to inform your advanced nursing or midwifery practice

Subject: RESEARCH FOUNDATIONS (NSM5RFN)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will develop advanced knowledge of nursing and midwifery research practice. You will examine the elements of the research process including: identifying a clinical issue or research problem, formulating a research question or statement, research design and methods, data collection and analyses, and disseminating results. You will also acquire skills required to conduct a literature review for research purposes and address the ethics of research. The subject is delivered via a flexible online mode (LMS) comprised of learning modules. It provides essential research knowledge that can be built upon in the Advanced Practice subjects.
Learning Outcomes:
- Conceptually map the strategies for dissemination of research.
- Contextualise and critically review the conduct of ethical research.
- Critique a body of research.
- Discriminate and analyse the important elements of the research process.
