## Description
The Master of Public Health (Extension) (MPHext) is designed to meet the continuing education needs of current and aspiring leaders in public health. It fosters careers in research and supports scholarly evidence-based professional practice in public health.

You will graduate with an advanced and integrated understanding of public health practice. Through the MPH you will gain the knowledge and skills to identify, understand, prevent and contain disease in populations as well as to identify, address the social determinants of population health inequities, and to design, undertake and evaluate research. You will be equipped with core skills in epidemiology, biostatistics, health promotion, health policy, planning and management. The MPHext provides the opportunity for you to extend your knowledge and skills in public health research by completing additional research methodology courses and/or an advanced research project in a public health-related topic.

You can tailor your MPHext to suit your goals through a broad program of study or a focused public health specialisation.

Through the research component, graduates learn specialised skills needed for research and professional practice in public health. This will equip you for careers that involve developing research strategy, funding and evaluating research, as well as undertaking research to improve population health outcomes. In addition to research roles, these skills are highly valued in population health roles in government Ministries and Departments of Health, NGOs, primary care and community healthcare services in Australia and internationally.

8. Identify and critically appraise research literature in an area of public health interest to determine research question/s that contribute to knowledge in the field and identify appropriate research methodologies to address these question/s.
9. Demonstrate understanding of and ability to evaluate the planning and execution of research within a specific domain
1. Integrate advanced knowledge and skills to enable protection of the health of populations and prevention of communicable and non-communicable diseases through the use of epidemiological methods and contemporary theories and techniques.
4. Investigate, analyse and synthesise complex population related data and issues to plan and evaluate public health strategies, projects and programs informed by current theoretical debates, techniques and practices.
2. Appraise the health promotion needs of populations and determine effective interventions that draw on contemporary approaches and evidence.
7. Demonstrate capacity to engage in scholarly inquiry, ethical commitment and critical reflective practice for informing professional practice in public health
3. Interpret and apply relevant research and research-based methods to inform public health questions, priorities, policy and practice across a diverse set of contexts.
5. Demonstrate multidisciplinary ways of working and high level skills in communicating, working in teams and advocacy to enhance public health outcomes.
6. Demonstrate sophisticated cultural awareness and a global perspective for working productively as public health professionals in local, national and international settings.

## Program Structure & Degree Requirements
Students must complete 72 Units of Credit (UOC) structured across the following academic components:

### Core Courses (24 UOC)
Students must take 24 UOC of the following courses.

- **PHCM9012**: Health Promotion and Social Perspectives of Health [6 UOC]
- **PHCM9795**: Foundations of Biostatistics [6 UOC]
- **PHCM9041**: Foundations in Public Health and Health Care Systems [6 UOC]
- **PHCM9794**: Foundations of Epidemiology [6 UOC]

### Research Methodology or Extension Project and Coursework (0 UOC)
Extension program students have two options to complete the extension component of their program (24UOC):

1.	Students must complete at least 6UOC of approved research methodology coursework listed below and additional prescribed electives (18UOC); or

2.	Students who achieve a WAM of greater or equal to 75% in a minimum 18 UOC of their program may elect to undertake a research project in place of course electives as either:
- 6 UOC research project and 3 additional prescribed elective courses (18 UOC); or
- 12 UOC research project and 2 additional prescribed elective courses (12UOC); or
- 18 UOC major research project and 1 additional prescribed elective course (6 UOC).

Part-time students can complete the 12 UOC Project (PHCM9144) part time by enrolling in PHCM9145 (6 UOC) in two consecutive terms or the 18 UOC Major Project (PHCM9150) by enrolling in PHCM9151 (9UOC) in two consecutive terms or PHCM9152 (6 UOC) in three consecutive terms.

## Admission Requirements
**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
There are two pathways for entry and a third exceptional pathway:

1. Applicants must have
a) A three year undergraduate degree in any field of study
and
b) Two years of full-time professional or volunteer experience in a relevant field (see list below)**. The professional experience may include experience in health or public health services acquired as part of a degree of 4 or more years duration, e.g. MBBS, MD.

OR

2. Applicants must have

a) An undergraduate degree plus honours in a relevant field of study (see list below)*
or
b) Postgraduate degree in a relevant field of study (see list below)*

OR

3. In exceptional circumstances, applications will be considered for entry when they have extensive professional health or public health experience but do not hold a relevant degree qualification. A minimum of five years full-time relevant professional experience is required and an interview with the School of Population Health may be conducted.

* Relevant fields of study include medicine, nursing, allied health, health sciences, biomedical sciences, dentistry, social sciences, social work, psychology, paramedicine, environmental health, health administration, policy studies, development studies.

** Fields of professional experience that are relevant include:

- Roles in health (including allied health, medicine, nursing, oral health, public health, environmental health, psychology)

- Health service provision or health management roles in human services (e.g., social work, community development, service navigation, case management)

- Roles that involve working specifically on health-related activities or portfolios, such as health-focused positions in emergency services, management, research, engineering, law, science, mathematics and statistics, economics, journalism, or policy.

Subject: Health Promotion and Social Perspectives of Health (PHCM9012)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Can public health actions be designed to shape social change and to avoid exacerbating social inequalities and discrimination? Can actions to improve population health at global and country levels reach beyond appeals to individual responsibility and address drivers of health that are not under individual control? In this course you will learn to be attuned to public health actions that over-emphasise individual responsibility for population health outcomes. The course gives you foundations for developing inclusive public health interventions that attend to the social drivers of and reasons for people’s actions. You will learn that promoting population health is not about fixing problematic individuals, but involves shaping our broader social, cultural, political, economic and physical environments so that healthier choices become easier choices. You will adopt the role of a practitioner who, before developing public health action, first asks the right questions with the aim of avoiding exclusion or discrimination. You will be challenged to use tools such as root cause mapping to critically and systematically analyze health problems and identify priority health needs that go beyond individual responsibility. You will learn skills in applying best practice health promotion principles and health promotion strategies that involve intersectoral collaboration, engage and empower people and foster community ownership.
Learning Outcomes:
- Integrate relevant and appropriate Ottawa Charter action areas and health promotion strategies in the planning of health promotion interventions.
- Analyse how social determinants prevailing in a community can enable or challenge communities and individuals to make healthier choices.
- Present convincing arguments for the relevance of health promotion for addressing current global and public health problems.
- Evaluate how fundamental social science concepts enable and constrain public health research and practice.
- Demonstrate best practice health promotion principles in the planning of health promotion interventions across a range of settings.
- Analyse the implicit and explicit concepts of social change used in Public Health.

Subject: Foundations in Public Health and Health Care Systems (PHCM9041)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course will introduce students to public health and health care systems providing foundational knowledge for future studies and practice. Student's understanding of contemporary public health problems and approaches will be enhanced through an introduction to: the history of public health; core functions of public health; surveillance and measurement of population health; principles of communicable and non-communicable disease control; health promotion and social determinants of health; Indigenous health and well-being; health policy; and health care systems. Students will have the opportunity to discuss contemporary issues in public health with their peers and hear from a range of experts.
Learning Outcomes:
- Examine and critique the role of government and other actors and their impact on public health policy and practice.
- Describe the history of public health and explain how it relates to current public health practice.
- Identify and critique approaches to improving population health including their impact on health equity.
- Examine and critique how social determinants impact health, well-being and disease across different population groups.
- Explain key public health principles and concepts and how they apply to different public health issues.

Subject: Qualitative Research Methods (PHCM9120)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Are you curious about how the meanings people and institutions ascribe to health issues can make or break effective health polices, programs and practices? Are you interested in how qualitative research can inform work to address social exclusion and inequities in health systems? This course introduces you to qualitative study design and it involves an opportunity to practice your data collection, data analysis and reflexivity skills. These skills can be used to research: personal and professional experiences of public health and health systems; the social processes shaping the meanings we develop and ascribe to health; and how people creatively navigate between policy spaces, program development, program implementation and health service delivery. Adopting the role of a qualitative researcher will also help you to judge whether specific qualitative studies can usefully inform your professional practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Undertake and critically reflect on qualitative data collection
- Conduct and critically reflect on qualitative data analysis methods
- Demonstrate knowledge of the rationales, methodological principles, and ethical and social justice considerations entailed in qualitative study design
- Demonstrate reflexivity in qualitative study design, data collection and analysis

Subject: Regression Methods in Biostatistics (PHCM9517)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: We have access to more data than ever before, but how do we find the signal in the noise? And how much trust should we put in the answers we find? We can answer these types of questions with statistical models. This course will introduce you to a family of statistical models called regression models, powerful tools for answering real-world health questions. For example, we can use clinical data to investigate how body composition correlates with lung function in children with cystic fibrosis. Or calculate a person’s likelihood of developing disease from their risk factors. If your doctor has ever calculated your cardiovascular disease risk, that was based on a regression equation. In this course you will learn that different types of data require different types of models. You will also learn how to critically assess the models you build and how to interpret and communicate the results. We will introduce how code can be used to create reproducible analysis. The course will cover some theory, with a strong focus on practical application of the methods using statistical software.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify an appropriate regression model for your research study
- Critically evaluate statistical analyses and present findings at a standard that is sufficient for submission to scientific journals
- Implement statistical analysis using regression models on complex datasets with different types of variables
- Identify and account for confounding and effect modification in epidemiological studies
- Interpret research findings and draw valid conclusions addressing the research question
- Produce analysis scripts using statistical software to foster reproducible and transparent research

Subject: Epidemiological Methods (PHCM9518)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: A core task of epidemiology is to understand what causes adverse health outcomes and ‘what works’ to improve population health. This course takes an in-depth look at the core public health discipline of epidemiology, with a focus on methods used to answer causal questions. It has been designed to help you develop essential knowledge and skills in quantitative public health research that are directly relevant to everyday decision-making in policy and practice, as well as designing and implementing epidemiological research. This course builds on the concepts that were introduced in PHCM9794 Foundations of Epidemiology.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and ask causal questions relevant to public health policy and practice.
- Identify when randomised and observational studies are appropriate to answer causal questions in public health.
- Critically review epidemiological research addressing causal questions, considering the implications for public health policy and practice.
- Apply methods relevant to the estimate of causal effects in epidemiological studies, and report and interpret results for public health policy and practice.
- Describe methods to minimise bias and confounding in epidemiological studies focused on causal questions.

Subject: Foundations of Epidemiology (PHCM9794)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: An epidemiological approach to the collection, analysis, and interpretation of data is the foundation for public health practice. An understanding of epidemiological principles and methods is fundamental to the design, analysis, and critical evaluation of public health studies. This course has been designed to develop your essential knowledge and skills in the epidemiological principles of descriptive and analytical methodologies used to answer population health questions. You will have the opportunity to calculate and interpret measures used to describe the distribution, determinants, and impact of disease in populations. A major focus of the course is the design principles and critical appraisal of common intervention and observational studies and identification of study limitations and sources of bias, essential for the translation of quality evidence into public health practice and policy. You are encouraged to enroll in this subject early in your program. If you have concerns about numeracy it is advisable to consider enrolling in the course as an internal student.
Learning Outcomes:
- Calculate and interpret measures of disease occurrence, measures of association between exposures and disease, and measures of public health impact.
- Critically appraise epidemiological studies, demonstrating the ability to assess study design, interpret study methods, results and conclusions for error and bias.
- Identify the types of selection and measurement bias and confounding in epidemiological studies and discuss methods to reduce their impact on study validity
- To identify the three main types of research questions in epidemiology: to describe population health, predict population health, and to understand the causes of population health and disease
- Describe epidemiological study designs and identify their strengths and limitations
