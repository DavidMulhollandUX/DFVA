##### EC003O

You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
Get the language and literacy skills you need to advance your career through La Trobe's Graduate Certificate in Education (Language and Literacy).

Delivered fully online, this program is designed to empower educators, speech pathologists and psychologists to improve literacy outcomes for students and clients through evidence-based practices. Acting as a pathway from our renowned SOLAR short courses, you'll develop advanced and explicit knowledge about language and literacy skills, as you draw upon the extensive experience, research and leadership of academic staff in the School of Education.

By completing La Trobe's Graduate Certificate in Education (Language and Literacy), you will:

  * explore the structure and development of oral language for early reading instruction, and unpack linguistic concepts that define the English writing system for teaching, reading and spelling
  * learn how to evaluate theoretical models of reading and writing proficiency, and apply cognitive science principles to instructional design for language and literacy teaching
  * discover how to design effective instructional strategies using multi-tiered systems of support, and utilise standardised and non-standardised screening and diagnostic monitoring tools
  * understand how to design, deliver and evaluate classroom instruction based on assessment data.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Foster partnerships and communities of practice in applying robust evidence to pedagogical design and intervention models to support academic success and student wellbeing across a range of settings.
**2.**
Critically engage with learning science and science of reading theories to inform evidence-based practice at school and system levels.
**3.**
Critically interpret and apply specialist knowledge concerning the nature of the English writing system and the ways in which speech and print map to each other, to foster reading, writing, and spelling success in primary and secondary settings.

Subject: LANGUAGE AND LINGUISTICS FOR READING INSTRUCTION (EDU5006)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, students will learn about the structure and development of oral language and how this pertains to early reading instruction and progress across the school years. In particular, students will master the linguistic concepts that define the English writing system as a basis for teaching reading and spelling as reversible processes.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply knowledge of structural and figurative aspects of oral language to the promotion of spoken language development across the primary and secondary years
- Apply knowledge of the linguistic structure of the English writing system to the design and delivery of explicit classroom instruction and support in reading, writing, and spelling.
- Demonstrate an understanding of the distinction between oral language and written language, from both developmental and educational perspectives

Subject: COGNITIVE SCIENCE MODELS OF READING AND WRITING (EDU5007)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject is concerned with a range of theoretical models describing the components and the processes essential for reading and writing proficiency. Drawing on linguistic models and cognitive models of information processing, you will critique the empirical support for these models and make research-informed decisions about classroom practice at primary and secondary levels.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand and apply evidence-based models of reading and writing instruction for novice and established learners across year levels.
- Apply knowledge of information processing to the teaching of reading and writing.
- Critique selected theoretical models of reading and writing and examine multi-tiered levels of instruction.

Subject: INSTRUCTIONAL DESIGN FOR LANGUAGE AND LITERACY TEACHING (EDU5008)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will explore and critique contemporary models for language and literacy instruction through the cognitive science lens. You will apply this to a whole-of-school approach that incorporates a low-variance curriculum.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the theoretical foundations and empirical support for contemporary instructional design models and pedagogical practices.
- Employ cognitive psychology frameworks to make informed pedagogical decisions regarding language and literacy (reading, writing and spelling) instruction for all learners.
- Examine the role of the low-variance curriculum as a whole-of-school approach to instructional design and implementation

Subject: MULTI-TIERED SYSTEMS OF SUPPORT ACROSS YEAR LEVELS (EDU5009)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject is concerned with effective instructional design using the principles of Multi-Tiered Systems of Support, including Response to Intervention. You will learn about standardised and non-standardised screening and diagnostic monitoring tools and how to effectively use assessment data to design, deliver and evaluate classroom instruction. The importance of whole-school policy and practice will be emphasised.
Learning Outcomes:
- Implement and evaluate pedagogical practices across instructional tiers of intervention informed by reliable and valid diagnostic assessment data.
- Apply effective and evidence-based principles of progress monitoring within a Multi-Tiered Systems of support model.
- Select and apply standardised and non-standardised assessment tools including administration, scoring and interpretation.
