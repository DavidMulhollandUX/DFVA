## Master of Engineering Management - Melbourne (Bundoora)
##### LMEM
##### 240 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
La Trobe’s Master of Engineering Management will develop your commercial acumen and help you set yourself apart. You’ll undertake specialist subjects in management, innovation and sustainability – all designed for engineers. Back up your specialist skills with a strong foundation in business, so you can confidently address issues relating to marketing, finance, organisational behaviour, and the analysis of business data.

In your second year, you’ll discover advanced subjects in risk and cost, project management, strategic management and computer-aided engineering. You’ll also advance your knowledge in an engineering specialisation – choose from 12 disciplines.

Learn from experts in both engineering and management. Graduate ready to advance your career as a leader and manager in your field.

You’ll learn:

  * Advanced engineering management  * Develop specialist skills in project management, innovation, risk and cost engineering, environmental sustainability, marketing, finance and more.

  *  Business acumen  * Learn how to lead and develop organisations, teams and individuals, interpret and develop financial documents and take a strategic approach to management decisions.

  * Environmental sustainability practices  * Understand Australian industry best practices so you can lead engineers to create a more sustainable future.

  *  Specialist engineering skills  * Get the latest knowledge with specialist electives from 12 engineering disciplines and take it back into your practice.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Undertake research, evaluate information, and synthesise relevant issues in professional engineering management contexts.
**2.**
Display awareness of social responsibility, ethical and sustainable business practices in engineering management contexts.
**3.**
Identify and critically interrogate contemporary approaches to management, relevant to the engineering industry.

### Course features
### Career outcomes
Rapid technological change is driving the need for engineering managers with a blend of technical knowledge, soft skills and commercial acumen. You could find employment in a range of industries, including manufacturing, technology, telecommunications, geotechnical, government and construction. You could be a:

  * Engineering project manager
  * Take a leading role in engineering projects. Plan, monitor and deliver across all elements of projects, including budgets, human resources, setting objectives and reporting.

  * Senior engineering management consultant
  * Apply industry knowledge and technical expertise to solve engineering problems or improve existing processes and systems.

  * Engineering development manager
  * Plan, direct and coordinate all activities related to engineering development and manage the activities of the development team.

Subject: ACCOUNTING AND FINANCE FOR MANAGERS (BUA4002)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Accounting and finance are critical skills for all professionals in the modern global environment. In this subject, you will be provided with an introduction to key concepts, theories and practical applications associated with accounting and finance in diverse organisational contexts. You will be exposed to content which will cover the nature of the accounting and financial environment and the types of accounting and financial practice and decision-making required from a business perspective.
Learning Outcomes:
- Construct, interpret, analyse and communicate information contained in a firms financial statements using appropriate digital and information tools.
- Examine and apply financial mathematics tools and techniques to determine cash flows, cost of capital, capital investments.
- Prepare budgets, manage working capital, and make appropriate pricing and production decisions.
- Examine the importance of basic accounting and finance principles, and differentiate the appropriate sources of finance for a firm.

Subject: ECONOMICS FOR GLOBAL LEADERS (BUA4003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Economics impacts on all aspects of business and society. Key individual, organisational and government decisions around the allocation of scarce resources are at the core of economics, and an understanding of economic concepts, frameworks and language can greatly enhance a leader’s capacity to make sound decisions and influence the decisions of those around them. This subject develops students’ knowledge of economic concepts and provides the opportunity to apply this knowledge to real world situations. Students will apply economic concepts and frameworks to current issues of concern for organisations and society. Students will learn how to evaluate and apply critical aspects of economics such as the main market forces of supply and demand; the choices producers make in relation to their costs; revenues and profit; competitive and monopoly market structures and how these affect market prices; the use of game theory to make more strategic economic decisions; measuring a nation's income and the cost of living; the drivers of economic growth and the causes of unemployment.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply economic frameworks to business and social problems for policy and decision-making.
- Construct and present logical and persuasive economic arguments and communicate concepts professionally.
- Research and apply economic concepts to predict how changes in economic conditions may impact individuals, businesses and industries within market structures.
- Critically analyse economic data to explain relationships between economic variables and their impacts on policy, business and individuals.

Subject: COMPUTER AIDED ENGINEERING (EMS5CAE)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, fundamental knowledge of the Finite Element Method (FEM) applied to engineering applications for solid structures will be introduced. General linear and non-linear analyses will be presented in a manner that enables students to develop a comprehensive understanding of how to integrate different computational tools in a real engineering business environment. Topics covered include common software platforms available to perform the engineering analyses design, modelling, manufacturing and control. Principles of modelling and analysing, such as stiffness matrix using hand calculation and Matlab as well as FEA (Finite Element Analysis) based on commercial packages will also be introduced. Upon successful completion of this subject, students will have developed a range of skills which will allow them to comprehend and effectively design engineering systems, using modern CAE tools, in a real-world industrial setting.
Learning Outcomes:
- Interpret the theory, design and modelling techiques of finite element analysis
- Develop stiffness matrices for spring, truss, beam and plates and finite element formulations for static, vibration and buckling problems
- Evaluate the convergence of solutions and assess the accuracy of modelling results
- Communicate effectively through written reports on design of engineering structures
- Create and design engineering structures using commercial finite element software.

Subject: RISK AND COST ENGINEERING (EMS5RCE)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Engineering economic analysis is the science of making decisions about investments to achieve cost savings, increase revenue or improve quality of life with benefits that accrue over many years. This subject aims to equip students with the tools to properly analyse and solve economic problems that are commonly faced by engineers. This includes estimating costs and benefits of these investments, measuring and considering risk when making economic decisions, performing economic life and replacement analysis, incorporating the effects of inflation and the cost of borrowed money, and selecting a minimum attractive rate of return. Students will apply these concepts through simulations and scenario-based case studies of engineering projects, and develop oral presentation and negotiation skills, and the development of detailed economic proposals.
Learning Outcomes:
- Work as a member of a team to utilize risk management frameworks to mitigate risks that may arise within project management process.
- Estimate and predict the costs and benefits of engineering investments using advanced analytical skills.
- Assess and evaluate risk in accordance with a risk management framework when making economic decisions.

===== SOURCE: https://handbook.latrobe.edu.au/subjects/2026/MGT5006 =====
Subject: CIRCULAR ECONOMY IN PRACTICE (MGT5006)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: The circular economy is an economic system designed to minimise waste and continually use resources. The circular economy aims to generate economic and social value while ensuring environmental sustainability by creating closed-loop systems that minimise resource input and waste output. As businesses transition towards this model, job requirements are evolving to prioritise sustainability and circular economy practices. This subject explores the interplay between the circular economy and value chain management. In consultation with industry partners, it is designed to provide an engaging learning journey to understand, strategize, and apply circular economy principles, including sustainable resource utilization, waste minimization, and the integration of repair, reuse, and recycling through the lens of value chain management. Students will have the opportunity to work on selected industry projects to enhance their practical skills in identifying and addressing circular economy challenges, utilizing various tools and data-driven methods. Emphasizing the significance and urgency of circular value chain strategies, this subject aligns with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals, preparing students to make informed and responsible decisions in various business settings, both individually and collectively. The applied nature of the subject delivery enables the development of critical business skills essential for roles in the circular economy and sustainability domains.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate teamwork and leadership skills to collectively strategise, communicate, and execute practical circular economy practices in various business settings.
- Exercise professional judgment in addressing complex circular economy challenges.
- Critically analyse circular economy principles within value chains to enhance competitive advantage of organisations in various settings.

Subject: HUMAN RESOURCE DEVELOPMENT (MGT5DPP)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Organisations that support their employees to learn and develop across their careers can benefit from high performance. In this subject you will examine factors that contribute to high performing organisations through the lens of the human resource development (HRD) function. You will examine contemporary theory and practical examples of the overall process of HRD in organisations, including learning and development, career planning, team learning, and organisation development. - Analyse the links between HRD and performance at the individual, team and organization level.
- Critique and apply the main theoretical positions describing HRD, high performance and high performance issues.
- Analyse the problems and challenges in human resource development (HRD).
