Subject: Teaching Foundations (EDHE6001)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Participants will be granted RPL credit for this course on the basis of successfully completing the Teaching Foundations course, which is completed as a professional learning activity in the choice of streams - Teaching@UQ, Coordinating@UQ, CaPE@UQ, LD@UQ or RPL module - and achieving the Teaching Foundations Certificate. Please visit the ITaLI Teaching Foundations webpage for further information and to apply to enrol. Teaching Foundations introduces key principles of effective learning and teaching in higher education. It is designed for UQ staff who are new to teaching at UQ or higher education more broadly and want to develop their teaching careers. The course ensures that new educators or educators new to UQ understand what constitutes effective teaching and learning at UQ. This introductory program helps participants build essential teaching skills, create supportive learning environments, and enhance student engagement. It balances theory and practice, exploring foundational learning theories while providing opportunities to apply them. Assessment tasks enable participants to develop an e-portfolio that showcases teaching practice. Successful completion of assessment tasks will result in recognition as an HEA Associate Fellow - an international accreditation that can be used to evidence foundational teaching experience.
Learning Outcomes:
Assessment Tasks:
- E-portfolio Reflection Teaching observation: 100%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Workshop 18 Hours/ Ad Hoc Contact 6 Hours/ Ad Hoc

Subject: Teaching in Practice (EDHE6003)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course introduces how to develop and maintain effective and inclusive learning environments in higher education programs. It draws on contemporary theories of higher education pedagogy and uses an evidence-informed reflective approach to improve practice. Participants attend common workshops and in other weeks, engage in learning activities specific to their chosen specialisation. A detailed schedule for each specialisation stream will be available on Blackboard. Participants must have current teaching or course design responsibilities. These are defined more broadly than course coordination and include any role supporting learning or curriculum development in formal, informal, or professional settings. Examples include lecturing, lab demonstrating, PhD supervision, tutoring, designing workshops or online modules, creating extracurricular programs, or developing learning resources such as videos or assessment guides. They also include mentoring, supervising placements, developing digital tools, and contributing to student engagement or professional learning. In summary, any activity that facilitates learning or enhances educational experiences. In addition to course credit, successful completion of Curriculum Design at UQ (EDHE6002) and Teaching in Practice (EDHE6003) within three years can also lead to conferral of HEA Fellowship, subject to external moderation.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically review current practice and use an evidence-informed approach to plan a strategically aligned enhancement initiative
- Implement and evaluate the effectiveness of an enhancement initiative.
- Collaborate with members of a community of practice in teaching and learning at UQ
- Critically reflect on personal learning across course and impact on professional practice and personal development.
Assessment Tasks:
- Enhancement Proposal Hurdle: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- Presentation and Dialogue Hurdle Identity Verified: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- Teaching Observation Statement Hurdle: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- Case Study and Reflection Hurdle: Pass/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Seminar 10 Hours/ Ad Hoc Contact 16 Hours/ Ad Hoc Workshop 10 Hours/ Ad Hoc Seminar 3 Hours/ One Off

Subject: Leading Teaching Project (EDHE6004)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides participants with an opportunity to network and collaborate with one another while testing and leading innovation through the development of a scholarly educational project. Participants will investigate an aspect of teaching and learning of mutual interest to them and to their School or department. Supported by workshops, mentors and networks, each participant will develop a complete and viable proposal in an area of relevance to teaching and learning in their context. This course enables participants to engage with innovation, educational reform and scholarship in order to develop their teaching expertise through scholarship and inquiry. At the conclusion of the course, participants are able to share an overview of their proposal that demonstrates how they intend to use scholarship, or research, or professional learning, or other evidence-informed approaches as a basis for effective practice The completed proposal may subsequently be used as the basis for an application for funding, or project implementation that could lead to publication or a conference presentation or a case study for application for Senior Fellowship of the Higher Education Academy.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate relevant scholarly literature to formulate a practice-based research or design question.
- Justify methodological decisions to investigate practice-based research or design question.
- Apply active participation in relevant collegial communities in practice and argue for its importance in contributing to advancing teaching and learning.
- Appraise own educational leadership capabilities and potential.
- Identify and reason about ethical issues and considerations associated with leading teaching innovations and initiatives.
Assessment Tasks:
- Project Proposal Hurdle: pass/fail% (Hurdle: True)
- Seminar Presentation (Showcase) Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: pass/fail% (Hurdle: True)
- Reflection on leading practice Hurdle: pass/fail% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Workshop 20 Hours/ Ad Hoc Contact 25 Hours/ Ad Hoc Seminar 3 Hours/ Semester
