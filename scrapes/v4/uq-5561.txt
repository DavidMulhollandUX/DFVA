Subject: Advanced Agronomy (AGRC7005)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Advanced study of agronomic practices, concentrating on plant adaptation and the evaluation of agronomic practices used in field crop and pastoral production systems. This course was last offered in external mode in 2025.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate the adaptation of specific crops and pastures (including cultivars/varieties/species) to diverse environmental conditions.
- Using advanced concepts of plant physiology, explain management options available in dryland agricultural systems to ensure resource use efficiency and crop yield.
- Evaluate and discuss strategies to mitigate herbicide resistance, and implement integrated approaches for effective weed and disease management for sustainable crop production.
- Explain existing and alternative agricultural systems (including precision agriculture) in terms of sustainability of crop production systems.
- Critically analyse management strategies to mitigate climate change effects in crop and pasture production systems.
- Use a farming systems model (APSIM) to run simulations that vary for agronomic or genetic factors and interpret the results of these simulations.
Assessment Tasks:
- Impact of Climate Change - Assignment 1 (Presentation) Identity Verified In-person: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Sequential APSIM Practicals - Quiz Series Identity Verified Online: 10% (5% Quiz 1, 5% Quiz 2)% (Hurdle: False)
- Sustainable agriculture - Assignment 2 (Sequential tasks) Identity Verified In-person: 25% (3-minute pitch 20%; reflection 5%)% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Examination Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Applied Class 3 Hours/ Week Fieldwork 3 Hours/ One Off

Subject: Leadership in Rural Industries &; Communities (AGRC7046)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The course covers the principles and practices of effective leadership as applicable in rural business, industry and communities, in the emerging world of rapid change, hyper-connectivity and uncertainty. It describes theories, methods and tools for leading through change, harnessing diversity, attributes and practices of leadership, negotiation and conflict management and contributing leadership in groups and teams.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply key principles and procedures of leadership as it can be applied in a rural and regional context
- Apply the dynamics of personal attributes in leadership
- Apply leadership in a group, industry and community context, and be able to practise leadership effectively in at least one of these contexts
- Demonstrate the ability to manage issues that arise in leadership such as manage conflict, foster the development of teams, mentor others, create collective vision etc.
- Demonstrate the ability to sustain leadership functions in groups and in other settings
- Demonstrate the skills and processes to learn from experience and learn collaboratively
- Demonstrate the ability to orally articulate, justify, and defend leadership judgement in response to questioning, including reflection on evidence use, uncertainty, and alternative perspectives.
Assessment Tasks:
- Learning project plan: 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Literature review: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Pre-recorded Oral Presentation with Oral Q&A Check-in Hurdle In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: True)
- Learning project report: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Workshop 3 Hours/ Week INT: Gatton 3W EXT: Online 3W

Subject: Global Challenges in Agriculture (AGRC7047)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The course introduces participants to the many challenges that affect agriculture and food systems across global regions. The course is relevant to students of agriculture and food science, agribusiness, and rural development. It equips participants to apply and extend knowledge and acquire skills for a rapidly changing world. Analysis of global megatrends, futuring and foresighting skills, globalisation, food security, climate change and followed by seeking solutions for sustainability and resilience. Participants are guided in their development as leaders who shape the future, emphasising self-directed learning that is guided by international experts.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse global challenges in agriculture, food, and rural development to identify their causes, interconnections, and implications.
- Evaluate the role of your discipline in responding to complex global agricultural problems.
- Communicate evidence-based insights clearly and persuasively to diverse audiences.
- Justify ethical and socially responsible approaches to agricultural transformation.
- Collaborate in interdisciplinary teams to explore sustainable agricultural responses.
- Articulate your leadership potential to influence sustainable futures in agriculture and food systems.
Assessment Tasks:
- Critical analysis report (group) Team or group-based: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Critical analysis reflection (individual): 10%% (Hurdle: False)
- Sequential Online Tests Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 40% Each sequential item contributes 20% towards the sequential tests (total 40%)% (Hurdle: True)
- Policy critique project: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Plant Physiology (AGRC7048)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Plants are integral to agricultural production systems. But do you understand the processes that drive plant growth and development? Plant physiology is the study of plant structure and function. This course will examine the main physiological processes, how plants interact with their environment and aspects of plant biology relevant to currently active or promising research areas. A strong emphasis is placed on aspects of physiology that are relevant to crops important in agriculture and horticulture.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the basic stages of vegetative plant growth, development and reproduction
- Explain plant resource acquisition and use
- Explain control of plant development driven by internal and environmental signals
- Relate physiological processes across scales (cell-organ-plant)
- Concisely communicate the use of plant phenotyping tools and results of experiments in written and oral formats
Assessment Tasks:
- Presentation: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Practical Manual Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Exam Identity Verified In-person: 45%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Workshop 2 Hours/ Week Practical 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: Sustainable Soil Management (AGRC7053)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course examines how soils can be managed sustainably to provide the ecosystem services that we rely upon for our survival, whilst simultaneously ensuring that these soils are not degraded for future generations. In particular, we will examine conservation agriculture, sustainable irrigation practices, soil nutrients and organic matter dynamics, soil degradation and soil health issues. In addition, we will also examine how soils determine sustainable land use and give consideration to impacts of soil management on the broader environment.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the importance of soils for regulating plant growth, food production, and climate change
- Evaluate how soils interact with the broader environment, including their influence on a range of environmental processes
- Explain the role of agriculture production systems in the sustainable management of soils
- Use soil water relationships to examine plant water use and optimise irrigation systems
- Experimentally investigate approaches for solving a range of soil science problems, including for management of soil degradation
Assessment Tasks:
- Sequential - Practical assessments In-person: 50% (all 4 reports are of equal weighting 12.5%)% (Hurdle: False)
- Final exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 3 Hours/ Week Practical 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: Integrated Crop Health & Management (AGRC7054)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Provides an understanding of integrated pest management for sustainable production systems. There will be special emphasis on weeds and their management measures.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate factors affecting pest ecology, with special emphasise on weeds.
- Estimate the impact of different pests on crops, with special emphasise on weeds.
- Compare and contrast different pest control measures.
- Propose integrated pest management strategies to manage pests in different scenarios.
- Synthesise information from plant protection literature.

Subject: Horticulture Production (HORT7002)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course introduces the production and management of major horticultural crops, including fruits, vegetables, flowers and nursery plants. Students will develop an understanding of horticultural botany, growth cycles, and the biological and environmental factors that influence productivity and quality. The course examines cultural and management practices used in commercial horticulture and their role in achieving successful production outcomes.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate production practices used in commercial nursery crop production.
- Explain the factors that shape the horticultural industry with reference to associated cultural and management practices.
- Evaluate the positive and negative interactions of invertebrates with horticulture crops pre- and post-harvest.
- Describe postharvest factors affecting fruit and vegetable quality, shelf life, and produce losses/wastage.
- Prepare a solution proposal addressing a contemporary challenge relating to production horticulture.
- Communicate scientific solutions clearly in both written and spoken forms to a general audience.
Assessment Tasks:
- New horticultural plant development plan: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Oral presentation: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Final Exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Fieldwork 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: Plant Protection (PLNT7005)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Advanced biology & diagnostics of major pest groups. Pest monitoring including pest & crop sampling & evaluation; development, implementation & analysis of pest management information, professional services, duty of care and ethics.
Learning Outcomes:
- More fully understand the biology of pests and pathogens
- Evaluate interactions between pests, pathogens, and their host plants and explain the implications for plant health
- Understand the biotic and abiotic factors that influence pest and pathogen dynamics
- Demonstrate knowledge of the classification (identification) of plant pest and pathogen groups and use the most appropriate diagnostic procedures
- Use current methods for monitoring pests
Assessment Tasks:
- Behaviour Impact Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Lab Report on Plant Disease/Pests: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Threats to International Agriculture: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Exam Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
