##### TPI002O

You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
Get ready to study at La Trobe University! The Tertiary Preparation Program (TPP) is a comprehensive enabling program, designed to prepare you for university studies, and provide you with a pathway into La Trobe University courses.

The program focuses on building your learner confidence and skills in the areas of academic literacy, critical thinking, science and mathematics. At the successful completion of the program, you will achieve a La Trobe Entrance Score which may be used to gain entry to a wide range of undergraduate or diploma courses.

Our dedicated team will be with you every step of the way; from enrolling in your subjects, to academic development and transitioning into your dream course, we can also connect you with all the supports and services La Trobe University has to offer.

Your subjects are delivered in a supportive, online learning environment. TPP-Intermediate consists of six micro-subjects, taken across three La Trobe Terms. The subjects are delivered asynchronously, however you must meet assessment deadlines. Students completing TPP-Intermediate have access to online academic support through their subject facilitators as well as access to all on campus university services.

You will learn:

  * Academic Literacy: academic integrity, writing and presentation skills.
  * Critical and Creative Thinking: critical thinking, reasoning and inquiry skills.
  * Foundation Mathematics: algebra, statistics and mathematical problem-solving skills.
  * Foundation Science: scientific innovations, applied numeracy and scientific thinking skills.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Apply time management and effective learning strategies to meet study and life goals in the context of individual circumstances.
**2.**
Demonstrate the ability to independently and/or collaboratively, write, present and submit work at an acceptable level for university entry
**3.**
Apply transferable critical thinking and problem-solving strategies to generate ideas and solutions in a range of disciplinary contexts.
**4.**
Curate and synthesise information for research purposes, according to academic integrity and the principle of fairness relating to the attribution of work.
**5.**
Navigate and participate in an interactive online learning environment for the purposes of tertiary study.

Subject: WOMINJEKA LA TROBE: INDIGENOUS CULTURAL LITERACY FOR HIGHER EDUCATION (ABS0WOM)
Credit Points: 0
Overview: Wominjeka La Trobe is a short one-hour compulsory online subject that introduces all La Trobe students to Indigenous Australian history, culture and customs. A rich and relevant cultural heritage education is important for everyone. This topic highlights the link between Indigenous knowledge and values and broader cultural literacy. It asks you to think critically about your own attitudes, values and beliefs.
Learning Outcomes:
- Reflect critically on your own cultural attitudes, values and beliefs
- Demonstrate knowledge of and respect for Indigenous Australian perspectives and experiences, history, culture and customs, including an appreciation of the local people and country
- Recall important details regarding the history, culture, customs and people of Indigenous Australia
- Understand the need for and value of appropriate and effective intercultural communication

Subject: ACADEMIC CULTURE (LTU0001)
Credit Points: 10
Overview: This subject introduces students to academic culture and different forms of academic communication. Students examine the conventions of academic culture and identify strategies to support learning success. They compare the purposes of different genres of academic communication in the context of discipline differences and are introduced to the concept of academic integrity. Students develop transferable skills in time-management, note-taking, academic writing, critical reading, case study analysis and online learning. Assessments are low stakes and include text summary and written comprehension as well as text analysis.
Learning Outcomes:
- Recognise and apply the specific characteristics of academic communication and culture
- Compare different academic genres and styles and identify their requirements required for specific purposes
- Identify principles of academic integrity and relate them to the attribution of work
- Identify and apply study and learning strategies in an online academic environment

Subject: THINKING MATHEMATICALLY (LTU0004)
Credit Points: 10
Overview: This subject introduces students to a spectrum of mathematical thinking and mathematics skills required for future learning in general university programs. Students will develop conceptual knowledge and procedural skills in a range of topics including arithmetical rules and operations, percentages and decimals, rates and ratios and measurement and data. Students will be enabled to solve a variety of mathematical problems using appropriate strategies, methods and techniques and to apply mathematical concepts and knowledge to different real-world situations. Assessments tasks are based on cumulative development of skills and include online quizzes and two project tasks.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply study and learning strategies in an online academic environment
- Apply appropriate methods to solve mathematical problems
- Formulate problems from everyday and scientific contexts into mathematical concepts
- Articulate foundational operations and notation in mathematics, including whole numbers, fractions, percentages, decimals and ratios
- Explain mathematical solutions for different audiences and purposes

Subject: THINKING CRITICALLY (LTU0007)
Credit Points: 10
Overview: Students are introduced to critical thinking as concept and as practice. Students are encouraged to engage with differing viewpoints, question received knowledge and to recognise the difference between lower- and higher-order thinking. Opportunities are provided for the development, transference and application of metacognitive thinking, critical analysis and creative problem-solving. Using the concept of "world view", students interrogate their own opinions and analyse how social, cultural, environmental and biological factors impact their perspectives. Through a range of learning tasks, students develop capacity for reasoning, analysis and reflection. Assessments include reflective journal entries and a short essay.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate principles of academic integrity in relation to the attribution of work
- Explain and apply critical thinking concepts related to metacognition and higher-order thinking skills
- Demonstrate skills in reflective and critical writing for academic purposes
- Apply study and learning strategies in an online academic environment
- Evaluate different perspectives as the result of diverse biological, social, cultural, ideological and environmental factors

Subject: CRITICAL ENQUIRY (LTU0009)
Credit Points: 10
Overview: Students develop and enhance their critical thinking skills and critical literacy through the analysis of a diverse range of media and popular cultural texts. In the context of contemporary issues and arguments, students learn to identify a range of persuasive techniques and representational strategies and to recognise that all media is socially constructed. Students are encouraged to engage with differing view points, challenge received knowledge and to question issues of voice, identity, access and power. Through a range of learning tasks, students develop capacity for textual analysis, creative reasoning and critical reflection.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify and give examples of specific representational strategies and persuasive techniques
- Analyse the direct and indirect impacts of media on structures of power and received knowledge
- Deliver a presentation using digital technology
- Apply study and learning strategies in an online academic environment
- Explain why media representations should be subject to critical analysis

Subject: WORLD CHANGING SCIENCE (LTU0012)
Credit Points: 10
Overview: Students are introduced to a range of world changing ideas in science, from initial concept to gradual adoption to mainstream understanding. Key scientific advances in agriculture, medicine, technology, energy and conservation are overviewed in the context of their global impact. The ethical considerations of scientific advancement are analysed and applied to real-world situations, as well as how scientific information is reported and communicated. Students will write a report on a selected field of scientific research and development.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the relationships between science, technology, human society and the environment
- Synthesise information to form a well-supported academic argument
- Interpret ethical considerations and their applications in science
- Apply study and learning strategies in an online academic environment

Subject: ACADEMIC INTEGRITY MODULE (LTU0AIM)
Credit Points: 0
Overview: The academic integrity module introduces academic integrity standards to inform and support students in avoiding plagiarism and academic misconduct. Students complete four parts that encompass academic misconduct and academic integrity decisions, including cheating, plagiarism, and collusion. Students learn about Turnitin (the text-matching tool) used at La Trobe University, and how to get help and support their development of referencing skills.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the importance of the acknowledgment and the referencing of sources
- Locate, select, and utilise information about La Trobe's approach to academic integrity to support appropriate acknowledgement of references used.
- Recognise and understand academic integrity responsibilities.
