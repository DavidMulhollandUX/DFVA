## Description
The Master of Actuarial (Extension) is suitable for students with a strong background in a quantitative discipline such as Mathematics, Statistics, Data Science, Econometrics, Physics, Engineering, or Actuarial Studies. The program is professionally accredited, and will provide you with a solid training in actuarial studies, business and analytics, and prepare you for an actuarial career as well as entry into the broader financial services industry.

The Master of Actuarial Studies (Extension) lengthens the Master of Actuarial Studies degree program, allowing you to include an additional 4 courses (24 UOC) from the more advanced postgraduate Actuarial Studies courses (including the potential to complete all professionally accredited actuarial courses), or to include course options from related disciplines within the Business School such as data analytics, economics, finance, or statistics.

## Career Opportunities & Employment Areas
The Master of Actuarial (Extension) allows you to obtain and develop the required competencies to enter an actuarial career and provides data analytics and quantitative risk management training for entry into the financial services industry. The program covers the professional actuarial subjects and includes options in actuarial studies, data analytics, quantitative risk management, and other related disciplines.

## Admission Requirements
**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
To be eligible for the program, you must have:

A recognised bachelor degree (or equivalent qualification) with a substantial component of mathematics, statistics, econometrics or actuarial studies, with a credit average, as determined by the UNSW Business School. Entries are also opened to students with background in related disciplines including financial engineering, data science, data engineering, physics, engineering, any related quantitative field, with a credit average, as determined by the UNSW Business School. Applicants are expected to have a mathematical background equivalent to a full year of calculus and linear algebra at first-year university undergraduate level.

Recognition of prior learning or professional recognition

Advanced standing may be granted for courses covered in an undergraduate program or the examinations for the Fellowship of the Actuaries Institute (Australia), the Institute of Actuaries (UK) or the Society of Actuaries through the professional examinations up to a maximum of 4 core courses and 2 electives. Students will be exempted from equivalent courses completed in prior learning where they have not been granted advanced standing.

Courses covered in a completed undergraduate program equivalent to those in the core may be granted advanced standing from the corresponding core courses up to a maximum of 4 core courses. They will be required to complete at least 12 courses in the program.

Students completing the examinations for the Fellowship of the Institute of Actuaries of Australia, the Institute of Actuaries (UK) or the Society of Actuaries through the professional examinations at the same time as enrolled in the degree may receive advanced standing for up to two courses in addition to those granted for the core courses (up to 4). They will be required to complete at least 10 courses in the program.

- Identify the critical features and properties of Markov Chains and their significance in actuarial studies.
- Classify and explain different types of stochastic processes, incorporating the main terminologies and their practical implications.
- Employ Monte Carlo simulation techniques to solve introductory actuarial problems, highlighting the process and outcomes using pseudo-random numbers.
- Evaluate the application of Markov processes in insurance, survival, sickness, and financial modelling, discussing their benefits and limitations.
- Describe the primary features of Markov Processes and demonstrate their use in analyzing models related to insurance and finance.
- Construct logically structured and professionally presented written reports detailing actuarial modelling and applications.
- Apply maximum likelihood method to estimate transition probability and matrix of discrete-time and continuous-time Markov chains.

Subject: Survival Analysis and Prediction of Life and Health Related Risks (ACTL5104)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course covers the actuarial profession syllabus for survival models, focusing on understanding and predicting mortality, morbidity, and longevity risks. It applies these techniques to life, health, and disability insurance, superannuation, finance, and epidemiology. These models help forecast risks related to events such as mortality, health changes, and credit defaults, leading to better risk quantification, pricing, and management. Key topics include: survival models and actuarial notation; Estimation of lifetime distributions; Maximum likelihood estimation of transition intensities in multi-state models, considering age and duration; graduation of crude estimates and tests for fidelity and smoothness; and models for projecting mortality trends. The course emphasizes implementing these concepts using numerical computer packages. Additionally, it will address ethical considerations and issues related to discrimination and heterogeneity in mortality and health risks.
Learning Outcomes:
- Devise exact and census approximation methods to estimate transition intensities dependent on age,
- Apply estimation procedures for lifetime distributions.
- Explain and critically appraise the ethical dimensions and implications of the modelling introduced in the course.
- Explain the concepts of survival models.
- Explain the Binomial and Poisson models of mortality and contrast these models with multiple state models.
- Apply and contrast alternative graduation methods and graduation tests.
- Apply and contrast the main methods of projecting/forecasting mortality rates.
- Derive maximum likelihood estimators for the transition intensities of a multiple state model.

Subject: Life Insurance and Superannuation (ACTL5105)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course covers the actuarial mathematics, models and techniques for valuing long-term contingent cashflows. In-depth understanding of valuation of claims is a key aspect of actuarial work, specifically in the areas of life insurance, health, disability insurance, superannuation and pensions, as well as broader financial institutions. This course combines the present value principle with various stochastic tools in pricing, reserving, cashflow projection and profit testing tasks for various products in life insurance, health and disability, and pension and superannuation space. The fundamental techniques covered in the course help insurers to adequately price and manage the risks of various contracts in life insurance and pension systems, hence allowing the creation of products to help individuals manage risks. They are also increasingly applied in broader areas including social protection and government policy.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply contingent valuation concepts to practical scenarios.
- Evaluate critically the risks associated with cash flows from contingent events.
- Explain and apply fundamental techniques for valuing cash flows involving contingent events such as death and survival.
- Explain the basic valuation and funding principles of superannuation benefits.
- Evaluate critically the reasonableness of premium and policy value calculations for financial contingent products.
- Describe and contrast the different life insurance and life annuity products available in the market.
- Interpret and communicate life insurance and superannuation issues clearly and effectively.
- Present orally and explain Life insurance and superannuation problems in simple terms.

Overview: This course positions generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) as a transformative tool for identifying, assessing and mitigating risks across financial services and related sectors. Through research-infused seminars, hands-on labs and real-world case studies, students will learn to: integrate GenAI into enterprise risk management frameworks; apply stochastic and simulation techniques to quantify uncertainty in model outputs; generate synthetic risk scenarios for stress-testing and capital planning; and design responsible prompt-engineering workflows. Ethical, governance and regulatory considerations—including transparency, bias mitigation and auditability—are embedded throughout. Graduates will be equipped to lead the responsible adoption of GenAI in high-stakes risk management environments.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop responsible prompt-engineering pipelines tailored to risk-management use cases, balancing model performance with compliance requirements.
- Critically assess emerging research and tools in generative AI for risk applications, synthesising findings to inform best practices.
- Evaluate and mitigate ethical, governance, and regulatory risks in GenAI workflows, ensuring transparency, bias control, and auditability.
- Design and execute stochastic simulations and synthetic scenario generators to quantify tail risks and stress-test exposures.
- Integrate generative AI techniques into end-to-end enterprise risk-management frameworks, from risk identification to monitoring
- Communicate complex GenAI-driven risk analyses effectively in professional reports and executive presentations for both technical and non-technical audiences.
