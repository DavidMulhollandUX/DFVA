## Master of Nursing Practice - Mildura
##### HM020M
##### 240 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
Gain the clinical skills and experience you need to become a registered nurse (RN) in just two years with La Trobe's Master of Nursing Practice.

In this degree, you'll build on your existing Bachelor's degree qualification – in any discipline – and develop the clinical knowledge and skills you'll need to upskill or change careers in the health sector. You'll learn the theory from leading clinicians and academics at the La Trobe Rural Health School and then apply it in the real world with clinical placements (800 hours) in leading regional and rural hospitals and healthcare providers.

By studying La Trobe's Master of Nursing Practice, you will:

  * develop foundational knowledge in primary care, acute care, chronic illness, Indigenous health, mental health, rehabilitation, community and emergency care, biosciences and research
  * gain in-demand skills in leadership, time management, critical thinking and communication
  * apply what you've learned in our state-of-the-art nursing simulation labs
  * study closer to home at our Albury-Wodonga, Bendigo and Mildura Campuses
  * graduate ready to support and strengthen your local community as a registered nurse (RN).

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Effectively communicate with colleagues, patients/consumers, and stakeholders.
**2.**
Demonstrate inclusive and responsive professional nursing practice that respects individuals from diverse backgrounds with sensitivity to culture, values, and beliefs.
**3.**
Critically evaluate and integrate their knowledge of biosciences, psychology, public health, and pharmacology with specialist nursing discipline knowledge in the practice of nursing.
**4.**
Analyse and integrate theoretical concepts and frameworks, research evidence, and person-centred care, to inform culturally safe nursing care in a diverse range of settings.
**5.**
Apply clinical reasoning, ethical and critical frameworks to integrate skills, knowledge and problem-solving that is appropriate and promotes health and best evidence care in a diverse range of settings.
**6.**
Plan, administer, and document evidence-based nursing care based on a
comprehensive and systematic assessment and person-centred goals.
**7.**
Function professionally, safely and ethically within scope of practice
**8.**
Engage in reflexive practice to support self-directed, lifelong learning, and maintain personal well-being.
**9.**
Interpret digital health concepts and health informatics systems, and effectively navigate and contribute to the evolution of healthcare technology.

### Course features
### Work based learning (placement) requirements
Compulsory placements (Work Based Learning)
During this course, you will participate in one or more supervised Work Based Learning (WBL) placements designed to allow you to practice newly acquired skills, behaviours and knowledge while integrating theory with practice. The placements will immerse you in professional work culture, enabling you to develop and further enhance your skills. WBL provides you with the opportunity to gain valuable industry experience at one or more of our local, interstate or international industry partners or agencies. WBL placements will generally be arranged by the University based Placements Operation Team or Experiential Learning Team at La Trobe University. The number of hours undertaken can vary considerably, depending on the requirements of the course. Students may incur additional costs and it may be necessary to travel or relocate to complete a specific placement. Please refer to the individual subject entries for more information.
### Work integrated learning opportunities
Industry informed curriculum, Interprofessional learning, Professional simulations
### Career outcomes
Graduate ready for nursing positions in acute, subacute, primary health and community health settings, caring for patients across the lifespan, from paediatrics to the older adult.

As a registered nurse (RN), possible career settings include:

  * clinics and hospitals
  * homes and hospices
  * aged care settings
  * schools and universities
  * community healthcare settings
  * government agencies and private industry.

### Additional costs
Applicants will be required to hold a valid Police Check which is renewed annually. Students will have access to a reduced fee when applying via a La Trobe University provider. For more information on what a police check entails, refer to the Victoria Police website.
First Aid and/or CPR certification is required to meet the professional requirements of the degree. Further information will be provided post enrolment.
Applicants will be required to hold a valid Working with Children Check (WWCC). There is no cost for a volunteer WWCC. For more information and costs refer to the Working with Children Check Victoria website.
Prior to commencing a placement, students will be required to complete a National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS) worker screening check. For information please refer to the Services Victoria website. Further information will be provided post enrolment.
Prior to commencing a placement, students are required to meet all of the requirements of the degree in relation to immunisation. Students must visit a medical practitioner who will complete and sign the Immunisation and Health Record form.

Depending on a students immunisation history, the overall costs for vaccination may be up to AUD$1030. Students are responsible for all costs incurred. Further information will be provided post enrolment.
Clinical placements may incur accommodation, meals and travel costs and these will vary depending on the placement venue (occasionally interstate) and duration. Payment of these costs are the students responsibility.
Students should allow approximately AUD$450 for a uniform, name badge and equipment. Students can purchase these items from any supplier; however La Trobe University branded uniform must be purchased from a university approved vendor. These items remain the student's personal property.
Bridging course fees are additional to course tuition fees and must be paid in full before completion. Details and costs are available here:

  * Stream 1: Bridging Course in Physiology and Anatomy

  * Stream 2: Sociocultural Concepts in Health Care

 Note: Completion of the required bridging course(s) ensures adequate preparation for the program. Requirements and costs depend on your academic background.

Subject: PROFESSIONAL NURSING 1: PROFESSIONAL IDENTITY (NRS5001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will focus on professional nursing practice in Australian healthcare and the development of a professional nursing identity. Through a combination of theoretical, practical, and reflective activities, you will gain a deep understanding of the knowledge, abilities and attitudes required for professional practice as a registered nurse in Australia. You will be guided in constructing your professional identity by fostering situational awareness, self-reflection, and the development of a professional portfolio. By the conclusion of the subject, you will be equipped to navigate the complexities of practice, while upholding nursing standards, ensuring cultural safety, and contributing to the advancement of healthcare outcomes for diverse populations.
Learning Outcomes:
- Examine and identify strategies for self-care, stress management, and burnout prevention for personal well-being.
- Reflect on personal values and beliefs to promote self-awareness and culturally safe nursing care.
- Critically evaluate professional standards, codes, and guidelines for evidenced based nursing practice.
- Classify the roles of nurses and other health professionals within Australian healthcare.
- Establish a professional portfolio as evidence of professional identity development and continuing professional development (CPD).
- Integrate and apply knowledge, skills, and values necessary for safe, quality, person-centred nursing care in the Australian healthcare context.

- Apply ethical theories and principles to real-world interprofessional and nursing scenarios.
- Reflect upon personal values and how these may influence ethical decision making as a registered nurse.
- Examine health-related legislation and ethical frameworks relevant to nursing.
- Integrate legal principles and ethical frameworks into nursing practice.

Subject: CONTEMPORARY STUDIES 2: FIRST NATION PEOPLES HEALTH AND HEALING (NRS5003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will focus on building your capability for working in culturally safe ways with First Nations peoples across the lifespan and within varied healthcare contexts. The subject content integrates key elements of cultural safety for Indigenous peoples, including critical self-reflection, privileging Indigenous knowledges, supporting the right of individuals and groups to self-determination, and addressing racism within the health system. You will examine First Nations understandings of health and healing, and current health outcomes and priorities. The impact of historical, political, and socio-cultural contexts on health outcomes, access, and equity will be analysed. A commitment to improved health outcomes for First Nations peoples will be fostered through the development of advocacy skills that promote social justice and health equity and clinical practice that is holistic, accessible, and safe, free of bias and racism.
Learning Outcomes:
- Review Australian history and analyse the ongoing impacts of colonisation from the perspective of First Nations peoples.
- Critically analyse personal and professional values and beliefs to develop reflexive, culturally safe nursing care for First Nations peoples across the lifespan.
- Demonstrate an understanding of how cultural safety improves healthcare experiences and outcomes for First Nations peoples.
- Critically analyse systemic barriers to health equity and apply decolonising approaches to develop culturally safe practice that encompasses equity and social justice in health.
- Explain First Nations peoples’ concept of health and healing, and critically analyse how cultural, historical, and social determinants interconnect and influence health outcomes of First Nations peoples.
- Examine responses to different forms of racism and evaluate anti-racism strategies that cultivate culturally safe healthcare environments.

- Demonstrate safe quality use of parenteral medicines using the National Safety and Quality Standards Health Service (NSQHS) Medication Safety Standard.
