## Bachelor of Sport and Exercise Science - Melbourne (Bundoora)
##### HBSES
##### 360 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
Help people reach peak performance with La Trobe's Bachelor of Sport and Exercise Science.

You'll gain a detailed understanding of the science behind training, explore the strengths and limitations of the human body, and build strong foundations in physiology, anatomy and biomechanics. You'll use data and scientific principles and techniques to help people build strength, regain movement, prevent injuries and perform at their peak.

By completing La Trobe's Bachelor of Sports and Exercise Science, you will:

  * choose from our recommended majors – human performance, nutrition science and sport and recreation development in Melbourne or food and nutrition,* health promotion and rural health in Bendigo – or tailor your degree with open access majors from a range of disciplines
  * get out of the classroom and on to the field with practical learning and access to our elite athlete performance spaces
  * build real-world experience and connections with 150 placement hours with organisations like Carlton Football Club, Rugby Victoria and Bendigo Spirit
  * join a university of choice for studying and playing sport in Australia – alongside our community delivery partners and major partnerships, we're proudly the official home of the CommBank Matildas
  * graduate ready to launch your career or pursue postgraduate study in areas such as sport analytics, strength and conditioning, and other allied health disciplines.
*The food and nutrition major is offered online only.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Apply theoretical knowledge and practical skills to deliver ethical and professional practice within a range of sport and exercise science environments.
**2.**
Work independently and collaboratively to devise evidence-informed solutions to complex problems in sport and exercise science.
**3.**
Communicate ideas and information effectively through diverse modes and media, ensuring cultural sensitivity in interactions with clients, mentors, and colleagues.
**4.**
Design and safely conduct evidence-informed assessments of human health and function.
**5.**
Critically assess established and emerging trends in sport and exercise science to design exercise and physical activity interventions to improve human performance, health and wellbeing.

### Work based learning (placement) requirements
Compulsory placements (Work Based Learning)
During this course, you will participate in one or more supervised Work Based Learning (WBL) placements designed to allow you to practice newly acquired skills, behaviours and knowledge while integrating theory with practice. The placements will immerse you in professional work culture, enabling you to develop and further enhance your skills. WBL provides you with the opportunity to gain valuable industry experience at one or more of our local, interstate or international industry partners or agencies. WBL placements will generally be arranged by the Office of the Provost Placements and WBL Team at La Trobe University. The number of hours undertaken can vary considerably, depending on the requirements of the course. Students may incur additional costs and it may be necessary to travel or relocate to complete a specific placement. Please refer to the individual subject entries for more information.
### Work integrated learning opportunities
Industry presentations, Industry informed curriculum, Industry-informed projects, Career development learning
### Other opportunities
Student exchange and overseas short programs offer a unique opportunity for a meaningful learning and educational experience abroad. La Trobe University encourages all students to make the world their campus by participating in an exchange or overseas short program that fits within your degree structure.
### Career outcomes
With an ageing population and an increasingly sedentary lifestyle, graduates in the sports and exercise science industry are in high demand. Possible career paths include:

  * sport scientist
  * health and wellness officer
  * personal trainer
  * injury prevention or rehabilitation coach.

If you're considering pursuing an advanced degree, you can extend your expertise and strengthen your career through our Master of Sport Analytics, Master of Strength and Conditioning, Master of Physiotherapy Practice or Master of Exercise Physiology.

Subject: APPLIED HUMAN ANATOMY (ANA2001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, students will build on previously learned anatomical principles and terminology, to cover the applied regional anatomy of the upper limb, lower limb and the trunk (including the vertebral column, as well as the thorax and its role in breathing), and neuroanatomy in relation to sensation and motor control. The concept of integrated function of multiple body systems will be developed in each region. A strong focus on functional anatomy and on applying anatomy to a living body will prepare students for using this knowledge in a professional context.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify musculoskeletal structures through surface anatomy.
- Describe normal structure and function of the central nervous system including neural pathways for sensation and motor control.
- Describe normal structure and function of the musculoskeletal and nervous systems within the upper limb, lower limb, and trunk (vertebral column, and the thorax in relation to mechanisms of respiration).
- Analyse movements and exercises in terms of muscle activation and function, and joint range of motion.
- Apply observational and communication skills, using appropriate anatomical and lay terminology for use with clients or other health care professionals, to make accurate observations of anatomical structures or events and infer their relationship to function using cadaveric material, the living body or medical imaging.

Subject: SPORT AND EXERCISE PSYCHOLOGY (PHE3SEP)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will focus on the scope, aims and techniques of sport and exercise psychology. The scope of sport psychology is vast and expanding and goes beyond the sporting field. Sport and exercise psychology aims to enhance both performance and wellbeing through the application of psychological principles. This field goes beyond the goal of winning, focusing on improving various aspects of life, including sport, fitness, health and overall enjoyment. This subject combines theory with practical application, employing cognitive-behavioural techniques to boost motivation, reduce competition anxiety, foster teamwork and manage stress in various areas of life such as work, study and family. It also addresses issues such as injury management, promoting physical activity, substance use awareness, working with diverse populations and navigating life transitions. This subject is designed for third-year students. To maximise your chances of success in achieving the intended learning outcomes, it is recommended that you complete this subject in your third- year.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse performance scenarios and identify tailored solutions to influence positive behaviours in sport and exercise settings.
- Demonstrate an understanding of the psychological influences on injury risk, rehabilitation, and performance to improve outcomes for individuals and multi-disciplinary teams.
- Collaborate as part of a team to analyse the impact of psychological factors on athlete performance, and develop an evidence-based intervention to improve performance.
- Understand and apply Sport and Exercise Psychology theories and evidence to increase performance in sport and exercise settings.

Subject: GROWTH, DEVELOPMENT AND NUTRITION (SPE1002)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will be introduced to foundational concepts of nutrition, growth, and development in sport and exercise science. The subject will provide you with an understanding of growth and development from prenatal life through to older adulthood, factors that influence these processes, and key considerations relevant to each life stage. Additionally, you will be taught the core concepts of human nutrition, including basic nutritional assessment, the components of human nutrition and diet, and their relationship to health and exercise. The subject aims to develop your knowledge and application of how physical activity influences (and is influenced by) growth and development across the lifespan and the role of nutrition in sport and exercise science.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the strengths and limitations of commonly used methods for measuring and analysing dietary intake including implications for general health and wellbeing.
- Describe the stages of growth and development throughout the lifespan, and the factors that affect these.
- Identify and describe the basic function and common sources of macronutrients and micronutrients including their role in energy balance and health.
- Explain the relationship between physical activity and the structural, physiological, motor and cognitive changes occurring throughout the lifespan.
- Use peer-reviewed literature and evidence-based physical activity and exercise principles to interpret lifespan case studies.
- Understand the scope of practice for exercise scientists and use current Australian guidelines to provide general advice on nutrition as appropriate or refer onwards as appropriate.

Subject: BIOMECHANICS AND MOVEMENT ANALYSIS (SPE2BMA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will be introduced to the biomechanical principles of human movement. - Apply principles of functional anatomy and biomechanics to critically analyse movement patterns and identify strategies to enhance performance, identify deficiencies, and minimise injury risk.
- Apply biomechanical principles to the analysis of human movement.
- Describe biomechanical principles and their relationship to human movement.
