## Career Opportunities & Employment Outcomes
Graduates of the School's programs have always enjoyed an excellent reputation among translation and interpreting agencies and clients for their market-readiness and high level of professionalism. The GCAI, with its heightened emphasis on digital translation and interpreting technology trends, will further add to the marketability of our graduates in an increasingly globalised market. The program is NAATI endorsed. Students will need to seek NAATI accreditation to become a Certified Interpreter.

Subject: Professional Skills for Translators and Interpreters (TRIN7120)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course focusses on core preparation skills for professional translators and interpreters. Students will acquire the skills to effectively research for translation and interpreting assignments, including analysing and organising information. Concordance tools, electronic corpora and glossary building will also be covered, in addition to professional ethics, business skills, networking and how to adapt to market demands. Topics covered will include government, international organisations, foreign affairs, social policy and the environment.
Learning Outcomes:
- develop a solid understanding of the core skills required for professional translators and interpreters.
- become equipped with the knowledge and tools necessary to conduct research for translation and interpreting assignments.
- effectively build and manage glossaries and navigate complex topics.
- develop an understanding of professional ethics, business skills and networking for translators and interpreters.
Assessment Tasks:
- Three Reflections Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Professional & ethical case analysis Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Essay about Australian society/culture Online: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Ad Hoc Workshop 2 Hours/ Week One 2 hour class (lecture or workshop) held per week.

Subject: Interpreting Skills and Practice (TRIN7130)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course aims to introduce students to the basic skills of consecutive interpreting. Students will study the preparation, writing and delivery of short, set-time speeches on a wide variety of topics, thus familiarising them with the type of language and set expressions they may encounter when working as interpreters. Emphasis will be placed on content, structure, delivery and non-verbal skills. Note-taking will be introduced so students can learn to take effective notes in actual interpreting situations.
Learning Outcomes:
- be equipped with the English and LOTE language skills required for a public speaking situation and prepare and effectively deliver short speeches in English and LOTE for a variety of scenarios with minimum reference to notes.
- use basic skills in performing dialogue interpreting on non-technical topics and skilfully manage the interpreting situation, including in basic remote dialogue interpreting settings.
- take effective notes to aid consecutive interpreting on general topics.
Assessment Tasks:
- Consecutive Interpreting of Short Speech Segments Exam Identity Verified In-person Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- In-semester Dialogue Interpreting Exam Identity Verified In-person Online: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- Final Dialogue Interpreting Exam Identity Verified In-person Online: 60%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 3 Hours/ Ad Hoc Tutorial 3 Hours/ Week Workshop 3 Hours/ Ad Hoc One 3 hour class (lecture, tutorial or workshop) held per week.

Subject: Translation and Interpreting in Community Settings (TRIN7220)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course further consolidates students' translation and interpreting skills for community and liaison settings. Topics to be covered include general health, legal and education. Sight translation and whispering will be introduced, in addition to the most common forms of remote interpreting including telephone and video interpreting. A WIL component will be included.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop an understanding of translation and interpreting practice in community settings.
- Develop skills and strategies to effectively comprehend non-specialised texts in the source language and produce translations that conform to the standards of appropriateness expected in community settings.
- Develop skills and strategies to effectively manage interpreting situations and deliver a reasonably polished and accurate interpretation in dialogue interpreting and sight translation modes on topics commonly arising in community settings.
- Have a good understanding of the roles and responsibilities of T&I professionals and demonstrate appropriate professional conduct in a work environment.
Assessment Tasks:
- Practical weekly homework Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Practical sight translation assessment Identity Verified In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Monologue interpreting Identity Verified In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Tutorial 3 Hours/ Week

Subject: Consecutive Interpreting (TRIN7230)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course builds on the interpreting skills introduced in semester 1, focussing on consecutive interpreting for formal speeches of the type likely to arise in a professional context, such as business and official governmental settings. Skills to be introduced include memory skills, logical analysis and dealing with numbers, dates and names. Note-taking skills will be further consolidated. Over the course of the semester the length and content of the source speeches will progress from entry-level standard (short, commonly encountered speeches on general topics) to intermediate level (on non-technical topics). Topics to be covered include government, business and seminars.
Learning Outcomes:
- Master essential skills for consecutive interpreting, including listening and analysis, short-term memory skills, note-taking skills, target language production, and dealing with numbers, dates and proper nouns.
- Use the consecutive mode to interpret source speeches that vary from entry-level standard (short, commonly encountered speeches on general topics) to intermediate level (on non-technical topics) in professional contexts such as business, official governmental settings, and seminars and produce highly accurate and effective interpretations.
Assessment Tasks:
- In-semester Interpreting Examination Identity Verified In-person Online: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Final Interpreting Examination Identity Verified In-person Online: 70%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Contact 3 Hours/ Week
