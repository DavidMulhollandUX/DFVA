Learn from highly experienced teachers with an industry-connected curriculum designed in consultation with industry partners and delivered by La Trobe's award-winning School of Education*. You'll gain the skills and knowledge to support the next generation of pre-school children develop foundational skills across social, emotional, physical and academic areas.
Explore key areas of early childhood education through core subjects exploring early childhood development, assessment and reporting, teaching strategies, and safety and ethics as you put your knowledge to work in the real world with work-based learning, integrated placement and an embedded professional mentoring program.
You'll graduate ready to start your career in early childhood education environments or advance directly into La Trobe’s Master of Education, further preparing you for leadership or specialist positions within the sector.
By studying La Trobe's Graduate Diploma in Early Childhood Education, you'll learn:
  * **Foundations of child development**  

    * Investigate the social, emotional and educational needs of children and gain a core understanding of child development and evidence-based pedagogies to develop teaching strategies.
  * **Early childhood leadership**
    * Explore the complexities of early childhood leadership, such as policy, legal requirements, political influences, professional ethics and operational management.
  * **Theories of cognition and play**
    * Examine the role of fundamental role of play and play-based learning in the development of skills in science, creativity, literacy development and mathematical thinking.
  * **Assessment and reporting**
    * Investigate the authentic use of assessment in early childhood education, including examining the way that assessment is used to inform understanding.

Course features 
### 
Work based learning (placement) requirements 
During this course, you will participate in one or more supervised Work Based Learning (WBL) placements designed to allow you to practice newly acquired skills, behaviours and knowledge while integrating theory with practice. The placements will immerse you in professional work culture, enabling you to develop and further enhance your skills. WBL provides you with the opportunity to gain valuable industry experience at one or more of our local, interstate or international industry partners or agencies. WBL placements will generally be arranged by the Office of the Provost Placements and WBL Team at La Trobe University. The number of hours undertaken can vary considerably, depending on the requirements of the course. Students may incur additional costs and it may be necessary to travel or relocate to complete a specific placement. Please refer to the individual subject entries for more information.
### 
Career outcomes 
After graduation, students will be eligible for teacher accreditation as early childhood educators and may find employment across private, independent, or public education.
Possible roles include:
  * Early childhood teacher
  * Family day care educator
  * Early childhood education service manager


The Graduate Diploma in Early Childhood Education is a pathway into the Master of Education, preparing graduates further for leadership or specialist positions within the early childhood education sector.
### 
Professional recognition 
The Graduate Diploma in Early Childhood Education is approved by the Australian Children's Education & Care Quality Authority (ACECQA). Individuals should contact their relevant state/territory [teacher regulatory authority](https://www.acecqa.gov.au/qualifications-0/early-childhood-teacher-registration-and-accreditation) prior to application. This will help determine the suitability of this course and the registration requirements of the state/territory you plan to work in.

Core participation requirements 
Expand all
#### Behavioural and relational skills
_keyboard_arrow_down_
_**Core requirement**_  
  
Students must be able to manage their own responses and behaviour to work effectively in a range of learning environments with diverse people and tasks. This includes the capacity to be receptive and respond appropriately to constructive feedback. Students should also be able to work with others in a team or group environment and make and maintain effective relationships with a wide range of individuals in order to complete tasks or for professional practice.  
  
See also [Student behaviours policy](https://policies.latrobe.edu.au/document/view.php?id=60) for more information.  
  
_**Course-specific requirement**_  
  
Early Childhood Education students must demonstrate strong interpersonal communication skills that will allow them to manage learning environments and continuously develop their expertise during placements and throughout their careers.  
Students must be able to manage personal responses, beliefs or preferences such that they can work with diverse groups, for example they must be able to work with students of the opposite sex and students with different sexual orientations.
#### Sensory (visual, auditory, tactile, olfaction)
_keyboard_arrow_down_
_**Core requirement**_  
  
Students should possess the ability to interact with visual, auditory or tactile inputs sufficiently to manage their learning environment and to meet professional performance needs.  
  
_**Course-specific requirement**_  
  
Early Childhood Education students must be able to process visual information and monitor the broader environment for child safety, including observing multiple people and events simultaneously. 
#### Strength and mobility
_keyboard_arrow_down_
_**Core requirement**_  
  
Gross motor skills: Students should possess the strength, range of motion, coordination and mobility sufficient to manage their learning environment and ultimately, professional performance needs.  
  
Fine motor skills: Students should possess the manual dexterity and fine motor skills sufficient to manage their learning environment and ultimately, professional performance needs.  
  
_**Course-specific requirement**_  
  
Early Childhood Education students must possess sufficient physical capacity to undertake common tasks such as lifting, carrying, pushing, pulling, standing, twisting and bending. Students must be able to demonstrate and perform these tasks consistently and safely to reduce the risk of harm to self and others.
#### Cognition
_keyboard_arrow_down_
_**Core requirement**_  
  
Students must be able to locate, acquire, retain and apply knowledge as part of their learning and for assessment. They must have the ability to sustain their attention over a designated period, maintain their focus in a variety of learning environments and remember information long enough to complete tasks in a reasonable and safe timeframe.  
  
_**Course-specific requirement**_  
  
Early Childhood Education students must be able to use retained knowledge to determine safe and appropriate care decisions.

Subject: SCIENCE, TECHNOLOGY AND PLAY (EDU4034)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, preservice teachers examine how learning environments are designed to support young children to develop their understandings and skills in science and technology through play. Preservice teachers learn to develop curriculum to facilitate problem solving and inquiry learning using social constructivist, cognitive science, educational psychology, neuroscience and place-based theories. The content will be informed by core concepts in science, technology, play and Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander knowledges in conjunction with requirements of relevant state and national curriculum documents. Preservice teachers will engage with real world examples to explore evidence-based pedagogies and develop teaching strategies to convey science concepts to children in culturally responsive, meaningful, and engaging ways. This subject contains 15 days of placement.
Learning Outcomes:
- Synthesise research-informed theories to design learning environments, engagement and feedback strategies that support inquiry-based science and technology learning.
- Critically evaluate inquiry-based planning, pedagogy, and assessment practices that support inclusive and high-quality science and technology learning.
- Design and evaluate learning and teaching experiences in connection with authentic assessment tools with informed understanding of the concepts, skills, and capabilities important to science and technology curriculum streams.
- Evaluate curriculum and pedagogical decisions in relation to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander knowledges, including Country, community, and history.
- Outline and apply knowledge of key science and technology concepts, and evidence-based pedagogical approaches to play-based learning in early childhood contexts.

Subject: SAFE AND ETHICAL EARLY CHILDHOOD SETTINGS (EDU4035)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, preservice teachers will establish core understandings about how to provide children with safe and ethical early childhood settings. This includes learning about child safety, child development, and how to use established principles of cognitive science and contemporary behavioural psychology to design learning experiences for children across age ranges. Pre-service teachers will consider the importance of family, culture and context to ensure that early childhood settings are culturally safe and inclusive. Pre-service teachers will begin to investigate early childhood curriculum, including the interconnections between learning, development and play, and how these provide a context for designing learning experiences. The implications of policy, curriculum and quality are considered in connection with curriculum approaches. An emphasis on translating these principles into practice will enable students to make connections between ideas and early childhood teaching.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse how early childhood curriculum promotes cultural safety, children’s learning and agency in accordance with National Quality Standards and Early Years Learning Framework.
- Document and analyse learning information to inform inclusive teaching practices and build meaningful, safe relationships with children, families, and colleagues.
- Evaluate and apply relevant theories from the learning sciences and child development to analyse early learning and development in diverse contexts .
- Critically examine growth, safety, development, learning, and play in children from birth to five, and apply this to ethical early childhood teaching practice.

Subject: LANGUAGE, LITERACIES AND PLAY (EDU4036)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject pre-service teachers explore opportunities for engaging with children’s literacies in early childhood. The fundamentals of language development and literacy learning are explored from various perspectives including cognitive, developmental and cultural lenses. Pre-service teachers will reflect on the way that theory, development, and pedagogy translate into the intentional way that they work with children. Literacy curriculum for young children is explored, including the Early Years Learning Framework V2.0, the Victorian Early Years Learning & Development Framework, State/Territory interpretations and the Victorian/Australian Curriculum documents. Students will learn to identify early literacy concepts, skills, and resources, and will engage with the notions of multimodal and multiliteracies, and cross curriculum connections. Students will consider the crucial role of culture and families in emergent language and literacy and begin to explore their own developing pedagogy.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and apply research-informed language and literacy learning processes for children from birth to 5 years.
- Select, adapt and evaluate a range of culturally responsive, multimodal, and play-based strategies to support young children’s language and literacy development, including children with English as an Additional Language
- Critically evaluate theoretical perspectives and concepts relevant to language and literacy teaching for children from birth to 5 years
- Demonstrate respectful and informed engagement with First Nations language diversity, including the selection and integration of appropriate local Indigenous language and knowledge into early years practice.
- Research and develop appropriate and effective learning experiences and pedagogy, including play based and intentional teaching, to shape literacy and language curriculum for children from birth to 5 years of age, in line with relevant curriculum documents.
Workload:

Subject: EARLY CHILDHOOD ASSESSMENT AND REPORTING (EDU5036)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject investigates the authentic use of assessment in early childhood education. Preservice teachers examine the way that assessment is used to inform their understanding of the child, to identify intent behind supports needed, pedagogy, curriculum and making children’s learning and development visible. The purpose of assessment is critiqued and considered in connection to culture, the view of the child, curriculum practices and inclusion. Collaboration with families and colleagues are positioned as integral in developing rich authentic assessment practices and in showcasing assessment leadership. This subject contains 20 days of placement.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate leadership in assessment practice by aligning assessment philosophy, curriculum, and pedagogy in professional contexts.
- Document, interpret, plan, and reflect on children’s learning by observing and analysing their emergent interests, development, and capabilities across relevant domains.
- Apply assessment-aligned curriculum decisions to support children’s learning, and demonstrate leadership in equitable and aspirational teaching.
- Select and justify the use of authentic, everyday assessment practices and tools aligned with early childhood curriculum frameworks.
- Outline and justify a relevant, reliable, and research-informed pedagogical philosophy for understanding children’s learning and development that includes varied tools and strategies.
- Critically evaluate and apply the purposes and implications of assessment in early childhood education focussed on collaboration with First Nations peoples, families, colleagues, and inclusion professionals.
Workload:

Subject: EARLY CHILDHOOD LEADERSHIP (EDU5037)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, pre-service teachers will examine the significance and complexities of early childhood leadership. Connections will be made with professional practice, ethical practice and leadership aspirations. Issues that shape early childhood leadership, such as policy, legal requirements, political influences, professional ethics, and operational management, are explored and implications for leadership practice are addressed. Additionally, pre-service teachers will engage in leadership research, developing a small-scale research project focused on transforming practice through leadership. The insights from the research project will be used to inform future leadership growth and development.
Learning Outcomes:
- Synthesise and communicate research findings to diverse audiences to support leadership development and professional discourse
- Design and conduct teacher research in connection with an aspect of early childhood leadership.
- Critically evaluate the significance and complexities of leadership in early childhood contexts.
- Apply and justify leadership decisions in early childhood practice using relevant policy, legislation, ethical frameworks, operational principles, and research.
- Develop future leadership aspirations based on the research process including implications for future practice.

Subject: MATHEMATICS, NUMERACY AND PLAY (EDU5038)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, preservice teachers will explore pedagogies important to facilitating numeracy understandings in early childhood education. As part of this exploration the interconnection of mathematics and numeracy will be deconstructed, particularly the way that numbers, patterns, measurement, time, spatial awareness, chance, data, and mathematical thinking can be supported within play-based learning. Preservice teachers will think critically about the intentionality in observing, facilitating, planning for and assessing mathematics and numeracy learning and development. Drawing from contemporary approaches to supporting numeracy and mathematical development and in consultation with Australian curriculum frameworks preservice teachers will develop trialed resources to support practice. This subject includes 25 days of placement.
Learning Outcomes:
- Design and evaluate intentional provocations that respond to children’s interests and support mathematical learning through interdisciplinary approaches, including ICT, the arts, nature play, and literacy.
- Analyse and explain numeracy and mathematics learning processes in young children, drawing on relevant research and theoretical perspectives.
- Critically analyse pedagogical and assessment strategies that support numeracy and mathematical learning in early childhood.
- Research and design innovative numeracy and mathematics learning experiences and intentional teaching strategies aligned with relevant curriculum documents and play-based pedagogies.
- Evaluate mathematics learning and teaching resources that support play-based learning in early childhood contexts.

Subject: THE ARTS AND PLAY (EDU5039)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Preservice teachers (PSTs) will explore how young children learn in and through the arts. They will become familiar with pedagogies and practices across the visual arts, 3-dimensional arts, music, creative movement/dance, and drama. PSTs will engage in and critically reflect upon their own creative processes, with reference to arts-based theories, practices, and research. They will create an informed teaching resource that connects their learning in this subject with their work-based experience.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe and justify impactful arts-based pedagogies with reference to relevant literature
- Design, implement and critically reflect on teaching support for engaging and educational arts experiences for young children.
- Critically analyse and apply arts education pedagogies and practices to support Early Childhood Education.
- Design, implement, and critically reflect on arts-based learning experiences that align with early childhood curriculum and children’s developmental needs.

Subject: ACADEMIC INTEGRITY MODULE (LTU0AIM)
Credit Points: 0
Overview: The academic integrity module introduces academic integrity standards to inform and support students in avoiding plagiarism and academic misconduct. Students complete four parts that encompass academic misconduct and academic integrity decisions, including cheating, plagiarism, and collusion. Students learn about Turnitin (the text-matching tool) used at La Trobe University, and how to get help and support their development of referencing skills.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the importance of the acknowledgment and the referencing of sources
- Locate, select, and utilise information about La Trobe's approach to academic integrity to support appropriate acknowledgement of references used.
- Recognise and understand academic integrity responsibilities.
