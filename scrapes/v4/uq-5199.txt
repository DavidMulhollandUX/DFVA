## Career Opportunities & Employment Outcomes
As a graduate, you can work in varied and challenging roles in business, the public-sector and the community. The main areas for employment include:
selection and recruitment
training and development
organisational development and change
job design, ergonomics, measurement and evaluation
academic teaching and research.
The work of an organisational psychologist revolves around the development of an organisation's human resources to enhance motivation, performance and satisfaction. As a graduate, you may be involved in developing effective selection systems, designing and delivering training programs, introducing organisational change, designing jobs and organisational structures, facilitating the development of teams and training and developing executives, managers and supervisors.

This program prepares students for organisational psychology practice, focusing on building skills such as critical analysis, problem solving, communication, consultation and organisational change skills. Organisational psychology seeks knowledge of behaviour in organisational settings by systematically studying individual, group and organisational processes. Organisational psychology employs a 'scientist-practitioner' method of enquiry, first to seek understanding of behaviour within employment contexts, and then to develop techniques and skills which foster employee well-being and organisational effectiveness.

Subject: Occupational Health & Safety Management Systems (OHSS7005)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course has been designed to provide students with high level skills and knowledge to develop, implement, evaluate and manage OHS Management Systems. The course also prepares students to undertake OHS related audits by evaluating and utilizing a variety of audit tools and criteria. Using a combination of underpinning theory and current practice the course will explore the various aspects of OHS management systems; including organisational theory, behaviour based safety, management system frameworks, the history of OHS management systems and future directions.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the role and the limitations of occupational health and safety (OHS) management systems and OHS theories to a range of stakeholders.
- Critically analyse and apply knowledge of systems and systems thinking to develop elements of an OHS management system in accordance with relevant standards and organisational requirements.
- Develop performance indicators and explain to key stakeholders the rationale underpinning their development.
- Develop processes to monitor and evaluate the impact of business, technology and industrial factors or conditions on OHS and OHS-related decision-making, ensuring decisions are made based on accurate data.
- Make recommendations for criteria to measure OHS performance and develop processes to monitor and evaluate the effectiveness of an OHS management system, including audits.
- Critically analyse and apply concepts of safety culture and the role of leaders in creating culture to develop strategies to influence the organisational culture and resultant decision-making affecting OHS.
- Motivate and lead others within a team of OHS personnel, encouraging and empowering others.
- Develop team building and teamwork skills during Group Work activities
Assessment Tasks:
- OHS Management Case Study: 20%% (Hurdle: False)
- OHS Management System Audit Report Team or group-based: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- End of Semester Exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Contact 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Adult Psychopathology (PSYC7113)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course provides an exploration of psychological disorders, with a strong focus on diagnostic systems, assessment methods, and understanding of treatment approaches. Through both theoretical and applied learning, students will develop a thorough and critical understanding of widely used classificatory systems, and gain hands-on experience in clinical interviewing, mental status examinations, and diagnostic assessments when working with adults. Special emphasis is placed on the biopsychosocial approach to diagnosis, case formulation and treatment planning, considering developmental, cultural, and ethical factors. Throughout the course, students will engage in critical analysis of the research evidence-base, fostering a reflexive and evidence-informed approach to their professional development.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply and critically evaluate diagnostic classification systems and their role in diagnosing psychological disorders in adults.
- Demonstrate competency in clinical assessment techniques.
- Apply biopsychosocial approaches to diagnostic case formulation and evidence-based interventions.
- Understand the psychosocial and pharmacological management for common psychological disorders.
- Understand how to apply ethical principles, cultural considerations when working with diverse populations, and reflective/reflexive practice to formulation and communication of clinical findings in multidisciplinary team settings (relevant to accurate documentation, case report writing, and verbal presentation of case summaries).
- Understand the impact of inequitable access to healthcare from a human rights perspective and its implications for working with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples.
- Critically engage with the research evidence base to inform clinical decision-making and competence in all (including digital) environments to foster ongoing professional development in psychological practice.
Assessment Tasks:
- Role play: Client Descriptions, Formulations and Reflection Hurdle: Pass/Fail Recycle% (Hurdle: True)
- Written Case Study Report: 40%% (Hurdle: False)
- Indigenous Health Modules and Reflection: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Exam Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 45%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Lecture 2 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Psychological Skills & Evidence-Based Therapies (PSYC7114)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This foundational course introduces students to evidence-based therapeutic approaches and the core psychological skills needed to apply them. It covers fundamental concepts of intake and risk assessment, case planning and treatment, and covers Cognitive Behaviour Therapy (CBT), Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), trauma-informed care, and Motivational Interviewing. Students will develop key therapeutic skills for case formulation, risk assessment, and intervention planning while integrating research evidence into psychological practice. Emphasis in working across diverse populations, including Indigenous peoples, and considering the social determinants of mental health. Through theoretical learning, case studies, observation, modelling, role-playing, and practical applications, students will gain competencies and a strong foundation in evidence-based psychological practices.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the foundations of evidence-based therapy and demonstrate knowledge of core principles in psychological interventions, that include face to face and digital applications
- Develop therapeutic skills and apply foundational therapeutic strategies in practice
- Perform psychological risk assessments: assess and manage suicide risk and other psychological risks effectively.
- Develop structured case formulations and treatment plans using evidence-based principles
- Engage in self-reflection for professional practice and self-care
- Apply culturally responsive practices in their work with clients from culturally and linguistically diverse and Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander backgrounds.
Assessment Tasks:
- Intake interview and self-reflection Hurdle: Pass/Recycle/Fail% (Hurdle: True)
- Risk Assessment Competency Hurdle In-person: 20%% (Hurdle: True)
- Skill Demonstration and Clinical Reasoning Competency Hurdle: 50% Each component is weighted 25%% (Hurdle: True)
- Self-Practice/Self-reflection Tasks Hurdle: 30%% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Workshop 3 Hours/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week Workshop 8 Hours/ Semester

Subject: Business Skills for Consultants (PSYC7405)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The aim of this course is to develop the knowledge and skills that a consultant needs to work effectively in an organisational context. Issues addressed include consulting models; consulting in different business contexts; organisational systems & functions; diagnosis, intervention & evaluation; budgeting; project management; negotiation & conflict management; strategic thinking and judgment; report writing; and presentation skills. The course is structured using an action-learning approach. Students work in teams on a series of case studies throughout the semester under the supervision and guidance of an experienced consultant. This involves assessing the needs of the client, defining the scope of the project, developing a project plan (including milestones and deliverables), developing a budget, preparing the proposal or tender, and making a pitch to the client.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand diagnostic and analysis tools that a business consultant needs to address organisational problems including appropriate scoping, process mapping and stakeholder management
- Conceptualise, design and conduct interventions and evaluations showing practical application of business consultancy skills and knowledge of psychological science through experience in practical case studies.
- Understand and demonstrate skills in business communications, contracting and evaluation to support the end to end consultant process and show professional skills
- Understand some of the key contemporary topics in business psychology
Assessment Tasks:
- Scoping report: 15%% (Hurdle: False)
- Group Presentation: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Case Study report: 55%% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 3 Hours/ Week 3L

Subject: Advanced Organisational Psychology (PSYC7454)
Credit Points: 2

- Plan and appraise implementation and evaluation strategies (e.g., logic models, KPIs, process/outcome measures).
- Communicate diagnostic insights, recommendations, and evaluation plans in professional written and oral formats tailored to diverse stakeholders, using culturally appropriate language.
- Demonstrate ethical, collaborative, and competency-aware practice (e.g., record-keeping, consultation/referral, and self-care).

Subject: Organisational Change: Theory & Practice (PSYC7464)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The aim of this course is to overview the theory and practice of organisational change. Particular attention will be paid to psychological concepts that facilitate an understanding of organisational change at three levels of analysis (ie the individual, the group and the organisation). The course will address issues that arise at different stages of the change management process.
Learning Outcomes:
- Critically evaluate contemporary psychological theories and empirical research relevant to organisational change, development, and management.
- Apply advanced psychological knowledge of organisational paradigms, including culture change, health and well-being, organisational systems, and workforce performance, to the diagnosis and conceptualisation of organisational problems.
- Apply evidence-informed methods to the assessment of organisational and individual functioning, drawing on multiple sources and appropriate methodologies to generate coherent case conceptualisations of presenting organisational issues.
- Select, design, and justify psychological or structural interventions appropriate to organisational and individual-level problems, taking into account social, political, and economic contextual factors that influence workplace design and workforce well-being.
- Interpret and communicate findings in oral and written formats using culturally appropriate language, including the preparation and delivery of a professional presentation and a structured project proposal that addresses the diagnosis, planning, implementation, and evaluation of an organisational change intervention.
- Demonstrate ethical decision-making and self-reflective practice in the context of organisational psychology and change, including recognition of the boundaries of professional competence, consultation with appropriate others, and critical awareness of the impact of one's own values and assumptions on practice.
Assessment Tasks:
- Organisational Change Diagnosis and Assessment Hurdle In-person: 40%% (Hurdle: True)

Subject: Advanced Personnel Assessment (PSYC7494)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: The aim of this course is to develop an understanding of advanced personnel assessment issues, from both theoretical and applied perspectives. It assumes that students have knowledge of principles of measurement and psychometrics and theories and techniques of selection in an occupational setting. The course pays particular attention to psychological testing (cognitive ability, personality, and vocational interests), and behavioural assessment (job interviewing, work samples, and multi-method strategies associated with assessment centres). It also explores contemporary issues in recruitment, selection, and appraisal that offer special challenges to the personnel practitioner.
Learning Outcomes:
- Define and explain the six principles of personnel selection including job relevancy, reliability, validity, fairness, applicability, and cost.
- Identify and critically evaluate the empirical literature on the effectiveness of personnel selection techniques as predictors of job performance.
- Demonstrate practical skills in the administration, scoring, and interpretation of individually-administered tests of cognitive ability, personality, and memory.
- Demonstrate practical skills in conducting behavioural assessment activities for the purposes of personnel selection.
- Explain the legal environment context in which personnel selection systems and techniques in the Australian context operate.
- Recognise and reflect on interprofessional practice skills including role clarification, team functioning, interprofessional communication, conflict management, collaborative leadership, and client-centred care.

Subject: Public Health Psychology (PSYC8017)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Public health psychology encompasses the critical application of psychology at a population level. It focuses on maintenance of well-being from a perspective that considers the influence of systemic and economic factors on individual behaviour and well-being, and acknowledges the effects of gender, ethnicity, age, and other sociocultural factors on individual choice. It uses lectures and discussion to provide in-depth knowledge and ethical and social understanding of the issues.
Learning Outcomes:
- Possess an in-depth understanding of the field of public health psychology, the principles, and some current issues.
- Approach and assess psychological and health-related issues from a critical perspective.
- Understand the role of systemic, economic, and sociocultural factors such as age, gender, and ethnicity, on individual choice and behaviour, and the implications for public health.
- Discuss population-level health inequities within the context of political, institutional, economic, and other sociocultural factors.
- Apply the principles and application of public health psychology to promote health, and address population-level health inequities and issues.
- Possess research and written skills to work in the area of health promotion, health-related research, and health policy formation.
- Analyse and deconstruct the impact of historical, cultural, and social determinants on the health of First Nations Australians and the importance of cultural responsiveness in public health.
- Adapt communication style and content to persuade diverse stakeholders and audiences.

Subject: Performance Psychology for Coaches (SPCG7023)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Coaches, athletes, and sport administrators now recognise that physical talent alone does not guarantee success. It is the "mental game" that often differentiates performers, especially at the elite level. The role of a high performance coach has evolved beyond instruction in technical, tactical, and physical skills to that which now includes the application of psychological principles to enhancing performance. In this course, students are introduced to the field of performance psychology and will become knowledgeable in training, practice, and research skills that will enable them to get the best out of themselves and others. There is emphasis on both theoretical understanding and personal practice in developing the student's knowledge and application of areas including: learning styles; basic neuroscience; emotion and emotional intelligence; leadership; performance culture; passion; mindset; solution-focused coaching; mindfulness; and acceptance-commitment therapy. With the primary focus of this course being applied in nature, a core outcome of this unit is the development of an individualised program that can be readily implemented into the student's current or future practice. Each seminar has a lecture component and an experiential learning component, which requires students to evaluate each topic in relation to their own personal life experiences and to participate in group discussion and coaching practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate the ability to clearly convey ideas about psychological theories and applied practice relevant to elite performance in sport.
- Demonstrate the ability to critically reflect upon personal development as a coach as well as coaching practices.
- Demonstrate the ability to retrieve, analyse, synthesise, and critically evaluate information.
- Select strategies and approaches to establish, integrate, and manage a performance psychology program in a coaching environment.
- Demonstrate an understanding of the role that psycho-social variables play in enhancing performance.
