Subject: Biomaterials: Materials in Medicine (BIOE4305)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Introduction to biomaterials science, artificial organs, prosthetic devices, regulatory testing and evaluation of new biomaterials. Polymeric, ceramic, metallic and composite biomaterials.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate a knowledge of biomaterial requirements in medical devices, understand the properties of biomaterials used in medical devices, and be able to engineer solutions for the use of biomaterials in medical devices.
- Conduct a detailed scientific literature search and critique the literature to generate a report of the historical and current state of play of research.
- Conduct a comprehensive IP search and generate an insightful report of IP relating to a biomaterial or medical device.
- Demonstrate effective spoken and written communication to a non-scientifically literate audience, or an audience with diverse backgrounds.
- Manage personal independent research and learning through pro-active and responsible use of resources such as online course material, library databases and internet.
- Analyse scenarios and develop solutions to multidisciplinary biomaterials problems.
- Work and cooperate effectively in a team consisting of students from a range of academic disciplines and personality types.
- Demonstrate an understanding of the social and ethical context of biomaterials in society that set medical related biomaterials apart from other material engineering disciplines.
Assessment Tasks:
- Quizzes 1 & 2 Hurdle Identity Verified: 30% Individual (Hurdle*)% (Hurdle: True)
- Prac Reports: 20% Individual% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Metabolic Engineering (BIOE6028)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Systems based analysis and design of biological systems for the production of valuable products. Assumed background: (i) understanding of biochemistry and microbiology equivalent to BIOE1001/CHEE1001, (ii) understanding of bioprocesses equivalent to BIOE4020/CHEE4020, (iii) basic computer skills.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate bio-based processes and apply advanced design and optimisation strategies towards a wide range of bioprocess applications.
- Interrelate knowledge of cellular metabolism and molecular biology with quantitative approaches for regulating cell function to achieve a desired purpose.
- Develop and apply metabolic modelling procedures to analyse metabolic networks.
- Interface between metabolic engineering, systems biology and synthetic biology, with an ability to cross-communicate within the bioscience, biotechnology and bioengineering fields.

Subject: Advanced Process and System Modelling (CHEE7111)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course builds on CHEE3007 Process Modelling and Dynamics and considers a range of techniques required to model complex and unfamiliar processes. Students use advanced features of relevant modelling and programming software. Large scale experimental data from processes or experiments are used to build 'data driven' models for a range of applications. Following this, students integrate data-driven and mechanistic models to develop and apply hybrid modelling to model industrial or experimental processes. The projects are derived from current industry applications.
Learning Outcomes:
- Clean, tidy, explore, visualize, analyze and develop models to provide insight into large industrial datasets
- Explain the opportunities and challenges for advanced modelling approaches in a wide range of system applications, across research, design and operations
- Evaluate, select and apply appropriate modelling methodologies for particular outcomes, including both mechanistic and empirical models, and hybrid systems
- Critically assess the validity of the assumptions underpinning your model, using data, logic and mechanistic knowledge; assess whether the model is fit for purpose; evaluate the reliability of model output; identify what information is required to improve confidence in model predictions
- Apply critical thinking skills to analyze, synthesize and effectively communicate technical information
- Demonstrate professional conduct in engaging with industry, including an appreciation of sustainability
- Improve teamwork skills through practice, reflection, feedback and change
Assessment Tasks:
- Non-Disclosure Agreement: Compulsory% (Hurdle: False)
- Individual exercises Hurdle Online: 20% Hurdle% (Hurdle: True)
- Project Presentation and Project Part A Identity Verified Team or group-based In-person Online: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Project Presentation and Project Part B Team or group-based Online: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Exam During Exam Period (School) - Individual Oral defence Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 30% Hurdle% (Hurdle: True)

===== SOURCE: https://my.uq.edu.au/programs-courses/course.html?course_code=CHEE7113 =====
Subject: Whole of Process Optimisation and Control (CHEE7113)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course involves the application of optimization and advanced control to identify potential improvements of industrial processes. Fundamental principles of formulating and solving optimization problems are introduced, along with critical interpretation of results and practical considerations in implementation of optimization output. The principles of Advanced Process Control are introduced and applied to industrial applications. Emphasis is on applying advanced control structures (e.g., equation-based control, etc.) to optimise existing processes. Students will be able to model the benefits of proposed control structures to justify their use in complex unit operations. Part of the justification will involve consideration of hardware and cost factors in implementing various control structures.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify opportunities for optimization and advanced control to improve performance in process systems
- Identify and formulate a range of optimization approaches to address design, control, sustainability and scheduling problems in the process industries, taking into account the characteristics of the process
- Generate optimal solutions to complex industrial problems through the application of specialised optimization codes, and interpet what actions should (or should not) be taken based on your findings
- Synthesize, apply and evaluate advanced SISO methods for difficult-to-control systems
- Formulate appropriate models and apply them to generate MPC applications to industrial processes, assessing the performance benefits of advanced process control
- Apply critical thinking skills to analyze, synthesize and effectively communicate technical information
- Demonstrate professional conduct in engaging with industry, including an appreciation of sustainability
- Improve teamwork skills through practice, reflection, feedback and change
Assessment Tasks:
- Individual exercises Hurdle Online: 20% Hurdle% (Hurdle: True)
- Project Part A Presentation and Report Team or group-based In-person Online: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Project Part B Presentation and Report Team or group-based In-person Online: 25%% (Hurdle: False)
- Exam During Exam Period (School) - Individual Oral Defence Hurdle Identity Verified In-person: 30% Hurdle% (Hurdle: True)
Workload: Workshop 4 Hours/ Week

By correlating the determined structural and chemical characteristics of a material with its fabrication/processing, the formation mechanism of the material can be clarified. Subject: Advanced Unit Operations in Water Management (WATR6108)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: There is a diverse range of process equipment and a diverse range of process configurations used in the management of water and wastewater. Unit Operations in Urban Water Engineering deals with the design, selection and operation of process equipment or process plants used in urban water. In water management, equipment can be combined to make a "unit" in a process with a clearly defined function. For example: material transport, preliminary treatment (screening, grit and oil removal, flow equalization), primary treatment (settling and primary sludge production), biological treatment for the removal of organic substrates (activated sludge systems: concepts and design, attached growth processes, membrane bioreactors), biological nutrients removal (nitrification/denitrification, phosphorus removal, secondary settling), sludge stabilization (biological digestion and biogas production, dewatering), odour control, disinfection. The most important and basic Unit Operations within these processes are governed by the fundamental laws of mathematics, physics, chemistry, thermodynamics and mechanics, which provide an approximate description of the real processes. The 'Unit Operations' concept allows for the analysis of unit operations in terms of fundamental principles such as mass and energy balances, phase equilibria, biology; and transport of momentum, energy and mass. This course examines a systematic way for selection and detailed design of process equipment
