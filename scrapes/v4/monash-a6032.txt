## Master of Marketing and Digital Communications
##### A6032
##### 72 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
The Master of Marketing and Digital Communications provides you with the professional skills, specialist knowledge and key capabilities relevant to career pathways in a wide range of strategic communication and marketing related fields. This industry focused degree is designed for those seeking cutting edge theoretical knowledge and problem solving skills in marketing and develops core competencies in critical areas of advocacy and persuasion, public relations and crisis communications, audience research and data analytics which can be applied to the government, corporate, not-for-profit and community sectors. Drawing on best practices locally and globally, graduates will be equipped to build effective and ethical communication and marketing strategies across local and international cultures and be responsive to a digitally integrated world. Taught by a combination of leading academics and practitioners in the field, you can also choose to develop deep knowledge in key elective fields including digital law, privacy and regulatory issues, and search engine optimisation and data analytics.

### Structure
The course is structured in two parts: Part A. Core studies and Part B. Elective studies. 

Part A.  Core studies

These studies draw on best practices within the broad realm of communications and media studies practice and research to further your understanding of communications and media systems both locally and globally. Studies focus on the challenges posed by the emergence of digital media, globalisation and increasing levels of cross-cultural exchange. 

These studies are further enhanced and expanded on with theoretical and industry based perspectives to provide an understanding of how organisations can connect with and influence their consumers to achieve specific marketing and promotional objectives. These studies will provide insight into the marketing communications industry and how it interacts with society and the changes associated with new technologies and new media. The impact of technological change on marketing and consumer behaviour will also be critically analysed. 

Part B. Elective studies

The focus of these studies is professional or scholarly work that can contribute to a portfolio of professional development. 

Master's entry points

Depending on prior qualifications you may receive entry level credit (a form of block credit) which determines your point of entry to the course:

  * If you are admitted at entry level 1 you complete 72 credit points, comprising Part A. and Part B.
  * If you are admitted at entry level 2 you complete 48 credit points, comprising Part A. (24 credit points) and Part B. 

Note: If you are eligible for credit for prior studies you may elect not to receive the credit and complete one of the higher credit-point options.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
apply advanced research skills to communicate findings in a range of formats to professional and non-professional audiences;
**2.**
apply a variety of research methodologies to real world problems of marketing and digital communication, and develop effective solutions within an ethical framework;
**3.**
understand and analyse the possibilities, challenges and impact of marketing and digital communication;
**4.**
demonstrate intercultural and global competence to undertake work in a world of constant technological and social change.
**5.**
critically interpret and evaluate secondary literature in the field of study, and the key texts and cultural products pertaining to marketing and digital communication;

### AHEGS
The Master of Marketing and Digital Communications is a postgraduate degree. The course is taught in English and takes 1.5 years of full-time equivalent study to complete. Graduates develop expert critical knowledge and skills to deal with marketing and digital communications analysis issues and resolutions across the spectrum of public and private sector activities. The Master of Marketing and Digital Communications is a Level 9 Australian Qualification Framework (AQF) qualification. The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Principles of global communications (APG5191)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This unit will develop foundation knowledge and skills in the field of global communications at graduate level. It engages with a range of contemporary professional communications practice and introduces principles and theories appropriate to understanding and analysing them. Particular focus is given to understanding the critical roles played by communications fields and industries across diverse sectors including politics, government, business and everyday society. These roles are further explored in the context of the complexities posed by a world of digitalisation and globalisation.

- read and critically analyse scholarly publications in communications.
- critically analyse and appraise the relationship between theory and practice in the field of communications;
- identify and analyse core concepts and theories underpinning contemporary communications approaches;

Subject: Data analytics in communications (APG5193)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The strategic application of ‘big data’ is transforming the field of communications. As our everyday communication practices increasingly become digital, mobile and social, there has been an explosion of data that is routinely collected and analysed for strategic purposes. Building on APG5373, this unit explores the intersection between audiences and publics, and digital data. In this unit, you will learn how to make use of data analytics to examine the dynamics, flows and effectiveness of communication processes across digital and social media platforms. You will acquire skills in gathering, analysing and visualising digital data to gain a better understanding of audience sentiment, behaviour and engagement. Drawing on case studies and practical research, you will understand the value of data analytics in informing communication strategies. You will also engage with the ethical and legal concerns at the heart of public debates relating to the collection and use of ‘big data’.
Learning Outcomes:
- apply advanced communication, research and analytical skills.
- gather, analyse and visualise digital data in the context of communication flows;
- assess the distinct ethical, legal and public interest implications raised by the use of big data;
- critique the role of big data in the field of strategic communications;
- discuss and interpret the results of data analytics to provide insights to processes of communication;
Assessment Tasks:
- Report: 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Analytical Exercise: 60% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study.
