Subject: Contemporary Australia (AUST1000)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course explores key issues and debates in understanding Australia as a nation, a culture and a society. Topics examine the forces shaping contemporary Australia and their historical context. The course is designed for both local and international students. AUST1000 is an interdisciplinary course, just as Australian Studies is an interdisciplinary field. Students will be introduced to recent developments in the fields of media/cultural studies, literature, history, politics, Indigenous studies and gender studies. A range of cultural texts including film, television, print media and advertisements will be used to develop the key concepts of the course. Employability Statement This course develops a range of skills valued by employers. You will develop knowledge, attributes, skills, and experience in the following: An understanding of historical and current political policies that have and continue to impact First Nations people, migrants, asylum seekers, and refugees. A sensitivity and respect for different, diverse, and intersectional experiences/perspectives. Knowledge of current issues and debates that circulate in contemporary Australian media. Verbal communication abilities including the capacity to discuss complex issues in group settings. Critical thinking skills and competence in questioning, analysing, interpreting, evaluating, and synthesising information. The ability to interpret, negotiate, and make meaning of information presented in different media forms. Written communication skills including proficiency in applying academic theory and research-based evidence to case studies.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate knowledge of the central issues affecting contemporary understandings of Australia as a nation
- Be able to compare, contrast, and analyse a range of different perspectives on current debates about Australian society and culture
- Be able to make links between individual identities and national identities
- Show reasonable knowledge of the historical context of current issues and recognise ways in which history is re-interpreted to meet contemporary needs
- Demonstrate an understanding of concepts and methods important to the field of Australian Studies
Assessment Tasks:
- Workshop Assessment Activities In-person: 30% 10 x 3%% (Hurdle: False)
- Focused Analysis 1 Online: 15% 500 words (+/- 10%)% (Hurdle: False)
- Focused Analysis 2 Online: 15% 500 words (+/- 10%)% (Hurdle: False)
- Digital Exhibition Concept and Rationale Online: 40% 2000 words (+/- 10%)% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Seminar 2 Hours/ Week

Subject: Understanding the News (COMU1050)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: You will examine news as a global cultural product and practice across print, broadcast and online platforms. The course utilizes methods such as framing, agenda setting, content analysis and discourse analysis to analyse the structure and content of news.
Learning Outcomes:
- understand news, news values, and the various considerations that go into news production across multiple platforms;
- appreciate the importance of objectivity as one of the canons of journalism, and the constraints on professional reporting/production of news;
- understand issues affecting diversity in the newsroom;
- reflect on and understand alternative forms of journalism;
- understand how media frame and represent various groups and social movements; and
- understand and apply fundamental theories of journalism and communication.

Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course is designed to explore the centrality of the internet in contemporary communication and culture. You will learn about the various technologies of the internet impact on how we organise and communicate online. You will also actively engage in critical debates relating to anonymity, surveillance, censorship, online abuse, algorithmic culture, the digital divide, and digital disruption. Subject: Social Media Communication (COMU3150)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: This course explores the role of social media platforms in generating engagement, managing brands and promoting products, services and causes. We critically examine the promotional culture of social media platforms and consider competing frameworks for understanding their benefits and drawbacks. Trends in the development of social media, their participatory cultures, and their uses by organisations are examine. Students create a social media strategy and work towards developing and curating social media content on social media platforms such as YouTube, TikTok, Twitter, Google, Snap Chat and Facebook. Students use analytics tools that enable organisations to track real time metrics related to engagement and sentiment.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply promotional theory and strategic thinking to analyze case studies and design campaigns for specific target audiences.
- Utilize social media tools to create multi-modal content that prioritizes clarity, concision, and high audience engagement.
- Evaluate social media campaigns and peer performance using industry-specific metrics and tools.
- Collaborate with colleagues to pitch communication solutions via structured oral presentations.
- Defend strategic communication choices and demonstrate subject knowledge during live, spontaneous questioning.
- Critically reflect on key industry themes, personal development, and individual contributions to group environments

Subject: Audio Journalism (JOUR1001)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Audiences rely exponentially on digital content creators as their primary source of news and information. Audio stories resonate with audiences looking for authenticity, spontaneity and depth. This provides opportunities for students to respond to these demands journalistically in the audio medium via the production of reliable information and content that reaches audiences and supports society. In this subject, students learn how to find, record, structure, produce and edit audio stories for radio and podcast audiences. Using radio and audio theory and traditions as a platform, this course explores a range of audio genres, from radio news to conversational and documentary audio. Students would learn to: interview for sound; record for immersive experience and soundscape; write and script for audio; present and narrate; edit layered audio narratives; and publish and distribute on-demand audio. This is a very practical course, and regular attendance (supplemented with production workshops offered by MaPS) will be important in staying on track with assessments.
Learning Outcomes:
- Conceptualise, research and produce audio stories and podcasts by applying a range of journalistic reporting methods.
- Demonstrate competency in industry standard technical equipment and production platforms to create audio outputs.
- Respond journalistically in the provision of professional content creation using reliable, creative and ethical information gathering judgements.
- Critically analyse journalistic practices using relevant theories while exploring a range of audio genres, from radio news to conversational and documentary audio.
- Work collaboratively in production teams to plan, produce, and critique audio journalism stories.
Assessment Tasks:
- Short news story Team or group-based: 30%% (Hurdle: False)
- Pitch: Current Affairs Story In-person: 30%% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: News Practice (JOUR1112)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: Where does a news story begin? Sometimes with a rumour that needs checking, often with a question you can't ignore, a detail that feels out of place, or a voice that deserves to be heard. Journalism starts with curiosity, and it grows through confidence. The confidence to ask difficult questions, verify information under pressure, and communicate clearly when the stakes are high. JOUR1112 - News Practice is an introduction to that craft. You'll learn how reporters think, how they gather facts, and how they turn uncertainty into accurate, timely stories across text, images, and video. Along the way, you'll practise: analysing sources, spotting weak arguments, speaking to people you¿ve never met, and explaining complex issues with precision. This course is practical. You will pitch ideas, write news stories, analyse evidence, create social-media news content, and produce a final multimedia piece that demonstrates your developing voice. You will begin building the confidence required to operate in fast-paced environments, the ability to investigate a problem, make sense of competing perspectives, and communicate what you've found with clarity.
Learning Outcomes:
- Students will analyse news topics and write a news story.
- Students will observe, investigate and gather information for a news story.
- Students will learn to pitch a news story in the appropriate format to a news editor.
- Students will identify news sources and interview them for publication.
- Students will learn to exercise their ethical judgement to report in a fair, balanced and accurate manner.
- Students will create a contemporary multimedia news story in the appropriate style and structure.
Assessment Tasks:
- News writing task-Mid-Semester Exam Identity Verified In-person: 20% 60 minutes% (Hurdle: False)
- News Story Pitch and Story Brief Online: 30% 800 words% (Hurdle: False)
- Final News Story: Story, Images and Video Online: 50% 500-650 words, 2 images, 30-45 second video% (Hurdle: False)

Subject: Journalistic Investigation (JOUR2720)
Credit Points: 2
Overview: In this course students will utilise advanced skills in how to research, locate, interrogate and analyse information, and to practise investigative reporting as journalists. Investigative journalism has power to deliver reporting for the benefit of society via uncovering secrets, holding power to account, correct injustices and demand and interrogate solutions. When done well, it is the kind of reporting that can deliver meaningful change. In this course, students will apply a variety of journalistic research methods - from observation and interviewing to digital and social media investigation, document mining and FOI requests - to deliver a substantial piece of public-interest journalism. Students will make use of technological developments including AI to search and organise information. They will also explore and critique a wide range of industry examples, ensuring their own investigations are inspired and informed by the best industry standards, trends, contexts, and ethical frameworks.
Learning Outcomes:
- Source, construct and disseminate a piece of investigative journalism.
- Formulate, compose and assess advanced investigative measures such as Freedom of Information or Right To Information requests.
- Critically analyse the diverse needs of audiences in the interests of working towards a more fair, just, inclusive and equitable society.
- Embed the traditions, theories, frameworks and codes of ethics in reporting practice with moral courage in decison making.
- Apply a wide range of journalistic research methods, including data-driven reporting, social media investigation, interviewing, observation and document-driven reporting.
- Critically assess, interpret and apply use of technological developments including AI to search and organise information.
Assessment Tasks:
- Practical Skills Portfolio In-person: 50%% (Hurdle: False)
- Major News Investigation: 50% 800-1000 words% (Hurdle: False)
Workload: Lecture 1 Hour/ Week Tutorial 2 Hours/ Week

Overview: This course applies journalistic research and storytelling techniques to the vast array of available digital information to empower students to generate advanced data-driven reporting. Learning Outcomes:
- Critique existing examples of data journalism (in their professional, social and ethical contexts), and understand the role that data plays in story construction
- Locate and obtain relevant data for informing journalistic investigation
- Apply relevant sense-making processes to data to reveal data narratives that are of public significance and relevance
- Construct original and engaging data-led stories, supported by visualisations
