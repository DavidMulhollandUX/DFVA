## Bachelor of Information Technology
##### C2004
##### 144 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview

### AQF level
Level 7 - Bachelor Degree
### Course duration (full time)


### Structure
This is a comprehensive course, structured in four parts:

Part A. Specified studies

This will provide you with foundation skills and knowledge for your IT education and ensure a breadth of understanding of IT and its applications in organisations and society.

Part B. Major studies

This will provide you with a focused program of study that will develop your expertise in software development. You will develop the practical and theoretical skills and knowledge needed to successfully plan, develop, implement and evaluate information products and systems.

Part C. Elective Studies

This will enable you to further develop your knowledge of the Indonesian context. You will enhance your innovative skills, cultivate your capacity for change-making, and formulate your leadership abilities.

Part D. Indonesian studies

This will enable you to gain an understating of key political, social and cultural values that define Indonesia.

Course progression map

The course progression map provides guidance on unit enrolment for each semester of study.

### Requirements
You must complete units as specified in Parts A, B, C and D (144 points): a minimum of 96 points of information technology study over Part A and Part B, and 48 points of Elective studies (or an Innovation and Leadership major) in part C.

In choosing units you must ensure that you complete no more than 10 level one units (60 points), and at least six units (36 points) at level 3 of which at least four (24 points) are Bachelor of Information Technology listed (FIT-coded) units.

Units are 6 points unless otherwise specified.

A. Specified studies (48 points)

You must complete 48 credit points, comprising:

- core studies (36 credit points)

- applied studies (12 credit points)

Core studies

  * FIT1045 Introduction to programming

  * FIT1047 Introduction to computer systems, networks and security

  * FIT1049 IT professional practice

  * FIT2001 Systems development

  * FIT2002 IT project management

  * FIT2094 Databases

Applied studies

  * FIT3047 Industry experience studio project 1

  * FIT3048 Industry experience studio project 2

B. Major studies (48 points)

You must complete the software development major. A major requires eight units with no more than two units at level one (12 points) and at least three units (18 points) at level three. 

  * FIT1050 Web fundamentals

  * FIT1051 Programming fundamentals in java

  * FIT2081 Mobile application development

  * FIT2104 Web database interface

  * FIT2175 Usability

  * FIT3077 Software engineering: Architecture and design

  * FIT3178 iOS app development

  * FIT3184 Cloud computing

C. Elective Studies (48 points)

You must complete 48 credit points of elective units.

Electives can be chosen from across the Monash Indonesia campus, as long as you have met the prerequisites and there are no restrictions on enrolment in the units. Please note that completion of the following Innovation and Leadership units will allow you to complete a second major:

  * BEI1501 Engaging complexity

  * BEI1067 Evidence for action

  * BEI2766 Shaping futures

  * BEI2515 Catalysing change

  * BEI2500 Design thinking for global challenges

  * MGI3991 Leadership principles and practices

  * MON3500 Research, experimentation and discovery

  * MON3750 Monash innovation guarantee

If you are in a double degree course you don't complete Part C. Some units required for the other degree will be credited as electives towards this degree.

Part D. Indonesian studies (0 credit points) 

You must complete:

  * BEI1270 Indonesian studies (Religion, Indonesian language, Pancasila, and Civic Education) (0 credit points)

Note: the workload requirements (including contact hours) for each of these subjects is 2 hours per week. These units must be undertaking in parallel with your other units and cannot be completed outside Indonesia.

Subject: Evidence for action (BEI1067)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Evidence for action focuses on the skills and mindsets needed to investigate complex problems using a range of qualitative and quantitative methods. You’ll learn how to conduct interviews, run surveys, engage in fieldwork, and explore datasets, while also learning how to interpret, evaluate, and communicate what you find. This unit emphasises not just collecting information, but thinking critically about it: identifying patterns, surfacing assumptions, recognising bias, and assessing reliability and ethics. You’ll also be introduced to the practicalities of research responsibility, including informed consent, ethical review processes, and data care. Whether you're working with lived experiences, statistical models, or spatial data, you’ll be encouraged to ask: What do I know? How do I know it? And what does it mean for what we do next? Evidence for action helps you build analytical confidence and methodological flexibility, grounding leadership and innovation in diverse forms of knowledge that are both credible and ethically sound.
Learning Outcomes:
- collect, analyse, and interpret both qualitative and quantitative data using appropriate techniques
- assess the quality, reliability, and ethical dimensions of different forms of evidence, including research responsibility and data care practices
- communicate evidence-based findings clearly and responsibly to support innovation and leadership in real-world contexts.
- demonstrate understanding of diverse research methods for investigating complex challenges
- apply critical thinking to draw meaningful insights from varied evidence sources
Assessment Tasks:
- 1 - Written: 50% (Hurdle: None)
- 2 - Artefact: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- 3 - Presentation: 20% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Independent study may include associated readings, assessment and preparation for scheduled activities. You are expected to complete all pre-class activities prior to your scheduled class, and post-class activities should be completed after your scheduled class. Learning activities may include a combination of teacher directed, peer directed and online engagement activities.

- reflect critically on personal and collective responsibilities as citizens within the framework of Pancasila and Indonesian civic values
- demonstrate an understanding of key concepts from Religion, Indonesian Language, Pancasila, and Civic Education in relation to Indonesian society and governance.

Subject: Engaging complexity (BEI1501)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Engaging complexity is an immersive introduction to the world of complex challenges - social, environmental, institutional, and technological - where clear-cut solutions are rare, and understanding takes time. In this unit, you’ll learn to observe, listen, describe, and reflect before jumping to conclusions. You’ll work with tools like stakeholder mapping, systems diagrams, and controversy analysis to explore how problems are shaped by context, power, and perspective. Rather than rushing to solve, Engaging complexity invites you to slow down and sit with complexity: What’s going on here? Who is involved? What’s being said, and what’s not? You’ll begin to understand how complex environments work, and why that understanding must come before any attempt to navigate or intervene. Understanding complexity isn’t a distraction from action, it’s what makes meaningful leadership and innovation possible. This unit develops your ability to approach challenges with curiosity, care, and the skills to ask better questions - grounded in ethical awareness, humility, and the patience to trace complexity carefully, thread by thread, knot by knot.
Learning Outcomes:
- identify and reflect on how context, power, and perspective shape the way problems are defined and understood
- use mapping and observational tools (e.g. stakeholder mapping, systems diagrams, controversy analysis) to describe complex social and institutional environments
- practice careful, ethical observation and inquiry as a foundation for future innovation and leadership work
- reflect critically on your own assumptions and positionality in relation to the challenges you encounter.
- demonstrate understanding of complex systems and the value of situated, relational approaches to problem exploration
Assessment Tasks:
- 1 - Written: 50% (Hurdle: None)
- 2 - Artefact: 30% (Hurdle: None)
- 3 - Presentation: 20% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Minimum total expected workload to achieve the learning outcomes for this unit is 144 hours per semester typically comprising a mixture of scheduled learning activities and independent study. Subject: Design thinking for global challenges (BEI2500)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Design thinking applies the skills and strategies developed by designers to a wide range of problems and situations. These skills and strategies include a range of conceptual and communication approaches, ways to explore and reconsider the context in which we work, as well as what should or could be achieved. Design thinking brings together some approaches that are known in the sciences and humanities, such as analysis and synthesis, but applies them in ways that are particular to a design approach. Design thinking can enable a more flexible approach to problem solving, and a more creative engagement with the complex issues of our contemporary world. This unit will introduce you to the key skills and practices associated with design thinking, and offer opportunities to explore the application of these basic creativity techniques through a series of projects.
Learning Outcomes:
- identify and analyse ill-defined, real-world project challenges and relevant design thinking approaches
- develop, test and evaluate original design proposals in response to a specific global challenge.
- demonstrate understanding of the methods and mindsets of design thinking and their application
- apply design thinking methods and mindsets to a project-based problem, to develop new perspectives on key issues and to identify potential opportunities

Subject: Web fundamentals (FIT1050)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The world-wide web is one of the most widely-used platforms for building applications. Although all students can be assumed to be experienced users of the web, very few have any clear understanding of the technologies upon which the web is based, and the way in which these technologies affect web-based applications. This unit aims to give you a sound basic knowledge of the web and a range of issues which may be involved in web application development. It will take a strongly practical focus in examining the technology, design and implementation problems a developer needs to address in developing applications for real-world systems. The diversity of web applications means that there are a wide range of issues which may be relevant to the development of any given web site. The unit will aim to give breadth of coverage of these issues, rather than focusing in depth on any particular development task or any specific type of web application. The unit will take a strongly practical focus in examining the technology issues involved, and highlight the key issues which a developer needs to address in developing applications of this kind for real-world systems.
Learning Outcomes:
- Create media assets to integrate into a website, with a focus on optimisation, compatibility and accessibility.
- Apply design principles as part of pre-production processes to create website designs.
- Apply collaborative design techniques for planning the design of web content and web sites.
- Critically analyse how fundamental web technologies work from a technical perspective and their impact on society.
- Develop and test websites using front-end technologies including HTML, CSS and JavaScript.
