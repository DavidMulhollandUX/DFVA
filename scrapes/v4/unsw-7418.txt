## Description
This Graduate Certificate in Technology Management is designed to align organisational and technology management fundamentals for individuals who wish to understand, analyse and adapt to today’s challenging and hyper-competitive market.

Organisations and leaders must increasingly innovate in order to survive and thrive however, technology alone is not sufficient in generating a competitive advantage. The Graduate Certificate in Technology Management teaches how to use data to create competitive advantages with innovative management, strategy and technology, to succeed in an increasingly complex market.

Subject: Data Analytics and Decision-making (AGSM6234)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course is designed to provide a broad strategic perspective on issues and challenges related to incorporating data analytics and AI into business decision-making for strategic gain and meaningful change. Due to the complex and multi-faceted nature of the world of analytics, leaders and managers need some basic and abstract familiarity with the technical building blocks of analytics. This enables them to speak the language of analytics with fluency. Also, since emerging tools and objects in the world of AI and analytics are underpinned by these technical building blocks, this familiarity enables leaders and managers to separate hype from reality with confidence and make better decisions when integrating analytics/AI into their organisations. We deliver this course in three phases to provide an integrated strategic–technical–execution view of data analytics. In Phase 1, we will explore the concept of data strategy and its connection to business strategy. In Phase 2, we aim to help you understand basic analytics and statistical concepts, building technical confidence to distinguish between hype and reality in analytics and AI, while learning to speak the language of analytics fluently. In Phase 3, we will address the challenges of incorporating analytics and AI into organisations and explore their potential to create business value and drive innovative changes in business processes.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop basic data literacy and explain how data analytics can support decision-making in business contexts.
- Explain the importance of ethics and governance in order to apply relevant frameworks in data-driven organisations, and evaluate the social and ethical implications of data collection and analysis.
- Select and appropriately apply various data-analytics techniques to identify business problems, trends and patterns used to inform business decision-making.
- Develop and integrate data-driven recommendations and solutions to enhance and support business strategies and decision-making through well-reasoned analysis.
- Use visual and digital mediums to communicate clear analytical insights and develop convincing narratives from complex datasets.

Subject: Project Management (AGSM9101)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: With the billions being invested in projects around the world, there is a burgeoning need to develop both individual and organisational project-management capabilities. Organisations are no longer debating whether there is value in adopting project management. The question now is how to develop their project-management capabilities in the most efficient, value-producing manner, and how to turn effective project management into key competitive advantage. Project [noun] a piece of planned work or an activity that is finished over a period of time and intended to achieve a particular purpose (Cambridge English Dictionary, n.d.). Projects are the means by which organisations implement their strategy. They are ubiquitous across industry and domain: as a manager or leader in any modern organisation, you will be directly involved in projects - as a team member, a project or program manager, a subject-matter expert, a sponsor or owner, or as a client. As your career progresses, you can expect to face increasingly complex challenges in dealing with larger projects, more demanding stakeholders, tighter constraints on human, financial and other resources, and a rapidly changing environment. You will be expected to improve the efficiency, productivity and performance of your teams, and determine which projects and initiatives will best deliver the organisation's mandate. An increasing number of organisations are 'projectising' routine work to gain the benefits of project management. At the same time, agile and flexible work practices are blurring the distinction between project and operational work. At the other end of the scale, the increasing number of complex projects and megaprojects require more rigorous approaches to both managing and governing an organisation's project portfolio. This course provides an introduction to the rapidly growing world of project, program and portfolio management (PPPM or 3PM). The course materials address various themes around managing projects in modern organisations, and take you through key processes that contribute to the successful delivery of a project. We start with an introduction to project management, and follow with requisite project-management methodologies and skills, management of the stakeholders and individuals assigned to project teams, project planning and project communications, project execution and control, and project closure. Woven through each weekly unit are the attributes of responsible leadership underpinning sustainable future organisations. However, we can only scratch the surface of what is an ever-expanding field of study. Every project is, by definition, unique: there is no one-size-fits-all approach to project management. While we present a simple framework for project management, and assessments and a simulation against which to apply it, our real objective is to challenge your thinking about project management, and how it can be exploited to deliver value to your organisations and stakeholders. Whether you contribute to projects, manage multiple small projects or a single large one, or are responsible for a project portfolio or a team of project managers, we trust you will find this course challenges your thinking and equips you to improve your own and your organisation's project delivery capabilities.
Learning Outcomes:
- Make recommendations on how best to select, structure and resource projects for success.
- Assess the impact of risk and uncertainty in a project, and recommend alternative courses of action.
- Demonstrate tolerance and acceptance of the diversity of people and skills in any project.
- Discuss the value of, and challenges and constraints in, utilising project management in the modern business environment.
- Identify and apply 'lesson learned' from past projects and apply them to future projects.
- Consider ethical dilemmas project managers face, and evaluate alternative courses of action.
- Analyse and evaluate your own and your team's performance in achieving project outcomes.
- Select and apply fit-for-purpose project-management tools and techniques to manage a project through its entire life cycle.
- Assess the role and responsibilities of a project manager in different types of projects.
- Provide constructive feedback to improve team-members' performance.
- Demonstrate team-leadership and communication skills to achieve project outcomes.

Subject: Managing with Digital Technology (AGSM9154)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In a fast-paced, technology-driven business landscape, a strong understanding of, and the ability to manage, the digital enterprise is no longer optional – it’s essential. This course is designed to equip you with the ability to communicate effectively with technical teams, evaluate technology investments, assess enterprise systems and guide technological initiatives that create measurable business value. You will learn to evaluate emerging technologies, develop cloud and data strategies, and modernise enterprise systems. Key topics include user-experience design, cybersecurity, sustainability in technology, and software value-chain optimisation. The course emphasises fluency in core digital concepts – information systems, software, internet, and telecommunications – and the use of digital tools to support executive decision-making, crisis response, and change management. Upon completion, you will be prepared to drive technology initiatives and create sustained value across industries.
Learning Outcomes:
- Employ digital technologies to support executive decision-making, crisis and change management
- Apply portfolio methods and technical debt analysis to modernise the digital core of an enterprise.
- Demonstrate digital literacy in information systems, software, the internet, and telecommunications in an organisational context and explain their role in enabling business strategy, operations and innovation.
- Critically assess user experience, interface design and machine-to-machine interfaces, as well as enterprise security, privacy and sustainability risks, to inform responsible and effective digital decision-making.
- Evaluate and justify the selection of enterprise technology, including cloud, data management and software systems, to optimise alignment with organisational goals and enhance measurable business value.
- Apply strategic foresight and process-management frameworks to classify emerging technologies and justify optimisation paths in software development.

Subject: Entrepreneurship and Innovation (AGSM9156)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: This course suits individuals looking to build their strategic abilities around entrepreneurship and the innovation associated with entrepreneurship. It provides exposure to both the fundamentals of business innovation and the practical aspects of identifying, evaluating and moving business ideas forward. The course uniquely exposes students to current ideas and thoughts from innovators, venture capital (VC) groups, and successful startup CEOs within the Sydney ecosystem. This dynamic course pulls together many of the components already learned in the MBA Program, providing those with entrepreneurial aspirations the opportunity to realise their dreams. Two streams of entrepreneurship are covered in the course: startup creation and startup innovation. Specific methodologies covered include integrated experiences of Design Thinking and The Lean Startup. This is a hands-on opportunity to learn about venture creation, startup resourcing, strategy and management, design and innovation involving your own startup. Entrepreneurship and innovation is a hot topic today - many startups continue to raise money through angel investors and VCs, but the road is a challenging one. Understanding what makes entrepreneurship and innovation in startups work is paramount to success. We will help you develop your confidence, your products, your pitch, and your funding to help get you started on the road to your startup success.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify a range of strategic options for growing an entrepreneurial venture and their strengths and weaknesses, including government funding and commercial funding
- Analyse ethical obligations and ethical professional practices within organisations as they apply to the startup and key stakeholders
- Identify common characteristics of successful entrepreneurs and ventures
- Use language, visual representations, and/or digital media to effectively and accurately interpret and convey qualitative and quantitative information
- Understand how social impact entrepreneurship has the potential to make the world a better place
- Develop the entrepreneurial mindset
- Apply the Lean Startup methodology to a project
- Understand and apply concepts such as minimal viable product, split-testing, ‘fast-fail’, product-market fit, the Business Model Canvas and Design Thinking

Subject: AI Strategy (AGSM9165)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: In the rapidly evolving landscape of artificial intelligence (AI), understanding and leveraging AI technologies is crucial for business leaders. The Age of AI is increasingly ushering in new kinds of enterprises that can compete in unprecedented ways and achieve unbridled growth and impact multiple industries. Whether you lead a startup or seek to revamp a traditional organisation, it is essential to understand the revolutionary impact of AI on strategy, competition and sustainable value creation. This course is designed to equip globally responsible senior executives and future leaders with the strategic insights and practical tools needed to integrate AI into business strategies effectively. Through a combination of theoretical foundations, case studies and hands-on projects, you will explore the transformative potential of AI, focusing on its role in driving innovation, operational efficiency and ethical digital transformation. This course prepares you to take on the above strategic roles and contribute to responsible, sustainable and competitive business practices.
Learning Outcomes:
- Advocate for and implement ethical AI practices, ensuring alignment with responsible governance, sustainability principles and organisational strategy by leveraging best practices from multiple governance and regulatory models.
- Effectively communicate AI strategy to diverse stakeholders, including senior executives, investors and policy makers, through structured evidence-based business cases, reports and presentations.
- Design AI-driven strategies that enhance organisational performance, foster innovation and create competitive advantage across industries, applying multiple evidence-based frameworks.
- Critically assess AI’s strategic role as an organisational enabler, distinguishing between AI-driven efficiencies and transformative AI-powered business models that create sustainable value.
