## Description
The Bachelor of Architectural Studies at UNSW is focused on the evolving and interrelated challenges of architecture, urbanism, and environmental sustainability. The program structure, with studio-based learning at its core, allows you to explore architectural design through hands-on projects and interdisciplinary learning activities.

From day one, courses will introduce you to real-world design challenges and issues such as climate-responsive architecture, housing, and cultural safety. Our curriculum seamlessly integrates environmental sustainability, construction principles, and digital design to equip you with the skills to create adaptive and resilient built environments.

A strength of the program is its focus on architecture in practice. You will engage with industry professionals and real-world projects and develop the ability to navigate design processes, construction methods, and professional legal and social responsibilities. You will explore First Nations knowledge and perspectives, ensuring that your work respects cultural heritage and fosters inclusive communities. Studio projects will expose you to the complexities of contemporary practice, preparing you to work effectively in teams and respond to environmental and social considerations.

Upon completing this degree, you will be equipped for diverse career pathways in architecture and related design-based industries or you may continue your studies in the accredited UNSW Master of Architecture program, which is a pathway to professional registration.

Subject: Architectural History and Theory 1 (ARCH1000)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Architectural History and Theory 1 presents an inquiry into Western architecture, with an emphasis on the relevance of historical theories to contemporary design strategies. The course begins with the principles set out in antiquity and the Renaissance, such as order, the human analogy, proportion and decorum. These were adapted during the ensuing centuries. A tradition survived, where an architect is concerned with a person, who feels an affinity with the human-like character of a building. Along with the study of architecture in Europe, you will examine how classical buildings served the ambitions of imperial powers in different parts of the world. Skills acquired in the course include academic referencing, as well as writing short visual essays.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate foundational architectural enquiry skills through research, interpretation and reflection.
- Communicate historical architectural concepts, principles, strategies and themes through sketches and text.
- Analyse the ideas, themes and theories relevant to the history of Western architecture

Subject: Architectural Fabrication (ARCH1331)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Architectural Fabrication expands your knowledge and understanding of construction material properties and their integration with architectural forms. The course is focused on exploring the role of the building façade, as a multi-layered system able simultaneously to deliver thermal comfort and empower interaction between inside and outside of a building. You will be provided with new tools and methods to understand the technical narrative behind architectural case studies. You will also explore and develop the relationship between design and tectonics in your own projects. Modern conceptions of architectural tectonics will be explored, and physical precedents analysed.
Learning Outcomes:
- Assess material and construction options informed by disciplinary knowledge.
- Apply knowledge of relevant legislation, quality and performance standards and codes in the selection of materials and construction systems.
- Communicate material, construction and structural information using drawings, models, specifications and schedules.

Subject: Architectural Science and Building Environment (ARCH1361)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Architectural Science and Building Environment focuses on applying the principles of acoustics, lighting and comfort to achieve indoor environmental quality (IEQ) and enhanced environmental performance. The course contextualises the science of acoustics, lighting and comfort within architectural design with an emphasis on environmental data collection, analysis and application to optimise IEQ and environmental performance. You will explore the role of architectural design in mitigating indoor and outdoor thermal comfort impacts through real-world case-studies and online tools. You will investigate the significance of daylighting and artificial lighting design in IEQ and building environmental performance and apply acoustic principles to your studio designs. You will learn how to identify, evaluate and apply strategies to optimise acoustics, lighting and thermal comfort integrated with architectural design compliant with the regulatory standards applicable to environmental performance.
Learning Outcomes:
- Communicate Indoor Environmental Quality using descriptive, visual and numerical formats, suited to building environmental specialists.
- Evaluate acoustic, lighting and comfort strategies to optimise Indoor Environmental Quality and building environmental performance.
- Apply Australian building codes and standards applicable to acoustics, lighting and comfort to architectural designs.
- Apply knowledge of scientific principles of acoustics, daylighting and lighting and thermal comfort to architectural design.
