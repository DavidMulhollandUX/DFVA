### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Apply professional communication skills to communicate with diverse audiences from a range of clinical and community settings.
**2.**
Apply principles of evidence based practice to appraise research literature and demonstrate effective clinical reasoning.
**3.**
Apply an understanding of health and wellbeing to analyse issues in health and human movement.
**4.**
Apply key human bioscience concepts and systems as they relate to health and human movement.
**5.**
Work effectively to complete projects and tasks independently and in teams.

Subject: ANATOMY FOR PHYSIOTHERAPY A (ANA1003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will be introduced to foundational concepts and terminology for the study of human anatomy and biomechanics. These concepts will then be applied in detail to the study of the lower limb, as relevant to physiotherapy. Teaching will include both online learning and face-to-face teaching sessions including practical classes involving cadaver materials and clinical anatomy. You will be required to work together in small groups, to take responsibility for your own learning and understanding of anatomy so that you are able to identify anatomical structure and function on a living body as you would in a clinical physiotherapy context.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify musculoskeletal structures within the lower limb through surface anatomy.
- Apply relevant anatomical principles to describe normal structure and function of body systems within the lower limb, including its role in support, locomotion and posture.
- Apply observational and communication skills, individually or as part of a team, using appropriate anatomical and lay terminology for use with clients or other health care professionals, to make accurate observations of anatomical structures or events and infer their relationship to function using cadaveric material, anatomical models, the living body or medical imaging.
- Apply foundational biomechanical principles, including the concepts of force, motion and leverage, to analyse human movement within the lower limb.

Subject: HUMAN BIOSCIENCES A (HBS1HBA)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject introduces you to human physiology - how the body works. You'll learn how the body is organised and the basics of cell structure and function, and how processes in the body are controlled. This will provide the foundation for the study of the major body systems important for support and movement (muscular system); regulation, integration and control (nervous and endocrine systems); fluids and transport (blood, heart, cardiovascular and immune systems); energy, maintenance and environmental exchange (respiratory, digestive and renal systems, metabolism); and the continuity of life (reproductive systems).
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the basic physiology of major organ systems of the human body.
- Apply knowledge of physiology concepts to clinical and disease states.
- Demonstrate oral and written communication skills.
- Demonstrate the skills for self-managing, reflection, and successful learning at university.
- Describe the structure and function of body cells, tissues, and organs.
- Apply specialist terms and language associated with physiology and anatomy to describe the human body.

Subject: FROM BAD SCIENCE TO GOOD HEALTH (HLT1001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: We are increasingly faced with controversial debates occurring in our media, with different groups arguing their viewpoints, and the scientific evidence is often swamped by misreporting or distorted evidence. This subject uses a case-based approach to examine the evidence underpinning current topics of debate in healthcare, and the motivations behind research and reporting of research, including the scientific process, peer-review, conflict of interest confirmation bias and cultural identity. This subject introduces skills in research that minimize bias, and are important in understanding and analysing evidence, including systematic approaches to acquiring evidence, critical appraisal of the literature and descriptive and inferential statistics.
Learning Outcomes:
- Identify psychological characteristics and reasoning fallacies that contribute to our susceptibility to believe non-evidence based health claims.
- Effectively search for evidence of the effects of a health product or treatment and evaluate the presence of characteristics that are necessary for a fair test of effectiveness.
- Examine factors that drive the promotion of health products and treatments, question the plausibility of these claims, and understand the importance of evidence-based healthcare
- Communicate effectively and with respect for diversity in opinions to explain complex concepts in understanding evidence-based health.

Subject: PATHOPHYSIOLOGY FOR HEALTH PROFESSIONALS (HPT2001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject provides you with the fundamental principles of the underlying mechanisms of human disease and how these mechanisms impact on clinical outcomes. Emphasis begins with general disease processes including cell injury, cell adaptations, inflammation, immunopathology, tissue healing, neoplasia and circulatory disturbances and disorders. Specific pathological conditions of body systems e.g. nervous, respiratory, renal, gastrointestinal, hepatic and skeletal systems along with diabetes will be addressed.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate effective communication skills, individually and as part of a team, by communicating scientific results, information, or arguments to a range of audiences, for a range of purposes, and using a variety of modes.
- Explain cellular and biochemical changes that underlie the development of systemic diseases (i.e. Systemic Pathophysiology and relate these changes to the likely clinical course and outcomes of an afflicted individual.
- Apply the principles of General Pathophysiology to selected human disorders and reparative states, describing how these general pathophysiologic processes impact on possible clinical courses and outcomes in an afflicted individual.

Subject: PHARMACOLOGY FOR HEALTH PROFESSIONALS (PCY2001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Pharmacology is the study of drugs. Drugs play a key role in the treatment of most human diseases and are used to both cure and prevent disease. In this subject you will learn the general principles of pharmacology including drug classification, formulation, administration and basic pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics. Drugs affecting the nervous system and major organs will be explored, and the concept of selective toxicity to treat infection will be introduced. You will gain knowledge in commonly prescribed drugs, clinical trials and over the counter and complimentary medications, drugs of abuse and dependence and drugs in sport. Emphasis is placed on human pharmacology including clinical topics, such as, adverse drug reactions and drug interactions.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the ways drugs are absorbed, distributed, metabolised and excreted in the body and explain the factors influencing these processes.
- Distinguish between the fundamental principles of pharmacology (pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics), and explain the ways that drugs can produce therapeutic actions in the body.
- Apply understanding of agonist and antagonist activity to drug action.
- Explain the causes of drug contraindications, interactions and adverse reactions using understanding of drug properties and human physiology.
- Explain how different classes of drugs used to treat various diseases act to effect living tissue.
- Communicate pharmacological concepts and information as part of a team

Subject: PHYSIOTHERAPY SKILLS AND REASONING 1 (PTY1001)
Credit Points: 30
Overview: In this subject, you will be introduced to the profession of physiotherapy. You will learn about the determinants of health, their influence on the wellbeing of individuals, communities and populations, and the role of a physiotherapist in promoting health and wellbeing for people from a variety of backgrounds. You will begin to create your professional identity, critical thinking and curiosity as a healthcare practitioner and reflect on personal and professional behaviour as governed by codes of conduct and relevant legislation. You will integrate your developing knowledge of anatomy, physiology and biomechanics principles to develop skills relating to physiotherapy for lower limb conditions, including how to assess clients, develop client-centred problem lists, and perform basic treatment for soft tissue injuries. You will learn strategies to communicate effectively and respectfully with clients from a variety of backgrounds, and clinical reasoning skills to interpret information to support the planning of tailored and culturally responsive physiotherapy care.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe and demonstrate basic treatment approaches for soft tissue injuries, with a focus on the lower limb.
- Reflect on personal and colleagues' professional behaviour based on relevant legislation, ethical standards and codes of conduct.
- Apply clinical reasoning to analyse and interpret physiotherapy assessment findings and develop problem lists for lower limb presentations.
- Identify the determinants of health and explain how they influence health and wellbeing of individuals, communities and populations.
- Demonstrate and apply effective subjective and objective physiotherapy assessments that are culturally responsive and relevant to specific clinical contexts, with a focus on the lower limb.
- Demonstrate professional practice and communication skills that are applicable to physiotherapy management of lower limb conditions and important to culturally safe practice, including verbal, interpersonal, reflective and written skills.

Subject: PHYSIOTHERAPY FOR OLDER ADULTS (PTY2002)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will progress your physiotherapy skills and reasoning to learn about the provision of tailored, effective and client-centred care for older adults, in the context of healthy or disease-related ageing. You will be introduced to the concept of ageism and the resultant need to develop advocacy skills to support the rights of your older clients to health care and self-determination. You will apply your developing knowledge of neuroanatomy and pathophysiology to the exploration of physiotherapy management of common conditions such as dementia, delirium, frailty and falls. You will also develop your skills in manual handling, working in partnership with families and carers, exercise prescription for older adults, and promoting enabling environments.
Learning Outcomes:
- Determine how concepts of autonomy, advocacy and ageism intersect with physiotherapy for older adults.
- Adapt physiotherapy practice to manage common risks, complexities and contextual factors such as cognitive impairment, multimorbidity, or working with families and carers, in a manner that is culturally safe and relevant to the client.
- Differentiate between healthy and disease-related ageing processes across cardiovascular, musculoskeletal and neurological systems.
- Differentiate the role of physiotherapists and the interprofessional team in supporting the health and wellbeing of older people in a variety of healthcare settings.
- Communicate effectively and respectfully with and about older clients from a variety of backgrounds, who are experiencing prevalent health conditions.

Subject: NEUROLOGICAL PHYSIOTHERAPY 1  (PTY2003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will apply your knowledge of neuroanatomy, neurophysiology, and pathophysiology to develop skills and reasoning relevant to client-centred physiotherapy assessment and management of common presentations such as stroke, haemorrhage, vestibular dysfunction, Guillain-Barre syndrome, multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's disease, dementia, and cerebral palsy. You will build on your subjective assessment skills to explore the experience of individuals with neurological impairments, and learn how to perform key neurological assessments of motor and sensory pathways, including vestibular. Case studies will be used to introduce clinical reasoning in this area, with a particular focus on WHO-ICF activity areas of standing, dynamic balance, and walking. You will learn about treatment principles for neurological impairments and will start to develop basic treatment and discharge plans.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe physiotherapy treatment principles that are relevant for people with neurological conditions.
- Design and implement a functional based assessment for a person with a neurological condition.
- Apply analysis and interpretation of assessment findings for common neurological conditions to produce prioritised problem lists and client-centred goals.
- Design and implement a subjective assessment for a person with a neurological condition.
- Demonstrate safe and sensitive neurological assessments of motor and sensory pathways.

Subject: MUSCULOSKELETAL PHYSIOTHERAPY (PTY2004)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will apply your knowledge of anatomy, physiology, biomechanics, exercise prescription, cultural safety and professional behaviour to appropriately assess and treat patients with peripheral musculoskeletal conditions. Client-centred models of clinical reasoning will be used as a framework to plan your physiotherapy management. - Consistently adopt ethical and professional behaviours to ensure a safe environment for all clients and members of the interdisciplinary team.
- Devise, perform and justify safe, effective and evidence-based client-centred management plans for common peripheral musculoskeletal presentations.
- Apply effective clinical reasoning to analyse and explain the findings of assessments to produce prioritised problem lists and client-centred goals relevant to common peripheral musculoskeletal presentations.

Subject: INTRODUCTION TO PLACEMENT (PTY2005)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will participate in a range of learning activities that will prepare you for clinical placements. - Produce a high standard of written documentation and verbal communication including problem lists, patient notes, handovers and discharge plans that are justified via clinical reasoning.
- Consistently adopt ethical and professional behaviours to ensure a safe environment for all clients and members of the interdisciplinary team.
