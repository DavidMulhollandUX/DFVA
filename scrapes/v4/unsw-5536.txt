## Description
The Graduate Diploma in Flying (GDF) is a program designed to meet the needs of graduates with an ambition toward a career as a professional pilot. Students may undertake the GDF as a standalone program, otherwise, it can be taken as a concurrent program with the Bachelor of Aviation (Management). Students from the Bachelor of Aviation Management who wish to enrol in the GDF are advised to contact the School of Aviation before Term 2 of their first year to organise enrolment in Flight Screening.

The GDF incorporates training and assessment for a Commercial Pilot Licence (CPL) and an Instrument Rating (IR), including academic study covering the underpinning theory. Award of the GDF requires the satisfactory completion of all courses, with the award of the CPL and the IR forming part of the assessment for the program.

The program has been designed by UNSW Academics and key aviation industry partners and provides students with an opportunity to gain real-world experience, which includes being directly mentored by professional pilots in the industry. In addition, students also have the opportunity to kickstart their flying careers through optional involvement in the Qantas - Aviation Career Enrichment Program (Q-ACE). Q-ACE is designed by Qantas to support students at UNSW to develop professional skills and behaviours by presentations delivered during second year. After completion of Q-ACE, students are provided a pathway at Qantas recruiting to update their aviation experience for employment consideration.

## Program Learning Outcomes (CILOs)
On successful completion of this program, students will be able to:

1. Apply technical interdisciplinary knowledge to successfully operate an aeroplane.
3. Handle and operate an aeroplane safely and to the standard specified by legislation for each qualification of a Commercial Pilot Licence, and Command Instrument Rating Multi-Engine.
5. Meet all applicable Civil Aviation Safety Authority standards for the award of the licences and certificates, as prescribed in Part 61 of the Civil Aviation Safety Regulations.
4. List and recall core theory components within the technical knowledge areas of air navigation, meteorology, aeroplane performance, planning and operation, air law, aerodynamics, aeroplane general knowledge, human factors, and aircraft handling.
2. Locate, utilise and implement documentation and information explaining techniques and standard operating procedures as required for the safe and legal operation of an aeroplane in a commercial operations.

## Professional Recognition & Accreditation
The Flight Screening short course is conducted at the UNSW Flying Operations Unit (http://www.aviation.unsw.edu.au/future/fou.html) at Bankstown Airport and at Kensington. Flight Screening consists of aeronautical knowledge and airborne training and assessment.

The aeronautical knowledge training is AVIA1111 Aeronautical Knowledge – an academic course at Kensington that commences in Term 3.  Students concurrently undertaking the Bachelor of Aviation (Management) program should complete AVIA1111 (12 UoC) as a free elective course.  Students not undertaking Bachelor of Aviation (Management) concurrently complete AVIA1111 classes as part of the Flight Screening enrolment.

The airborne training is for the issue of a Recreational Pilot Licence (RPL) and commences in the break between Term 2 and Term 3. RPL training consists of classroom briefings, pre-flight briefings, airborne flying lessons in DA40 aeroplanes and post-flight briefings. The culmination of the airborne training is the RPL flight test.

The entry requirements for Flight Screening are:

- Completion of Bachelor of Aviation (Management) at UNSW or equivalent, or any AQF level 7 Bachelor degree in a cognate discipline (see note below); OR Satisfactory completion of the first year (48 UoC) of the Bachelor of Aviation (Management) or Bachelor of Aviation (Flying) program at UNSW; AND
- Meet UNSW English Language entry requirements applicable to the Graduate Diploma in Flying; AND
- Be an Australian Citizen or Permanent Resident; AND
- Pass the aptitude selection test.

Applicants should complete the application form at https://www.unsw.edu.au/study/postgraduate/graduate-diploma-in-flying#entryRequirements and will be contacted to arrange the aptitude selection test.

To remain enrolled in Flight Screening, students must successfully obtain an Aviation Reference Number (ARN), hold an Australian Class 1 Aviation Medical Certificate, qualify for an Australian Aviation Security Identification Card (ASIC), and pass a CASA English Language proficiency interview conducted by the Head of Operations of the UNSW Flying Operations Unit.

Note: A cognate discipline is one commonly used as background in managing flying operations.  Cognate disciplines include engineering, physical science, mathematics, psychology, or business.

## Progression & Academic Rules
To remain enrolled in the program, students must maintain their Aviation Security Identification Card and Australian Class 1 Aviation Medical Certificate, and pass random drug and alcohol tests. Students failing to do this may be ineligible to fly and may be required to either take leave or discontinue their program pending discussions with the Head of Operations at the Flying Operations Unit.

## Admission Requirements

Minimum entry requirements for the Graduate Diploma in Flying are that students must:

- Pass the Flight Screening short course

- Hold an Australian Class 1 Aviation Medical Certificate

- Hold an Australian Aviation Security Identification Card

Subject: Aeronautical Knowledge (AVIA1111)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: This course is designed for students with no prior aviation flight experience who are enrolled in programs that include airborne flying training for the award of flight crew licences. Through the use of face-to-face lectures and instruction, students will undertake all aeronautical knowledge training in the units of competency specified in the Civil Aviation Safety Regulations 1998, Part 61 Manual of Standards, for the Recreational and Private Pilot Licences.
Learning Outcomes:
- Define and explain basic meteorological concepts and describe their effect on flight.
- Explain the components, methods of operation and, applicable cockpit indications in relation to single engine aeroplane systems.
- Define and explain basic aerodynamic concepts and describe how these apply to single engine aeroplanes in flight.
- Define and apply visual navigation concepts to day flying and calculate solutions to navigation problems after interpreting navigation data.
- Maintain, interpret, and apply the relevant regulatory documents to safely conduct Day Visual Flight Rules private flight operations.
- Define and explain basic human factors problems that affect Day VFR flying.
- Calculate take-off and landing performance, and determine weight, and balance solutions using typical performance and loading systems.
- Satisfy the theoretical knowledge requirements of the Civil Aviation Safety Regulations 1998, Part 61 Manual of standards, for the Recreational and Private Pilot Licences.

Subject: Flying Operations 1 (AVIG5911)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: This course is the first stage of the theoretical and practical flying training associated with the Commercial Pilot Licence. The theoretical component is taught as three discrete subjects at Commercial Pilot level: Navigation; Meteorology; and Flight Rules & Air Law. The Civil Aviation Safety Regulations 1998 Part 61 Manual of Standards is the primary source of the course syllabus. The material is covered at a theoretical level and then integrated into practical flying. The flying training covers the general and cross-country sequences required for the issue of a Private Pilot Licence.
Learning Outcomes:
- Recall and describe procedures and structures associated with typical private flying operations.
- Display competence measured against relevant professional standards for general and cross-country flying.
- Apply principles of single pilot threat and error management to the conduct of Visual Flight Rules (VFR) flying operations.
- Comprehensively describe the systems and procedures used in single-engine aeroplanes and aeroplanes fitted with CSU and G1000.
- Explain the various factors that underpin private and commercial flying operations.

Subject: Flying Operations 2 (AVIG5912)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: This course is the second stage in theoretical and practical flight training for the Commercial Pilot Licence. The theoretical component is taught as four discrete subjects at Commercial Pilot level. The subjects are Aircraft General Knowledge; Operations, Performance and Flight Planning; Human Factors; and, Aerodynamics. The Civil Aviation Safety Regulations 1998 Part 61 Manual of Standards is the primary source of the course syllabus. The material is covered at a theoretical level and then integrated into practical flying. The flying training covers the night flying and instrument flying elements of the Commercial Pilot syllabus and further consolidates the skills developed previously through solo flying practice.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate a familiarity with procedures and structures associated with a typical organisation that holds an AOC authorising commercial flying operations;
- Demonstrate a sound understanding of the knowledge that underpins private and commercial flying operations by day and night.
- Achieve competence measured against UNSW syllabi for night, instrument and cross country flying.
- Apply principles of single pilot threat and error management to the conduct of VFR flying operations by day and night;
- Demonstrate a comprehensive understanding of the systems and procedures that are used in single-engine aeroplanes and aeroplanes fitted with CSU, G1000 and conventional navigation aids;

Subject: Flying Operations 3 (AVIG5913)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: This course is the final stage of training for the Commercial Pilot Licence including the flight test and the introduction of multi-engine and instrument rating flying. The theoretical component covers the underpinning knowledge for a command instrument rating, including Airways Procedures, Radio Navigation Aids, Meteorology and Satellite Navigation taught at a theoretical level and then integrated into practical operations through flight and ground training. The flying training covers the operational aspects of commercial flying and consolidates the elements of the syllabus previously covered. Following the flight test for the issue of the Commercial Pilot Licence, the practical flying shifts focus to the initial multi-engine class rating and instrument rating with a combination of synthetic trainer and multi-engine flight exercises.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply principles of single pilot threat and error management to the conduct of VFR and IFR flying operations by day and night
- Demonstrate a familiarity with procedures and structures associated with a typical organisation that holds an AOC authorising commercial flying operations under the VFR and IFR
- Achieve competence measured against UNSW syllabi for commercial flights in single and multi-engine aeroplanes under the VFR.
- Demonstrate a comprehensive understanding of the systems and procedures that are used in multi-engine aeroplanes and aeroplanes fitted with CSU, G1000 and conventional navigation aids
- Demonstrate a sound understanding of the knowledge that underpins private and commercial flying operations by day and night under VFR and IFR.

Subject: Flying Operations 4 (AVIG5914)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: This course covers the practical flying of multi-engine aeroplanes in commercial operations in all but extreme weather by day and night. The theory component completed in AVIA5913 is applied practically to the conduct of instrument flight rules flying operations. The practical training is an integrated program of synthetic trainer exercises and flights. Flights are conducted day and night, in all weather conditions (except extreme) culminating in the flight test for the issue of an Instrument Rating.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the systems and procedures that are used in multi-engine aeroplanes, and aeroplanes fitted with CSU, G1000, retractable undercarriage and conventional navigation aids.
- Recall and describe the knowledge that underpins private and commercial flying operations by day and night under the Instrument Flight Rules.
- Explain the procedures and structures associated with a typical organisation that holds an AOC authorising commercial flying operations under the Instrument Flight Rules.
- Demonstrate competence measured against relevant professional standards for general and cross-country flying.
- Apply principles of single pilot threat and error management to the conduct of Instrument Flight Rules (IFR) flying operations by day and night.
