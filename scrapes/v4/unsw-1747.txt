## Description
The Professional Doctorate of Clinical Research in Optometry is an applied research professional degree that is designed specifically for clinicians in optometry or cognate areas. It provides an opportunity to combine a doctoral thesis with the coursework component of relevant Masters programs. Participation in the DCRO will lead to the presentation of a doctoral thesis.

The DCRO may be undertaken on either a part-time or full-time basis.

Programs Structure

The proposed DCRO is designed as a three year full-time equivalent program (3 years minimum, 4 years expected duration), structured as one-third coursework (equivalent to one year full-time) and two-thirds research (equivalent to two years full-time).

Students will undertake 48 UoC of coursework courses selected from relevant programs including the Master of Optometry (8073), or Master of Public Health (9045) or other suitable Level 9 AQF courses.Core courses will include a OPTM7302 Evidence Based Optometry (6 UOC) and an approved statistical training course appropriate for the field of research (6 UOC).

The 2-year research component of the DCRO may be undertaken on a part-time or full-time basis and may be based in the student's clinical practice or place of work. Similar to the PhD in Optometry and PhD in Vision Science, the research consists of independent study supported by an academic supervisor and successful completion requires that the study constitutes an original and significant contribution to knowledge.

See UNSW Higher Degree Research Learning Outcomes.

## Admission Requirements
**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
Standard Entry Pathway:

- A minimum 1 year post-qualifying professional experience in clinical practice;

- A demonstrated capacity for independent research under the guidance of a supervisor at the doctoral level (as defined by the UNSW Higher Degree Research Admissions and Pathways Guideline Version: 1.0 Effective 15 October 2014); and

- A first or upper-second class (Distinction) Honours degree in Optometry or in a cognate discipline.

Advanced Standing Entry Pathway:

- A minimum 1 year post-qualifying professional experience in clinical practice;

- A demonstrated capacity for independent research under the guidance of a supervisor at the doctoral level (as defined by the UNSW Higher Degree Research Admissions and Pathways Guideline Version: 1.0 Effective 15 October 2014); and

- A Masters (Coursework) degree with a distinction average and a significant research component, minimum equivalent 18 UOC consisting of methodology training and a stand-alone research project.

Subject: Immunisation Policy and Practice (PHCM9050)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Vaccines are one of the most effective ways to prevent and control infectious diseases. However, even the most effective vaccine is only successful if people have access to vaccines and vaccinate - vaccines don’t save lives, immunisation does. How do we translate vaccine science into effective immunisation policy? How can public health practitioners effectively communicate the benefits of vaccination to the public, particularly in the digital era? How can we ensure equitable access to vaccines within different socioeconomic contexts? What skills do you, as public health professionals, need to support evidence-based immunisation policy decision-making? In PHCM9050 Immunisation Policy and Practice you will develop an understanding of the complex issues that shape immunisation policy and practice applicable to a range of vaccine-preventable diseases and country contexts. Through the course you will critically analyse key and interrelated components of immunisation policy and their practical application to sustaining successful immunisation programs, including vaccine development, clinical guidelines, methods to monitor and evaluate immunisation program delivery, including immunisation coverage, vaccine effectiveness, and adverse events monitoring, the social and behavioural drivers of immunisation, vaccine equity and access, and strategies to engage communities and address vaccine hesitancy. Through expert lectures, Australian and globally relevant cases studies, and your assessments, you will develop skills in applying the vast immunisation-relevant evidence-base to implementing and evaluating immunisation programs.
Learning Outcomes:
- Examine the concepts of vaccine equity and access, social and behavioural factors, and public perceptions about vaccine safety and effectiveness, and appraise their impact on immunisation coverage in populations
- Recognise and interpret the complex issues shaping immunisation policy and practice, including changing disease epidemiology, vaccine coverage, adverse events and risk communication and social and behavioural factors, to a range of vaccine-preventable diseases and settings
- Appraise the capabilities and constraints of surveillance systems used to monitor and evaluate immunisation program outcomes and impact, including immunisation coverage, vaccine effectiveness, and adverse events following immunisation
- Compare and critique the range of strategies used to address barriers to vaccination that result in suboptimal immunisation coverage across diverse communities
- Apply and integrate basic concepts of vaccine-derived immunity and pre-licensure regulatory processes to the development of immunisation policy and vaccine delivery strategies
- Distinguish the suitability of different immunisation strategies to different vaccine preventable diseases and evaluate their potential impact on disease transmission and disease epidemiology across populations and settings

Subject: Ethics and Law in Public Health (PHCM9331)
Credit Points: 6

Subject: Outbreak Investigation and Intelligence (PHCM9788)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Outbreak investigation is a central aspect of field epidemiology and infectious diseases intelligence and surveillance underpins outbreak identification, response and control. The focus of this course is on understanding routine and unusual disease outbreaks and the application of methods for their detection and investigation and control in resource limited and developed settings. The course uses case studies to teach epidemiologic disease pattern recognition, identification of aberrant patterns, and interpretation of epidemic and surveillance data to inform outbreak investigation and disease control. The course explores a number of outbreaks from around the world in case studies, lectures, discussion forums, webinars and readings to teach principles of outbreak detection, verification, investigation, communication and control. You will learn about outbreak data analysis and interpretation, outbreaks in vulnerable populations as well as the role of the laboratory. An overview of field epidemiology methods and mathematical modeling of infectious diseases will also be provided. Case studies include salmonella, E. coli, enterovirus, hepatitis, avian influenza, MERS coronavirus, Ebola outbreaks and COVID-19.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain the role of disease surveillance and other intelligence in outbreak detection and investigation.
- Analyse outbreak data and interpret diverse and aberrant epidemiological patterns of infectious disease outbreaks.
- Demonstrate understanding of appropriate prevention and control measures for an outbreak response.
- Demonstrate understanding of modelling and forecasting of infectious diseases based on known transmission dynamics.
- Describe best practice principles of outbreak investigations in diverse global settings.
