### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Recognise the communication principles required to effectively communicate with mentors and peers in the sport and recreation industry.
**2.**
Discuss key issues facing the sport and recreation industry  in community, government and commercial contexts.
**3.**
Recognise fundamental skills in the design and delivery of sport and recreation experiences and programs
**4.**
Apply operational knowledge of sport and recreation management as an interdisciplinary field of practice in community, government and commercial contexts.

Subject: WOMINJEKA LA TROBE: INDIGENOUS CULTURAL LITERACY FOR HIGHER EDUCATION (ABS0WOM)
Credit Points: 0
Overview: Wominjeka La Trobe is a short one-hour compulsory online subject that introduces all La Trobe students to Indigenous Australian history, culture and customs. A rich and relevant cultural heritage education is important for everyone. This topic highlights the link between Indigenous knowledge and values and broader cultural literacy. It asks you to think critically about your own attitudes, values and beliefs.
Learning Outcomes:
- Reflect critically on your own cultural attitudes, values and beliefs
- Demonstrate knowledge of and respect for Indigenous Australian perspectives and experiences, history, culture and customs, including an appreciation of the local people and country
- Recall important details regarding the history, culture, customs and people of Indigenous Australia
- Understand the need for and value of appropriate and effective intercultural communication

Subject: HUMAN RESOURCE MANAGEMENT (MGT1003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will explore the strategic and operational issues involved in managing staff in the workplace. You will examine the challenges facing human resource managers in contemporary Australian organisations and seeks to develop an understanding of human resource management practices in our continually changing social, economic, industrial relations and organisational environments. You will explore the reasons underpinning the increasing recognition of human resource management (HRM) within organisations as well as the field's underlying purposes. Throughout this subject an emphasis is placed on a strategic approach to HRM in addition to the functional and operational aspects.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and defend the basic concepts of human resource management from theoretical and applied perspectives, including how these make human resource management a unique source of competitive advantage for contemporary organisations.
- Justify the need for organisations to facilitate the strategic partnership between management and employees
- Critically analyse in teams and individually academic, business and government approaches to a range of Human Resource Management topics

Subject: SPORT MANAGEMENT (MGT1ISM)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will be introduced to the sport industry and the unique features of sport while developing the skills of critical analysis, appraisal, and reflection. You will examine the relationship between sport and society, reflect upon the implication of the UN’s sustainability goals to the sport industry and sport managers, and be encouraged to challenge norms and ideologies. You will also reflect on the continued significance of sport, in Australian culture specifically, while understanding the role of sport managers in being sensitive to that significance and its impact on day-to-day management.
Learning Outcomes:
- Examine the role of sport in society and identify the unique features of sport management and the Australian sport industry
- Apply the theoretical principles of management to sport and sports organisations across the profit, non-profit and public sectors
- Critically assess and challenge the role of the sport manager in responding to sport’s significance in Australian culture
- Develop sport research appraisal skills and understand their application to the role of sport manager

Subject: GROWTH, DEVELOPMENT AND NUTRITION (SPE1002)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will be introduced to foundational concepts of nutrition, growth, and development in sport and exercise science. The subject will provide you with an understanding of growth and development from prenatal life through to older adulthood, factors that influence these processes, and key considerations relevant to each life stage. Additionally, you will be taught the core concepts of human nutrition, including basic nutritional assessment, the components of human nutrition and diet, and their relationship to health and exercise. The subject aims to develop your knowledge and application of how physical activity influences (and is influenced by) growth and development across the lifespan and the role of nutrition in sport and exercise science.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the strengths and limitations of commonly used methods for measuring and analysing dietary intake including implications for general health and wellbeing.
- Describe the stages of growth and development throughout the lifespan, and the factors that affect these.
- Identify and describe the basic function and common sources of macronutrients and micronutrients including their role in energy balance and health.
- Explain the relationship between physical activity and the structural, physiological, motor and cognitive changes occurring throughout the lifespan.
- Use peer-reviewed literature and evidence-based physical activity and exercise principles to interpret lifespan case studies.
- Understand the scope of practice for exercise scientists and use current Australian guidelines to provide general advice on nutrition as appropriate or refer onwards as appropriate.

Subject: FOUNDATIONS OF RECREATION (SPE1004)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will be introduced to the recreation industry and the unique features of recreation and leisure while developing skills of analysis, appraisal, and reflection. You will examine the value and history of society’s engagement in recreation, patterns of consumption of recreation, the role of all tiers of government in facilitating recreation for communities, the structure of the recreation industry, and the challenges and issues involved in providing sustainable, safe and meaningful recreation experiences for consumers and participants. You will reflect on the role of policy makers and recreation managers in shaping the recreation experiences for communities.
Learning Outcomes:
- Evaluate the role of policy makers and recreation managers in shaping the recreation experiences for communities.
- Analyse the role of all tiers of government in facilitating recreation for communities, and the challenges and issues involved in providing sustainable, safe and meaningful recreation experiences for consumers and participants.
- Examine the unique features of recreation management and the structure of the Australian recreation industry.
- Examine the value and history of society’s engagement in recreation, the role of recreation in society, and patterns of consumption of recreation.

Subject: FOUNDATIONS OF SPORT (SPE1005)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will be introduced to the sport industry and its unique features while developing skills of analysis, appraisal, and reflection. You will examine the value and role of sport in society, trends in the consumption of professional and elite sport, participation trends in organized sport, the role of government in facilitating access to sport for communities, the structure and governance of the sport industry, and the challenges and issues involved in providing sustainable, safe and meaningful sport experiences. You will reflect on the role of policy makers and sport managers in influencing the sport experiences for communities.

- Analyse the role of government in facilitating sport opportunities for communities, and the challenges and issues involved in providing sustainable, safe and meaningful sport experiences.
- Evaluate the role of policy makers and sport governing bodies in shaping the sport experiences for individuals and communities.
- Examine the value and history of sport’s role in society, and the unique features of sport and patterns of consumption of professional and elite sport, as well as trends in sport participation.
- Examine the unique features of sport management and the structure of the Australian and international sport industry.

Subject: SPORT AND RECREATION PROGRAM DESIGN (SPE1006)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject you will be introduced to the principles of designing sport and recreation programs for a range of participants and client groups. Sport and recreation programs are one of the key mechanisms for consumers to experience a variety of recreation services and this subject provides students with the knowledge and information to develop, plan, and document sport and recreation programs to different client groups.
Learning Outcomes:
- Develop a program plan for a sport and recreation program, event or activity.
- Undertake a needs analysis and feasibility assessment to inform the design of a sport and recreation program
- Employ the Rossman and Schlatter Program Development Cycle to designing sport and recreation programs.

Subject: CONTEMPORARY ISSUES IN SPORT COACHING (SPE1CSC)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, you will be introduced to contemporary social and cultural factors that have a bearing on coaching and exercise science practice, with a particular emphasis on sport coaching. These may include issues around; barriers to participation; violence and off-field conduct; gender, culture, race, and religion; social media usage; data and information storage, usage and privacy; and winning and ethics. You will be asked to reflect on contemporary issues and problem-solve specific scenarios from a number of perspectives.
Learning Outcomes:
- Working collaboratively in a group to explore, analyse and review a contemporary issue in sport, and present a collective, evidence-based, and supported view on the issue.
- Explain ways in which social issues are addressed in sport coaching.
- Discuss the key current social issues relevant to participation and competition in sport.
- Discuss the most relevant approaches used to understand social issues and ethical practice in applied sport coaching.
- Analyse contemporary case studies of relevant social issues in sport coaching practice.

Subject: GENERAL PRINCIPLES OF SPORT COACHING (SPE1GSC)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: You will be introduced to the profession of sports coaching and will be provided with a contextual model that will include the history and evolution of sports coaching, styles of coaching and lesson planning. Practical sessions will involve observation and evaluation techniques to self-evaluate coaching styles and effectiveness in coaching athletes at different levels. At the completion of this subject, you will be expected to hold a Community Coaching General Principles accreditation (or have proof of accreditation) from the Australian Sports Commission. This subject includes professional learning, practical experiences or portfolio activities which contribute to the completion of the Career Ready AdvantageTM award.
Learning Outcomes:
- Use a range of communication, risk mitigation and behaviour management strategies to facilitate participants' performance development.
- Demonstrate a range of communication skills relevant to the delivery of sports coaching sessions in training and competition.
- Apply foundational principles of coaching that inform coaching session plans to meet the needs of participants and achieve performance goals in sport.
- Collaborate in small groups to design and deliver a coaching session
- Review planned coaching sessions to identify strategies to assist in improving session designs and coaching practices.
