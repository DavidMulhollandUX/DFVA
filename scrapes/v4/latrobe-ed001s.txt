##### ED001S

You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
If you’re looking for a pathway to an accredited teaching degree, La Trobe’s Diploma of Teacher Education is the perfect opportunity to take your first step towards a rewarding career in teaching.

When you successfully complete the Diploma of Teacher Education, you could progress to a Bachelor of Education (Primary), Bachelor of Education (Secondary) or Bachelor of Education (Early Childhood and Primary). You’ll also receive advanced standing for up to one year of full-time study.

You’ll develop solid foundations in digital learning, literacy, numeracy and learning contexts – skills that will serve you throughout your teaching career. Immerse yourself in the full university experience, studying alongside Bachelor students in the same subjects.

We're at your side every step of the way. Receive personalised advice from a dedicated Student Adviser and connect with discipline-based peer mentors. We'll help you enrol, choose your subjects and navigate university systems and requirements. You can also access the university-wide careers service for advice and assistance on your resume, interview skills and career planning.

You'll learn:

  * Digital learning  * Explore the world of digital learning in a way that's creative, collaborative and innovative.

  * Learning contexts  * Learn how to create safe, diverse learning environments for people of all ages and backgrounds.

  * Literacy and numeracy
  * Understand key concepts and conventions of the English language and numeracy.

Subject: INTRODUCTION TO LANGUAGE AND LITERACY (EDU1001)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject engages students with skills required for academic language and literacy purposes at an introductory level for tertiary students. The subject explores the components and conventions of spoken and written English, as well as the developmental stages of language and literacy learning. An introduction to the English writing system provides a framework for students to gain preliminary knowledge about literacy instruction in the classroom. Students will be taught to apply skills in the (i) use of professional oral communication, (ii) critical appraisal of academic sources and (iii) academic writing skills. The subject is scaffolded through a hurdle diagnostic test where students will establish areas of strength and areas that require development.
Learning Outcomes:
- Define the components and conventions of spoken language and literacy at an introductory level.
- Demonstrate proficiency in speaking and writing in English for formal academic purposes at tertiary level.
- Critically appraise academic texts at an introductory level.
- Explain the processes of language development and proficiency in literacy and identify key differences between them.

Subject: HOW HUMANS DEVELOP (EDU1002)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject introduces preservice teachers to major theories and features of human development from infancy to late adolescence, from biopsychosocial perspectives. They will learn how the human brain typically develops from infancy to late adolescence, how neurological differences contribute to neurodiversity, and ways in which neurological changes (over time) and differences (between learners) impact on cognitive and socio-emotional development. This subject also examines how socio-ecological influences, including family structure, First Nations cultures, cultural capital, and trauma, impact on learner development. A focus throughout the subject is on the implications for teacher practice. Finally, this subject introduces basic cognitive science concepts that are important for teachers and educators to understand, to optimise student learning.
Learning Outcomes:
- Demonstrate an emerging understanding of the brain’s information processing and memory systems and their role in learning.
- Describe the influence of socio-ecological factors on infant, child, and adolescent development, and the implications for educational practice.
- Describe how the human brain typically develops from infancy to late adolescence and identify neurological differences associated with neurodiversity, and the implications for educational practice.
- Describe physical, cognitive, and socio-emotional developmental milestones that often occur in infancy, early and middle childhood, and adolescence, and their implications for educational practice.

Subject: DIGITAL LITERACIES (EDU1003)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: Through this subject, students will develop core knowledge and skills to learn through, with and about digital platforms, especially as they apply to their future teaching careers. They will learn to become critical and literate users of digital technologies in education, and to better understand the impact of digital technologies on the lives of their future students, both within and beyond the classroom. The implications of emerging technologies including AI will be explored, including ethical, legal and cognitive aspects. Students will undertake hands-on exploration of a range of educational technologies and begin to establish an evidence-based repertoire of practices for digitally-informed learning.
Learning Outcomes:
- Collaboratively illustrate ethical concerns in digital spaces using multimodal and visual literacies.
- Appraise contemporary research and demonstrate a foundational understanding of the relevant issues and the strategies available to support the safe, responsible and ethical use of ICT in learning.
- Demonstrate knowledge of a range of digital skills and tools and how these can be used in accessing, processing and communicating information.
- Build a professional digital presence applicable to providing evidence of learning.

Subject: FRAMING CULTURAL IDENTITIES (EDU1004)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This is a cornerstone subject in which first year education students develop core knowledge, skills and dispositions required to be equity-oriented teachers. The subject embeds intercultural understanding and the development and deepening of students’ understanding of the diversity of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander languages, societies, cultures, world views, histories and contemporary experiences. Students examine their own cultural identities, representations of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities and become critically reflective of the historical circumstances that have impacted on people in Australia. Students will investigate policies, programs and practices of direct concern to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people.
Learning Outcomes:
- Illustrate how media representations and personal histories and experiences shape and influence perceptions of and assumptions about Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples and their community life, and how this impacts on Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander students' attitudes towards school and education.
- Critically reflect on ethics, beliefs, and emerging teaching philosophies relating to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander students' culture and education to understand how to address the impact of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander culture on students.
- Examine significant events impacting on Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people, as well as current rights-based approaches, educational policy and practices related to Indigenous education.
- Demonstrate knowledge of and respect for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander cultures and histories, along with understanding of the impact of culture, cultural identity and linguistic background on the education of students from Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander backgrounds.
- By exploring theories of cultural identity, learn how Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islanders' cultural identity and linguistic background play a role in shaping their understanding of education and how to productively engage in challenging conversations around equity and diversity for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander education.

Subject: UNDERSTANDING AND PLANNING FOR LEARNING (EDU1005)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject introduces students to foundational understandings of curriculum, pedagogy and evaluating learning. Students engage with key concepts in curriculum and the influence of knowledge, ideology, and stakeholders in curriculum frameworks. Building on this knowledge, students develop an understanding of teaching and learning through analysis and deconstruction of the lesson plan to gain a sense of defining and sequencing learning. Using the framework of Universal Design for Learning (UDL) students understand how to sequence learning experiences that include all learners across a range of contexts, including digital learning environments. Students engage with a variety of worked examples to understand best practice for planning tasks and lessons, with a focus on explicit modelling. Finally, students begin to evaluate student learning and consider the role of feedback and coaching conversations and justifying their pedagogical approaches in teaching in impactful learning environments.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and evaluate the elements of a lesson plan.
- Design learning sequences that align with universal design for learning principles.
- Demonstrate an understanding of the curriculum, pedagogy and assessment cycle.
- Evaluate student data for the purpose of providing feedback and setting learning goals that provide achievable challenges for students of varying abilities and characteristics.
- Demonstrate integration of digital tools to enhance learning experiences.

Subject: UNDERSTANDING SELF, RELATIONSHIPS AND LEARNING ENVIRONMENTS (EDU1006)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject will support pre-service teachers to develop as critically reflective practitioners with the capacity to develop productive relationships and establish positive learning environments. Pre-service teachers develop a strength-based approach to developing productive relationships with members of their classroom and school community, and to understanding and valuing the perspectives of diverse groups including EAL/D, neurodivergent, gender-fluid, and First Nations students. They will develop an understanding of how positive relationships impact student behaviour and learning. Pre-service teachers learn about and plan to implement proactive practices that support productive classroom relationships, including creating safe and predictable learning environments, setting high expectations for learning and behaviour, and using specific positive feedback. Pre-service teachers also learn about and rehearse classroom management strategies for maintaining positive relationships, including modelling desired behaviour, use of pre-corrections, and verbal and non-verbal responses. Finally, this subject examines the role of teacher relationships with family, community, and other professions in maintaining positive learning environments, learning effective ways to engage with family, and the positive impact of engaging with community.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe the evidence showing the impact of relationships on behaviour and learning, and identify and plan proactive practices to cultivate positive relationships and positive, culturally safe, learning environments.
- Describe self-concept, classroom and community diversity, and how biases, assumptions and worldviews can impact both learner and teacher behaviours and attitudes in the classroom.
- Explain and plan classroom management strategies for maintaining positive relationships, including modelling desired behaviour, setting and reinforcing expectations, use of pre-corrections, verbal and non-verbal responses, and use of calm, expected and escalating responses.
- Describe effective practices for engaging with colleagues, families, community, and other professionals, and explain how these can contribute to maintaining positive learning environments.

Subject: NUMERACY IN EDUCATION (EDU1007)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: This subject provides the opportunity to identify and practise the numeracy knowledge and skills needed to understand how mathematical information is used in meaningful real-world contexts to make decisions. Emphasis is put on supporting learners’ development of mathematical critical thinking, reasoning, questioning, and problem-solving. Strategies are developed for teaching the key aspects of numeracy applicable to the various learning areas of the Australian Curriculum.
Learning Outcomes:
- Apply critical numeracy skills to identify the way in which mathematics is conveyed in media reports and other texts.
- Explain how answers to context-based mathematical problems are determined.
- Design numeracy teaching strategies and identify their application in various teaching areas.
- Illustrate mathematical skills through modelling, problem solving, and argumentation.

Subject: BECOMING AN ETHICAL EDUCATION PROFESSIONAL (EDU1008)
Credit Points: 15
Overview: In this subject, pre-service teachers (PSTs) will gain an introduction to what it means to become an ethical education professional. In doing so, PSTs will learn about formal codes and ethics that regulate the profession, as well as informal ethics and education ethical theories. PSTs will learn how to recognise different values and beliefs, including their own, and how these can affect professional practice. They will learn how they can use this information to bring about ethically informed outcomes in their future educational decision-making processes. PSTs will develop skills to critically reflect on their own values and beliefs and how these could impact their future students, parents/carers and members of the community. PSTs undertake service-learning in their local communities to engage with diverse groups to learn more about their own values and beliefs, and those held by other people.
Learning Outcomes:
- Articulate a professional ethical position in relation to education.
- Demonstrate knowledge of education ethics theories and approaches.
- Demonstrate critical awareness of own values and beliefs, and how this can affect professional practice as an educator.
- Demonstrate understanding of diverse values and beliefs in the community.
- Demonstrate knowledge of codes of conduct and ethics for education professionals.

Subject: ACADEMIC INTEGRITY MODULE (LTU0AIM)
Credit Points: 0
Overview: The academic integrity module introduces academic integrity standards to inform and support students in avoiding plagiarism and academic misconduct. Students complete four parts that encompass academic misconduct and academic integrity decisions, including cheating, plagiarism, and collusion. Students learn about Turnitin (the text-matching tool) used at La Trobe University, and how to get help and support their development of referencing skills.
Learning Outcomes:
- Understand the importance of the acknowledgment and the referencing of sources
- Locate, select, and utilise information about La Trobe's approach to academic integrity to support appropriate acknowledgement of references used.
- Recognise and understand academic integrity responsibilities.
