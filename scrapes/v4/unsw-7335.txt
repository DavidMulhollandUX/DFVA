## Description
The Graduate Certificate in Mining Engineering offered by the School of Minerals and Energy Resources Engineering is designed to provide a flexible entry point for individuals with qualifications in other technical disciplines or those who possess relevant professional experience in the mining industry. This program is ideal for individuals who wish to transition into mining engineering, enhance their existing skills, or gain specialised knowledge in mining engineering without committing to a longer qualification.

This postgraduate certificate is particularly suited to individuals with relevant industry experience or professional experience in the mining sector who seek to formalise their knowledge and skills with a recognised academic qualification. By offering a more flexible entry mode, the Graduate Certificate enables students to enter the field of mining engineering and pursue further education if desired, such as progressing into the Graduate Diploma or Master of Mining Engineering.

Upon successful completion of the Graduate Certificate, students will be awarded 24 Units of Credit (UOC). The program can be pursued either full-time or part-time, with flexible delivery options that accommodate the schedules of working professionals. Courses are typically delivered in block mode, either face-to-face or online, providing intensive, short-term study blocks that allow students to continue their professional commitments while enhancing their qualifications. Some courses may also be offered fully online, providing additional flexibility for those unable to attend in-person sessions.

## Program Structure & Degree Requirements
Students must complete 24 Units of Credit (UOC) structured across the following academic components:

Students must take 24 UOC of the following courses.

- **GSOE9010**: Engineering Postgraduate Coursework Research Skills [6 UOC]

## Progression & Academic Rules
Students may be eligible to articulate to a Graduate Diploma in Mining Engineering and then to a Master of Mining Engineering depending upon satisfactory academic progress. This usually requires maintaining a program WAM of 65.

## Admission Requirements
**Limitations on Recognition of Prior Learning**:
No advanced standing (RPL) will be allowed in this program.

**Minimum Entry Requirements**:
Entry to the UNSW Graduate Certificate in Mining Engineering requires one of the following:

- A minimum of 4 years of full-time relevant professional and/or responsibility in the mining industry.

- A Bachelor degree in other technical disciplines from a recognised institution. The UNSW School of Minerals and Energy Resources Engineering defines other technical disciplines as Surveying, Accounting, Business, Economics, Information Technology/Systems, Biology and Pharmacy for example. A minimum of 1-year relevant industry experience is essential for applicants with a 3-year Bachelor degree and is highly regarded for applicants with a 4-year Bachelor degree. Entry is considered for each individual based on portfolio supporting the application, including details of relevant industry experience. Relevant industry experience is defined by the UNSW School of Minerals and Energy Resources Engineering as professional and/or industry responsibility in mining or mining associated services.

The Graduate Certificate in Mining Engineering can be an entry point for obtaining foundational qualifications in Mining Engineering, and for articulating to a Graduate Diploma in Mining Engineering and a Master of Mining Engineering, with a program WAM of 65.

Subject: Mine Processes, Systems and Analysis (MINE8115)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The course provides an introduction to the generic concepts of process management and systems engineering, and develops models for how these techniques can be applied to different mining systems. Through case studies using a selection of mining methods, the elements of typical mining operations are examined as a system of complex and inter-related processes and systems. These are analysed to identify system dependencies, quantitative sensitivities, key drivers and management needs. On completion of this course students should be able to apply a systems approach in both the planning and ongoing management of a mining operation.
Learning Outcomes:
- Determine appropriate management strategies for each major component of a generic mining system.
- Demonstrate a working knowledge of all major generic mining methods.
- Identify dependencies and carry out quantitative sensitivity analyses within an overall mining system, and hence determine and quantify key drivers influencing performance of the overall mining system.
- Describe, apply and analyse a mining method/operation in terms of an array of inter-related processes and systems.

Subject: Hazard Identification, Risk and Safety Management in Mining (MINE8120)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: Welcome to MINE8120 Hazard Identification, Risk and Safety Management in Mining. This course looks at the principles of risk management and risk management concepts (such as losses, hazards, risks, controls/barriers, and risk analysis and assessment techniques) primarily in mining. Risk management systems and the integration of these systems into other management processes also form a major component of this course, which makes effective use of relevant case study material. The course will also introduce the core geotechnical risks in underground and open cut mining, and how geotechnical risks are managed.
Learning Outcomes:
- Explain and apply the hierarchy of control to core mining risks, and evaluate how control measures are implemented across different mining methods and environments.
- Apply key risk assessment techniques used in the mining industry to perform hazard analysis and carry out a structured risk assessment process.
- Identify and evaluate a broad range of hazards across multiple levels (e.g. mining industry, individual mine sites, and specific mining methods), and explain how these are managed through practical risk management strategies in mining.
- Recognise common mine safety hazards and propose effective control measures using geotechnical training, engineering techniques, and safe operating procedures.
