## Master of Clinical Simulation
##### M6039
##### 72 credit points
You are viewing the 2026 version

### Overview
The Master of Clinical Simulation qualifies you to apply principles and theories that inform educational practice in a range of clinical simulation and other relevant contexts to undertake professional and highly skilled work and as educators and simulationists in the health professions. This practice-based qualification builds on the existing Graduate Certificate of Clinical Simulation. It is suitable for a broad range of health professionals and others who are, or will be, engaged in simulation-based education. This includes the teaching, assessment, evaluation, curriculum design, scenario design, debriefing, mentoring and supervision of health professionals in a range of clinical and other contexts. The qualification is also suited to health professionals who use simulation for healthcare service quality improvement or systems testing. However, the use of simulation as an educational method is prioritised. You have the option of taking an education practice or a research practice capstone. The research capstone can articulate to a PhD.

### Structure
This course is structured into three parts: Part A. Core studies, Part B. Applied studies, and Part C. Specified elective studies. Each part comprises a combination of units and short courses.

Part A. Core studies

This study will provide you with professional teaching and advanced supervisory skills in clinical and other learning environments using simulation as an educational method. It will facilitate the application of principles and theories that inform simulation practices and specialist knowledge through the choice of elective studies.

Part B. Applied studies

You have the opportunity to choose one of the following pathways:

a) Coursework pathway – in this pathway, you will undertake a capstone unit and combine it with elective units to suit your career interests. In the capstone unit you will have the option of focusing on either education practice or research practice and will be required to integrate, extend and apply knowledge and skills gained in the course to professional practice development.

b) Research pathway – in this pathway you will undertake research in a health professions sciences unit and complete a research project. You will be required to demonstrate your ability to integrate and apply theories, principles and practice to a specific clinical simulation scenario or issue. Your choice of project must be aligned and approved by the Course Director prior to commencement of both Introduction to Education Research in the Health Professions and Sciences and MAP5010.

Part C. Specified elective studies

In this study, you will have the opportunity to expand your key areas of interest through completion of electives that will develop skills and knowledge of simulation for health professions education. Electives comprise assessed short courses and approved units.

List of short courses:

  * Learning and teaching with simulation
  * Quality supervision course
  * Scenario design for simulation-based education
  * Learning conversations: Feedback and debriefing practices in clinical simulation
  * Assessment in health professions education
  * Introduction to education research in the health professions and sciences
  * Qualitative research methods for public health
  * Fundamentals of clinical coaching

Note:

  * You will receive specified credit for assessed short courses that you completed successfully prior to your enrolment in this course.
  * Not all short courses are available every semester.
  * The above short courses are not eligible for FEE-HELP.

Master's entry points

Depending on prior qualifications you may receive entry level credit (a form of block credit) which determines your point of entry to the course:

  * If you are admitted at entry level 1 you complete 72 credit points, comprising Part A, Part B and Part C.
  * If you are admitted at entry level 2 you complete 48 credit points, comprising Part A and Part B.

Note: If you are eligible for credit for prior studies you may elect not to receive the credit and complete one of the higher credit-point options.

### Course intended learning outcomes
On successful completion you will be able to:
**1.**
Evaluate educational research related to educational simulation
**2.**
Develop a personal professional learning strategy to support ongoing practice
**3.**
Apply mentoring and supervision strategies to support safe clinical practice
**4.**
Evaluate learning theories and how they may impact and inform educational practice in simulation
**5.**
Identify and apply principles of curriculum design, and innovative educational approaches to meet learning objectives
**6.**
Evaluate and practice feedback strategies to support learning
**7.**
Review contemporary issues in healthcare simulation
**8.**
Plan, design, implement, assess and evaluate a simulation-based educational programme
**9.**
Critically analyse learner-centred education theories in simulation and articulate a personal philosophy
**10.**
Construct and evaluate a simulation-based educational initiative using sound educational theoretical principles

### AHEGS
The Master of Clinical Simulation is a postgraduate award. Graduates have developed the required educational skills, knowledge and attributes required of health professionals in simulation-based education. They have the skills to design educational curriculum and an understanding of teaching, assessment, evaluation, mentoring and supervision theories. The Master of Clinical Simulation is a Level 9 Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) qualification.  The minimum requirements for admission are set out in the Monash University (Academic Board) Regulations.

Subject: Foundations of health professions education: Clinical Simulation (HCS5100)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: This unit will introduce students to theoretical and practical aspects of learning and teaching. After reflecting on their educational philosophy, participants will apply theories, concepts, terms and processes of curriculum design to their own learning contexts. Students will then implement, assess and evaluate learning activities in selected clinical and other relevant settings.
Learning Outcomes:
- Describe and apply theories and concepts that inform simulation-based educational practice.
- Articulate a personal philosophy of learner-centred education
- Develop a strategy for further learning as a health professional educator.
- Describe and appraise contemporary issues in healthcare simulation.
- Plan, design, assess and evaluate a simulation-based education programme.
- Analyse and apply principles of curriculum design to a simulation program.
- Analyse approaches to feedback and debriefing in healthcare simulation
Assessment Tasks:
- Written assignment critical analysis (2,000 words): 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Written assignment reflection (1,000 words): 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Written assignment lesson plan (3,000 words): 40% (Hurdle: None)
- Multi-media presentation (10 minutes recording): 20% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: This unit will be delivered entirely online through the Monash Moodle learning management system. Activities include readings, pre-recorded lectures, online discussions, interactive sessions via Zoom cloud conferencing, self-directed learning and completion of assignments. Students will be expected to engage online in a unit related workshop, 3 hours a fortnight. Additional directed study (readings, engagement in online discussions, and reflective written work will comprise 104 hours).

Subject: Health professions and simulation education in practice (HPE5200)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: This unit will provide you with the opportunity to explore, design and implement an educational programme or concept, building on the skills and knowledge gained throughout the degree. This unit will present advanced concepts in health professions and simulation education and equip you to implement a new education program or conduct an educational research activity. You will be allocated a mentor to support them through the practical aspect of the unit.
Learning Outcomes:
- Analyse and synthesise contemporary issues in health professions education or clinical simulation using evidence-based literature.
- Design and implement an educational initiative in a health care or training context.
- Develop strategies for continued professional development as a health professions or clinical simulation educator.
- Practice reflection on educational leadership.
- Effectively communicate topics and issues related to health professions education and/or clinical simulation in written and oral formats.
- Appraise educational research using accepted appraisal tools for a range of different research methods and methodologies.
- Create an evaluation plan for a health professions educational initiative.
Assessment Tasks:
- Literature review (3,000 words): 30% (Hurdle: None)
- Teaching philosophy/educational leadership plan (1,500 words): 10% (Hurdle: None)
- Presentation (Individual x 15 mins + 10 mins Q and A): 10% (Hurdle: None)
- Educational program blueprint (1,500 words): 10% (Hurdle: None)
- Program implementation and evaluation report (4,000 words): 40% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: This unit will be delivered entirely online through the Monash Moodle learning management system. Activities include readings, online asynchronous lectures, online discussions, and interactive sessions via Zoom. There is also self-directed learning and completion of assignments. There will be 12 hours of teacher directed study per week, including pre-recorded lecture, online interactive workshops and tutorial classes. 12 hours per week of self-directed study including readings, engagement in online discussions, and reflective written work. Total per week = 24 hours.

Subject: Advanced health practice research project (MAP5010)
Credit Points: 12
Overview: MAP5010 is designed to allow you to implement the research proposal developed in the prerequisite unit (MAP5000 - Research in Advanced Health Care Practice). You will develop skills in gathering data, interrogating it using acceptable analytical processes, and developing an informed and defensible conclusion. Through an iterative and collaborative intellectual process with your supervisor, you will create either a written research report or a ready-for-submission journal article. The work will be also be presented orally to an academic and professional audience. Successful completion of MAP5000 and MAP5010 with an overall grade of 70 Distinction or above may permit a student to apply for a graduate research program including the PhD at Monash University.
Learning Outcomes:
- Synthesise critical arguments in relation to the existing literature about the topic.
- Manage time and resources independently to achieve predetermined goals.
- Implement persuasive data collection and management strategies, ensuring data integrity, ethical and legal responsibilities are maintained..
- Justify findings and generate defensible conclusions with respect to the existing literature on the chosen topic.
- Substantiate how writing skills have evolved overtime to meet academic standards associated within own thesis and/or publications.
- Communicate findings and impacts of research to a variety of audiences using different modalities.
- Interpret and justify implications and their significance in the context of the research question.
Assessment Tasks:
- Co-signed revised semester plan, including timeline and goal checkpoints (Pass/Fail): N/A% (Hurdle: Competency)
- Supervisory Partnership Agreement (Pass/Fail): N/A% (Hurdle: Competency)
- Draft Report (Pass/Fail): N/A% (Hurdle: Competency)
- Supervisory Meetings - documented (Pass/Fail): N/A% (Hurdle: Competency)
- Research report (8000-10000 words): 80% (Hurdle: Competency)

Workload: Minimum 1 hour per week consultation with supervisor either face-to-face or electronically, 11 hours per week directed research activities which may include on-line tasks (project dependent), 12 hours per week for self-directed study.

Subject: Improving Indigenous equity in professional practice (MAP5200)
Credit Points: 6
Overview: The aim of this unit is to develop and strengthen your practical skills to improve health equity for Indigenous peoples. Within this unit you will increase your knowledge about the five graduate capabilities of the Australian Government’s Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Health Curriculum Framework. This includes the capabilities of respect, reflection, advocacy, communication and safety and quality. This unit provides you with the opportunity to learn about one of the oldest continuing cultures in the world and create practical strategies to improve equity in healthcare. You will engage in online learning that features a diverse range of First Peoples via videos and podcast stories. You will also engage in reflection journaling and have opportunities to participate in online tutorials. Using design thinking techniques, you will additionally create a response to an Indigenous health equity challenge.
Learning Outcomes:
- Create advocacy strategies for equitable outcomes and social justice for Indigenous peoples.
- Reflect on culturally appropriate communication and relationship building with Indigenous peoples.
- Examine and reflect on how one’s own culture and dominant cultural paradigms, influence perceptions of and interactions with Indigenous peoples.
- Recognise Indigenous ways of knowing being and doing in relation to history, culture and diversity.
- Create wise practice approaches to Indigenous equity.
Assessment Tasks:
- Report (3,000 words): 50% (Hurdle: Competency)
- Critical reflection (7-min video): 20% (Hurdle: None)
- Reflective Journal (2,000 words): 30% (Hurdle: None)
Workload: Off-campus: a minimum of 10 hours of personal study per week.
